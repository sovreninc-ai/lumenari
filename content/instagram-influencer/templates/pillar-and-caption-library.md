# Pillar Framework + Caption Library + Story Sequences + Brand Pitch

Full templates with worked examples. Drop them into the AI when you need that specific artifact. The worked examples show what good looks like — if your draft doesn't feel like the example, the input was too thin.

---

## 1. Content pillar framework

### Prompt

```
Define 3-5 content pillars for an Instagram creator.

Niche in one specific phrase: [creator fills in — not "wellness" but
"the gap between therapy-speak and what actually helps overworked moms"]
Follower count: [X]
ER: [X%]
Voice in 2-3 adjectives: [creator fills in]
What they sell: [specific revenue line]

Past posts they're proud of (5-10 examples):
[creator pastes captions or describes the posts]

Past posts that flopped (3-5 examples):
[creator pastes or describes]

For each pillar produce:
1. Pillar name — a phrase the creator could say in 5 seconds at a party
2. Who this pillar is for — specific follower type
3. What kind of post lives here — feed, Reel, Story, or all three
4. 5 example post ideas, specific enough to film this week
5. Frequency

Check at the end: do the pillars together add up to a sustainable
posting cadence?
```

### Worked example output

```
Niche: the gap between therapy-speak and what actually helps overworked moms
Creator voice: warm but no-nonsense, occasionally dry, never preachy
What she sells: a $97 digital workbook + sleep/mental health brand partnerships

PILLAR 1 — "The thing therapy doesn't tell you"
For: women who've been to therapy and found it useful but incomplete —
specifically about parenting under pressure
Posts: feed (carousel) + occasional Reel
5 example posts to film this week:
1. Carousel: "What 'set a boundary' actually looks like when your toddler
   is screaming at 6:47 AM"
2. Reel: "The phrase my therapist used for 18 months that I finally
   realized was unhelpful"
3. Carousel: "The difference between regulation and suppression — with
   real examples"
4. Reel: "Why 'sit with the feeling' is bad advice when you have 14
   minutes before school pickup"
5. Carousel: "3 therapy concepts that work better when you reverse them"
Frequency: 2x/week

PILLAR 2 — "What I actually did this week"
For: women who want proof that the framework comes from a real life, not
a curated platform
Posts: Stories (primary) + occasional behind-scenes feed post
5 example posts:
1. Story sequence: a real Tuesday morning, photographed honestly, with
   the therapy concepts she used annotated
2. Feed (behind-scenes): "Here's what the inside of my workbook
   editor looks like at 11pm"
3. Story sequence: the bad parenting moment she had Sunday and what she
   actually did
4. Feed (behind-scenes): the photo of her own bedside table — books,
   notebook, evidence of the same struggle
5. Story sequence: a real conversation with her own therapist, paraphrased
Frequency: Stories daily, feed behind-scenes 1x/week

PILLAR 3 — "The workbook in action"
For: women who are considering buying the workbook or have already bought it
Posts: Reels + carousel
5 example posts:
1. Reel: a 30-second walkthrough of one specific page and why it works
2. Carousel: the 5 questions in the workbook that women report
   coming back to most
3. Reel: a customer's hand-written annotation she shared (with permission)
4. Carousel: "What the workbook is NOT" — counter-positioning against
   competitors
5. Reel: a real moment of using the workbook live
Frequency: 1x/week

PILLAR 4 — "Partner brands I actually use"
For: women who trust her recommendations because she only takes brands
that fit
Posts: feed + stories
5 example posts:
1. Feed: a sleep brand integration framed around a real bedtime moment
2. Stories: a 5-frame integration of a mental health brand's product
3. Feed: a carousel review of a product she's used for a year (the
   year-mark is the credibility lever)
4. Stories: a quick comparison between two products in the same category
5. Feed: a "what I'd buy again" round-up at the end of a quarter
Frequency: 2-4x/month (depending on partnership pipeline)

CADENCE CHECK:
- Pillar 1: 2x/week = 8/month
- Pillar 2: Stories daily + 4 feed/month
- Pillar 3: 1x/week = 4/month
- Pillar 4: 2-4x/month

Feed total: ~16-20 posts/month, ~4-5 per week. Stories: daily.
This is sustainable for a creator with a part-time VA or 1-2
half-days/week of content work. If she's solo with no help, recommend
cutting Pillar 3 to 2x/month.

---
Things to verify before committing:
- The pillar names need to feel like HER — read them out loud
- Pillar 4 (partner brands) only works if there are 2-3 brands she's
  comfortable being publicly associated with — confirm
- The "what therapy doesn't tell you" framing is direct — confirm she's
  comfortable being read by therapists in her audience
```

## 2. Caption rewrites — three styles, same image

The same photo (a creator in her home office at night, surrounded by workbook page proofs) gets three completely different captions.

### Style A: Storytelling

```
Tuesday at 11:43 pm.

The workbook I've been editing for ten months is almost done. I'm three
proofs into a section called "When the coping skills aren't cutting it"
and I just realized the part I'm most proud of is the page I almost cut
six times.

It's the page where I admit that "regulate before you respond" is
sometimes a thing you do AFTER the response, not before. That the
sequencing the books promise — calm, choose, act — is not how a Tuesday
6:47 am actually works in a house with two kids and an inbox.

I almost cut it because it felt too honest. But every reader I gave the
draft to circled that page.

The page stays.

Pre-orders open Thursday at 8 am Mountain. Workbook ships digitally; the
link goes live in the highlight called "workbook" Thursday morning.

(187 words)
```

### Style B: Educational

```
Three things "regulate before you respond" gets wrong about real life:

1. The sequencing assumes you have time. At 6:47 am with a toddler
   screaming and the school bus in 14 minutes, you don't have a regulate
   window. You have a response window.

2. It assumes the regulation tools work fast. The breathing techniques
   in most parenting books need 3-5 minutes to actually shift your
   nervous system. Most parents have 30 seconds.

3. It treats the response as the failure point. Sometimes the response
   IS the regulation — you act, you survive, and you regulate AFTER. The
   sequence is reversed.

The page in the workbook I keep coming back to is the one that admits
this. It's been in and out of the draft six times. I just decided —
sitting at my desk at 11:43 pm Tuesday — that it stays.

Pre-orders Thursday 8 am Mountain. Link in the "workbook" highlight.

(155 words)
```

### Style C: Behind-the-scenes

```
The view from my desk at 11:43 pm.

Three proofs of the workbook, all marked up in red because I keep changing
the order of two pages. The page I'm trying to figure out where to put
is the one where I admit that the "regulate before you respond" framing
doesn't actually work in a Tuesday morning kitchen.

Every reader I gave the draft to circled that page. So it stays. The
question is whether it goes at the start (set the frame) or in the middle
(after you trust me a little). I think it goes at page 23, but ask me
again at midnight.

Pre-orders Thursday morning. I'll be asleep by then.

(122 words)
```

## 3. Five-frame story sequence — product launch

Worked example for the same workbook launch above.

```
SEQUENCE: workbook pre-order launch day
Timing: Thursday morning, dropped between 7:55-8:30 am Mountain
Each frame includes: visual direction, on-screen text, voiceover or
caption direction.

---

FRAME 1 — TENSION
Visual: a phone alarm clock showing 6:47 AM, slightly blurry, kid's
voice presumably off-screen
On-screen text: "the part of the day no parenting book covers"
Caption / voiceover: none — let the visual breathe
Goal: ground the audience in a specific moment, not a thesis

---

FRAME 2 — BUILD
Visual: a tight shot of the creator's hand holding a coffee mug, her
face out of frame, slight tension in posture
On-screen text: "regulate before you respond" doesn't work at 6:47 AM"
Caption / voiceover: "I spent two years trying to make the standard
framing work. It didn't. So I wrote a different one."
Goal: name the problem and signal there's a solution coming

---

FRAME 3 — TURN
Visual: the workbook cover, photographed on her desk, clear and centered
On-screen text: "When Coping Skills Aren't Cutting It — pre-orders open today"
Caption / voiceover: "10 months of editing. 47 readers in the draft
phase. One page that almost got cut six times. It's here."
Goal: introduce the thing, with one piece of credibility (10 months,
47 readers, the six-cut page)

---

FRAME 4 — PROOF
Visual: a swipeable carousel of three real reader quotes (with permission)
or a single photo of an annotated draft a reader returned
On-screen text: "what 47 readers said about it"
Caption / voiceover: "Three women who read it before you can. They each
flagged the same page as the most useful. That page is page 23."
Goal: prove it's not generic; the SPECIFIC page reference is the lever

---

FRAME 5 — LAND
Visual: a clean product image with a link sticker overlay positioned
upper-third (not blocking the cover)
On-screen text: "Pre-orders open until Sunday at midnight Mountain.
Workbook ships digitally."
Caption / voiceover: "Tap up. $97. The page-23 conversation is the one
you actually need."
Goal: clear next step; specific reason to act now (pre-order deadline);
LINK STICKER GOES ON THIS FRAME

---
Things to verify before posting:
- Confirm "47 readers" is accurate or soften to "over 40"
- Confirm the three quoted readers gave permission for use
- Pre-order deadline language — confirm Sunday midnight Mountain is the
  actual cutoff
- Link sticker tested on the actual product URL before going live
```

## 4. Brand partnership pitch — full worked example

```
Subject: A note on your March "real mornings" campaign

Hi Maya,

The March "real mornings" campaign — specifically the version with
Hannah Reilly where you let her shoot it on her own phone at 6 AM — was
the first sleep-brand campaign I've seen in two years that didn't feel
staged. That tells me Hatch's brand team is making real decisions about
who you partner with and how.

I write about the gap between therapy-speak and what actually helps
overworked moms — specifically women in their 30s and early 40s,
parenting young kids, who've tried the standard tools and found them
incomplete. About 67% of my audience is women 28-42 in major US metros,
80% are parents of kids under 8, and the overlap with the "Hatch buyer
profile" you've described in past press is direct.

A few numbers:
- 38,200 followers, ER 4.1% over the last 90 days
- Save rate on parenting-and-sleep content: 5.8% (high for the niche)
- Audience: 91% women, 67% age 28-42, top US metros: NYC, LA, Denver,
  Austin, Minneapolis
- Email list: 6,400 — open rate 41%, click rate 8%
- Past sleep brand: a 4-piece campaign with Loftie in January 2026
  pulled a 12.3% trial-conversion on the discount code (their numbers,
  shared with permission)

What I'd propose:
- One feed carousel (5-7 frames) tying a Hatch product into a real
  bedtime moment in my house
- One story sequence (5 frames, with link sticker on the offer frame)
- One Reel (60-90 seconds), shot on my phone, in the "real mornings"
  spirit of your March campaign
- All deliverables within an 8-day window
- 3-month usage rights for organic + paid social
- $4,200 flat fee

If a 3-piece campaign is too large for a first conversation, I can do a
single feed-and-stories pilot for $1,400 with the same usage terms. I
can send the media kit, three sample concepts, and a 90-second clip from
the Loftie campaign so you can hear how an integration lands in this
audience.

Thanks for the work on the March campaign. It mattered.

— Sarah
@sarahatnight

---
Things to verify before sending:
- Maya is confirmed as the right contact at Hatch — verify name and title
- The "Hannah Reilly March campaign" reference — confirm name, date, the
  detail about her shooting it on her phone
- Audience geography data — pull current Instagram Insights screenshot
- The Loftie 12.3% conversion figure — confirm Loftie has cleared you to
  reference it externally
- $4,200 flat-rate proposal — confirm against current rate card; consider
  whether usage rights are exclusive or non-exclusive in this offer
```
