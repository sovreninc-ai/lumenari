# Memory — Blogger / SEO Site Owner Pack

## Domain context

Niche site owners build vertical content businesses that monetize through display ads (Mediavine/Raptive at scale, Ezoic at mid-size), affiliate programs (Amazon Associates, ShareASale, Impact, AvantLink for outdoor, etc.), and increasingly their own digital products (PDFs, courses, communities). At 50k-500k pageviews/month, a site can produce $5k-$50k/month in net revenue depending on vertical RPMs and affiliate conversion. The job is half writing and editing, half SEO operations — keyword research, internal linking, content refreshes, and watching analytics for what's working.

The work rhythm is publishing-driven. Most operators publish 2-8 new pieces per week, refresh older content monthly, and audit internal linking quarterly. Mornings before family time and weekend batches are the norm. Most owners are solo or have 1-2 part-time writers.

Success looks like compounding traffic — a content cluster that started at 5k visits/month becoming 30k/month over 18 months because the pillar earned backlinks and the children all rank in the top 5. The opposite is "deindexed in the helpful content update" — a real outcome for sites that leaned on thin, AI-generated content without real first-hand value.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- **RPM**: revenue per thousand pageviews — primary display-ad metric (Mediavine sites range $10-$60 RPM by niche)
- **EPC**: earnings per click — affiliate metric, deal-specific
- **Pillar content**: comprehensive parent article (3-5k words) that anchors a topic cluster
- **Cluster**: pillar + 5-10 child articles, all internally linked
- **Search intent**: what the searcher actually wants — informational, commercial-investigation, transactional, navigational
- **SERP**: search engine results page — what's currently ranking for a query
- **HCU**: helpful content update — Google's 2022-2024 algorithmic push against thin/AI/affiliate-heavy content
- **E-E-A-T**: experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness — Google's quality framework
- **Featured snippet**: the answer box at the top of a SERP, formatted as paragraph/list/table
- **PAA**: People Also Ask — Google's expandable question boxes on the SERP
- **Anchor text**: the clickable text of an internal or backlink
- **Topical authority**: a site's depth in a specific niche, signaled by clustered comprehensive coverage
- **Orphaned page**: a page with few or no internal links pointing to it
- **Affiliate disclosure**: FTC-mandated notice before the first affiliate link, "clear and conspicuous"
- **Cookie window**: the affiliate program's attribution period (Amazon: 24 hours, ShareASale varies, Impact varies)
- **Mediavine/Raptive minimums**: 50k sessions/month (Raptive, formerly AdThrive) and 50k sessions/month (Mediavine Journey) entry tiers

## Common workflows

- **Pillar brief from a keyword**: User provides target keyword + intent + their first-hand experience + 2-3 SERP observations → AI produces a pillar brief with H2/H3 outline, internal link plan to existing cluster articles, monetization plan (display ad section breaks, affiliate review structure, digital product CTA placements), and a list of "specifics the user must add" gaps. Output: a brief ready to write against.

- **Affiliate review section drafts**: User provides product list + their first-hand testing notes + intended audience → AI drafts each review using Pros/Cons/Who-it's-for/Who-it's-NOT-for structure. Output: 5-7 review sections that don't all sound the same.

- **Internal linking audit prompt**: User pastes a list of recent articles + their existing content map → AI produces a linking plan: which new articles link to which existing ones (with proposed anchor text variations), which existing articles should be updated to link to the new ones. Output: a 30-minute editing pass that turns orphaned content into a cluster.

- **Content refresh outline**: User provides an underperforming article URL + its current ranking + the current SERP → AI produces a refresh plan: sections to delete (thin/outdated), sections to expand (where competitors win), new sections to add (PAA questions not covered), internal links to add. Output: a refresh that recovers lost ranking.

- **Display ad structure pass**: User pastes a draft article → AI returns the same article with H2/H3 structure rebalanced for 300-500 word section breaks, lists/callouts inserted where natural, and flags for any 800+ word sections that need a break. Output: a Mediavine/Raptive-friendly structure without losing reading flow.

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **Thin "What is X?" intros that restate the keyword for 200 words.** Google's helpful content guidelines call this out specifically. Open with the direct answer in 2-3 sentences. Save context for after.

- **Listicles where every product is "great."** AI default. Real reviews have a clear winner, honest cons on each product, and at least one product with a "who it's NOT for" disqualifier. If every product sounds the same, the listicle gets ranked down by helpful content signals.

- **Fake first-hand experience.** Writing "I tested this for six weeks" when the user hasn't is both ethically bad and Google-detectable at scale. If no first-hand data, write in third person and source claims to named publications/manufacturers/Amazon reviews.

- **Buried affiliate disclosures.** FTC requires the disclosure before the first affiliate link, visible without scrolling past it. Bottom-of-article disclosures are not compliant and Mediavine/Raptive will flag the page.

- **AI-tells in the prose.** "Delve into," "navigate the landscape," "tapestry," "in today's fast-paced world," "it's important to note," "it's worth mentioning," "look no further." Strip these on every draft.

## Tone / register

Real niche site owners sound like helpful enthusiasts with strong opinions. They write in first person when they've used the thing, third person when they haven't. They use plain words ("good," "cheap," "doesn't work in cold") instead of marketing language ("premium," "value-priced," "all-weather rated"). They're funny in small doses and never about themselves. They disqualify readers — "this isn't for you if you weigh more than 220 lbs" — because honest disqualification builds trust. They cite their experience by date and place ("I took this on a five-day trip in the Tetons in August 2023") rather than vague claims. The voice should feel like a knowledgeable friend, not a brand and not an AI.
