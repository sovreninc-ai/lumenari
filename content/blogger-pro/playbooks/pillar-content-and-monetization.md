# Pillar Content and Monetization Playbook

The reference doc. Use this as the underlying structure for pillar briefs, affiliate review sections, internal linking, and ad-friendly section breaks.

---

## 1. The pillar + cluster pattern

The 2025 pattern that still ranks despite the helpful content updates:

**Pillar (parent article):**
- 3,000-5,000 words
- Targets the broad commercial-investigation or informational keyword
- Comprehensive — covers the topic end-to-end
- Links out to 5-10 children
- Earns the backlinks (longer, more cite-able)

**Children (cluster articles):**
- 1,200-2,500 words each
- Each targets a specific sub-keyword with stronger intent
- Each links back to the pillar
- Each links to 2-3 siblings where relevant

**Example cluster — "Backpacking coffee" pillar:**
- Pillar: "Best Backpacking Coffee Setup: 2025 Guide" (target: "backpacking coffee")
- Children:
  - "Best Camping Coffee Maker for Backpacking" (commercial-investigation, 5-7 product roundup)
  - "Aeropress Go vs GSI Java Drip: 3-Season Comparison" (commercial-investigation, head-to-head)
  - "How to Make Pour-Over Coffee in the Backcountry" (informational, how-to)
  - "Best Instant Coffee for Camping: Real Taste Test" (commercial-investigation, taste-test format)
  - "Backpacking Coffee Weight Comparison (Grams Per Cup)" (informational, data-led)
  - "Cold Brew on a Backpacking Trip: Yes, It's Possible" (informational, contrarian)

The pillar earns the link; the children convert traffic.

## 2. The pillar brief template

Use this exact shape when generating a pillar brief:

```markdown
# Pillar Brief: [Article Title]

## Target keyword + intent
Primary: [keyword]
Intent: [informational / commercial-investigation / transactional]
Secondary keywords: [3-5 related searches to address in the article]

## SERP map
Positions 1-3: [who's ranking and what they're doing well]
Positions 4-10: [Reddit, forums, niche sites, YouTube]
What's missing from the SERP: [the gap your article fills]

## Article angle
[1-2 sentences: the specific take or comprehensiveness that's missing from the SERP]

## H2/H3 outline

### H2: [Section 1]
- H3: [Subsection]
- H3: [Subsection]

### H2: [Section 2]
[Section purpose: what reader question this answers]
- H3: [Subsection]

[Continue for 8-12 H2s, with 1-3 H3s each as needed]

## Internal links
- Link to: [Existing article URL] — anchor text: "[descriptive 3-5 word phrase]"
- Link to: [Existing article URL] — anchor text: "[different phrase]"
[5-10 links to existing cluster articles]

## Monetization plan
- Display ad section breaks: every 300-500 words (flag any 800+ word stretches)
- Affiliate placement: [where in the article]
- Affiliate disclosure: placed before [specific section]
- Digital product CTA: [if applicable, where]

## First-hand specifics the user must add
- [Product or section where AI cannot fake experience]
- [Place where user's actual testing notes are needed]
- [Spec or claim that needs sourcing]
- [Internal link target that user must confirm exists]

## Target word count
[Number] words. Range: [low]–[high].
```

The "First-hand specifics the user must add" section is the moat. It forces the user to bring real experience to the sections where AI would otherwise fabricate.

## 3. The affiliate review section template

For every product in a roundup or comparison article:

```markdown
**[Product Name]** — best for [specific use case]

[1-2 sentences: what it is, why it's on this list. Lead with the most specific thing — the weight, the brewing method, the feature that distinguishes it.]

**Pros:**
- [Specific pro — not "good build quality." Say "stainless steel mesh filter, no paper needed, holds up to 200+ uses based on my testing"]
- [Specific pro, ideally something the user tested]
- [Specific pro]

**Cons:**
- [Honest con, even a small one]
- [Honest con]

**Who it's for:** [Specific reader profile — "weekend backpackers under 200 lbs who want a 4-season tent without the weight penalty" not "outdoor enthusiasts"]

**Who it's NOT for:** [The honest disqualifier — "anyone planning to ski-tour with this, the doors aren't gloved-hand friendly" not "may not suit everyone"]

[Where to buy: affiliate link. Disclosure already placed above the first affiliate link in the article.]
```

**Why "Who it's NOT for" matters:** It's the single most important credibility signal in an affiliate review. AI defaults to "great for everyone." Real reviewers know who shouldn't buy. The line should disqualify a specific reader — by weight, by skill level, by use case — not by vague language.

## 4. Internal linking patterns

**The cluster rule:**
- Every new article links to 3-5 existing articles
- Every new article gets linked from 2-3 existing articles within 30 days
- Pillars link to all their children; children link back to pillar; siblings link laterally

**Anchor text discipline:**
- Vary anchor text — never the exact keyword every time
- 3-5 word descriptive phrases work best
- Avoid "click here" and "read more" — Google sees these as low-value
- Example anchors for a pillar called "Best Backpacking Coffee Setup":
  - "my full guide to backpacking coffee"
  - "the backpacking coffee setup that's worked for me"
  - "everything I've tried for backcountry coffee"
  - Don't use "best backpacking coffee setup" verbatim every time

**Internal linking audit prompt:**

> Here's a list of articles I published in the last 30 days: [URLs + titles + target keywords]. Here's my existing content map: [content map]. Produce a linking plan: which new articles should link to which existing ones (with anchor text proposals), and which existing articles should be updated to link to the new ones. Flag any new article that should have at least 3 inbound links by day 30 and currently has fewer.

## 5. Display ad-friendly section breaks

If you're on Mediavine, Raptive, Ezoic, or AdThrive, the article's structure affects RPM:

**Rules:**
- H2 every 300-500 words minimum
- No more than 3 paragraphs per H2 section without an H3 or list break
- Lists, callouts, pull quotes create natural ad-placement spots
- Don't bury affiliate links in the last 20% of the article

**Section-break audit:**
- Read the article. Count words between H2s.
- If any section exceeds 500 words, look for natural H3 breaks or list opportunities.
- If any section exceeds 800 words without a break, restructure it.

**Visual elements that help:**
- Bulleted lists (4-7 items, not 20)
- Numbered lists for steps and ranked items
- Callout boxes for key takeaways or warnings
- Pull quotes for memorable lines
- Tables for comparison data (with real sources)

## 6. The monetization plan section

Every pillar brief includes a monetization plan that names:

**Display ad placements:**
- Section breaks every 300-500 words
- One or two key visual elements (table, callout, image) per major section
- No 800+ word sections without breaks

**Affiliate placements:**
- Disclosure placement: before the first affiliate link, visible without scrolling
- Affiliate link distribution: spread through the middle 60% of the article, not concentrated in the last section
- Comparison table position: usually after the intro and before the individual reviews

**Digital product CTAs (if applicable):**
- Soft CTA in the intro: "If you want my full setup checklist as a PDF, it's at [URL]"
- Mid-article CTA: in a callout box, contextual to the section
- End-of-article CTA: the primary conversion ask
- Email opt-in: in-line with the relevant content, not popup-only

## 7. The content refresh playbook

Underperforming articles can recover. The refresh pattern:

**Step 1: Diagnose**
- What's the current ranking? (Use GSC or Ahrefs)
- What's the current SERP? Who's beating you and on what dimension?
- When did traffic peak and when did it drop?

**Step 2: Refresh outline**
- Sections to delete: thin content, outdated stats, products no longer made
- Sections to expand: where competitors have depth you don't (PAA questions, comparison detail, first-hand sections)
- New sections to add: PAA questions not covered, sub-topics that have emerged
- Internal links to add: any newer cluster articles that should link in

**Step 3: Update the brief**
- Use the pillar brief template above
- Note this is a refresh of an existing article, not a new article
- Preserve the URL and the existing backlinks

**Step 4: Update internal linking after the refresh publishes**
- Add 2-3 new inbound links to the refreshed article from related content
- Update the pillar (if this is a child) to make sure the link is current

A refresh that ships in 90 minutes can recover 40-60% of lost traffic in 30-60 days if the underlying article was solid and just got out-of-date.

## 8. Worked example: pillar brief for "best camping coffee maker for backpacking"

```markdown
# Pillar Brief: Best Camping Coffee Maker for Backpacking (2025 Guide)

## Target keyword + intent
Primary: best camping coffee maker for backpacking
Intent: commercial-investigation
Secondary: best backpacking coffee maker, lightest backpacking coffee maker, aeropress vs gsi java drip, backcountry coffee setup

## SERP map
Positions 1-3: REI Co-op Journal, Outdoor Gear Lab, Switchback Travel — all big-publisher listicles, all praising the same 5 products equally, none with clear first-hand testing notes
Positions 4-6: Reddit r/Ultralight threads, one Reddit r/Backpacking thread, one YouTube video
Positions 7-10: smaller niche sites, mixed quality
What's missing from the SERP: a clear winner by use case, weight comparison in grams-per-cup, honest "who it's NOT for" reviews, multi-season testing notes

## Article angle
The roundup written by someone who's used these in the field over multiple seasons, with weight-per-cup math and clear disqualifiers per product.

## H2/H3 outline
### H2: My pick at a glance
- H3: For most backpackers
- H3: For ultralight purists
- H3: For coffee snobs who weigh grams
### H2: How I tested these
- First-hand testing notes, 3 seasons of use
### H2: The 7 best camping coffee makers for backpacking
- H3: GSI Java Drip (best overall)
- H3: Aeropress Go (best for coffee quality)
- H3: Wacaco Pipamoka (best for espresso lovers)
- H3: Snow Peak Titanium French Press (best for groups)
- H3: Soto Helix (best for fast brews)
- H3: Stanley Pour-Over (best budget)
- H3: Instant + cold brew comparison (the honest non-gear option)
### H2: Weight comparison (grams per cup)
- Table with sourced weights
### H2: How to choose
- H3: By trip length
- H3: By group size
- H3: By coffee preference
### H2: Common questions
- PAA-driven Q&A

## Internal links
- Link to: /backpacking-stoves-guide — anchor: "the stove you pair it with matters"
- Link to: /ultralight-backpacking-kitchen — anchor: "fits the ultralight kitchen setup"
- Link to: /backpacking-meal-planning — anchor: "morning routine in the backcountry"
[5-7 links total]

## Monetization plan
- Display ad section breaks: ~400 words per H2 average. Add H3 break inside the "7 best" section to avoid 800+ word stretch.
- Affiliate placement: each of the 7 products has an affiliate link in the product review section. Disclosure placed before the "My pick at a glance" section.
- Comparison table: after intro, before product reviews. Sourced from manufacturer specs + my scale.

## First-hand specifics the user must add
- Soto Helix: I haven't tested. Write in third person, source from manufacturer + 2-3 named published reviews.
- 3-season testing notes for GSI, Aeropress, Wacaco: dates, places, weather conditions
- Actual weights from my kitchen scale (manufacturer specs are sometimes optimistic)
- The "who it's NOT for" line for each product

## Target word count
2,600-2,800 words.
```

That's the shape. Fill it in once and the article writes itself in 2-3 hours instead of 8.
