# Client Communication — Executive Coach Pack

Templates for the four phases of an engagement: chemistry → contracting → between-session check-ins → end-of-engagement. Each template assumes the coach holds the relationship and edits to their voice before sending.

**Reminder: coaching is NOT therapy. NOT consulting. The coach holds the ICF / EMCC / CCE ethical line. Consult a licensed therapist for clinical concerns if anything in the client's communication surfaces clinical territory.**

---

## Phase 1: Chemistry call follow-up

Sent within 24-48 hours of the initial fit conversation. The job is to confirm fit, clarify scope (especially the coaching-vs-therapy line), and propose the next step (contracting) — without pressure.

### Template

```
Subject: Following up on [day] — and what's next

Hi [Name],

[ONE sentence that references something specific from the conversation — not generic. "The thing about the board interruption pattern stayed with me" or "Your description of the seat as 'half lonely, half thrilling' is one of the clearest framings I've heard."]

[2-3 sentences in their language about what you heard as the work. Curious, not prescriptive. Not "I see your challenge as X." More: "What I heard was [their words], and the thread that interested me most was [specific thread]."]

What coaching is — and isn't:
- Coaching is a structured learning relationship where the work is yours, the questions and frame are mine. We don't fix things together; we make space for you to do the thinking and the action
- Coaching is not therapy. If at any point in the work something surfaces that's clinical — anxiety, depression, trauma response, anything that would benefit from a therapist — I'll surface it and recommend referral. I'm not a clinician
- Coaching is not consulting. I won't tell you what to do about [specific business situation they raised]. I'll help you think through how YOU want to approach it
- Coaching is not a quick fix. The arc is usually 6-12 months. The shifts that last come from sustained practice between sessions

If you want to move forward, the next step is a contracting conversation — 60-90 min, free, where we'd cover scope, structure, fees, confidentiality, and the sponsor piece [if applicable]. After that you'd have a coaching agreement to sign and we'd schedule the first working session.

If you'd rather think on it, no pressure. I'm here when you're ready.

— [Coach name]
```

### Variations

**If declining (it's not a fit):**

```
Hi [Name],

Thank you for the time on [day]. After our conversation, I want to be honest: I don't think I'm the right coach for the work you're looking for, and I want to say so rather than start an engagement that wouldn't serve you.

Specifically: [one sentence on the actual reason — they need someone with deeper expertise in their function, they need a specific framework I don't work in, they need a different relational style, etc.]

What I'd offer: [referral if you have one, or "If it would help, I can suggest two or three coaches whose practice would map better to what you described."]

Wishing you well with this. The conversation was clarifying.

— [Coach name]
```

**If the client raised clinical content on the chemistry call:**

Pause and reflect. Don't send a contracting follow-up if the client's described situation includes clinical material (active depression, panic, trauma, suicidal ideation). Send instead:

```
Hi [Name],

Thank you for the open conversation on [day]. I want to share a thought before we go further.

Some of what you shared — [the specific piece, named gently] — sounds like it would benefit from work with a licensed therapist, in parallel with or before coaching. I want to be honest with you about this rather than start a coaching engagement that wouldn't be the right container for what's most live.

Coaching is well-suited for [the specific coaching-appropriate piece of what they shared]. The other thread is what a therapist is trained for.

What I'd suggest: if you don't already have a therapist, [refer to EAP if you know they have one, or suggest 2-3 ways to find a referral]. Once that thread is in good hands, coaching can be a useful complement.

If you'd like to keep this open and revisit in 3-6 months, I'm here. If you'd rather not, that's also fine. Either way, thank you for trusting me with what you shared.

— [Coach name]

CONSULT A LICENSED THERAPIST FOR CLINICAL CONCERNS.
```

## Phase 2: Contracting — agreement and pre-engagement comms

After the contracting conversation, send the coaching agreement (legal document) and a warmer welcome note.

### Welcome / kickoff email (sent with the agreement)

```
Subject: Coaching agreement attached + what to expect

Hi [Name],

Attached is the coaching agreement we discussed on [day]. Quick walkthrough:

- It covers scope, structure (sessions every [cadence], [duration] minutes), fees, confidentiality, and the refer-out protocol
- The confidentiality section: content of our sessions stays between us. With [sponsor] in the picture, we'll do three-way check-ins at [cadence] where I share themes only — not session content. You'll be there
- The refer-out protocol: if anything surfaces in our work that would benefit from clinical care, I'll surface it and we'll talk about referral. This isn't a failure of the coaching; it's how the line is held

Sign and return at your pace. Once signed, I'll send a calendar invite for our first working session.

In the meantime, an optional pre-work question — no pressure, no homework: between now and our first session, what would you want me to know that didn't come up on the chemistry or contracting calls?

Looking forward.

— [Coach name]
```

### Three-way kickoff with sponsor (if applicable)

If the engagement is sponsor-paid, the kickoff usually includes a three-way meeting in the first 30 days to align on outcomes and confidentiality.

Agenda for that meeting:

```
THREE-WAY KICKOFF — [Client] + [Sponsor] + [Coach] — [Date]

1. Coach opens (3 min)
   - Welcome, purpose: alignment on outcomes, confirmation of confidentiality structure
   - "I want to be explicit about what's shared and what's not. Themes and progress signals are shared in our check-ins, with [client] present. Session content is not shared. This is how coaching works and how trust is held."

2. Client speaks first (10 min)
   - What they're hoping to get from the engagement
   - What success would look like, in their words
   - What support they'd want from [sponsor] during this period

3. Sponsor speaks (10 min)
   - What they're hoping the engagement enables — for [client] and the business
   - What they've seen as the strength and the growth edge
   - What specific outcomes would be valuable to see

4. Coach reflects what's been said (5 min)
   - "Here's what I'm hearing as the work, in plain language: [synthesis]"
   - Surfaces any divergence between client's framing and sponsor's framing — without taking sides

5. Alignment on cadence (5 min)
   - Session cadence
   - Three-way check-in schedule (e.g., month 3, month 6, month 9, month 12)
   - When/how 360 happens if applicable

6. Confidentiality reaffirmation (3 min)
   - Coach states explicitly what crosses the line (themes) and what doesn't (content)
   - Sponsor confirms understanding

7. Close (2 min)
   - Next step (first 1:1 session date)
   - Sponsor's role between checkpoints (light, non-interfering)
```

## Phase 3: Between-session check-ins

Light touch between sessions. Often optional. Sometimes the right move is no message at all.

### The rule of three for between-session messages

A check-in is appropriate when:
1. The client named a specific event happening between sessions (a hard conversation, a board meeting, a decision deadline)
2. The session ended with the client articulating a specific practice or experiment they'd try
3. Something has surfaced in the coach's awareness (the client's company in the news, a sponsor signal) that warrants a light touch

A check-in is NOT appropriate when:
- It's been over a week with no event and the client hasn't surfaced anything — silence is fine
- The coach wants to "stay top of mind" — that's marketing, not coaching
- The coach is anxious about the engagement — that's the coach's work to do elsewhere
- It would be a fourth nudge in a row — back off

### Template — event-triggered check-in

```
Subject: [Specific event reference — not "checking in"]

[Name],

[ONE sentence — reference the specific event. "You have the board on Thursday." Or "You said you'd take the conversation with Marcus this week."]

[ONE question — open, curious, not leading. "Curious what's surfacing as you prep" or "What's the version of you that walks in differently than last time?"]

[ONE soft acknowledgment — no expected reply, see you [next session date].]

— [Coach]
```

### Template — practice-triggered check-in

```
Subject: no need to reply

[Name],

[ONE sentence — the practice they named, in their words. "Thinking of you in the week of [the specific practice they named]." Or "Whenever you want to compare notes on [the experiment they named], here when ready."]

— [Coach]
```

### Template — when silence is the right move

Don't send anything. The session-prep notes will hold the thread for next session.

### What never goes in a check-in

- "Just checking in!"
- "How are things going?"
- "Hope all is well!"
- A summary of last session
- Advice
- Multiple questions
- Anything that requires a reply
- Anything that sounds therapeutic ("How are you feeling?" "Holding space for you")

## Phase 4: End-of-engagement summary

Sent 1-2 weeks after the final session. 1-3 pages. Specific to this engagement. NOT a template applied to every client.

### Template

```
END-OF-ENGAGEMENT SUMMARY — [Client] — [Coach] — [Date]

Hi [Name],

Closing out our [duration] engagement. Here's what I'd want to leave on the page.

WHAT YOU CAME IN FOR
At our first working session you named [the original intake themes — 2-4 bullets, in their language as much as possible].

WHAT WE ACTUALLY WORKED ON
The arc shifted, as it usually does. The threads that became most active:
- [Theme 1 — what we kept coming back to]
- [Theme 2 — what evolved during the engagement]
- [Theme 3 — what emerged that wasn't in the original frame]

WHAT YOU NAMED AS SHIFTED
The pieces you said had changed by the end:
- [Their own words on what shifted, in 2-4 bullets]

WHAT'S STILL ON THE TABLE
Honest. The work isn't done. The pieces still live:
- [1-3 bullets — what's still in process, what remains a stretch]

THE PRACTICE YOU'RE TAKING WITH YOU
[The one or two specific practices that became habits during our work. Not "be more strategic." Specific: "the 5-minute reflection before high-stakes meetings" or "the one-on-one cadence with your direct reports that you redesigned"]

RECOMMENDATIONS GOING FORWARD
- [Continued coaching with [different focus / different coach / pause] — make a real recommendation]
- [If applicable: therapy referral — surface this only if it's already been discussed and you're confirming it in writing]
- [Specific stakeholder conversations that warrant continued attention]
- [Next checkpoint with sponsor if applicable: date]

WHAT I'M LEAVING WITH AS YOUR COACH
[1-2 sentences from you. Honest. Specific. NOT "you've grown so much." Something like:
"What I'll remember from our work is the moment in session [#] when you [specific moment]. That changed how I think about [the broader idea]." 
OR
"I learned [specific thing about leadership or coaching] from working with you. Thank you for letting me sit beside this work."

Optional — only if it's true. Empty sentiment is worse than nothing.]

[If sponsor-paid, add:]
THREE-WAY CLOSEOUT
We have our closeout three-way scheduled for [date]. Same structure as our prior check-ins: themes, progress, what's been useful, recalibration if there's a future engagement. You lead the conversation; I support the frame.

Thanks for the work.

— [Coach name]
```

---

## What never goes in end-of-engagement comms

- "It's been a privilege coaching you" (unless you mean it specifically; "privilege" is overused)
- "You've come so far" (vague — name the specific shift in their words)
- "I'll miss working with you" (true sometimes, but not the place for it)
- Therapeutic-tone framing ("I've witnessed your healing journey" — never)
- Specific recommendations the client didn't ask for
- Sponsor-facing language in a client-facing document
- Anything that sounds like a LinkedIn recommendation

---

## Inclusive-language and boundary checks for all client comms

Run these checks on every piece of client communication:

- **No clinical interpretation language** — "I sense you're carrying anxiety" → "I notice [observable behavior]"
- **No diagnostic framing** — never label the client or anyone they describe as clinical (depressed, anxious, narcissistic, ADHD)
- **No advice-disguised-as-question** — "Have you considered just [obvious answer]?" → cut
- **No demographic-coded language** — even in positive framing ("you remind me of strong women in tech I've worked with" → never)
- **No promises about outcomes** — "you'll see breakthroughs in your team's performance" → cut
- **No coaching-jargon they don't use** — "let's create the container for this" → use their language
- **No therapeutic-jargon ever** — "process," "hold space," "shadow work," "trauma response" → never (these are therapist's tools, not coach's)
- **No sponsor-facing detail in client-facing docs** and vice versa — confidentiality cuts both ways

---

Things to verify before sending any client communication:
- First name matches what they've signed
- Specifics in the body are accurate (no detail about another client accidentally migrated in)
- Confidentiality structure honored
- Timing commitments realistic
- Door state honest (open / closed / future-yes)
- Clinical-line check: nothing in the communication crosses into therapeutic interpretation
- Boundary held: the email reads as the coach, not the therapist, not the consultant

Coaching stance check: passed — language is present, curious, non-directive
Clinical-line check: passed — no clinical interpretation, no diagnostic framing
Confidentiality check: passed — no sponsor breach, no cross-client detail

**Final reminder: if at any point you're drafting a client communication that's drifting toward therapy, stop. Coaching is NOT therapy. Consult a licensed therapist for clinical concerns. Refer warmly.**
