# Lost Orders and the Rare Appropriate Upsell

Shipping problems are most of the volume in DTC support. Upsell-from-support is most of the missed-opportunity-vs.-burned-relationship judgment calls. Both below.

---

## Lost order — package shows "delivered" but customer says no

The most common shipping ticket. Carrier's tracking says delivered. Customer's porch says otherwise. You believe the customer — but you also need to ask the small set of questions that resolve most of these without a replacement.

### Formal

> Subject: Re: Missing package — order #38291
>
> Hi Marcus,
>
> I'm sorry your order hasn't arrived. The carrier's tracking marks this as delivered on May 11, which sometimes means it was left somewhere not immediately visible.
>
> Before sending a replacement, could you confirm a few things by reply:
>
> - Have you checked with neighbors or building management?
> - Is there a back door, side gate, or porch where it may have been left?
> - Is the shipping address on file correct? (1438 Elm Street, Apt 4B, Calgary AB)
>
> If the package isn't located within the next 48 hours, I'll send a replacement at no charge. Please let me know.
>
> Best regards,
> Sarah Chen
> Customer Support

## When upselling from support is appropriate

The rule, repeated: upsell only when **both**:

1. The customer is happy with the resolution
2. The upsell genuinely fixes their problem better than the current plan

If either fails, don't.

### Pass — customer hit plan limit, asked about it

The customer is using the product enough to hit a limit. The next tier exists for exactly this. This is the textbook appropriate upsell.

#### Friendly

> Hi Jordan,
>
> You hit the 1,000-row export limit on the Starter plan — that's the cap on row count per CSV export, not a bug.
>
> A couple of options depending on what you're trying to do:
>
> - **Stay on Starter**: split your exports into batches under 1,000 rows
> - **Upgrade to Pro**: 50,000-row exports, $19/mo more than what you pay now ($30 → $49). You can pro-rate the upgrade if you want it for the month and decide later.
>
> No pressure either way — let me know what fits.
>
> Sarah

What makes this work:
- Customer hit a real limit
- Both options listed (including "stay where you are")
- The math is explicit
- No urgency / FOMO pressure

### Pass — customer asked for a feature that's only on a higher plan

#### Friendly

> Hi Jordan,
>
> The Slack integration you're asking about is part of the Pro plan — Starter doesn't include it.
>
> If it's important enough to upgrade for, Pro is $19/mo more than Starter and gives you Slack + Salesforce + the API. Happy to switch you over with a pro-rated charge for the rest of this month, or you can take a free 14-day Pro trial first to see if it's worth it.
>
> Let me know which sounds useful.
>
> Sarah

What makes this work:
- The customer asked first
- The price delta is named
- A trial path is offered

### Fail — refund denied, then upsell

A customer who just got told no on a refund does not want to hear about your annual plan. Bury that instinct.

> Hi Marcus,
>
> Refund denied. By the way, our annual plan saves you 20% — interested?

Don't do this. Even if you've offered alternatives like "pause" or "downgrade." The upsell after a "no" reads as opportunistic and turns a salvageable interaction into a chargeback.

### Fail — NPS detractor recovery + upsell

NPS detractor recovery is for finding out what's broken. Upselling at this point is the support equivalent of "I noticed you're crying. Want to buy something?"

## Customer flagged a product defect

Different from a lost order — the package arrived but the product itself has a problem.

### Formal

> Subject: Re: Product issue — order #38291
>
> Hi Marcus,
>
> I'm sorry to hear about the issue with the candle. Could you reply with one or two photos of the product, including the wick area, so I can see what you're describing?
>
> Once I see the photos, I'll either send a replacement at no charge or refund the item, whichever you prefer.
>
> Best regards,
> Sarah Chen

### Friendly

> Subject: Re: Product issue — order #38291
>
> Hi Marcus,
>
> Sorry to hear that. Could you reply with one or two photos showing the issue — especially the wick area — so I can see what's going on?
>
> Once I have a look, I'll send a replacement or refund the item, your call.
>
> Sarah

### Warm

> Subject: Re: Product issue — order #38291
>
> Hi Marcus,
>
> Oh no, that shouldn't happen. Could you snap a photo or two — especially the wick — and send them my way?
>
> Once I see what you're seeing, I'll get a replacement out or refund you, whichever you'd rather. No need to send the bad one back.
>
> Sarah

## Notes

- **Don't ask for proof when the customer's claim is plausible and low-cost.** A $24 candle defect doesn't need a forensic investigation. A $400 refund might.
- **For lost orders, the 48-hour "give it a chance to turn up" beat is real.** Most packages marked delivered do turn up — under a porch chair, with a neighbor, in a side-gate area. Send the replacement after the 48 hours, not immediately.
- **For replacements, explicitly say "no need to send the bad one back" when the cost of return shipping exceeds the unit cost.** Customers offer constantly. Save them the trip.
- **Upselling in support is a tax on trust.** Spend it only when both the customer is happy and the upgrade is the right answer. Otherwise, you'll be paying that tax back in churn.
