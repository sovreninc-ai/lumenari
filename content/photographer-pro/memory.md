# Memory — Photographer Pack

## Domain context

A working photographer runs some combination of three business lines: wedding (high-touch, emotion-driven, $1,200-$8,000+ per event, 15-40 events a year for a busy solo), portrait (family, couples, branding, headshots — $400-$1,500 per session, 30-80 a year), and commercial/brand (product, lifestyle, content campaigns — $1,500-$15,000+ per shoot, 20-40 a year). Most working photographers do at least two of these. Pure specialists exist but are less common at the $50K-$200K solo income level.

The rhythm of the work splits into three phases: client acquisition (inquiry response, consultation, proposal, contract), shoot day (highly compressed, gear-and-people-management), post-production (culling, editing, gallery delivery, follow-up). The bottleneck is almost always post. A wedding photographer who shoots 30 weddings a year spends 80-120 hours per wedding in editing, gallery prep, and delivery. The math doesn't work if the photographer's also doing every proposal, contract, and delivery email from scratch.

That's where the AI is most useful. Templating the proposal, the sneak peek, the gallery delivery, the social announcement — the non-creative client communication — frees up the editing hours that actually pay the bills.

Success looks like: contracted deliverables hit the contracted timeline, the couple's families re-book for portraits, the brand campaign produces content the client uses for the full licensed term, the studio gets 3-5 referrals out of every job. Failure looks like: delivery slips past the contracted window without communication, a commercial client uses an image outside the licensed scope and there's no enforcement infrastructure, a wedding gallery comes back and the couple's underwhelmed by 3-4 specific missed shots that should have been on the list.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- **Sneak peek**: 10-30 lightly edited images delivered 3-7 days post-shoot, before the full gallery. The teaser that holds the client over
- **Full gallery**: the complete edited delivery — typically 50-100 images per portrait session, 400-800+ per wedding, depending on contract
- **Online gallery / proofing site**: client-facing image gallery (Pixieset, Pic-Time, ShootProof, SmugMug, ImageQuix). Print sales often happen here
- **Cull / culling**: first pass of selecting keeper images from the raw take. A wedding raw take is 3,000-5,000+ images; the cull narrows to 500-1,000 before editing
- **Edit**: color, exposure, crop, basic retouching in Lightroom. Standard for delivery
- **Retouch**: detailed skin/object/composite work in Photoshop. Often a paid add-on, especially for commercial/branding
- **Hero image**: the campaign-anchor commercial image — the one used on the homepage, hero ad, OOH
- **Lifestyle / product / editorial / brand**: commercial genres with different shot list shapes. Lifestyle = people using product in context; product = clean isolated catalog; editorial = magazine-style storytelling; brand = mixed content for marketing
- **Coverage hours**: the contracted shooting window. Wedding coverage starts at the bride/partner getting ready and ends at the exit or contracted end time
- **Overtime**: shooting past contracted hours, billed at hourly rate (often $200-$400/hour for weddings)
- **Second photographer / second shooter**: additional photographer covering parallel angles. Often the same studio or contracted in
- **First look**: pre-ceremony private moment between the couple. Optional. Changes the day's timeline significantly
- **Family formals**: posed family group photos, typically 15-30 minutes after the ceremony. The list MUST come from the couple
- **Golden hour**: 30-60 min before sunset, the highest-value light window of the day for portraits
- **Lightroom / Photoshop / Capture One**: editing software. Lightroom is the wedding/portrait standard, Capture One is more common in commercial
- **Tethered shooting**: shooting with the camera connected to a laptop, common in product and commercial work. Client can review on the spot
- **RAW / JPEG**: file formats. RAW is editable, large, the photographer's working file. JPEG is delivery-ready, compressed
- **Print release / print credit**: client's permission to make personal prints; usually included for wedding/portrait, controlled for commercial
- **Model release**: signed permission from a recognizable person to use their likeness in commercial/marketing. Required for many commercial uses
- **Usage rights / license**: the scope of the client's permission to use commercial images. Defined by territory (geography), term (time), media (web/print/OOH/social), and exclusivity
- **Buyout**: a complete or near-complete transfer of usage rights, often perpetual and worldwide. Significantly more expensive than a limited license
- **Day rate**: standard commercial pricing unit, typically $1,500-$5,000+ for a creative day depending on photographer tier and market
- **Retainer / booking fee / deposit**: non-refundable payment that holds the date. 25-50% is standard
- **Print sales / IPS (in-person sales)**: post-shoot session where photographer sells albums, wall art, prints. Common in portrait studios; less common in 2026 vs 2015 but still meaningful for some
- **CRM / studio management**: Honeybook, Dubsado, Sprout Studio, 17hats — software for client lifecycle. Most photographers use one

## Common workflows

- **Wedding workflow**: inquiry → consultation call (30-45 min) → proposal sent → contract + retainer → engagement session (often included) → 60-day check-in → wedding day → 3-7 day sneak peek → 4-8 week full gallery → print sales window → review request
- **Portrait workflow**: inquiry → quick proposal or pricing PDF → contract + payment → session prep email (what to wear, where to meet) → session → 1-3 week sneak peek → 2-4 week full gallery → optional print order
- **Commercial workflow**: brief from client/agency → pre-production (concept, shot list, location, talent, props) → contract + 50% deposit → shoot day → selects within 48 hours → final delivery 1-2 weeks → invoicing balance → usage tracking
- **Daily editing**: cull a session → import to Lightroom → bulk color/exposure → individual edits → export to gallery → upload → client email
- **Pricing inquiry response**: typically within 24 hours, lead with the lowest tier that matches the inquiry, one sentence on what's in it, offer a proposal or call

## What to upper-bound on delivery timelines

Standard contracted delivery windows by genre. Always build buffer into client-facing communications:

- **Wedding sneak peek**: 1 week
- **Wedding full gallery**: 6-8 weeks (some studios advertise 4 weeks, but life happens — 6 is a defensible promise)
- **Portrait sneak peek**: 1 week
- **Portrait full gallery**: 2-4 weeks
- **Commercial selects**: 24-72 hours post-shoot
- **Commercial final delivery**: 1-2 weeks for retouched final, longer for major campaigns
- **Brand/branding session full delivery**: 2-3 weeks

If the photographer commits to "delivered by X date," the AI should build a 1-2 week buffer into the client-facing version, not the photographer's internal target.

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **"Capture your special moments" / "Preserve your memories"**: every photographer's website says this. Replace with specifics — "8 hours of wedding day coverage, sneak peek in 7 days, full gallery in 6 weeks"
- **Delivery dates without buffer**: AI defaults to the contracted minimum; photographer often slips and looks unreliable. Always quote contract maximum + buffer
- **Usage rights language drafted without flagging**: commercial usage is contract-binding and price-sensitive. "Perpetual worldwide rights" is $$$$$; the AI will write it without realizing. Flag any usage line for review
- **Discount without trade**: a wedding photographer who discounts $500 without getting anything in return has just trained the client to negotiate next time. Trade for referral, social tag, off-season date, or content rights
- **Vague shot lists**: "wedding party shots" without specifying full group + individual sides + couple with each individual = missed shots. Phase-based lists with specific must-haves
- **Family formal list assumed**: the photographer doesn't know which grandparent is alive and which isn't. The list MUST come from the couple
- **Overpromising before delivery**: "every shot is amazing" before the client has seen the gallery sets the bar at impossible. Let the work do the bragging
- **Commercial proposals without usage scope**: a $2,000 lifestyle shoot with implied "do whatever you want with the images" is a commercial photographer giving away buyout-tier rights at content-budget pricing. Always specify usage

## Tone / register

A working photographer sounds confident, specific, slightly tired, and protective of the timeline. They name the deliverable count, the delivery window, the gear when relevant. They mention real workflow ("I'll send the sneak peek by Friday after I do my second pass in Lightroom"). They acknowledge what's hard ("we're going to be tight on light if the ceremony runs past 5"). They write delivery emails that read like a working pro, not a hyped-up brand. They never use "capture your special moments." They never call themselves an artist unguarded. They say "I shoot weddings," "the family session," "the brand campaign" — direct nouns, no aggrandizement. That's the voice.
