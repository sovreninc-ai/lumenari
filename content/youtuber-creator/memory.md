# Memory — YouTuber Pack

## Domain context

Long-form YouTubers (10-25 min videos) live and die by two numbers: CTR (click-through rate) on the thumbnail and AVD (average view duration) on the video itself. A healthy CTR is 6-10% on cold audiences; under 4% and YouTube stops recommending the video. AVD over 50% of total length usually signals the algorithm to push harder. Most growth deaths happen because either the thumbnail doesn't earn the click or the first 30 seconds doesn't earn the watch.

The rhythm of a working long-form creator is something like: ideate Monday, validate against search and competition Tuesday, script Wednesday/Thursday, film Friday, edit Saturday/Sunday, publish Sunday or Tuesday morning. Bigger channels split that across a team; solo creators are doing all of it. Most ship one video a week; some essay channels ship every 2-4 weeks for higher-budget pieces.

Success at the long-form level isn't subscribers (vanity) but: average views per video trending up, retention curves flattening (less drop at the 1-minute and 5-minute cliffs), CTR holding steady or improving as the channel scales, and a back catalog where evergreen videos keep earning views 12+ months after publish. The economics: roughly $2-15 RPM (revenue per thousand views) from AdSense depending on niche — finance/tech hits $15-30, gaming hits $2-5. Sponsorships at $20-40 CPM stack on top, and merch/products are where most channels actually make a living.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- CTR: click-through rate, % of impressions that became clicks (the thumbnail's job)
- AVD: average view duration, seconds watched on average across all viewers
- AVP: average percentage viewed, AVD as a % of total length
- RPM: revenue per thousand views (what creators actually earn)
- CPM: cost per thousand impressions (what advertisers pay)
- Retention curve: the line graph in YouTube Studio showing % of viewers still watching at each second
- Sub-hook: a mini-hook at the start of each chapter to win back scrubbers
- Cold open: the 0:00-0:30 segment before any branding, intro, or sponsor
- Pattern interrupt: a deliberate change in pace/framing/visual to reset attention every 90-180 seconds
- Open loop: a question or tension introduced early that the video must close
- End screen / outro card: the 15-30 second segment promoting another video before the autoplay
- Browse vs. Suggested vs. Search: the three biggest traffic sources, each with different CTR norms
- Suggested traffic: videos pushed alongside other videos — usually the biggest growth driver
- Impressions: how many times the thumbnail was shown, the denominator of CTR
- Click bait gap: the gap between what the thumbnail promises and what the video delivers — closes the channel's reach
- Re-upload: deleting and republishing a video with a new title/thumbnail; nukes existing data
- A/B thumbnail test: YouTube's native tool that rotates 2-3 thumbnails and picks the winner by CTR

## Common workflows

- **Video idea validation**: trigger = creator has 3-5 raw ideas → steps = run each through search-vs-trend filter, one-sentence pitch test, competitive scan of top 5 videos on the topic, 2-year test → output = ranked list with one to greenlight, one to kill, the rest to park
- **Script draft (10-25 min)**: trigger = idea greenlit, premise locked → steps = creator gives spine, audience, voice references → output = full script in hook → premise → meat (2-4 chapters with sub-hooks) → resolve → end screen structure
- **5 title variants**: trigger = script locked, before thumbnail → steps = creator gives the spine and any title direction → output = 5 titles across curiosity / specificity / contrarian / emotional / list frames, each with what-it-promises and failure-mode notes, plus a single recommendation for which to test first
- **3 thumbnail concepts**: trigger = title direction locked → steps = creator provides the spine and the title → output = 3 concepts with foreground/secondary/background hierarchy, text overlay specs (3-5 words max), color palette, emotion telegraphed, click rationale, and failure mode
- **Hook rewrite**: trigger = retention curve drops hard in the first minute → steps = creator pastes existing hook + the retention graph → output = 3 alternate hooks each opening a different loop, with specific notes on why each one might hold longer

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- "Hey guys, welcome back to the channel!" — strip every variation, no exceptions
- "Smash like and subscribe" as part of the cold open or anywhere in the first 5 minutes
- Titles with "might," "maybe," "could be" — hedged titles lose to confident ones
- Hooks that ask "what if..." as a placeholder — real hooks make a specific claim or pose a concrete question
- Recapping at the start of every chapter ("So as we discussed earlier...") — kills retention
- Thumbnail descriptions that say "eye-catching" or "exciting" — every spec must be concrete (face position, color palette, text, emotion)
- Scripts written at a flat energy level — real scripts breathe between fast and slow, dense and spacious

## Tone / register

A real long-form YouTuber sounds like someone with a strong point of view explaining something to a specific friend, not a teacher addressing a class. Sentences are usually short to medium. They reference specific moments and numbers ("$620B," "38-line script," "the 14-minute mark"). They drop unnecessary throat-clearing — no "before we get started," no "so without further ado," no "I want to take a moment to." They DO use direct address ("you're going to want to know this part") and pattern interrupts ("Quick aside — this is the part most people get wrong"). They write the way they'd tell the story to one viewer they respect, not the way they'd present at a conference.
