## 1. Annual review meeting prep (pre-retiree couple)

```
CLIENT REVIEW PREP — R & K — Date: 2026-05-22 — Annual Review

WHO'S COMING
Both R and K. 75 minutes booked. CPA not on this call (separate tax-strategy meeting scheduled for Q3).

AGENDA
1. Life / family update (10 min)
2. Plan progress check (15 min) — funded ratio, savings rate, time to target
3. Allocation drift discussion (10 min) — current vs IPS
4. Market context (10 min)
5. Roth conversion framing — discussion-only, hand off to CPA (15 min)
6. Estate / insurance check-in (10 min)
7. Action items + next meeting (5 min)

NET-WORTH STATEMENT TALKING POINTS
- Year-over-year: net worth up ~6% — driven by savings + market beta
- Savings rate: tracking target. R's consulting income is lumpy — Q1 light, Q2 catching up
- Account drift: taxable is ~4% over equity target (within IPS 5% band but worth flagging)
- Structural changes: R rolled over old 401(k) to IRA in February (paperwork closed)

ALLOCATION DRIFT — DISCUSSION POINTS
- Current: 68/27/5 equity/fixed/cash. Target: 65/30/5
- Drift driver: equity tailwind YTD + Roth contributions going to equity per IPS
- Inside the 5% band per IPS, but conversation: rebalance now or wait for natural drift back via planned cash flows
- If rebalance: do it inside the IRA (tax-neutral) before touching taxable. Confirm IRA models can absorb without exceeding fixed-income target

LIFE-EVENT CHECK-IN QUESTIONS
- How's the consulting income shaping up — pipeline through 2026?
- Any change in retirement target — still 65, or shifting?
- Kids: both out of school, both employed — any planned support beyond the gift you mentioned last year?
- K's mother — anything to flag on caregiving / power-of-attorney status?
- Wills and POAs last updated 2022 — anything need a refresh?
- Insurance: LTC conversation came up last year — want to revisit?

ROTH CONVERSION FRAMING — DISCUSSION ONLY
R's lumpy consulting income may create a low-income window. The concept worth discussing:
- Bracket-fill conversions in low-income years to use up bracket capacity before RMDs hit at 73
- IRMAA cliff to watch (Medicare premium tiers starting at age 65)
- 5-year rule applies separately to each conversion for the principal
- This is a structure-of-the-conversation document, not a recommendation. Specific dollar amount and timing is your CPA's call with their full tax picture. We'll bring the result back to the plan and decide on funding source.

OPEN ITEMS FROM LAST MEETING
- LTC conversation (deferred from 2025) — circle back
- 529 funding for nephews (K's project) — confirm direction

ACTION ITEMS LIKELY OUT OF THIS MEETING
- Coordinate Roth conversion modeling meeting with [CPA name] — Q3 target
- IRA rebalance — pending decision in meeting
- Estate doc refresh — referral to [estate attorney] if going forward
- LTC quote — refer to [insurance agent] if going forward

CONFLICT-OF-INTEREST CHECK
- No proprietary product in scope this meeting
- No revenue share to disclose
- Standard fee schedule applies; no fee changes pending

NOT-INVESTMENT-ADVICE NOTE
This is prep material for the advisor's use only. Recommendations for R & K come out of the meeting through licensed judgment, not from this document.
```

## 2. Weekly market summary (non-predictive)

```
SUBJECT: Week in Review — [Firm Name] — Week of May 19, 2026

What happened

US equities finished the week roughly flat, with the S&P 500 down about half a percent and small caps slightly weaker. International developed markets outperformed modestly. Bond yields drifted higher across the curve — the 10-year Treasury closed near 4.45%, up about 8 basis points on the week. Investment-grade credit spreads were stable.

Headlines that moved markets: the FOMC minutes from the May meeting showed continued debate about the pace of any rate moves; April CPI came in roughly in line with expectations; retail sales were softer than the consensus print.

What's still uncertain

Rate-path expectations have moved several times this year and remain data-dependent. Inflation is still working through the system at different speeds across categories — services slower than goods. Earnings revisions for the back half of the year have softened modestly across some sectors but the picture is mixed.

Things worth thinking about (not things to do)

- For long-term investors, a week of flat-to-slightly-down markets isn't a planning event
- Rebalancing happens on the IPS calendar / threshold rules, not headlines
- If your situation has changed — a job transition, a near-term cash need, a family event — that's the reason to call
- If you've been deferring a tax-loss harvesting conversation, the next planning window is mid-summer

What we're not doing

- Predicting where markets go from here
- Making tactical changes based on FOMC minutes
- Repositioning portfolios on a single week's data

When to reach out

If something has changed for you, that's the conversation. Markets are background; your plan is the work.
```

## 3. Retirement-plan check-in talking points (discussion-only)

```
RETIREMENT PLAN CHECK-IN — D & J — Annual

WHERE THE PLAN STANDS
- Years to target retirement: 3
- Target income in retirement: ~$110K/yr in today's dollars
- Funded ratio per planning software: ~104%, trending slightly up YoY
- Withdrawal-rate range the plan currently supports: 3.5-4.2% inside the Monte Carlo bands — a guide, not a guarantee

THE 4% RULE — WITH CAVEATS
The 4% rule (Bengen 1994, with later work by Pfau, Kitces, others) is a heuristic — a starting withdrawal rate of around 4%, adjusted for inflation, that historically lasted 30 years across a range of market environments in US data. Caveats:
- Built on US historical data; results vary by country and period
- Assumed a specific asset mix (originally 50/50 stocks/bonds)
- Doesn't account for taxes, fees, or sequence shocks in early retirement
- "4%" is a starting point for the conversation. Your specific number depends on the asset mix, the withdrawal pattern, and the sequence-of-returns risk profile in your retirement window. The planning software's withdrawal analysis is the better input, and even that's a probabilistic guide

SEQUENCE-OF-RETURNS RISK — DISCUSSION POINTS
- The returns in the first 5-10 years of retirement matter disproportionately. A bad early sequence is hard to recover from
- Mitigations worth discussing:
  - Bucket strategy: short-term cash bucket (1-2 yrs), medium-term bonds (3-7 yrs), long-term equity (8+ yrs). Refills bucket on a rule
  - Bond ladder for first 5-7 years of retirement income
  - Dynamic withdrawal rules (Guyton-Klinger guardrails — raise or cut withdrawals based on portfolio performance vs initial path)
  - Deferring Social Security to age 70 if other resources permit
  - Phased retirement / part-time consulting in early years
- These are concepts to discuss together. We'll choose the structure that fits your situation in the meeting

SOCIAL SECURITY CLAIMING — DISCUSSION STRUCTURE
- Full Retirement Age (FRA) for both of you: 67
- Early claim at 62 — 30% reduction from FRA, locked in
- Delayed retirement credits — 8% per year of deferral past FRA up to 70
- Spousal benefit: lower-earning spouse can claim 50% of higher-earner's PIA at their own FRA (subject to current rules)
- Survivor benefit: surviving spouse receives the higher of the two benefit amounts. Often the deciding factor for the higher-earner's claiming age
- Tax interaction: provisional income formula affects how much of SS is taxable. Coordinate with the CPA before deciding
- Discussion question: when would you claim if you didn't need the income? When if you did?
- For specific math on your situation, run it through the SSA.gov calculators and the planning software, then review with your CPA

RMD REMINDERS
- Current RMD age (under SECURE 2.0): 73, moving to 75 in 2033
- First RMD trap: deferring to April 1 of the year after you turn 73 means two RMDs in that year. Usually not optimal
- QCDs: up to the current annual limit, direct from IRA to qualified charity, satisfies RMD without taxable income. Useful for charitably inclined retirees once at RMD age
- Inherited IRA: 10-year rule for most non-spouse beneficiaries (post-SECURE). Estate coordination matters

ROTH CONVERSION — DISCUSSION POINTS
- Bracket-fill in low-income years (the gap between retirement and RMD age is often the window)
- IRMAA cliff awareness — conversion income can push Medicare premiums into a higher tier
- 5-year rule per conversion for the principal
- The tax cost in the year of conversion vs future RMD relief is a real tradeoff — your CPA needs to model this against your full tax picture, including any future RMD projections
- "Talk to your CPA" isn't a brush-off here — it's the right move. They have visibility we don't

QUESTIONS FOR YOU
- What does retirement actually look like for each of you — full stop, phased, second-act
- Where do you want to live in retirement — tax implications matter
- Anything you want to plan for that we haven't talked about: travel, second home, family support, charitable intent
- What's changed in how you're thinking about retirement since last year

HAND-OFFS
- CPA / EA — for specific Roth conversion amounts, RMD projections, tax-bracket modeling
- Estate attorney — for any document update tied to retirement planning
- Insurance agent — for LTC and life insurance review at this stage
```

## 4. Life-event check-in prep (job change)

```
LIFE-EVENT CHECK-IN — M — Job Change — May 2026

WHAT'S CHANGED
M is leaving her VP role at a public company for a senior role at a Series C startup. New comp: lower base, higher equity (RSUs + ISOs). Equity-heavy package changes the household balance sheet shape.

PLANNING IMPLICATIONS TO DISCUSS
- Cash flow: base income drops ~$80K. Joint household savings rate needs reassessment. Emergency fund adequacy given new variability
- Equity comp: ISO vs NSO mechanics, AMT exposure on ISO exercise, 83(b) election timing if applicable, vesting schedule, the lockup / IPO calendar. This is a coordination point with the CPA — the tax planning for equity comp is its own conversation
- 401(k) handling at old employer: 4 options to discuss (leave it, roll to IRA, roll to new 401(k) if accepted, cash out — the last one essentially never the answer). The roll decision has implications for backdoor Roth strategy if relevant
- Health insurance: COBRA or new employer plan or marketplace. HSA strategy if new plan is HDHP-eligible
- Insurance review: life, disability — does the old employer's coverage end at termination?
- Estate / beneficiary docs: anything tied to old employer (life insurance beneficiaries, etc.)

QUESTIONS FOR M
- Most pressing concern for the next 90 days?
- Is there a backstop if the startup doesn't work out (track record of safety net, household runway)?
- Equity package — has anyone walked through the tax planning end-to-end with you?
- 401(k) rollover decision — has your new employer's plan accepted incoming rollovers yet?
- Anyone else in the financial picture we should coordinate with?

HAND-OFFS
- CPA — equity-comp tax planning, AMT exposure modeling, 83(b) timing if relevant
- HR at old employer — confirmed termination date, severance terms, benefits cutoff dates
- HR at new employer — plan documents, vesting confirmation, equity grant documentation

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT
Conversation prep. Not a recommendation on the 401(k) rollover, equity comp strategy, or any specific tax move. Those decisions come out of the meeting and the work with the CPA.

---
What I assumed; what to verify or challenge before using:
- ISOs in the new equity package (confirm — could be NSOs)
- Joint household — M has a spouse / partner relevant to the planning conversation
- M's current HDHP / non-HDHP status under old employer

Recommendation-line check: no specific rollover recommendation, no specific equity-comp tax strategy
Predictive-language check: no market or IPO predictions
Confidentiality check: M is initial only
Compliance-review note: internal prep, not client-facing
```

```
- Allocation: discussed drift in taxable account (3% over equity target, inside the IPS 5% band). Decision: rebalance inside the IRA, not the taxable, for tax efficiency
- Income strategy: starting Social Security at S's FRA (June 2026 — confirmed). T deferring her own claim until FRA in 2028. Discussed survivor-benefit math
- Tax planning: bracket-fill Roth conversion discussion led by CPA. CPA modeled $35K conversion this year, sized to top of 22% bracket without crossing IRMAA threshold. Funding source: from T's traditional IRA. Decision: yes, proceed in Q3 once CPA confirms final number against year-to-date income
- Estate: documents from 2021 — agreed to schedule a refresh with [attorney name]. T to make the call this week
- Insurance: LTC discussion deferred to mid-year separate meeting

CLIENT'S STATED OBJECTIVES / TIME HORIZON / RISK TOLERANCE
Confirmed unchanged. T retiring in 4 years. Target retirement income $135K (today's dollars). Risk tolerance: moderate (60/40 IPS target, with capacity to be more aggressive given funded ratio).

RECOMMENDATIONS MADE
1. IRA-side rebalance to bring fixed-income closer to IPS target.
   - Basis: portfolio is inside the IPS drift band but trending; rebalancing inside the IRA avoids realizing gains in the taxable account, consistent with stated objective of tax efficiency
   - Alternatives considered: rebalance taxable (rejected — would realize gains unnecessarily); do nothing (rejected — drift trend if uncorrected approaches band edge)

2. Bracket-fill Roth conversion as modeled by CPA — execute in Q3 once CPA confirms final dollar against YTD income

   - Alternatives considered: no conversion (rejected — leaves bracket capacity unused in a low-income window); larger conversion (rejected — would cross IRMAA threshold per CPA's model)
   - Costs: federal + state tax on conversion amount in the year of conversion; modeled by CPA
   - Conflicts disclosed: none

CONFLICTS DISCLOSED
None this meeting. Fee schedule unchanged. No proprietary product, no revenue share, no related-party arrangements in scope today.

CLIENT'S DECISIONS
- Rebalance: approved
- Roth conversion: approved subject to CPA's final dollar amount in Q3
- Estate doc refresh: T to call attorney this week
- LTC: separate mid-year meeting scheduled

OPEN ITEMS / NEXT STEPS
- Advisor: place rebalance trades inside IRA — by 2026-05-22
- Advisor: send CPA a copy of YTD account activity to support Q3 modeling — by 2026-05-20
- Client (T): call [attorney name] re: estate doc refresh — by 2026-05-22

NEXT MEETING TARGET
```

## 6. Fee-disclosure / fiduciary language (fee-only RIA paragraph)

For use in client comms, proposals, or onboarding documents — gets edited by the advisor and goes through compliance review:

```
[Firm Name] is a fee-only registered investment adviser. We act in a fiduciary capacity for our clients — meaning we are required to act in your best interest, disclose any material conflicts of interest, and charge reasonable fees for the services we provide.

Our fees are based on a percentage of assets under management, billed quarterly in arrears: [X]% on the first $[Y], [X]% on the next $[Y], and a sliding scale beyond. Fees are described in detail in our Form ADV Part 2A, available at [URL] or upon request.

We do not receive commissions on the sale of investment products, revenue-sharing from fund companies, or other forms of compensation that could create a conflict between our advice and our compensation. If a conflict arises that is material to a recommendation we make, we will disclose it in writing and offer alternatives where appropriate.

For tax-specific decisions, we coordinate with your CPA or EA — we do not provide tax advice. For estate planning, we coordinate with your estate attorney — we do not provide legal advice. For insurance coverage decisions, we work alongside your insurance agent.

Past performance does not predict future results. Investment advice is based on the information you provide to us; the accuracy of that information is your responsibility. We rely on the planning software and assumptions in good faith, and we will revisit those assumptions with you at every annual review.
```

For dually-registered or B/D affiliated advisors, the equivalent paragraph names the standard of care that applies to each side (fiduciary on advisory; Reg BI / best interest on brokerage), names any commission earned, and notes Form CRS as the summary disclosure.
