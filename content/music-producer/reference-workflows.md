## 1. Artist intake form — new indie folk record

```
ARTIST INTAKE — Iris Park — May 15, 2026

1. ABOUT THE PROJECT
- Format: full-length album (debut)
- Songs: 8 total (5 confirmed, 3 demo stage)
- Working titles: "Tin Roof," "Mona," "Lighthouse," "The Year Without," "Carry It Home" + 3 untitled demos
- Target release: October 24, 2026
- Already committed publicly? Not yet. Pre-save campaign planned for late August.

2. SOUND
- Genre: indie folk with electronic textures — finger-picked acoustic foundation, programmed beats and synth pads building under the second half of most songs
- Reference songs:
  • Bon Iver — "22 (OVER S∞∞N)" — for the vocal sub bass and the layered harmony approach
  • James Blake — "The Wilhelm Scream" — for the way the song builds with one element added per minute
  • Sufjan Stevens — "Death With Dignity" — for the dry, close vocal sound and the picking pattern as the song's backbone
  • Phoebe Bridgers — "Garden Song" — vocal closeness and the room reverb on the acoustic
  • Big Red Machine — "Latter Days" — the way percussion enters late and changes everything
- Vocal-specific references:
  • Phoebe Bridgers — close mic, airy, almost dry
  • Maggie Rogers — present but warm, slight room sense
- Production approach: hybrid. Live acoustic and lead vocal first. BVs and electronic layers built around. No live drums; programmed percussion + processed acoustic textures.

3. GOALS
- What success looks like: 50K+ first-month streams across the album. Two singles that get a sync placement opportunity (she's repped by a small sync agency). Critical coverage in 2-3 of: Pitchfork (Sunday Review tier), Stereogum, Exclaim!, CBC Music.
- Different from last release: her two EPs to date were home-recorded on a Scarlett 2i2 in her bedroom. This is the first project with a studio chain. She wants the vocals to feel "in the room with you" instead of "behind a wall."
- The one thing she's worried about: that the electronic elements will swallow the acoustic intimacy. She's seen it happen to other folk artists who went into a studio and came out sounding like a Spotify playlist. We're explicitly going to keep the picking pattern audible under everything.

4. DELIVERABLE FORMAT
- Master format: WAV 24-bit at 48 kHz
- Stems: yes — 6 groups (lead vocal, BVs, acoustic guitar, electric/synth, programmed percussion, FX/atmos)
- Instrumental: yes (for sync agency)
- Acapella: yes (for any potential remix opportunity)
- MP3 references: yes, 320 kbps after every mix revision
- DSP-ready master target: -14 LUFS integrated, -1 dBTP peak
- Mastering: external (engineer TBD — short list: Heba Kadry, Sarah Register, Greg Calbi)

5. ROLES AND PRELIMINARY SPLITS
- Primary artist: Iris Park
- Songwriters: Iris Park (sole writer on all 8 songs, per her note)
- Producer: [me]
- Engineer: [me]
- Featured artists: TBD on 2 tracks (vocal feature on "Mona" being discussed)
- Preliminary publishing splits: Iris Park 100% (sole writer). Producer (me) discussing 3-5% producer points on net master receipts via a separate producer agreement.
- Master ownership: artist (Iris) retains master ownership. Distribution via DistroKid under her own LLC.

NOTE: splits and ownership above are working notes. A formal split sheet and producer agreement should be drafted by an entertainment attorney before release. The producer-points conversation in particular needs counsel — even a 3% point on the master, if not papered correctly, can become a dispute on year three.

6. TIMELINE (buffered)
- Pre-production / demo review: May 27 – June 5
- Tracking (vocals + acoustic, my studio): June 8 – June 19 (two weeks)
- Production / programming (electronic layers): June 22 – July 17
- Mixing: July 20 – August 14
- Revisions (2 rounds standard): August 17 – September 4
- Mastering: September 7 – September 18
- Final delivery to artist + distributor: September 25
- Distributor lead time to October 24 release: 30 days minimum

7. LOGISTICS
- Studio: LP Logan Park Studio (mine, Toronto)
- Vocal chain: Neumann U87 → Heritage Audio HA73 → UA Apollo x8 → Pro Tools (24-bit/48 kHz)
- Acoustic guitar: Iris's Martin OM-21 mic'd with a Royer 121 + Neumann KM84 (small diaphragm) pair
- Players: Iris on all acoustic and vocal. Programming and electric textures: me. Potential feature vocal (TBD) tracked remotely.
- Travel: none — Iris is local (Toronto)
- Budget total: $14,000 CAD (8 songs × ~$1,750/song bundled)

---
Things to verify before sending:
- Iris's LLC name for the master ownership / distribution line
- Whether the sync agency has any contractual say in deliverable format we need to match
- Mastering engineer short list — confirm pricing and availability for September window
- Producer-points percentage range — Iris's lawyer (if she has one yet) will likely have a view
- Confirm October 24 release date with the DistroKid lead time (30 days minimum)
```

```
9:30 AM call / 6:00 PM wrap

ROOM / GEAR
Studio: LP Logan Park (Toronto)
```

## 3. Mix revision response — clean, productive round

```
MIX REVISION RESPONSE — "Mona" — R1 — August 12, 2026

Hey Iris,

Cut through your notes on the R1 of "Mona." Here's what I did with each.

Notes addressed:
0:14 — VOCAL — Pulled back 1.5 dB on the verse vocal, was sitting too far forward. Now sits behind the acoustic the way you wanted.
0:32 — DRUMS — Tightened the programmed kick at 60-80 Hz, took 2 dB out of the 250 Hz range where it was muddying with the acoustic. The acoustic finger-picking now reads cleaner under it.
0:48 — BV — The third harmony layer was too prominent on the chorus. Pulled it down 3 dB and panned slightly wider. Now it's atmosphere, not a third lead.
1:14 — REVERB — Shortened the vocal reverb tail by about 30% on the verses. The chorus reverb stayed as-is — you said you liked that one.
1:42 — ARRANGEMENT — Moved the synth pad entrance back by 4 bars per your note. It now comes in on the second half of the second verse instead of the first half. Gives the verse more breathing room before the lift.
2:08 — MASTER — Pushed the overall master up 0.8 dB to land closer to -14 LUFS integrated (was sitting at -14.8). Peak still hitting -1 dBTP clean.
2:34 — DRUMS — Replaced the trap-style hat with the brushed-feel programmed hat I'd held back from R1. You'll hear it. If it's wrong, I can swap back.
3:12 — FX — Added the riser you mentioned wanting before the bridge. Subtle — 4 bars, fading in from -inf to -18 dB. Tell me if you want it louder.

Notes I want to discuss before changing:
2:50 — "More energy in the chorus" — I held off on this one. The arrangement is already building three layers per repeat. If I add more energy I'd be sacrificing the dynamic gap between verse and chorus that's making the chorus feel like a chorus. Two paths I'd offer:
  Option A — push the BVs up 1 dB and add a doubled tambourine on the back half of each chorus. Subtle energy add.
  Option B — actually cut the third chorus repeat by 4 bars so the song breathes more before the bridge. Energy stays, song gets tighter.
Tell me which (or neither) and I'll address in R2.

What I changed that wasn't in your notes:
- Took 0.5 dB out of the 6 kHz range on your lead vocal. Was getting slightly sibilant on the "s" sounds. Subtle — you may not hear it, but mastering will thank us.
- Pushed the room sense on the acoustic slightly — moved a tiny bit more of the Royer 121 signal into the mix, gives it the close-but-still-in-a-room feel you've been asking for since "Tin Roof."

If either of those isn't what you want, I can revert. Both are reversible in 5 minutes.

New mix linked: [Splice link]
MP3 reference: [WeTransfer link]

Next round window: August 17 – August 21. Leave notes on Splice with timecodes and the element tag if you can — saves a round.

Reminder: two rounds of revisions in the agreement. This is R1 going into R2. We're on the standard track.

Talk soon,
[Name]

- Confirm Iris is okay receiving 24-bit/48 WAV via Splice or wants WeTransfer with download
- The two "energy" options I offered — make sure I have both ready to spin up in case she picks one in R2
```

## 4. Mix revision pushback — "this can't be done that way"

Sometimes the artist asks for a thing that won't work. This is the email that handles it without making them feel dismissed.

```
MIX REVISION RESPONSE — "Lighthouse" — R2 — August 23, 2026

Hey Iris,

Two of your R2 notes need a longer conversation before I act on them. The rest are addressed in the R2 file (link below).

Notes I want to discuss:

3:42 — VOCAL — "Make the vocal louder in the chorus, but also more intimate."

Here's the conflict: louder and more intimate are pulling in opposite directions in the chorus.

The chorus arrangement currently has 5 voice layers (lead + 4 harmonies), programmed perc, a synth pad, the acoustic, and the bass synth. That's already a wall. If I push the lead vocal up to be more prominent, it has to compete with those layers, which means more compression and more presence boost — both of which kill intimacy.

If we want both, we need to choose:

Option A — keep the arrangement, lose intimacy on the chorus, gain volume. Acceptable result for a streaming-tier listener but not the "in the room with you" feel you're after.

Option B — pull two of the BV layers back significantly in the chorus, which opens up sonic space so the lead can sit forward without being compressed to death. This keeps the intimacy. Trade-off: the chorus loses some of the "wall of voices" feel.

Option C — change the arrangement: cut the lower BV pads in the chorus and replace with a high, airy fifth harmony only. Different from what's in the R1 — the chorus becomes vocal-led with thinner background, like the second chorus of [Bon Iver — "29 #Strafford APTS"] at 2:14. Most likely path to "in the room with you" but it's an arrangement change, not just a mix change.

My vote is C. Tell me if you want to try it or stick with what we have.

4:08 — MASTER — "Less low end on the master."

I held off here. The low end you're hearing is the bass synth at 80-120 Hz, not a master issue. If I pull low end on the master, I'll thin out the kick and the acoustic body too, which neither of us wants.

Two fixes that get you the result:
- Pull the bass synth down 1.5 dB at the 80-120 Hz region (cleanest fix, doesn't touch anything else)
- Or — high-pass the bass synth at 80 Hz, removing some of the sub. Tighter, more "studio" feel.

I'd recommend the first. Tell me yes/no and I'll do it.

What was addressed in R2 (no discussion needed):
[full R2 notes addressed list goes here in the actual email — abbreviated for this template]

New mix linked: [Splice link]

Round count update: we're on R2 finishing. After this round, additional rounds are billed at $250/round per song. We have one song ("Carry It Home") that may need an R3 because we haven't landed on the bridge arrangement — flagging in advance, not assuming.

Talk soon,
[Name]

---
Things to verify before sending:
- The "29 #Strafford APTS" reference at 2:14 — confirm timecode is accurate before sending (I'm pulling from memory)
- Whether Iris's R3 budget is okay to flag now or wait until we know for sure
- Confirm Option C estimate of time — the arrangement change is ~3 hours of work, not just a mix tweak
```

## 5. The "we're at round 4" honest reset email

Records can spiral. This is the email that resets the conversation before it ends the relationship.

```
Subject: "Lighthouse" — let's have an honest conversation

Hey Iris,

We're at R4 on "Lighthouse." That's three rounds past where we were supposed to land. I want to take a step back before I start mixing again.

What I'm seeing:
- R1 → R2 we made big changes. Productive.
- R2 → R3 we made medium changes. Productive but slower.
- R3 → R4 the notes have gotten conflicting. Round 3 you wanted the vocal more intimate; round 4 you want it more present. Round 3 you wanted less low end; round 4 you mentioned the kick feels small.

This is normal when a mix gets too close to perfect-in-the-head and not-quite-perfect-on-the-speakers. The fix isn't another round. The fix is two things:

1. A reference listen on a different system. The mix is sounding one way on whatever you're checking it on (laptop / car / phone / Airpods). Can you commit to one full listen on a real system — your studio monitors at the workspace, or my room — before you write R5 notes?

2. A 48-hour gap. Don't open the file tomorrow. Come back to it Tuesday morning, fresh.

If after both those things you still want R5 changes, I'll do them — and I'll waive the round-4-extra charge on this round because we're working through something together. But I think 80% of what's left is ear fatigue, not the mix.

This song is close. I want you to feel good about it the day it comes out. I'm telling you what I'd tell any artist in this seat.

Talk soon,
[Name]

---
Things to verify before sending:
- Whether I want to formally waive the round-4 fee or just float it (mention the kindness, don't necessarily promise it)
- Whether to copy management on this email (probably yes — they should know the conversation is happening)
- That I'm being honest with myself: is it ear fatigue, or did I actually drift the mix between R3 and R4? Listen to my own R3 and R4 back-to-back before sending.
```
