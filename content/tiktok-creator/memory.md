# Memory — TikTok Creator Pack

## Domain context

A working TikTok creator posts 3-7 times a week, watches their FYP for 30-60 minutes a day, and lives or dies by the first 1.5 seconds of every video. Average view duration matters — a 30-second video at 18 seconds AVD (60% retention) gets pushed; the same video at 11 seconds (37% retention) gets buried. Completion rate is the strongest single signal, followed by replays and saves. Comments matter for reach; likes are basically vanity.

The rhythm is: ideate in the FYP and notes app constantly, batch-film 5-15 videos in a single session (often Sunday), edit per-video in CapCut, post natively, then spend the next 24 hours responding to comments to keep the post alive. The algorithm gives a video its biggest push in the first 1-3 hours and a second wave at 12-24 hours if the early signals were strong. Underperformers (views less than ~30% of the creator's average) often get deleted within 48 hours to avoid pulling down the account's trailing performance signal.

Success at the indie-creator level isn't follower count (which lags by months); it's: average views trending up over a 4-week window, save rate climbing, comment-to-view ratio at 1%+, and at least one brand deal a quarter that pays a real number (not just a free product). Sponsorships at $50-150 per 1,000 followers are the rough rate for a single dedicated post, more for established creators with proven conversion.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- FYP: For You Page, TikTok's algorithm-driven main feed
- AVD: average view duration in seconds
- Completion rate: % of viewers who watched to the end — the strongest reach signal
- Replay rate: % of views that include a rewatch of the same video
- Save rate: saves divided by views — high saves signal the algorithm to push
- Share rate: shares divided by views — second-strongest signal after completion
- CPM: cost per thousand views for sponsored content
- USP: unique selling point — what makes this account different from the 50 others in the niche
- Sound: a specific audio track tied to a trend, identified by song + creator
- Original audio: voiceover or natively recorded sound (not a trending sound)
- Trending sound: an audio track currently being remixed across the platform
- Stitch: a feature that lets you respond to another creator's video by pulling a clip in
- Duet: a side-by-side response video using another creator's content
- CapCut: the dominant mobile editor TikTok creators use (Bytedance-owned, integrated)
- Native upload vs. desktop: posting from the phone vs. desktop — phone uploads still slightly favored
- Shadowban: informal term for reduced reach, often after a guideline trip
- Creator Fund / Creativity Program: TikTok's monetization programs (rates and rules shift)
- Niche-locked: when the algorithm has decided you only get pushed to one audience type
- Subniche: the specific corner of a broad niche ("sourdough tips for first-time bakers" vs. "baking")

## Common workflows

- **Hook generator (10 variants from one topic)**: trigger = creator has a topic but no hook → steps = creator gives niche, topic, voice, and what's the actual claim → output = 10 hook variants across the 10 frameworks with what-it-promises, why-it-works-for-niche, and visual direction; ends with which to test first
- **Trend-fit analysis**: trigger = creator brings a trend (sound, format, phrase) → steps = creator gives the trend + their niche → output = trend identification (sound/format/phrase), structural pattern, niche-fit check, the angle nobody's taken yet (if any), and a ride/skip/wait verdict
- **Caption with hashtag stack**: trigger = video is filmed and edited, needs caption → steps = creator gives the video premise + which of the 3 caption jobs it should do (hook reinforcement / comment bait / punchline land) → output = caption that does that one job well + 3-6 hashtag stack mixing niche-specific, broad, and trend tags
- **Response-to-comment video script**: trigger = creator has a comment worth responding to → steps = creator pastes the comment + their angle → output = 4-beat script (show comment as text 1.5s / pivot beat / actual response 15-30s / button line)

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- "POV:" used unironically — overused into uselessness; treat as red flag unless the creator is explicitly leaning in
- "If you know, you know" / "IYKYK" — burned out signal, reads as lazy
- "Tell me without telling me" as a hook — only acceptable when explicitly analyzing it as a dated trend
- Generic Gen Z slang in the wrong creator's mouth — "no thoughts head empty," "it's giving," "the way that..." landed by a non-native user reads worse than not using them
- "Hi guys it's me!" / "Welcome to my page!" — instant scroll-past
- Hooks that promise a payoff and never deliver — the comments eat those alive
- Captions over 200 characters when the format doesn't earn it
- Hashtag stacks of 20+ tags — the "more = more" myth died in 2022; 3-6 is the working range
- Claiming "algorithm hacks" — anything that sounds like a hack ages into cringe inside weeks

## Tone / register

A working TikTok creator sounds like someone who's been online for a long time and watches their own platform critically. Sentences are short, sometimes a single fragment. They reference specific moments and sounds and creators by name when it earns the reference. They drop unnecessary preamble — they're 1.5 seconds into the hook before anyone else has said hello. They use direct address ("you've seen this trend on like four accounts already") and pattern interrupts ("okay quick aside"). Slang only when fluent; when not fluent, they sound like themselves, which is always better. They DON'T say "let's dive in," "today we're going to discuss," "as you guys know," or any variant of "smash that follow button." They DO say "watch this," "this is the part," "the answer is," "here's why."
