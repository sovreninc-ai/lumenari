## 2. Day-by-day itinerary template

Use this skeleton for any full day. The AI fills in based on your intake.

```
DAY [N] — [city or area]

Theme: [one line — what this day is for]

MORNING (8-12)
- Anchor: [1 thing, 2-3 hours]
- Breakfast: [neighborhood suggestion or "hotel"]
- Transit to next stop: [walk X min / metro line Y, Z min]

AFTERNOON (12-5)
- Lunch: [neighborhood + cuisine type, not a specific name]
- Anchor: [1 thing OR 2 lighter things]
- Buffer: [30-60 minutes explicitly left open]

EVENING (5-9+)
- Dinner: [neighborhood + cuisine type]
- Optional: [1 evening activity OR "back to the hotel"]

NOTES
- Walking distance today: ~[X km / Y miles]
- Weather backup: [what to do if it rains]
- One thing to skip if running behind: [the lowest-priority item]
- Bathroom + water locations: [pre-located]
```

**Worked example (Day 1 in Tokyo, 2 adults, medium pace, landing mid-morning):**

```
DAY 1 — Asakusa + Ueno

Theme: light pacing for jet lag, get outside, eat early.

MORNING (10-12) — assumes landing 9 AM at Haneda
- Anchor: drop bags at hotel, then walk Sumida River from Asakusa to the Sensoji area
- Breakfast: grab onigiri at a 7-Eleven or Lawson en route (real food, not a stereotype — Japanese convenience store food is excellent)
- Transit: short walk

AFTERNOON (12-5)
- Lunch: in Asakusa, around the Nakamise area. Many small soba and tempura shops; pick one with a line.
- Anchor: Sensoji Temple grounds (free, ~90 min wandering)
- Buffer: nap window 3-4 PM if jet-lagged
- Optional: walk to Ueno Park (~25 min) for the museums-from-the-outside loop

EVENING (5-8)
- Dinner: Ueno or back near hotel — izakaya or ramen, nothing fancy on night 1
- Early night recommended. You'll be wrecked at 7 PM.

NOTES
- Walking distance: ~6-8 km
- Weather backup: covered arcades in Asakusa, plus the Tokyo Skytree mall if rain is heavy
- One thing to skip if behind: Ueno Park — save for another day
- Bathrooms: any train station; convenience stores; the temple grounds
- Verify: temple opening hours (Sensoji is usually 6 AM - 5 PM but check)
```

## 3. Lodging comparison matrix

| Option | Total all-in cost (incl. fees + taxes for full stay) | Commute to main areas | Amenities that matter to YOU | Sleep noise risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Option A | $______ | ______ | ______ | ______ | ______ |
| Option B | $______ | ______ | ______ | ______ | ______ |
| Option C | $______ | ______ | ______ | ______ | ______ |
| Option D | $______ | ______ | ______ | ______ | ______ |

**How to evaluate each column:**

- **Total all-in cost:** every fee, every tax, every "resort fee" or "cleaning fee." Per night × nights + fees. Compare apples to apples.
- **Commute:** to wherever you're spending most of your time. 20 minutes by transit is fine. 45 minutes is bad.
- **Amenities that matter:** YOUR list. Common ones: washing machine, AC, kitchen, bathtub vs shower, real bed for kids, walkable to coffee, parking.
- **Sleep noise risk:** before booking, search reviews for "noise," "loud," "thin walls," "above bar," "street." If multiple reviews mention noise, believe them.
- **Notes:** check-in flexibility, neighborhood feel, breakfast included, safety of the area at night, whether it has an elevator (matters for mobility + luggage).

**Sleep-noise red flags worth knowing about:**
- "Lively neighborhood" in marketing copy = bars below the window
- "Steps from the train station" = train noise
- "Heart of the action" = sirens, drunks at 2 AM
- "Boutique character" = sometimes thin walls and creaky floors

## 4. Packing list — fill-in by climate + activity

**Universal essentials (every trip)**
- Passport + 2 copies (one in luggage, one in cloud storage)
- Cards in two places (don't put all cards in one wallet)
- Cash in home currency for departure + small destination currency on arrival
- Phone + charger + correct plug adapter
- Medications (in original packaging, with extra)
- Basic first aid: bandaids, painkillers, anti-diarrheal, sunscreen
- Water bottle (refillable, fits airport security)
- Refillable day pack

**By climate**

Cold (sub-10°C):
- Base layers (2 sets)
- Mid layer (fleece or wool)
- Insulating layer (down or synthetic puffy)
- Waterproof outer shell
- Warm hat, gloves, scarf
- Wool socks (3-4 pairs)
- Waterproof boots

Temperate (10-20°C):
- 2-3 light layers
- 1 packable rain jacket
- 1 light sweater
- Walking shoes
- Mix of pants / shorts depending on user

Hot (20-30°C+):
- Breathable shirts (4-5)
- Lightweight pants + shorts
- Sun hat
- Sunglasses
- Reef-safe sunscreen
- Swimwear (2 sets if beach)
- Sandals + walking shoes
- Lightweight long-sleeve for sun protection or AC

Mixed (mountain trip, shoulder season):
- Layers, layers, layers
- Versatile shell that handles rain and wind
- Walking shoes that survive both warm and cold
- Hardest packing list — accept it

**By activity**

Walking-heavy city:
- Shoes that survive 15,000 steps/day (broken in BEFORE the trip)
- Blister care: moleskin, Band-Aid blister pads, tape
- Day pack
- Compression socks for long days (or long flights)

Hiking:
- Boots or trail runners (broken in)
- Wool or synthetic socks
- Layers (always)
- Headlamp
- Water bladder or 2+ bottles
- Snacks
- Whistle, basic emergency supplies

Beach / water:
- Swimwear (2 sets)
- Water shoes
- Reef-safe sunscreen
- Dry bag
- Quick-dry towel
- Rash guard

Business / formal:
- Pack flat — roll, don't fold
- Wrinkle-resistant fabrics
- One nice outfit covers most needs; layer for variety

## 5. Pre-trip budget tracker (rough framework)

For trip cost realism, allocate roughly:

- Transportation (flights, trains, rental car, taxis): 30-40% of total
- Lodging: 25-35% of total
- Food + drink: 15-25% of total
- Activities + admissions: 5-15% of total
- Travel insurance: 4-8% of trip cost
- Buffer: 5-10% of total

Adjust hard for trip type. A 5-day all-inclusive beach trip is 80% lodging. A 2-week Europe rail trip is 35% transportation. A camping road trip is 40% food.

The buffer line is non-negotiable. There's always an unexpected taxi, a lost item, a meal that ran over, a tour you decided to add.
