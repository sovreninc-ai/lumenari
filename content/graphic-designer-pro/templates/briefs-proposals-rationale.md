# Briefs, Proposals, Rationale Templates

The reference doc. Five sections. Use directly or as scaffolding.

---

## 1. Discovery questions — the 24-question bank

Pick 8-12 per project, weighted to the project type. Identity work leans audience+brand. Web leans audience+project. Packaging leans audience+business. Illustration leans brand+project.

### Audience (6 questions)
1. Who specifically does this design serve? Not "small business owners" — "the operations lead at a 12-50 person tech-services business making the buying decision."
2. What are they doing in the 60 seconds before they encounter this design? On a phone in line at a coffee shop? Behind a laptop at 4pm? At a trade show?
3. What do they already think about your brand? (If "nothing," that's also useful.)
4. What's the emotional outcome you want them to have? Not the action — the feeling that precedes it.
5. How design-literate are they? Will they notice the typography, or will they only notice if it goes wrong?
6. Where does this design live? Screen, print, outdoor, in-hand, embroidered, etched, projected?

### Brand (5 questions)
7. Describe your brand's voice without using adjectives. (Force them off "professional yet approachable.")
8. What are your stated values, and where do they not actually show up in how you operate? (The gap is the brief.)
9. If we polled your leadership team about what the brand stands for, where would they disagree?
10. If the brand were a person at a dinner party, what would they NEVER say?
11. What is your brand NOT? (Sometimes the easier question.)

### Business (6 questions)
12. What's your revenue model in one sentence? Who pays whom?
13. Where are you in growth — finding product-market fit, scaling, defending share, repositioning?
14. Who's the competitor that keeps you up at night, and why?
15. Who internally is enthusiastic about this project, and who's quietly skeptical?
16. In 6 months, what would tell you this project worked? In specific terms — leads, recognition, internal pride, retention, a moment in a meeting where someone said something?
17. What's the cost of NOT doing this? (Sometimes the strongest framing.)

### Project (7 questions)
18. What deliverables are you expecting? (If they list 14 things, the budget conversation just got real.)
19. What's the stated deadline, and what's the actual deadline behind it?
20. Who signs off? Who has veto power? Are those the same person?
21. What technical constraints will this design have to live within? CMS, print partner, packaging substrate, app guidelines?
22. What integrations matter — existing assets we keep, third-party templates, parent-brand systems?
23. What MUST be included? (Heritage marks, regulatory language, accessibility minimums?)
24. What MUST be avoided? (Past attempts that flopped, color associations with competitors, anything legal flagged?)

## 2. Written brief template (from kickoff notes)

```
PROJECT BRIEF — [Client Name] — [Date]

1. PROJECT IN ONE SENTENCE
In their words: "[client's stated ask, in quotes]"
In design terms: [the same project translated into deliverable+outcome language]

2. THE REAL PROBLEM
What's actually broken (often distinct from what they asked for):
[1-2 sentences naming the underlying problem the design needs to solve]

3. AUDIENCE
Primary: [specific description, e.g., "Operations leads at 50-200 person SaaS companies who handle vendor evaluation"]
Quote-style line — how they'd describe the client: "[invented but plausible quote based on what we know]"

4. BRAND POSITION
Wanted (three adjectives, but treat as direction not aesthetic): [adj1] / [adj2] / [adj3]
Allergic to: [adj1] / [adj2] / [adj3]

5. CONSTRAINTS
Budget: $[X]
Deadline: [stated date] (real deadline: [actual driver])
Technical: [CMS, print specs, accessibility, brand parent guidelines]
Organizational: [who decides, who reviews, who can derail]

6. SUCCESS METRIC
In 6 months, success looks like: [specific, observable, ideally not vibes-based]

7. DELIVERABLES AND STAGES
Phase 1 — Discovery & Concept ([X weeks]): [deliverables]
Phase 2 — Development ([X weeks]): [deliverables]
Phase 3 — Refinement & Handoff ([X weeks]): [deliverables]
Review gates: [where the client signs off before moving forward]

8. RISKS NAMED
Risk 1: [specific risk + how we'll address]
Risk 2: [specific]
Risk 3: [specific]

9. WHAT'S OUT OF SCOPE
[Explicit list — things the client has mentioned that aren't part of this engagement]
```

### Worked example: Section 2 — "The real problem"

> Client asked for: "a brand refresh because we're looking dated."
> The real problem: Smaller competitors with cleaner identities have started winning enterprise procurement reviews against this client, even when this client's product is stronger. The "looking dated" complaint is downstream — the brand isn't matching the maturity of the company it's become. A refresh that doesn't grow up the system won't fix this.

That's a brief. The client's words plus the designer's diagnosis.

## 3. Scope-tiered proposal template (lite / standard / premium)

```
PROPOSAL — [Client Name] — [Project Title] — [Date]

[Optional: one-paragraph project summary — who you are, what you're proposing to do. NO design philosophy paragraphs. NO "why design matters." One paragraph maximum.]

LITE TIER — $[X,XXX]
Deliverables:
- [Core deliverable 1]
- [Core deliverable 2]
Concept directions: 1
Revision rounds: 2 (each round = one consolidated set of feedback)
Source files: not included (final exports only)
Timeline: [X] weeks

STANDARD TIER — $[X,XXX] (recommended)
Deliverables:
- All of Lite, plus:
- [Expanded deliverable 1]
- [Expanded deliverable 2]
- [Brand guidelines, 10-15 pages]
Concept directions: 2
Revision rounds: 3
Source files: included
Timeline: [X] weeks

PREMIUM TIER — $[X,XXX]
Deliverables:
- All of Standard, plus:
- [Extended applications: web, social, print, packaging]
- [Brand voice doc]
- [1-hour launch consult]
Concept directions: 3
Revision rounds: 4 + 30-day post-launch tweak window
Source files: included + brand portal
Timeline: [X] weeks

WHAT'S INCLUDED IN ALL TIERS
- Discovery work
- Kickoff and concept presentation
- One in-person or video review per phase
- Final files in standard formats (PNG, JPG, PDF, vector where applicable)
- Email support throughout

WHAT'S NOT INCLUDED IN ANY TIER
- Stock photography licensing
- Custom photography or illustration commissions (quoted separately)
- Development / coding
- Printer/vendor coordination beyond spec handoff
- Ongoing retainer work post-launch

REVISION POLICY
A revision round is one consolidated set of client feedback delivered as a single document or call summary. Feedback delivered as scattered comments across multiple emails or weeks may extend the timeline. Feedback that exceeds the scope of revisions (e.g., a new direction after concept approval) is handled via a scope-change memo and billed at $[X]/hour or as a fixed addition to the project total.

PAYMENT
50% deposit on signing
25% on concept approval
25% on final delivery

NEXT STEPS
[Specific next step — sign and return, schedule kickoff call, etc.]
```

## 4. Design rationale framework (intent → choice → tradeoff)

```
DESIGN RATIONALE — [Project] — [Phase / Decision area]

DECISION 1: [What was decided, in one line]
INTENT: [What this element needed to do for the brand — functional + emotional]
CHOICE: [What was made. Specific. Named.]
TRADEOFF: [What this gave up]

DECISION 2: [...]
INTENT: [...]
CHOICE: [...]
TRADEOFF: [...]

(Repeat for each major decision)

PHASE 2 / ROADMAP NOTES
[Things this engagement doesn't solve that should be tracked for later — flagged as roadmap rather than left invisible]
```

### Worked example: brand identity wordmark decision

DECISION: Custom wordmark design
INTENT: The wordmark needed to feel grown-up enough for B2B procurement contexts (where the brand competes against established 20+ year-old players) without losing the warmth that the consumer audience associates with the brand. It also needed to read at small sizes (mobile nav, browser favicons, embroidered merch).

CHOICE: A modified humanist sans — based on a customized cut of [Typeface Name] with rounded terminals on the lowercase letters and adjusted optical sizing for sub-32px web usage. Two weights: Regular and Medium. No italics.

TRADEOFF: Custom modifications mean this wordmark cannot be reproduced in a standard licensed typeface — anyone trying to recreate it will get close but not exact, which we consider a feature for brand defensibility. The cost is that quick partner-led collateral (where partners might want to set headlines in the brand voice) requires either a brand toolkit handoff or a fallback typeface direction, which we've documented in the brand book as a Phase 2 deliverable.

## 5. Scope-change memo template

```
SCOPE CHANGE MEMO — [Date] — [Project Name]

ORIGINAL SCOPE (one line)
[The deliverables the original brief and proposal covered.]

NEW REQUEST (one line)
[What's been added or changed since the proposal was signed.]

IN SCOPE VS NEW

In scope (no change):
- [Item]
- [Item]

New work introduced:
- [Item] — [estimated effort]
- [Item] — [estimated effort]

COST IMPACT
Hourly rate: $[X]
Estimated additional hours: [range]
Estimated total impact: $[X] to $[Y]

OR (if fixed-fee):
Fixed addition to project total: $[X]
Revised total: $[X]

TIMELINE IMPACT
Original delivery: [date]
Revised delivery: [date]
Reason: [specific — e.g., "added 4 application mockups requires an additional concept-review cycle"]

RECOMMENDATION
[Option A: proceed at revised cost and timeline]
[Option B: defer to Phase 2 / separate engagement]
[Option C: descope an equivalent item to keep budget and timeline whole — name what would come out]

SIGN-OFF
To proceed with this scope change, please reply approving by [date]. Work on the original scope continues while we await sign-off. New work begins on approval.
```

### The kickoff-call line for revision caps

The single sentence that sets the expectation before contract:

> "One thing that helps every project I work on stay close to the timeline and the budget — feedback rounds work best when one person collects everyone's thoughts and sends them as a single batch. Scattered comments end up costing twice as much to address as one consolidated review. I'll send you a feedback template at each round so it's easy to collect."

That sentence does three things: defines a round, names the cost of doing it wrong, and offers a tool. Better than a clause.
