# Memory — Independent Hotel / B&B Operator Pack

## Domain context

An independent hotel, B&B, or boutique inn (10-80 rooms) lives between two worlds. On one side: corporate hotel brands with billion-dollar marketing budgets, central reservations, and chain-standard guest experiences. On the other: short-term rental hosts who can undercut on price and turn over a property for less. The independent's job is to be more interesting than the chain and more reliable than the STR. That requires actual personality (something a Marriott can't replicate) and actual operational discipline (something a one-off Airbnb often lacks).

Revenue comes from three channels. OTAs (Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, sometimes Hotel Tonight) drive 30-60% of bookings at a 15-25% commission. The direct website drives 20-40% — the highest-margin business, the operator's actual personality on display, and the channel that builds repeat. Group, corporate, and event business drives the balance, typically through a sales coordinator or the GM directly. The mix matters: a property that's 80% OTA is at the mercy of platform commission changes and search algorithm shifts. A property that's 60%+ direct has pricing power.

The rhythm of the work is daily, weekly, and seasonal. Daily: front desk operations, housekeeping, F&B if there's a restaurant, OTA inbox, direct inquiries. Weekly: RevPAR review, channel mix check, weekend group sales follow-ups, review responses, marketing decisions for the next 30 days. Seasonally: rate strategy, marketing pushes (shoulder season is when the writing matters most — peak season sells itself), property maintenance cycles, brand standard refreshes if part of a soft brand collection.

Success looks like: ADR holding or growing year-over-year, RevPAR up 5-10% in line with the market, OTA share trending down as direct share trends up, a TripAdvisor rating above 4.5, regular repeat guests who book direct, and the group sales calendar booking 12-18 months out. Failure looks like: OTA dependence creeping past 70%, a 1-star review that names a staff member, a group proposal that loses on price because the tax-and-gratuity line wasn't transparent, and an accessibility lawsuit because someone called a room "wheelchair accessible" that doesn't meet ADA.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- ADR: average daily rate — average paid per occupied room
- RevPAR: revenue per available room — ADR × occupancy. The headline metric
- Occupancy: % of available rooms sold
- OTA: online travel agency (Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, Hotwire, Priceline)
- BAR: best available rate — the published rack rate before discounts
- Direct booking: a reservation made through the property's own website (no OTA commission)
- Channel mix: the % split of bookings across OTA, direct, group, repeat
- F&B: food and beverage — restaurant, bar, catered events, room service
- BEO: banquet event order — the operational document for a group event covering F&B, AV, setup
- Room block: a group of rooms held for an event or group at a negotiated rate
- Attrition: the % of room block a group can release before paying penalty (usually 80%/20% attrition)
- Cutoff date: the date by which group attendees must book or the unsold block returns to inventory
- Group rate: discounted room rate for groups, usually requiring a minimum block size
- Walked: a guest who arrived with a confirmed reservation but no room available (the hotel was oversold). The hotel pays for an alternative property
- Comp / comp room: free room provided to a guest as service recovery, marketing, or contract clause
- Service recovery: the process of fixing a guest issue mid-stay or post-stay to preserve the rating
- Soft brand: hotels affiliated with a major brand's collection program (Curio by Hilton, Tribute by Marriott, Autograph) — get distribution and loyalty access while keeping independent identity
- Loyalty: chain loyalty programs (Bonvoy, Honors, World of Hyatt). Independents that aren't soft-branded build their own
- ADA: Americans with Disabilities Act — US accessibility law with specific structural requirements
- AODA: Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act — Ontario's accessibility framework
- Provincial accessibility regs: vary by Canadian province (Manitoba Accessibility Act, BC's Accessible British Columbia Act, etc.)
- Lodging tax / occupancy tax / DMF: city/county/provincial tax on hotel stays, often collected by the operator
- GST/HST: federal/harmonized sales tax in Canada
- TripAdvisor / Google / Booking.com reviews: the three review pages that matter most for independents
- Concierge: the staff or service that helps guests with restaurant reservations, activities, transportation, requests
- FOM: front office manager — runs the front desk team
- GM: general manager — runs the property
- RM: revenue manager — sets rates and manages channels; many independents don't have a dedicated RM

## Common workflows

- Daily OTA inbox: review new bookings, modifications, cancellations. Confirm any special requests. Respond to messages from booked guests with pre-arrival info.
- Direct inquiries: emails or website forms from prospective guests asking about availability, group rates, or specific needs. Respond within hours, not days. Drive to a direct booking.
- Pre-arrival communication: 48-72 hours before arrival, send a personalized email with arrival details, parking, area recommendations, any pre-stay add-ons (spa, dining, transfers).
- Concierge requests: come in by email, phone, or at the desk. Restaurant recs, activity bookings, transportation, mid-stay logistics, mid-stay issues.
- Group sales cycle: inquiry → site visit (or virtual tour) → proposal → contract → BEO → execution → post-event follow-up → repeat booking. 12-18 months for big groups, weeks for small corporate.
- Review response: GM or owner monitors TripAdvisor, Google, Booking.com, Expedia daily. 5-stars get same-day response. 3-4 stars get a thoughtful response within 48 hours. 1-2 stars get held 24-48 hours then carefully drafted.
- Rate strategy: weekly review of next 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 day pacing. Adjust BAR. Override on event dates, holidays, soft windows.
- Staff training: front desk staff on script, service recovery, group operations, accessibility compliance. SOPs documented.
- Marketing: shoulder season push, repeat-guest email, social, partnership outreach with local businesses.
- ADA compliance: annual audit, staff training on accessibility communication, accurate description of room features in all channels.

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- "We are pleased to welcome you," "It would be our pleasure to assist," "Please do not hesitate to contact us." This is corporate hospitality voice — it's everywhere on OTAs and it has no personality. Independent properties win on personality.
- Writing direct-website copy in OTA voice. Defeats the entire reason for direct copy. The reason a guest is on your website is because they want what an OTA can't give them.
- Over-apologizing in review responses. "We are deeply sorry" admits liability before the operator has decided the framing. Better: "The boiler issue affected three rooms that weekend — we've since replaced the unit."
- Bundling taxes and gratuity into the per-person F&B number on a group proposal. Surprises on the final invoice end a relationship.
- "Wheelchair accessible" used loosely. Has specific ADA meaning in the US and provincial equivalents in Canada. Name what's actually there: roll-in shower, grab bars, doorway width, ground-floor entrance route.
- Promising room features over the phone that the actual room doesn't have ("yes, all our rooms have AC" when one doesn't).
- Staff comping rooms without authorization, or refusing to comp when service recovery clearly warrants it. SOPs should pre-authorize the front desk up to a specific level.
- Walking guests with a "we'll find you something nice" attitude. Walked guests need a real plan, a paid stay at a comparable property, transportation, and a written confirmation that the original reservation is honored on the operator's dime.
- Treating Google and TripAdvisor reviews as equal. Both matter, but TripAdvisor's audience skews older and more affluent, and the review tone there reads differently than Google's casual local reviews.
- Group proposals that read like contracts rather than invitations. Lead with the experience, then the terms.

## Tone / register

A real independent hotel operator sounds warm but operationally tight. They speak of "the property" or "the inn" in the first person plural ("we"). They reference specific rooms by name or number ("Room 12, the corner suite with the bay window"), specific staff by role ("the breakfast team," "the front desk"), and specific neighborhood details ("two blocks past the bakery, on the right"). They acknowledge limitations without making them sound like flaws ("we're a heritage building, so the rooms vary in size and configuration"). They thank guests with a reference to something specific the guest mentioned. They never say "delighted," "pleasure," "do not hesitate," or "elevated guest experience." They say "looking forward to having you," "let us know how we can help," "the team will be glad to hear it." That's the voice.
