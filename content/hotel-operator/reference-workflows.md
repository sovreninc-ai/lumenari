# Reference Workflows — Independent Hotel / B&B Operator Pack

Real worked examples. Steal whichever ones fit your property. Names and details are placeholders.

## 1. Same room, two voices — direct vs. OTA

**The room (factual brief):** Room 12, corner suite, 1908 brick heritage building in downtown Banff. King bed, claw-foot tub, gas fireplace, two windows over the cafe street, second floor (3 floors of stairs, no elevator), 380 sqft.

**Direct website version (warm, personality-led):**

> Room 12 sits at the front corner of the second floor, with two tall windows looking down onto the cafe street where the morning bakery line starts forming around 7:30. The bay window has a built-in bench with cushions and a lamp — we end up reading there as much as our guests do. King bed, claw-foot tub deep enough to actually sit in, and a gas fireplace that takes about a minute to figure out the first time. Heritage building, so the stairs are part of the deal — 380 sqft means it's a real room, not a hotel cubicle. We're a few doors down from the bakery and four blocks from the gondola.

**Booking.com / OTA version (benefits-led, conversion-optimized):**

> Spacious 380 sqft corner suite on the second floor with king bed and city-view bay window. Features include claw-foot soaking tub, gas fireplace, premium linens, and complimentary fast WiFi. Free continental breakfast at the on-site cafe (7-10 AM daily). Walking distance to Banff Avenue shopping, restaurants, and the Banff Gondola (4 blocks). Heritage building — please note the property is three floors with no elevator. Non-smoking. Mountain views from front-facing rooms.

Why they're different: the direct version sells personality and the reason to book direct. The OTA version is scannable, keyword-dense, and answers the conversion questions a Booking.com searcher is asking (sqft, bed, breakfast, WiFi, walkable to what, any limitations).

The personality line (Long Beach Trail + gondola tip) is the difference between a confirmation and a confirmation that builds a relationship.

## 4. Concierge reply — restaurant recommendation

Guest message: *"Hi Mike — looking for a dinner recommendation for our anniversary on Saturday. Two of us, not too touristy, willing to spend up to $250 for the meal with wine. Any suggestions?"*

Response:

```
Hi Sarah,

For an anniversary at that level, three options I'd put on the list:

The Bison — a Banff fixture, modern Canadian, mid-priced ($150-200 for two with wine). The bison sirloin and the bone marrow are the things to order. Reservations a few days out.

Eden at the Rimrock — fine dining, multi-course tasting menu, $250+ a person before wine. Worth it if you want the full event. Book a week+ ahead, request a window table.

Park Distillery — neighborhood feel, wood-grilled everything, $130-180 for two. They take walk-ins at the bar if you can't get a reservation. Best brisket in town.

I'd lean Bison or Park for your budget. If you want me to call any of them for the reservation, send me your preferred time.

— Mike
```

Why it works: three named options, specific dishes mentioned, real budget guidance, offer to make the reservation. Not "we have many wonderful restaurants in the area."

## 5. Concierge reply — mid-stay issue (heating)

Guest message: *"Hi front desk — the heating in our room doesn't seem to be working. We're freezing."*

Response (sent within 5 minutes):

```
Hi — sorry to hear that. I'll be up to your room in 5 minutes with a portable heater while we look at the main unit. If the main unit can't be sorted tonight, we'll move you to Room 8 (same king bed, similar size) for the night. Either way, we'll have you warm in 10 minutes.

— Mike, front desk
```

Then later, after resolution:

```
Hi Sarah,

The thermostat was on a low overnight schedule from a previous setting — I've reset it and confirmed the room is heating normally. As an apology for the cold start, I've credited your account for the equivalent of one breakfast (which the cafe will charge to me, not you, when you go down in the morning).

Anything else, just text the desk.

— Mike
```

Why it works: response time (5 min), real plan (heater + room move backup), specific resolution, modest service-recovery credit named explicitly. The guest knows what was done, what was offered, and that the issue is closed.

**1-star (Google), held 36 hours before posting:**

Original review: *"Terrible experience. Room was cold, owner didn't care, place is overpriced and dated. Save your money."*

Response:

> David — I see your stay from June 6-8 in our records. Looking back at the night you flagged the heating, my team came to your room within 5 minutes, brought a portable heater, and reset the thermostat — the timestamps are in our system if you'd like to review. I also offered a one-breakfast credit, which you accepted. We hadn't heard anything from you after that resolution, so the rating took us by surprise. If there was something else that didn't go right, I'd genuinely like to hear about it — please email me directly at mike@northinnbanff.ca. — Mike

Why the 1-star response works: doesn't argue emotionally, states the facts in a calm timeline (we responded, we fixed, you accepted the resolution), offers a direct channel for what we don't know. The audience is every future searcher, not David. They read a host who was responsive and is now confused about what changed.

## 8. Front desk SOP — handling a comp request

```
SOP: Comp Requests — Front Desk

When a guest asks for a comp (free night, free breakfast, credit on room) at the desk:

1. Listen completely. Don't interrupt. Acknowledge the specific issue they're raising.

2. Determine if the issue is:
   a. Property fault (heating broken, plumbing leak, missed reservation)
   b. Shared/ambiguous (noisy guest in next room, wifi outage from ISP)
   c. Out of scope (weather, traffic, restaurant they didn't like)

3. Your pre-authorized service-recovery levels:

   Tier 1 (up to $50 value, no manager approval needed):
   - Free continental breakfast for the stay
   - $25 credit on incidentals
   - Free parking for the stay
   - Upgraded amenity (champagne split, charcuterie board)

   Tier 2 (up to $200 value, GM approval):
   - One free night
   - Significant rate adjustment
   - Comp upgrade to a premium room

   Tier 3 (over $200, owner approval):
   - Multiple free nights
   - Full stay refund
   - Cash refund / partial refund

4. For Tier 1 issues, offer the recovery in the moment. Don't escalate.
   For Tier 2, say "Let me check with our GM right now" and call.
   For Tier 3, say "I want to get this right — can I have our owner call you back within an hour?"

5. Document every comp in the daily log. The owner reviews these weekly.

6. If the guest is escalating in a way that feels coercive or threatening, get a manager involved and document the conversation.

Note: A guest who's right and treated quickly tells 1 friend. A guest who's right and treated poorly tells 30. Get to Tier 1 fast.
```

Why it works: front desk knows what they can offer without escalation, the guest doesn't wait for approval, the owner sees the pattern weekly. The AI tends to write SOPs that escalate everything — defeats the purpose.
