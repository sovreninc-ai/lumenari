## 1. Quote walkthrough — worked example

Carrier Travelers, HO-3 in suburban Atlanta, single-family 2,800 sqft, built 2008, no prior claims, finished basement, fenced yard with two dogs.

```
SUBJECT: Travelers HO-3 quote — review before we bind

Hi Maria,

Quote is attached. Walking you through the four pieces that matter, plus two limits we should talk about before binding.

THE BIG NUMBER
$2,140/year. Or $186/month with the installment plan (which adds $40 in fees over the year).

WHAT YOU'RE BUYING
- Dwelling (Coverage A): $485,000, replacement cost basis. This is what it would cost to rebuild your home at today's labor and materials. Comes from Travelers' replacement cost estimator, which I ran with the square footage, age, and finishes you gave me.
- Personal property (Coverage C): $339,500 (70% of dwelling), replacement cost. Replaces your stuff at new cost rather than depreciated cost.
- Liability (Coverage E): $300,000. Pays for bodily injury or property damage you're legally responsible for.
- Loss of use (Coverage D): $97,000. Hotel, food, additional living expenses if you're displaced.
- Deductible: $2,500 all perils. No separate wind/hail deductible in your zip — we're far enough inland.
- Endorsements added: water backup ($10K, $42/yr), service line ($10K, $35/yr), scheduled jewelry rider for the ring you mentioned ($8K appraised, $80/yr).

WHAT'S NOT COVERED — THE PARTS WORTH KNOWING
- Flood: never covered on an HO policy. NFIP or private flood is separate. Your zip is X-zone (low risk) but I can quote it if you want.
- Earthquake: standard exclusion. Georgia's seismic risk is low, but worth knowing.
- Wear and tear / maintenance: insurance covers sudden and accidental loss, not deferred maintenance. The 17-year-old roof is fine for coverage but worth a conversation if you're staying long-term.
- Business activity from the home: if anything beyond occasional remote work develops, we need to talk about endorsements or a separate policy.

THE TWO THINGS WORTH A CONVERSATION
- Your dwelling at $485K is the estimator output. If the kitchen reno from last year added more than the system thinks, we should refresh. Send me the contractor invoice and I'll re-run it.
- Liability at $300K is the legal floor most underwriters want for a household at your asset level. A $1M umbrella from Travelers runs about $280/yr based on your driving record. With two dogs and a pool — that conversation is worth having before binding.

NEXT STEPS
To bind, I need: signed application, proof of prior coverage, and a credit auth (Georgia uses credit-based insurance score). Effective date can be your current renewal date or any time before. Standard turnaround once everything's in: 24-48 hours.

Best,
[First name]
```

## 2. Renewal letter — premium up 18%

Same client a year later. Renewal comes back at $4,820, up from $4,090.

```
SUBJECT: Your renewal — what changed and what we can do

Hi Maria,

Your Travelers HO-3 renewal came back at $4,820/year, up from $4,090. That's a $730 increase, about 18%. Here's what's behind it and what your options are.

WHAT DROVE THE INCREASE
Three things. (1) Atlanta-area homeowner loss costs are up materially — Travelers and most carriers are taking 10-25% in this region. (2) Reinsurance pricing — carriers buy insurance from reinsurers, and reinsurance is up across the board, especially for wind-exposed regions. (3) Replacement cost inflation — your dwelling coverage went from $485K to $521K because rebuild costs are up. The dwelling increase alone accounts for about $190 of the $730.

This isn't a Travelers-specific story. It's a market story. That doesn't make it easier to write the check.

WHAT YOU CAN DO
- Stay as-is. Cleanest path. Premium is paid; nothing changes underwriting-wise.
- Raise the deductible. Going from $2,500 to $5,000 saves roughly $180/year. Going to $10,000 saves about $360. Tradeoff: bigger out-of-pocket if you have a claim. With no prior claims and a stable financial picture, $5,000 is a reasonable move.
- Adjust limits — I don't recommend this. Your dwelling at $521K is what the estimator says it costs to rebuild. Going under that means you're underinsured at a claim, which can also trigger a coinsurance penalty.
- Let me shop. I can run Liberty, Safeco, and Auto-Owners and have numbers in 5-7 business days. No commitment. Worth doing every 2-3 years even when you're happy with the current carrier.

WHAT I DON'T RECOMMEND
Dropping the water backup endorsement to save $42/year. That endorsement has paid out for two of my clients in the last 18 months. It's the cheapest coverage in your stack.

NEXT STEPS + DEADLINE
Your renewal date is Aug 4. To shop and bind elsewhere, I need a decision by Jul 24 to have everything in place. If you want me to shop, reply with a yes and I'll get started this week.

Best,
[First name]
```

## 3. Claim-support note — recent loss

Client called Tuesday morning to report a water loss from a failed water heater overnight.

```
SUBJECT: Your claim — what we have on file, what's next

Hi Maria,

Following up on what you told me this morning about the water heater leak. Here's where we are and what to do next.

WHAT YOU REPORTED
Failed water heater overnight Monday into Tuesday. Discovered Tuesday at ~6 AM. Water on the basement floor, into the finished area, soaked the carpet and the bottom of the drywall on the south wall. You shut off the water at the unit and called the plumber, who replaced the heater Tuesday afternoon. You also called ServPro for water mitigation; they're scheduled for Wednesday morning.

POLICY ON FILE
Travelers HO-3, form HO 00 03 10 00 (with Georgia amendatory endorsements). Coverage A (dwelling) $521K, Coverage C (personal property) $364K, deductible $5,000 all perils. Water backup endorsement is in place (separate coverage with its own sublimit) — though a failed water heater is typically covered under main dwelling/personal property, not water backup. The adjuster will make the coverage call.

CARRIER CLAIM INFORMATION
Claim filed Tuesday 7:14 AM (your call). Travelers claim number: [CLM-XXXXXX]. Adjuster assigned: [Adjuster name, phone, email]. Adjuster expects to call you within 24 hours; on-site inspection within 48-72 hours.

WHAT YOU'VE ALREADY DONE RIGHT
- Mitigated the loss (shut water off, called the plumber)
- Called ServPro for professional water mitigation — this is what the policy expects
- Photographed the damage before mitigation started

WHAT TO DO NEXT
- Keep every receipt: plumber, ServPro, anything you replace.
- Don't throw anything damaged out until the adjuster has seen it or photographed it.
- Document any contents loss with photos and a list (room by room).
- When the adjuster calls, walk through what you observed, what you did, and where the damage is. Be factual; let the adjuster make the coverage call.

I'M NOT THE ONE WHO DECIDES COVERAGE
The adjuster decides what the policy covers. If you disagree with the adjuster's decision, you have appeal rights through Travelers (the adjuster can explain the process). If it gets escalated, the Georgia Department of Insurance has a consumer complaint process. For a contested or large loss, a public adjuster or attorney is sometimes worth consulting — that's a separate professional from me.

I'm available if you want me to sit on a call with the adjuster or walk through the dec page again. Send me a note any time.

Best,
```

## 4. Life cross-sell — soft surface, then direct

Same client. Renovation just bumped the home coverage materially.

```
SOFT SURFACE (one line at the bottom of the renewal letter)

PS — Since the renovation pushed your home coverage up $40K, worth a 20-minute conversation about whether your life coverage is keeping up with where the family is now. No pressure — just a check-in.

---

DIRECT FOLLOW-UP (separate email, after client expressed interest)

SUBJECT: Life coverage — let's do the math

Hi Maria,

Glad you're open to the conversation. Here's how I'd run it.

WHAT I'D ASK FIRST
- Current life coverage in force — type (term, perm), face amount, carrier, when issued
- Your and David's current incomes
- Mortgage balance and rate
- Other debts (HELOC, car loans, business obligations)
- Kids: ages and education plans
- Anything in motion (business stake, real estate, expected inheritance)

WHAT WE'D DO WITH IT
Run a needs-analysis: income replacement (typically 7-10x earnings, with adjustments), debt payoff, education funding, final expenses. Compare to coverage in force. Find the gap or confirm there isn't one.

WHAT I WON'T DO
Quote you a product before we've done the needs analysis. Push a specific carrier or face amount as the "right" answer before the math says so. Reach for permanent life as a default — for most households, term is the right tool.

If you have an existing policy you might replace, Georgia has a replacement-form requirement we'd need to walk through together. That's a process step, not a stop sign — just something we do properly.

Want to do the needs analysis on a call this week? Half an hour by phone, or I can come by.
```

## 5. Dec page explainer — confused homeowner

Client called and said "I have no idea what any of this means" after pulling the dec page from the carrier portal.

```
SUBJECT: Your dec page in plain English

Hi Maria,

Here's what's on your dec page, line by line, in plain language. The policy contract is the binding document; this is a translation guide.

COVERAGE A — DWELLING ($521,000)
The cost to rebuild your home if it's destroyed. Pays on a replacement cost basis (new for old, not depreciated). The $521K is what Travelers' estimator says rebuild costs today. If you've done renovations the system doesn't know about, this number's stale.

COVERAGE B — OTHER STRUCTURES ($52,100)
Anything detached: fence, shed, detached garage, in-ground pool structure. Standard is 10% of Coverage A. If you have a big detached structure, we should talk about scheduling it up.

COVERAGE C — PERSONAL PROPERTY ($364,700)
Your stuff. Furniture, clothes, electronics, kitchenware. Standard is 70% of Coverage A. Replacement cost basis (you added that endorsement when we bound). Note: certain items have sublimits — jewelry, firearms, cash, business property. We scheduled the engagement ring separately for that reason.

COVERAGE D — LOSS OF USE ($104,200)
Hotel, food, additional living expense if you can't live in the home after a covered loss. 20% of Coverage A.

COVERAGE E — PERSONAL LIABILITY ($300,000)
Pays bodily injury or property damage you're legally responsible for. The kid throws a baseball through a neighbor's window; the dog bites someone; you're sued for slip-and-fall on your sidewalk. Maxes out at $300K per occurrence — this is where the umbrella conversation matters.

COVERAGE F — MEDICAL PAYMENTS ($5,000)
Small medical bills for guests, regardless of fault. Mostly a "good neighbor" coverage.

DEDUCTIBLE ($5,000 all perils)
What you pay before the policy pays. Same for fire, theft, water — no separate wind/hail in your zip.

ENDORSEMENTS
- Water backup ($10K): drains backing up into the home. Standard HO excludes this; we added it.
- Service line ($10K): underground utility lines from the street to your house. Carrier-specific endorsement.
- Scheduled jewelry rider ($8K): the engagement ring, scheduled separately so it's covered for full appraised value against any peril including loss.

MAJOR THINGS NOT COVERED
- Flood: separate policy required (NFIP or private)
- Earthquake: separate or by endorsement (Georgia: low risk, usually not added)
- Wear and tear: gradual deterioration isn't covered. Sudden and accidental loss is.
- Ordinance or law: extra cost to bring damaged building up to current code. Limited coverage built in; can add more by endorsement.
- Business activity from the home: limited coverage; if anything beyond occasional remote work, we need to talk

Want to walk through this on a call? 20 minutes, I can answer anything specific.

Best,
[First name]
```
