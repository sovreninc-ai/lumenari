# Memory — Cleaning Service Owner Pack

## Domain context

A cleaning service owner runs a labour-driven business with high turnover, thin per-job margins, and customer retention as the primary profit lever. A typical operation runs 3-15 cleaners on a route of 30-100 recurring residential accounts plus occasional one-time, deep, and move-out jobs. Cleaners are usually paid hourly or per-job; the owner takes the spread between route price and labour cost, minus supplies, gas, insurance, and admin.

Success looks like: every cleaner on the route stays past 90 days (industry norm is closer to 60), recurring clients hit month 6 (the cliff is month 3), and the owner spends more time on the route as a quality check than as a stand-in cleaner. Most owners started as a solo cleaner, hired a second when they couldn't keep up, and grew from there. The hardest transitions are owner-of-one to owner-of-three (your time gets split four ways) and owner-of-five to owner-of-ten (you need a real ops system, not a calendar app).

Margins on recurring clean: 15-25% net good month. Deep cleans and move-outs: 25-40%. Commercial recurring (offices, small retail): lower per-hour but more predictable. The biggest profitability lever is reducing rework (cleans that have to be redone or refunded) — every rework eats 1.5x the labour cost. The second-biggest is retention: keeping a client past month 6 turns the customer-acquisition cost into pure margin from month 7 onward.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- **Recurring:** Repeat scheduled clean — weekly, bi-weekly, every 3 weeks, or monthly
- **One-time / occasional:** Single clean, no commitment
- **Deep clean / detail clean:** First clean or annual refresh; catches what recurring assumes is already done (baseboards, inside oven, inside fridge, blinds, ceiling fans, grout)
- **Move-out / move-in / turnover:** Empty house clean, usually scoped to landlord standards; inside cabinets and appliances
- **Post-construction / post-reno:** Specialty deep clean with drywall dust focus; charges 2-3x normal deep clean
- **Airbnb / STR turn:** Quick turn between guests; tight time window, scope is reset-to-listing-photos
- **Make-ready:** Property-manager term for turning a rental between tenants
- **Recurring price / first-time price:** Most owners charge first clean at deep-clean rate, then drop to recurring rate from clean 2 onward
- **Top-to-bottom / S-pattern:** Cleaning method — start high (light fixtures, ceiling corners) and work down to floors
- **HEPA / microfiber / colour-coded:** Equipment basics — colour-coded cloths prevent cross-contamination (red for toilets, blue for glass, etc.)
- **CGL:** Commercial General Liability insurance — $2M is standard
- **WCB / WSIB / Workers' Comp:** Provincial or state workers' compensation coverage
- **Janitorial bond / dishonesty bond:** Insurance against theft by employee while on the job — different from CGL
- **Reference check / police check / vulnerable sector check:** Pre-hire screening — vulnerable sector required if cleaners may enter homes with children or elderly
- **No-show / lockout fee:** Charge for client cancelling within 24 hours or cleaner arriving to locked door
- **Scope creep:** Client adding tasks during a clean that weren't quoted (the #1 margin killer)
- **Walk-through / quote visit:** In-person estimate vs. phone or photo estimate
- **Booking / scheduling software:** Common ones — Jobber, Housecall Pro, Launch27, ZenMaid, ServiceM8
- **Tenant rights / damage deposit:** Move-out cleans are sometimes tied to deposit return; document your work with photos

## Common workflows

- **New client onboarding:** Inquiry → quote (phone or in-home walk-through) → schedule first clean → first-clean follow-up email → schedule recurring cadence → automated retention sequence kicks in.

- **Recurring clean visit:** Cleaner arrives → checks instructions in app → cleans per checklist → photo final state → leaves → owner reviews flagged issues from photos.

- **Move-out / move-in:** Booking confirmed with property address, key/access details, scope (with appliances Y/N) → cleaner completes detail clean → owner does walk-through and photos → invoice sent same day → if disputed by landlord, photos available.

- **Scope change request:** Client texts asking for an add-on ("can you also do windows this time?") → cleaner texts owner → owner approves and tells client cost → cleaner adds task → owner invoices the add-on.

- **Cleaner training (day 1):** New hire arrives → 30-min product walkthrough → ride-along with senior cleaner for 2-3 days → solo on a low-stakes recurring → review with owner after week 1.

- **Retention sequence:** After clean 1 → after clean 3 → month 3 → month 6 → month 12 — five total emails, automated where possible.

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **Don't use "sparkling, spotless, pristine, immaculate, gleaming."** Customers tune that language out. Replace with specifics: "dust-free baseboards, polished hardware, vacuumed under furniture."

- **Don't say "fully bonded and insured" without specifics.** Use real numbers (or placeholders for them): "$2M CGL coverage" and "[CONFIRM: bond amount] janitorial bond."

- **Don't quote prices in the AI output.** Per-hour and per-clean rates vary $25-$60+/hr depending on city, scope, and operator. Use placeholders.

- **Don't promise specific cleaners.** Routing changes. "Your team" or "our cleaners" is safer than "Maria will be your cleaner every week."

- **Don't quote a move-out without confirming: inside oven, inside fridge, inside cabinets, blinds, walls, baseboards, garage, exterior windows.** Each one of those is a scope line. Missing one is how you lose money on the job.

- **Don't write training docs longer than 1-2 pages.** Day-one cleaners don't read 12-page manuals.

- **Don't beg for reviews.** "We'd love a 5-star review!!" is cringe. Ask if they're happy first; only then ask for a review, in a separate later email.

- **Don't promise the same cleaner forever.** Cleaners leave. Set the expectation that quality is consistent, not personnel.

## Tone / register

A cleaning service owner sounds like someone who has spent the morning quoting a move-out, the afternoon covering a sick cleaner's route, and the evening doing payroll. The voice is direct, practical, and respectful of both customers and cleaners. They don't apologize for the price. They don't oversell quality (cleaning is cleaning — the differentiator is reliability, not magic). They use specifics ("we do baseboards on deep cleans, not on bi-weekly") rather than adjectives ("amazing attention to detail"). They text more than they email. Their training docs are short. Their customer follow-ups are warm but not chatty. They sign things "Thanks, [first name]" — never "Best regards" or "Sincerely."
