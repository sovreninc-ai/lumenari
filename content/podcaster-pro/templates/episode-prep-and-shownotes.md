# Episode Prep + Show Notes Templates

Five templates with worked examples. Drop them into the AI when you need that specific artifact. The worked examples show you what good looks like — if your draft doesn't feel like the example, the input was too thin.

---

## 1. Pre-interview research doc

### Prompt

```
Build a pre-interview research doc for an episode of [SHOW NAME].

Show: [name, niche, audience in one specific sentence]
Guest: [name, title, company, the ONE thing they're publicly known for]
What the host wants to ask: [the angle the host actually cares about — often NOT what the guest is known for]
Episode spine (one sentence): [the through-line]

Source material the host has reviewed:
- [list 2-4 sources, e.g., "Her 2024 SaaStr talk," "Lenny's Podcast ep #189," "her recent Substack post on burnout"]

Produce:
1. What this guest is asked in every interview (3-5 questions to SKIP)
2. The under-covered story — something she's alluded to but never fully unpacked
3. The contradiction — somewhere her public stance has shifted or two things she's said don't reconcile
4. 3 questions only this host would ask, given the show's angle
5. One topic to avoid — over-talked, will tank the first 10 minutes
```

### Worked example output

```
Pre-interview research: Amy Chen, founder of LinearCraft

1. What she's asked in every interview (skip these):
- "How did you go from idea to $30k MRR solo?"
- "What's your tech stack?"
- "What do you wish you knew when you started?"
- "Best productivity tip?"

2. The under-covered story:
In her Lenny's Podcast appearance she mentioned "the month I almost shut it down"
and immediately moved on. In her SaaStr talk she referenced "a stretch where I
didn't open the dashboard for three weeks." Both point at the same period
(likely month 9-10 of building) — she's never told that story straight through.

3. The contradiction:
In 2023 she wrote a viral thread arguing solo founders should never raise.
In her March 2025 newsletter she said "I'm reconsidering whether outside
capital would have shortened the journey." She hasn't reconciled those publicly.

4. Three questions only this host would ask:
- Most of our audience is in months 6-18 — what specifically happens in that
  window that solo founders aren't ready for, in your experience?
- When you say you stopped opening the dashboard, what were you doing instead?
- The 2023 you and the 2025 you on raising — what changed?

5. Topic to avoid:
"Walk us through your typical day." She's done that at least 6 times in
public interviews. The energy will drop and you'll lose the first 10 minutes.

---
Things to verify before recording:
- Confirm the SaaStr talk reference and date
- Confirm she's still building LinearCraft solo (last public confirmation March 2025)
- Skim her last 3 newsletter issues for any newer angles
```

## 2. Episode outline (interview)

### Prompt

```
Build an episode outline for [SHOW NAME], episode with [GUEST]. The spine is: [one-sentence spine].

Target episode length: [45 min / 60 min / 90 min]
Format: interview, host + guest only

Produce:
- One opening hook question — narrow, concrete, NOT "tell us about your journey"
- 4-6 topic anchors. For each: 1-line topic, 1-2 follow-up question stubs, one "if they go here, ride it" branch
- One strong closing question — NOT "what's next for you?"
- A short "questions to skip" list at the bottom (the over-asked ones)
```

### Worked example output

```
Episode outline — Amy Chen, "The month she almost shut it down"

Opening hook (narrow, concrete):
"In your Lenny's appearance you mentioned 'the month I almost shut it down'
and moved on. What month was that and what was happening that week?"

Anchor 1: The week before it broke
- Follow-up: What were the numbers actually doing? Churn? Stalled signups?
- Follow-up: Who did you tell, and who didn't you tell?
- Ride-it branch: if she names a specific email she didn't send, follow that.

Anchor 2: The three weeks she didn't open the dashboard
- Follow-up: What were you doing with that time?
- Follow-up: Was it depression, was it strategy, was it both?
- Ride-it branch: if she talks about what she was reading, follow that.

Anchor 3: What she did differently when she came back
- Follow-up: What did you stop measuring?
- Follow-up: What did you start measuring?

Anchor 4: The 2023-vs-2025 shift on raising
- Follow-up: What specifically changed your mind?
- Follow-up: What would you tell 2023 Amy about that thread?

Anchor 5: What our audience (founders in months 6-18) should hear
- Follow-up: What's the signal vs. noise on "should I keep going?"

Closing question:
"If you could go back to the morning of the day you almost quit, what's
the one thing you'd say to yourself?"

Questions to skip (already covered everywhere):
- Tech stack
- Daily routine
- "What's a productivity tip?"
- "How did you find your first 100 users?"
```

## 3. Show notes — long-form

### Prompt

```
Write long-form show notes for [SHOW NAME] episode [#]. 250-400 words.

Episode: [title]
Guest: [name, one-line credential]
Spine: [one-sentence spine]
Chapter map or transcript notes:
[paste]

Mentioned in episode (books/people/tools/articles):
[list]

Format:
1. Hook paragraph (2-3 sentences, friend-describing-it voice)
2. Why this matters to the listener (1-2 sentences)
3. What we got into (5-8 specific bullets)
4. Timestamps (only from the chapter map I just gave you)
5. About the guest (1-2 plain-language sentences)
6. Mentioned in this episode (bulleted)
7. Subscribe / follow line at the bottom, once
```

### Worked example output

```
Amy Chen built LinearCraft to $30k MRR in 14 months without a co-founder,
without funding, and without — she'll be the first to tell you — the
emotional infrastructure to handle what happens after the early traction.
This episode is about month 9, when she stopped opening the dashboard for
three weeks and almost quit.

If you're in the months 6-18 window of building something solo, the part
where she describes "what I was actually doing with that time" will
probably hit hard.

What we got into:
- The specific week the numbers stopped looking like growth and started
  looking like a flat line, and what she did the morning she noticed
- Why she stopped telling anyone — including her group chat of fellow
  founders — what was happening
- The three weeks she didn't open Stripe or her admin panel, and what she
  did instead
- What she changed when she came back, including the one metric she
  stopped tracking entirely
- Her shift between her 2023 anti-raising thread and her March 2025
  newsletter — and what specifically changed her mind
- The honest difference between "burnout" and "I built the wrong thing"
- What she'd tell herself the morning of the day she almost quit

Timestamps:
00:00 The opening question that took her three minutes to answer
06:42 What month 9 actually looked like
14:18 The three-week dashboard blackout
28:30 Coming back, and what she stopped measuring
41:05 The 2023-vs-2025 raising shift
52:10 The closing question

About Amy:
Solo founder of LinearCraft, a developer-tool company she's run since
late 2023. She writes about building solo at amychen.substack.com.

Mentioned in this episode:
- The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)
- Her March 2025 newsletter: "I was wrong about raising"
- LinearCraft → linearcraft.dev

If this episode pulled something loose for you, the easiest way to help
the show is to send it to one founder friend who's in the messy middle.

---
Things to verify before publishing:
- Timestamps came from the chapter map you supplied — spot-check 14:18 and 28:30
- "March 2025 newsletter" — confirm the exact title and link
- "linearcraft.dev" — confirm domain is current
```

## 4. Show notes — short-form (Apple / Spotify, under 600 chars)

### Prompt

```
Compress the long-form show notes above to under 600 characters for Apple Podcasts and Spotify.

Keep: the hook paragraph (or a tighter version) and the 5-8 bullets.
Drop: timestamps, "about the guest," "mentioned in episode," subscribe line.
```

### Worked example output

```
Amy Chen built LinearCraft to $30k MRR solo in 14 months. This episode is
about month 9, when she stopped opening the dashboard for three weeks and
almost quit.

What we got into:
- The week the numbers flattened and what she did that morning
- Why she stopped telling anyone what was happening
- Three weeks of not opening Stripe — what she did instead
- The metric she stopped tracking when she came back
- Her shift between her 2023 anti-raising thread and her 2025 reconsideration
- What she'd tell herself the morning she almost quit

(587 characters)
```

## 5. Social clip suggestions (5 candidates)

### Prompt

```
From this episode of [SHOW NAME] with [GUEST], suggest 5 social clip candidates for vertical short-form video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts).

Episode spine: [one-sentence spine]

Transcript or chapter notes:
[paste]

For each clip, produce: approximate timestamp, what happens (2 sentences with specifics), why it works (1 sentence), caption (Line 1 hook 3-5 words, Line 2 payoff 3-5 words, optional Line 3 only if earned), suggested length (30/45/60/90s).

Mix: at least one funny, one "wouldn't say on the record," one tactical, one emotional. Don't produce five identical wisdom clips.
```

### Worked example output

```
Clip #1 — "What I did with those three weeks" (emotional)
Timestamp: ~15:00
What happens: Amy describes opening her closet at 11am on a Tuesday and
realizing she hadn't worn anything but the same hoodie for nine days, and
that nobody had noticed because nobody was watching.
Why it works: It's the moment "burnout" stops being abstract.
Caption:
  Line 1: Nobody was watching.
  Line 2: That was the problem.
Length: 60s

Clip #2 — "The metric I stopped tracking" (tactical)
Timestamp: ~30:20
What happens: Amy names the specific metric she deleted from her dashboard
when she came back, and walks through why it was the wrong North Star.
Why it works: Specific advice, controversial-adjacent, savable.
Caption:
  Line 1: I deleted this metric.
  Line 2: Revenue went up.
Length: 45s

Clip #3 — "The thread I'd delete now" (wouldn't say on the record)
Timestamp: ~43:15
What happens: She names her 2023 anti-raising thread by tweet number and
says straight out she was wrong about most of it.
Why it works: Founders publicly contradicting their own viral takes are
rare and get shared.
Caption:
  Line 1: I went viral.
  Line 2: I was wrong.
Length: 30s

Clip #4 — "How I told my partner" (emotional, vulnerable)
Timestamp: ~22:40
What happens: She describes the exact sentence she used to tell her
partner she was thinking about shutting it down, and what her partner said
back.
Why it works: Real conversation, no startup-speak.
Caption:
  Line 1: I told him at dinner.
  Line 2: His answer surprised me.
Length: 60s

Clip #5 — "The investor I almost emailed" (funny / cringe)
Timestamp: ~38:00
What happens: She describes drafting a desperate email to an investor she
barely knew, getting to the send button, then closing her laptop and
walking to get coffee.
Why it works: Funny in the way founders find funny — recognition humor.
Caption:
  Line 1: I almost sent it.
  Line 2: Thank god I didn't.
Length: 30s

---
Things to verify before cutting:
- All timestamps are approximate from your chapter notes — confirm in the
  edit before exporting
- Clip #3 (the deleted-thread moment): confirm she actually names a tweet
  number on tape, or rewrite as "her 2023 thread"
```

## 6. Sponsor pitch email

### Prompt

```
Draft a cold sponsor outreach email to [BRAND].

Show: [name, audience in one specific sentence]
Why this brand fits the audience: [one specific reason]
Specific thing about the brand the host noticed recently: [campaign, founder interview, product launch — the host fills this in]

Show metrics (be conservative):
- Avg downloads per episode: [number, IAB-compliant 30-day window if known]
- Listener geography: [top 3 countries or regions]
- App split: [Apple X%, Spotify Y%, other Z%]
- Audience age/role: [if known]
- Past sponsor conversion data: [if available]

Ask:
- Placement type: [host-read mid-roll / pre-roll / custom integration]
- Episode count: [single / 3-pack / 6-pack]
- Rough CPM range: [if the host has one]

Subject under 50 chars, not "Sponsorship opportunity." Open with the specific brand reference. Lead with audience, not download count. Soft close — offer a media kit or sample read, not "let me know!"
```

### Worked example output

```
Subject: A note on your March founder interview

Hi [name],

Caught your interview with [founder name] on the [Brand] blog last month —
the part about why you stopped running paid acquisition for six months and
what changed afterward. That decision is the exact shape of conversation
my listeners have weekly.

I host The Build Log, a podcast for solo SaaS founders in the $5k-$50k
MRR window — the people who have product-market fit but haven't yet
hired anyone. Most of them are deciding right now whether to spend on
marketing tools or build it themselves. [Brand] sits dead-center of
that decision.

Quick numbers:
- Avg downloads per episode (30-day, IAB-compliant): 8,400
- Geography: 62% US, 18% Canada, 11% UK
- App split: 54% Apple, 38% Spotify, 8% other
- Audience: solo or 2-person teams, mostly technical founders 28-42
- Past sponsor conversion: HelloWorks ran a 3-pack last quarter and saw
  a 4.2% click-through on their trial offer (their numbers, shared with
  permission)

What I'd love to discuss:
- A 3-episode host-read mid-roll placement
- Roughly 60-second integrations, written by me, reviewed by you
- CPM in the $30-40 range, exact number depends on placement specifics

If it's a fit, I can send the media kit and a 60-second sample read so
you can hear how the integration would land. Either way, the [founder
name] interview was a good one.

— Chris
The Build Log

---
Things to verify before sending:
- Confirm the founder interview reference and date
- Confirm the 8,400 download average is conservative for last 3 months
- HelloWorks 4.2% CTR — confirm they've cleared you to reference it
- CPM range — confirm with the host's actual rate card
```
