# Memory — Podcaster Pack

## Domain context

Indie podcasters wear every hat: host, producer, editor, booker, sponsor liaison, social cutter, newsletter writer. A weekly show is roughly 8-15 hours of work per episode if it's interview-format, and 4-8 if it's solo. The rhythm is typically book guests 2-6 weeks ahead, batch-record 1-3 episodes when a recording window opens, edit on weeknights, publish Tuesday or Thursday mornings (the two strongest download days), then push social clips for 3-5 days post-launch.

The honest economics: most indie shows under 5,000 downloads/episode don't have host-read sponsors yet — they're running on programmatic ads at $15-25 CPM if they're running ads at all. Between roughly 5k-25k downloads/episode, host-read sponsorships open up at $25-45 CPM. Above 25k, dynamic insertion + direct deals get serious. CPM is per thousand downloads in a typical 30-day window; some networks use 60- or 90-day windows which inflates the number.

Success at the indie level looks less like raw downloads and more like: a backlog of guests who want to come on, a listener email list that opens at 35%+, social clips that get reposted by the guest's audience, and 2-3 sponsors who renew because their attribution numbers work.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- CPM: cost per thousand downloads, the standard unit for podcast ad pricing
- CPA: cost per acquisition, what direct-response sponsors actually care about
- IAB-compliant: download counting that follows IAB Tech Lab podcast measurement standards (the industry's anti-fraud baseline)
- Pre-roll / mid-roll / post-roll: ad placements at the start, middle, and end of an episode
- Ad pod: a sequence of multiple ads stitched together, common in dynamically inserted spots
- DAI: dynamic ad insertion, where ads are swapped in at delivery time rather than baked into the file
- Host-read: an ad voiced by the host, usually mid-roll, commands a 2-3x premium over DAI
- Promo swap: trading ad reads with another podcast in your niche for free
- Drop: the day a new episode publishes; "Tuesday drop" is the most common cadence
- Evergreen: an episode that keeps pulling downloads 6+ months after publish
- Apple chart / Spotify chart: category rankings used as social proof in sponsor pitches
- Chapter markers: timestamp-based section breaks listeners can skip between (Apple, Pocket Casts, Overcast)
- Cold open: a 30-60 second teaser before the show theme
- Riverside / SquadCast / Zencastr: the three remote-recording tools indie podcasters actually use
- 90-day window: the standard measurement period sponsors use for download promises
- Make-good: a free additional ad spot a host runs when a sponsored episode under-delivers on promised downloads

## Common workflows

- **Pre-interview research doc**: trigger = guest booked → steps = scan the guest's last 3-5 interviews, their book/most recent talk, their social, and pull the contradiction or under-covered story → output = a 1-page doc with what to skip, the spine, and 3 questions only this host would ask
- **Episode outline with topic anchors**: trigger = recording in 1-3 days → steps = host gives the spine + guest context → output = an outline with 4-6 topic anchors, not a script — each anchor is a place to land if the conversation drifts
- **Show notes (long + short)**: trigger = episode edited and ready to publish → steps = host pastes a chapter map or transcript → output = website/email long-form version + Apple/Spotify short-form version
- **5 social clip suggestions**: trigger = episode locked → steps = host shares transcript or chapter notes → output = 5 clips with timestamp ranges, why-it-works rationale, and 2-3 line captions for burned-in subs
- **Sponsor pitch email**: trigger = host has a brand in mind → steps = host gives brand name, why they fit, and current download numbers → output = a personalized cold email with specific brand reference, audience-first paragraph, metrics block, and a clear ask

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- Starting show notes with "On today's episode..." — every AI-generated show note opens this way and listeners scroll past it
- Inventing timestamps when no transcript is provided — they will be wrong and listeners notice
- Sponsor pitches that lead with download count — sponsors care about audience fit and conversion, not vanity numbers
- Writing the host as "we" when they're a solo host with no co-host — match the actual show format
- Listing every topic as "we discussed leadership" — the bullets need specifics ("how she fired her co-founder in the second week of YC")
- Promising "rate, review, subscribe" as the whole CTA — useful, but not the headline; the bottom of the notes, once

## Tone / register

A real indie podcaster sounds like a curious friend, not a host. Sentences are short to medium. They use the guest's actual words when they remember them. They name specific moments ("the part where she paused for like ten seconds before answering") rather than describing the conversation in abstract. They don't say "dive deep," "unpack," "today we explore," "join me as we," or "in this episode." They DO say "I asked her about," "she walked me through," "we got into," "the part that surprised me." They write the way they'd describe the episode to one specific listener over coffee, not the way a network promo would write it.
