# Memory — E-commerce / Shopify Owner Pack

## Domain context

A Shopify store owner runs an entire business out of a tab: product, photography, copy, ads, email, customer service, fulfillment, and reporting. Most of them are solo or 2-3 people, with revenue between $10k and $500k a month. The work is highly repetitive — every SKU needs a product description, an ad set across Meta/Google/TikTok, a Klaviyo welcome series tag, and a review response policy. The pain isn't strategy. It's volume.

The owner usually has good product instincts and weak copy instincts. They know what their best customer says about the brand and what their margins are, but ad headlines, subject lines, and product descriptions consistently underperform what they think is possible. They tried an agency once and it produced generic DTC voice that didn't match what they'd built. They tried a copy subscription ($300/mo) and got the same problem at smaller scale. They want a tool that respects their voice, fits their platform's character limits, and ships in minutes — not a 3-week creative brief.

The day-to-day is fragmented: a 4pm push to publish three new products before Friday's launch, a 9pm hour to write the abandoned-cart sequence, a 30-minute slot Sunday to respond to the week's reviews. Speed matters. So does honesty — readers can smell generic luxury language on a $24 product, and it costs conversion.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- **SKU:** stock-keeping unit — a unique variant of a product (size, color, scent). One product = many SKUs.
- **AOV:** average order value — total revenue / total orders. The number the owner runs everything against.
- **CAC:** customer acquisition cost — total ad spend / new customers. Tells the owner whether ads pay back.
- **LTV:** customer lifetime value — total revenue per customer over their relationship. Sets the ceiling on CAC.
- **ROAS:** return on ad spend — revenue / ad spend. Platform reports this; differs from real profitability.
- **MER:** marketing efficiency ratio — total revenue / total marketing spend. The honest version of ROAS.
- **PDP:** product detail page — where the product description lives. Above-the-fold matters most.
- **PLP:** product listing / collection page — where SKUs are listed for browsing.
- **CRO:** conversion rate optimization — the practice of tweaking the funnel to lift conversion.
- **Klaviyo:** the dominant DTC email platform. Flows = automated sequences; campaigns = one-off sends.
- **Mailchimp:** legacy email platform; still used by smaller stores.
- **Meta Ads Manager:** Facebook + Instagram ad UI.
- **Google Shopping:** product-feed-driven ads on Google search and surfaces.
- **TikTok Spark Ads:** organic posts boosted as ads from a creator's handle.
- **GMC:** Google Merchant Center — the feed that powers Shopping ads.
- **MOQ:** minimum order quantity — the smallest order a supplier will accept.
- **DTC:** direct-to-consumer. The dominant business model for the kit's audience.
- **B2B:** wholesale / retail accounts — selling to other stores rather than end consumers.
- **Above-the-fold:** the content visible before the user scrolls. Highest-conversion real estate on a PDP and the most important characters of an ad.

## Common workflows

- **New product launch:** owner pastes product name, category, materials, brand context, target price → AI produces the PDP description in 1-3 angle variants → ad set across Meta + Google + TikTok → 2-email launch campaign.

- **Weekly review response batch:** owner exports the week's reviews from Judge.me / Yotpo / Loox → pastes 5-15 reviews with star ratings → AI produces response variants by rating + tone → owner edits and submits.

- **Abandoned cart fix:** owner pastes their current abandoned-cart copy + Klaviyo flow analytics → AI rewrites the sequence to fix the underperforming send (usually email 2 — the one that's neither immediate nor a discount).

- **Supplier outreach:** owner identifies a potential supplier (Alibaba, Faire, a trade show contact) → pastes the product context + their projected volume → AI produces a first-contact email with appropriate MOQ negotiation framing.

- **Seasonal campaign in 24 hours:** Black Friday / Mother's Day / a flash promo → owner gives offer + product range → AI produces the full set (PDP hero updates, ad copy across platforms, 3-email campaign, SMS short copy).

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **Generic DTC voice.** "Transform your routine," "Elevate your wardrobe," "Curated for the modern [audience]." This is the default failure mode. Banned-word enforcement is the highest-leverage rule in this kit.
- **Luxury language on mid-range products.** A $24 candle is not "artisanal hand-poured craftsmanship." Match register to price point. Pretending breaks trust.
- **Ignoring character limits.** Meta primary text past 125 characters gets cut off above the fold. Google headlines past 30 characters get rejected. TikTok captions past 100 characters lose engagement. The AI must count.
- **Claims it can't make.** "Reduces wrinkles by 47%" without a clinical study, "boosts immunity" on a food product, "clinically proven" without a citation — these are FTC / Health Canada / ASA exposure.
- **Fake urgency.** "Only 3 left!" when there are 400 in stock. "Sale ends in 24 hours!" on an evergreen discount. Customers track this and the trust erodes faster than the lift it produces.
- **Stuffing every product with five-adjective stacks.** "Premium, luxurious, curated, elevated, indulgent." Pick one descriptor and earn it.
- **Writing as if the platform is the user.** Product descriptions get read on mobile, half a second after a thumb tap. Write for the scroll.

## Tone / register

A real Shopify operator writes in a voice that's specific to their brand and audience. A premium home goods brand sounds slower, more confident, fewer words. A supplements brand sounds direct and benefit-led. A streetwear brand sounds tighter, edgier, almost terse. What they share: concrete language ("merino, 18.5 micron, 230 gsm"), buyer-aware framing (they know the buyer is comparing to two other tabs), and no apology for what they don't include. The best DTC copy is confident about its scope — "this is what it is, this is what it isn't, here's the price, here's how to buy." The AI should match this confidence and avoid the generic "passion / curation / transformation" register that flattens every brand into the same brand.
