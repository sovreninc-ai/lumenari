# Memory — Moving + Junk Removal Pack

## Domain context

A moving company owner runs a logistics-heavy, labour-intensive business with thin margins, high seasonality (May-September is 60-70% of annual revenue), and customer experience as a primary differentiator in a commoditized market. A typical local/intrastate operation runs 5-25 trucks with 2-3 movers per truck, plus a dispatcher and sometimes a sales coordinator. Owner is often dispatching, quoting, and stepping in on crews when someone calls in sick.

Success looks like: trucks run 5-6 jobs per truck per week in peak season, the average local move is 4-7 hours, claims rate is under 3% of jobs, and Google review average stays above 4.5. The biggest profit lever is route density (back-to-back jobs in the same neighbourhood vs. dead-head miles) and the second is reducing rework — re-deliveries, claims, and partial refunds eat into thin margins fast.

Margins on hourly local moves: 15-25% net good month. Binding estimates: 20-35% if the inventory walkthrough was accurate; can go negative if it wasn't. Junk removal: 30-50% depending on disposal fees. Storage (if offered): 50-70% but capital-intensive.

The two biggest customer experience failures are (1) day-of late arrival with no communication and (2) hourly creep without warning. Both are fixable with a single text from the lead mover.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- **Hourly / non-binding:** Customer billed per hour from yard-out to yard-in. Common for local moves under 50 km. Estimate is just an estimate.
- **Binding / flat-rate:** Quote is the price, regardless of time. Requires inventory walkthrough. Risk on the mover.
- **Not-to-exceed:** Hybrid. Quote is the ceiling. Customer pays less if move is faster; mover eats the overage if slower. Pro-customer.
- **Weight-based:** Interstate/long-haul model. Out of scope for this kit.
- **BOL (Bill of Lading):** Legal document of the move; lists shipper, mover, inventory, terms, signatures.
- **High-value declaration:** Customer declares items over a threshold value separately; gets specific handling and coverage.
- **Released value protection:** Default liability — usually $0.60/lb in US, similar low rate in Canada. Customer often misunderstands this.
- **Full-value protection / replacement value:** Higher-tier coverage, customer pays premium.
- **Tariff:** The mover's published rate schedule (more formal in interstate contexts).
- **Long carry:** Distance from truck to entry that exceeds a normal threshold (usually 50-75 ft). Surcharge applies.
- **Stair carry:** Per-flight surcharge for stairs above ground floor.
- **Shuttle:** When the big truck can't access; goods are moved via smaller truck. Surcharge.
- **Hoist / rigging:** Crane or rope-and-pulley for items that can't fit through normal access (pianos, large furniture in tight buildings).
- **Wardrobe box:** Tall box with hanging rod; 1 foot of closet = roughly 1 wardrobe box.
- **Dish-pack / china-pack:** Reinforced box for dishes and breakables.
- **Pad / blanket:** Furniture pads — bring 5-10 per piece of furniture for a long-haul, fewer for local.
- **Hand truck / dolly / 4-wheeler:** Movers' standard equipment.
- **Glider / slider:** Used on hardwood floors.
- **CGL:** Commercial General Liability insurance — $2M is standard, $5M for commercial jobs.
- **Cargo coverage:** Insurance on the goods in transit (separate from CGL).
- **WCB / WSIB / Workers' Comp:** Required for crew.
- **USDOT number / MC number:** US interstate authority. Required for interstate moves.
- **FMCSA:** Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. Regulates interstate moves.
- **CAM (Canadian Association of Movers):** Canadian industry body.
- **Junk hauler vs. mover:** Different licensing in some jurisdictions; some operators do both, some just one.
- **Dump fee / tipping fee:** Per-load disposal cost at landfill or transfer station.
- **Hazmat:** Paint, propane, batteries, electronics, chemicals — can't go to standard landfill.

## Common workflows

- **Local move inquiry (hourly):** Customer calls or fills form → quick scope check (size, distance, access) → hourly estimate with low/high range → confirmation email with crew arrival window → day-before confirm → day-of comms (start, midpoint, end) → invoice + payment.

- **Binding estimate move:** Customer requests binding → owner schedules in-home (or detailed virtual) walkthrough → full inventory + access assessment → quote produced as binding → customer signs → move executed → no surprises unless scope changes (which get re-quoted).

- **Junk removal job:** Customer inquiry with photos → volume estimate (cubic yards or fraction-of-truck) → quote → schedule → arrive with truck → load and haul → dump → invoice.

- **Day-of late arrival:** Crew running behind on previous job → dispatcher calls/texts customer ASAP → reschedules window → updates ETA as it firms up → arrives → no surprise.

- **Damage claim:** Customer reports damage post-move → owner acknowledges within 24 hr → requests photos + specifics → reviews BOL and inventory → produces claim response (pay / partial / decline with reason / repair through contractor) → customer signs release on resolution.

- **Inventory walkthrough (binding prep):** Owner or estimator visits home → walks room by room with phone or clipboard → counts boxes, identifies specialty items, photos access → produces detailed inventory → binding quote.

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **Don't quote interstate moves.** Interstate moves are federally regulated (FMCSA in US, federal rules in CA). Bills of lading, tariff filings, weight-based pricing, 110% rule — defer to legal counsel and USDOT authority. The AI refuses these.

- **Don't promise zero damage.** "We treat your belongings like our own" is a setup for claims. Honest disclosure: "On long carries through tight stairways, small marks happen. Here's how we handle them."

- **Don't write claims responses that admit liability.** Acknowledge, investigate, then respond. Owner (and legal on big claims) reviews everything before it goes out.

- **Don't quote binding from a phone call.** Binding requires inventory walkthrough — virtual or in-home. Phone-quoted bindings are how owners lose their shirts.

- **Don't skip the day-of midpoint update.** The single most cost-effective customer experience move. A 30-second text at the 4-hour mark of an estimated 6-hour move prevents the "you ripped me off" complaint.

- **Don't promise specific crew members.** "Mike and Dave will be your movers" — what happens when Mike calls in sick? "Our crew" or "your lead mover [first name]" is safer.

- **Don't move hazmat or gassed equipment without flagging.** Paint, propane tanks, gas-powered lawn equipment with fuel — separate handling, often customer's responsibility to transport.

- **Don't claim "family-owned for X generations" or "voted #1" without verification.**

## Tone / register

A moving company owner sounds like someone who has spent the morning quoting a 4-bedroom on the phone, the afternoon dispatching three crews, and the evening dealing with a damage claim. The voice is direct, practical, and honest about what could go wrong. They don't apologize for the hourly rate. They don't oversell quality (every mover claims to be careful — the differentiator is communication and reliability). They use specifics ("3-truck job, 2 movers per truck, 6-8 hours including drive time") rather than vague reassurance ("we'll take great care of you"). They text more than they email. Their day-of communications are short and informational. They sign things "Thanks, [first name]" — never "Best regards" or "Sincerely."
