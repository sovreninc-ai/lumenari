# Subcontractor Onboarding Checklist

Use this before a subcontractor's crew sets boots on site. Skipping any of these is how a small problem becomes a stop-work order or a denied insurance claim.

---

## Phase 1 — Pre-qualification (before signing the contract)

- [ ] **WCB / WSIB / Workers' Comp clearance letter** dated within 60 days. Subcontractors in good standing only. If they can't produce one, they can't carry a sub on your jobsite — your premium will get hit when their worker gets hurt.
- [ ] **Commercial General Liability** certificate, minimum $2M per occurrence / $5M aggregate. Verify your company is listed as **additionally insured**.
- [ ] **Automobile liability** $2M minimum.
- [ ] **Umbrella / Excess** $5M is typical for larger jobs.
- [ ] **COR (Certificate of Recognition)** or **SECOR** for Alberta projects > $X (varies by client). Even when not required, ask — it's a quality signal.
- [ ] **Safety statistics**: LTI frequency, TRIF, incident reports for the last 3 years. If they can't produce them, that's a sign.
- [ ] **Reference check**: two recent project clients. Call them.
- [ ] **WHMIS / SDS readiness** — they're bringing chemicals to your site. Where are the SDS sheets?
- [ ] **Trade-specific certifications**: welders (CWB tickets), electricians (Master Electrician + journey tickets), riggers, fall protect, confined space, etc.

## Phase 2 — Contract sign-off

- [ ] Scope of work clearly defined in writing
- [ ] Schedule milestones
- [ ] Payment terms (net 30, retention amount, lien hold-back)
- [ ] Site rules attached and signed
- [ ] Stop-work clause for safety violations
- [ ] Change-order process documented
- [ ] Indemnity language reviewed

## Phase 3 — Mobilization (day 1 on site)

- [ ] **Site-specific orientation** — your safety officer walks the sub's lead through:
  - Site map, muster point, first aid station
  - Hazard inventory specific to this site (medical gas lines, asbestos, energized equipment)
  - Sign-in/sign-out procedure
  - PPE expectations + verification
  - Reporting expectations (who, what, when)
- [ ] **Site-specific JHA** for the sub's first task — co-signed with your lead
- [ ] **PPE verification** for every worker entering the site (hard hat, safety glasses, gloves, footwear, hi-vis as required)
- [ ] **Tool inspection** for major equipment (slings, harnesses, ladders) — at minimum the visual pre-use inspection
- [ ] **Emergency contacts** — sub's lead has your safety officer's cell

## Phase 4 — Ongoing (weekly while sub is on site)

- [ ] Sub attends your weekly site safety meeting
- [ ] Sub submits their toolbox talk records (you don't have to read them all, but you need them on file)
- [ ] Any near-miss or first-aid event in the past week is documented and shared
- [ ] WCB / insurance clearance is still current (re-check at 60 days)
- [ ] Payment milestones reviewed — late payment is correlated with safety shortcuts

## Phase 5 — Demobilization

- [ ] Punch list signed off
- [ ] Final WCB clearance for billing
- [ ] Site cleanup verified
- [ ] As-builts / O&M docs delivered
- [ ] Lien waiver signed (where applicable)
- [ ] Performance file updated for future jobs

---

## Red flags that should make you walk away

- Won't share WCB letter
- "We don't usually do JHAs"
- Pushes back on PPE rules
- Their references are vague or evasive
- Their bid is 20%+ below the next lowest

The cheapest sub on paper is rarely the cheapest sub in practice.
