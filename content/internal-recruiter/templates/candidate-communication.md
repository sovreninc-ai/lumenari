# Candidate Communication — Internal Recruiter Pack

Emails and conversation scripts at every stage. The bones can be templated; the body has to feel like a person wrote it. Every candidate gets human treatment — even (especially) the rejection.

The full worked sequence is in `reference-workflows.md` Example 3. This file is the per-stage structure and the patterns.

---

## The rule of human

Every candidate email passes these checks before sending:

1. First name basis (match what they signed)
2. Specific to the role and the stage (not "thanks for your interest")
3. Honest about timing ("decision by Friday 4pm; if it slips, you'll hear from me Monday by 10am")
4. Never overpromises ("you'd be a perfect fit" before the offer is in their inbox — never)
5. Inclusive-language audit applies (no protected-class language, no proxy targeting)
6. Door-state is honestly stated (open / closed / "for this seat, no; for future, yes")

## Stage-by-stage templates

### Outreach (if recruiter is sourcing directly — see talent-acquisition pack for fuller outreach playbook)

Short. Specific. Three sentences:
1. One specific reason — something they couldn't have guessed you'd notice
2. One reason your role might be interesting to them
3. One soft ask (15 min, no pressure)

### Screen confirmation

After candidate has accepted the recruiter screen.

```
Subject: Looking forward to [day] — [Role] @ [Company]

Hi [Name],

Confirmed for [day, date], [time], [timezone]. 30 minutes. [Zoom/Meet/phone] — link in the calendar invite.

Here's what we'll cover:
- Your work in your own words
- The role specifics — [team], [main scope item], [logistic item like on-call]
- Comp expectations vs our band, so we don't waste each other's time later
- Anything you want to ask me about [Company] or the loop

If [time] doesn't work, hit me with two windows that do and I'll re-book.

— [Recruiter name]
[Title] at [Company]
```

### Post-screen — positive, moving forward

```
Subject: [Company] — moving forward to HM screen

Hi [Name],

Good conversation. I'd like to move you to the next step — a [duration] screen with [HM name], [HM title] and the hiring manager for this role.

[HM] is the person you'd report to. [One sentence about HM's background — career arc, recent work, blog post, talk]. The conversation will be more [technical / scope-focused / customer-focused] than a typical HM screen.

LinkedIn: [URL]
[Optional: a relevant recent post or blog from the HM]

I have [HM]'s availability:
- [Day, date], [time-range], [timezone]
- [Day, date], [time-range], [timezone]
- [Day, date], [time-range], [timezone]

Reply with what works and I'll send the invite.

Two things from our conversation I want to flag back to you so we're aligned:
- Comp: our band is [range]. You mentioned target is [their stated number] — [in band, no concerns / outside band, here's the conversation we need to have at HM screen]
- Timing: you're at [notice period]. Our target start date is [date], which [works / would need conversation]

Talk soon.

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Post-screen — no move forward (warm rejection)

```
Subject: [Role] @ [Company] — update

Hi [Name],

Thanks for the time today. After our conversation, I'm not going to move you forward to the hiring manager screen.

The specific gap: [one specific thing, tied to a must-have from the scorecard — be honest]. That's the central must-have for this seat.

[Optional, if applicable: For a different role at a similar stage company, the work you described is right in the zone.]

[Door state: "If a different role opens here where [their strength] is more central, I'll reach out specifically." OR "Wishing you well in the search."]

Thanks again.

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Onsite scheduling

```
Subject: [Company] — Final loop, scheduling

Hi [Name],

[HM] gave a strong signal — let's get the full technical loop scheduled.

Format: [remote/hybrid/in-person], [duration] total ([breakdown]), [can be split / one block].

[If applicable: "We don't do brainteasers or whiteboard algorithms. Every session is real work."]

The panel:
1. [Name] — [title]. [Format of their session], [duration]. [One line on what they assess / a note about their style if relevant — e.g., "Jordan is direct and asks 'why' a lot — that's how she works, not a sign she's unhappy"]. [LinkedIn URL]
2. [Name] — [title]. [Session description]. [LinkedIn URL]
3. [Name] — [title]. [Session description]. [LinkedIn URL]
4. [Final stage with VP/Director] — [duration].

Slots open for the technical loop:
- [Day, date], [time-range, timezone]
- [Day, date], [time-range, timezone]
- [Day, date], [time-range, timezone] (split format option)

[Final stage] will be the [day after / week after] the technical loop concludes.

Tell me which window works and I'll lock the calendar invites + send a one-pager prep doc by [day].

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Pre-onsite prep email

```
Subject: [Company] — Prep for [Day]'s loop

Hi [Name],

A few things ahead of [day]:

Schedule:
[Specific timeline with each session's start/end + breaks]

The panel + what each session is:
[Quick recap with links to LinkedIn or relevant team content]

What to expect:
- [Specific format notes: "the system design session is whiteboard-free; we'll work in a shared Miro doc you'll keep access to after"]
- [Specific cultural notes: "Jordan likes to be challenged; don't agree with her if you disagree"]
- [Logistics: lunch, breaks, who handles handoffs between sessions]

What we'd want to hear in your strongest answers:
- [Specific signals tied to the must-haves, framed as "this team values..." not "we will grade you on..."]

What we don't ask:
- [If applicable: "We don't do brainteasers, we don't do whiteboard algorithms, we don't ask you to whiteboard data structures."]

If anything comes up between now and [day], reply to this thread.

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Post-onsite — during debrief window

```
Subject: [Company] — Decision timing

Hi [Name],

Wrapped your [final] earlier today. Thanks for the long week.

We're debriefing [tomorrow / day] at [time]. I'll have a decision communicated to you by [day, time] at the latest. If it slips [common reasons], you'll hear from me by [explicit backup day, time] either way — no radio silence.

Whatever the outcome, I'll call. Not email-only.

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Offer extension — phone call script

This is what the recruiter says on the call. Always phone first, not email.

```
"Hi [Name] — [Recruiter] from [Company]. I have an offer for you. Got a few minutes?

[pause for confirmation]

The team loved the loop. [Specific panelist feedback — what they said the candidate did well, in 2-3 sentences. Make it real.]

So — the offer.

Base [$X], sign-on [$Y], equity [Z%] which works out to about [$RSU value] at the current valuation, vesting 4 years with a 1-year cliff. [W%] target bonus paid annually. Standard benefits. Start date [date].

[Summary in one line: "That's [base], [sign-on], [equity], [bonus target]. Year 1 cash [$total], total comp roughly [$all-in]."]

I'm sending the written offer in about an hour via DocuSign. Take 5-7 business days. Talk to your partner, talk to anyone you need to talk to. No pressure on response time, but I want to be honest with you about one thing.

Counter-offers from current employers are common at this point. I want to flag now so we're aligned later:
(1) You leaving is a problem for them. They'll offer something. Probably money.
(2) The reason you started this conversation wasn't money. It was [their actual stated motivation from earlier conversations — scope / manager / growth / brand].

When the counter comes, I'd love to talk it through. I'm not going to pressure you. I just want you to make the decision you actually want, not the one that's easier in the moment.

Questions?"
```

### Offer extension — written follow-up email

Sent 1 hour after the call.

```
Subject: Offer — [Role] @ [Company]

Hi [Name],

Per our call — sending the written offer.

Summary:
- Base salary: $[X]
- Sign-on bonus: $[Y] (paid in first paycheck)
- Equity: [Z%] ([#] RSUs, 4-year vest with 1-year cliff)
- Target bonus: [W%] of base ($[bonus dollar value] at target, paid annually)
- Start date: [date]
- Benefits: [link to one-pager]
- Reports to: [HM name, title]

DocuSign packet incoming separately from [legal contact].

Decision window: 5-7 business days. Let's plan to talk [specific check-in day] either way.

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Post-onsite rejection — more substantive

```
Subject: [Company] — Decision on the loop

Hi [Name],

Thanks for the full loop [yesterday / this week]. After the panel debrief, we've decided not to extend an offer. I want to give you the real reason rather than a form letter.

The panel was [positive on X, Y — be specific to what they actually saw]. The gap was [specific area tied to a must-have — e.g., "system-design depth at the multi-region scale we operate at"]. [Sometimes useful: "Two of the panelists wanted to move forward; the call from the hiring manager was that the gap was the central must-have for this seat."]

This isn't a rejection of you — it's a calibration mismatch. If a different role opens at [Company] where [their strength] is the central need, I'll reach out specifically.

Thank you for the time and for letting us learn from you. I know this is the one part of the loop that doesn't get easier.

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Post-onsite rejection — no-offer after panel debate

```
Subject: [Company] — Decision

Hi [Name],

A tough one. Thanks for the time with our team this week.

After a long debrief, we decided not to move forward with an offer for this seat. The panel was genuinely split — [number] strong-hires, [number] lean-no-hires — and when the panel is that divided, the hiring manager's call is to wait for higher confidence.

The specific thing the lean-no-hires raised: [one specific area]. The strong-hires were anchored on [one specific strength].

This is the kind of close call where I'd love to keep in touch. If a role opens in [adjacent area or future timing] in the next 6-12 months, I'll reach out specifically.

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Reference check coordination

```
Subject: [Role] @ [Company] — references

Hi [Name],

We're at the reference-check stage — last step before offer prep.

I need 3-5 references, ideally:
- 2 managers you've reported to (former, NOT current unless you explicitly authorize)
- 1-2 peers who've worked closely with you
- 1 direct report or junior you've mentored (if applicable to the level)

For each: name, title, current company, relationship to you (and how long), email and/or phone preferred.

I'll reach out by email first, offer a 15-min call window. The conversations are about scope of work, growth, work style, areas where they saw you stretch. Never personal life. Never anything outside the work itself.

Send the list by [day] and I'll start the outreach [day].

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Reference call structure (recruiter does the call)

Questions to ask (every call):
1. "How did you work with [candidate] and for how long?"
2. "What did they own? What did they ship?"
3. "Where did you see them stretch?"
4. "What's an area you'd coach them on if they joined your team today?"
5. "If they applied for a role on your team tomorrow, would you hire them again?"
6. "Is there anything I should know that I haven't asked about?" (open-ended catch-all)

Questions you DO NOT ASK:
- Anything about marital status, parental status, age, religion, race, disability, sexual orientation, citizenship beyond work authorization, political affiliation
- "Is there any reason we shouldn't hire them?" (invites unfair dirt-digging)
- "Are they difficult to work with?" (invites bias)
- Anything about salary they were paid (illegal in some jurisdictions)

### Background check disclosure (US, FCRA-compliant)

```
Subject: [Role] @ [Company] — Background check next step

Hi [Name],

We're moving to the offer-prep stage and background check is the next gate.

Two documents incoming separately:
1. FCRA disclosure — standalone document, required by federal law to be separate from anything else
2. Written consent form

Once you sign both, our background check provider (Checkr) will reach out to you directly to confirm the data they need (employment history, education, criminal record check — varies by jurisdiction).

Standard timing: 3-7 business days. If it takes longer (sometimes verifying older employment records takes time), I'll keep you posted.

If anything in the check comes back that affects the offer, FCRA requires a pre-adverse-action notice with 5 business days for you to respond. You'd hear from me directly first, not from Checkr.

Questions? Reply to this thread.

— [Recruiter name]
```

## Inclusive-language audit on candidate communication

Less common to trip the audit on per-candidate emails than on JDs, but still check for:

- **Demographic-assumption language**: "you'd be perfect for our young team" → cut
- **"Culture fit" framing in feedback**: "the panel felt you weren't quite a culture fit" → never. Use specific behavioral feedback
- **Patronizing language in rejection**: "We'd encourage you to apply when you have more experience" → cut. Specific gap or nothing
- **Gendered closing**: "Cheers, [name]" reads neutral. "Best regards" reads neutral. "Take care, darling" — never (yes, it happens)

---

## What never goes in candidate communication

- Form-letter language ("We were impressed with your background and would love to move forward")
- "Rockstar," "ninja," "guru," "wizard," "passionate," "fast-paced," "world-class"
- Promises before the offer is signed ("you're going to love it here")
- Vague timing ("we'll be in touch") — always specific
- Reasons that aren't true ("we've decided to pursue other candidates" when really the panel didn't like the system-design depth)
- Comp numbers before the offer is approved by HRBP + finance
- Any commentary on protected-class characteristics

Things to verify before sending any candidate email:
- First name matches what they've signed
- Specifics in the body are accurate (panel names, dates, comp figures)
- Timing commitments are realistic (don't promise Friday 4pm if the HM is out)
- Door state is honest (open / closed / future-yes)
- Inclusive-language audit run, especially on rejection emails
