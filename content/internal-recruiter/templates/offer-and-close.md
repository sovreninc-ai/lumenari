# Offer Prep + Close — Internal Recruiter Pack

The offer doesn't get extended until the prep is done. The prep is the document you walk into the offer call with, knowing the counter is coming. Surprise = lose.

Full worked offer prep example is in `reference-workflows.md`. This file is the structure, the counter-offer playbook, and the comp conversation scripts.

---

## Offer prep checklist (use for every offer)

```
OFFER PREP — [Candidate name] — [Role] — [Date]

COMP COMPONENTS
- Base salary: $___ (within band $___ to $___; at [percentile])
- Sign-on bonus: $___ ([standard for level / exception with HRBP approval])
- Variable / target bonus: $___ at target ([%] of base, payout cadence, metric)
- Equity: [#] shares / [%] / RSUs valued at $___ at strike, vesting over [X] years with [Y] cliff
- Start date target: [date]
- Benefits: [link to one-pager]
- Relocation: [yes/no, amount, structure]
- Other: [allowances, stipends, signing perks]

TOTAL YEAR 1 COMP: $___ (base + sign-on + target bonus)
TOTAL YEAR 1+2 (with vesting equity): $___

CANDIDATE'S COMPETING CONTEXT (gathered from screens, references, network)
- Current comp at present role: $___
- Other offers in play: $___ at [Company], $___ at [Company]
- Stated minimum acceptable: $___
- Motivations beyond comp (in priority order, candidate's own words):
  1. ___
  2. ___
  3. ___
- What they said they don't want: ___

LIKELY COUNTER SCENARIOS
- Counter on base: we can flex from $___ to $___ with HRBP approval (pre-discussed with HRBP [name], date [date])
- Counter on sign-on: we have $___ exception authority
- Counter on equity: [typically firm / flexible — explicit]
- Counter on start date: flexible up to [date]
- Counter on bonus target: [firm / flexible]

WALK-AWAY POINT
[The package level at which we don't extend, agreed by HM + HRBP + recruiter beforehand]

EXTENSION CALL SCRIPT
- Who calls: [recruiter only / recruiter + HM / HM only — depends on relationship]
- When: [scheduled date and time]
- Format: phone call → verbal → written offer 1 hour after → DocuSign within 24 hours
- Decision window: [typical 5-7 business days; extend if competing with another active offer]

COUNTER-OFFER PREP (script the recruiter uses on the call, BEFORE the candidate has any counter)
"Counter-offers from current employers are common at this point. I want to flag now so we're aligned when it happens:
(1) You leaving is a problem for them — they'll offer something, probably money
(2) The reason you started this conversation wasn't money — it was [their stated motivation 1, 2, 3]
When the counter comes, I'd love to talk it through. I'm not going to pressure you. I just want you to make the decision you actually want."

APPROVAL STATUS (must all be ✓ before extension)
- HRBP: [name] signed off [date]
- Finance: [name] signed off [date] (especially for equity, sign-on)
- Legal: offer-letter template reviewed by [name] [date]
- HM: signed off on package [date]
- (For senior roles) VP / CEO sign-off: [name] [date]

POST-EXTENSION CHECK-IN PLAN
- Day 1 (24h after extension): light check-in by text — "how's it landing?"
- Day 3: substantive check-in — "any questions on the package, anything to think through?"
- Day 5: pre-deadline check-in — "where are you at, anything I can clear up?"
- Decision day: respond within 4 hours of accept or counter
```

## Comp conversation scripts

### Script 1: First mention of comp at recruiter screen

```
"Before we go too far, let's talk comp so we don't waste each other's time later.

Our band for this role is $[base low] to $[base high] base, plus [equity / variable] and standard benefits. Where are you in terms of what would work for you?

[Listen — they'll either name a number in band, above band, or hedge.]

[If in band]: Good — we're aligned. Let's keep talking.

[If above band]: Be honest about the gap. "That's outside our current band — what I can do is flag it now so neither of us spends time if it's a no-go. Is the gap on base specifically, or are you weighting the total comp picture differently? Sometimes there's room with sign-on or start date."

[If hedging]: "Totally fair. Let me ask differently: what's the lowest number you'd say yes to if everything else was right?"
```

### Script 2: Verbal offer extension

(Full script in `reference-workflows.md` Example 3, Stage 5. Key beats:)

1. Acknowledge what the panel saw (specific, not generic)
2. Lead with base
3. Walk through each component with the candidate (don't read the whole package as one long sentence — pause)
4. State the total Year 1 cash and total Year 1+2 comp (helps the candidate frame the offer)
5. Set the timeline for the written offer (1 hour) and decision window (5-7 business days)
6. Deliver the counter-offer prep paragraph
7. Stop and ask if there are questions

### Script 3: Candidate counter-proposes on the call (sometimes happens)

```
Candidate: "Can you do $220k base?"

Recruiter: "Let me be straight with you. My band ceiling is $215k. I have some exception authority but I can't promise a $5k flex on the spot — I need to talk to HRBP first.

A couple of questions to help me make that case:
- Is $220k a number you'd accept, or a starting point for further negotiation?
- What's the gap doing for you specifically — is it about matching a competing offer, or about your minimum walk-away?
- If I came back with $215k base + $8k more in sign-on (so Year 1 cash is $223k), would that close it?

I'll call you back tomorrow morning either way."

[Then: walk the conversation to HRBP, model the counters, come back with a real answer the next day]
```

### Script 4: Candidate brings up a counter-offer from current employer

```
"OK — what did they offer?

[Listen.]

A few things:

First — the fact that they came back at [their counter number] tells you what they value. Glad they did, because it confirms what we already thought, which is that you're worth that comp on the market.

Second — money is real, but it's not the only thing on the table. When we first talked, you said three things you wanted next: [list their stated motivations]. That counter doesn't change any of those.

Third — let me check what flexibility we have. I'm not promising anything. I want to talk to HRBP and [HM] specifically and come back to you tomorrow with an honest answer. The band is $X-Y. We may have exception authority for part of the gap. We may not. I'll be straight with you either way.

Fourth — your current company would have offered this counter 6 months ago if they'd valued you at this number. They're offering now because losing you is the problem. I'd weight that.

Take 24 hours. Talk to whoever you talk to about this stuff. We'll talk tomorrow at [time] and I'll have the comp answer plus anything else you want to walk through."
```

### Script 5: The candidate says yes (verbal accept)

```
"That's great. I'm thrilled.

Couple of next steps:
1. DocuSign packet is incoming from [legal contact] — sign by [day] when you're ready
2. Background check kicks off as soon as DocuSign is signed — Checkr will reach out directly. Usually takes 3-7 business days
3. Start date is [date] — I'll get you connected to the People Ops team for onboarding paperwork the week before
4. Equipment shipment usually goes out 5 business days before start date — I'll have IT reach out to confirm your shipping address
5. Your manager [HM name] will reach out by next week to set up a virtual coffee before start date

If you have questions in the next week or two, hit me. After your start date, you'll work directly with [onboarding contact / HM].

Welcome to [Company]."
```

### Script 6: The candidate says no

```
"OK. Can I ask: what was the deciding factor?

[Listen. Don't rebut on the call. The decision is made. Your job now is to understand why and protect the relationship.]

That makes sense. Thanks for being straight with me.

Two things before we wrap:
1. If anything changes in the next [6 months / 12 months] — different role at us, different timing on your side — I want to be the first call. Let's stay in touch on LinkedIn at minimum
2. I'd love a quick read-out on what you'd have wanted to be different in the offer or the process. Not now — when you have headspace. I'll send a 3-question email tomorrow. Honest answers help us hire the next person better

Genuinely, all the best at [their next move]."
```

## Counter-offer playbook — full decision tree

When candidate's current employer counters:

### Step 1: Find out what the counter is

- Exact dollar amount on base
- Exact amount on sign-on / retention bonus
- Equity component (RSU grant size, vesting timeline)
- Promotion / title change?
- Scope change?
- Any non-comp elements (manager change, project assignment, location flex)

### Step 2: Find out why the candidate is considering it

Often, the counter-offer hits because:
- Comp gap is real and our offer doesn't bridge it
- Loyalty / inertia kicked in once they had to tell their boss
- Family logistics (relocation cost, partner job, kid's school) is harder than they expected
- Cold feet on the new role / company

Ask: "What part of the counter is making you reconsider?" Listen.

### Step 3: Decide what flex you have

- Within recruiter authority: usually base flex +5%, sign-on up to $15k for senior
- HRBP authority: typically +10-15% on base, additional sign-on up to $25-50k for senior+
- VP / CEO authority: variable, escalate selectively

Don't bluff. If you don't have authority, don't say you do.

### Step 4: Make the case beyond comp

- The role you offered solves the thing they said they wanted (scope, growth, manager, ceiling). Their current company is offering money because losing them is the problem
- 70-80% of candidates who take counter-offers from current employers leave within 12-18 months anyway. Industry data
- The fact that they had to threaten to leave to get this number means their employer was underpaying them. That's not loyalty — it's late acknowledgement

### Step 5: Set a decision deadline

48 hours from the counter conversation. Not "take your time." Specific deadline. Otherwise the offer drags and your other candidates ghost.

### Step 6: Decide whether to flex or walk

- If flexing: come back with a specific number, framed as "this is what we can do, this is the best we can do, take 24 hours"
- If walking: "I want to be straight with you — we can't get to that number. The offer is open at [our number] until [day]. After that we move forward. Wishing you good things either way."

### Step 7: Document what happened

Log in the ATS req file:
- The counter amount
- Our response
- Outcome (candidate stayed / candidate joined us)
- What we learned for future offers (e.g., "the band for this level needs a benchmark refresh — we lost a strong candidate on $X gap")

## What never happens

- Verbal commitment to comp before HRBP signs off
- Email-only offer extension (always phone first)
- Pressuring tactics ("if you don't decide by tomorrow we withdraw") unless there's a real reason and the candidate was given notice
- Lying about band ceilings ("the band is firm" when you have flex)
- Lying about competing offers ("we have another candidate" when you don't)
- Discussing one candidate's comp with another candidate
- Dropping a comp number in a candidate-facing email that isn't in the official offer letter

## Post-offer to start date — the close-out window

After the candidate accepts, the work isn't done. Falling-off rate (verbal accept → no-show on start date) is real, especially in heated markets.

```
POST-OFFER CHECK-IN CADENCE — [Candidate] — [Role]

Day 0 (verbal accept): congratulations call, immediate next-step email
Day 1 (DocuSign sent): "let me know when you have signed; here's what's next"
Day 3 (after DocuSign signed): light check-in
Day 7: progress on background check, any blockers
Day 14: equipment shipping, manager intro setup
Day 21+: weekly light touch until start date
Day -7 (week before start): final logistics, day 1 plan
Day -1: day-before friendly note, anything outstanding
Day 0 (start date): warm welcome, hand off to People Ops / HM
Day +30: 30-day check-in with the new hire — how's it going, anything broken
```

The recruiter owns the relationship through the start date. After that, hand-off to HM and People Ops.

---

Things to verify before any offer extension:
- All approvals in place (HRBP, finance, legal, HM) — named and dated
- Comp components are clean (base + variable + equity + sign-on + benefits + start date + relocation + other)
- Year 1 and Year 1+2 totals calculated
- Counter-offer prep paragraph is ready to deliver on the call
- The candidate's stated motivations beyond comp are top-of-mind
- Walk-away point is agreed with HM and HRBP in advance
- Written offer email is drafted but unsent — to send 1 hour after the call
- DocuSign packet is queued with legal
- Background check provider is ready to trigger on signed offer

Inclusive-language audit: passed for all extension comms (focus on comp and scope, not demographic)
Comp band confirmed approved: yes (all components signed off with HRBP)
HRBP/legal review needed before send: any exception flex above standard authority
