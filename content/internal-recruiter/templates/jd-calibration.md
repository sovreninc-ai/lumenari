# JD Calibration — Internal Recruiter Pack

Turning the HM's wishlist JD into a defensible, inclusive, candidate-respecting posting. The recruiter owns this calibration step before any posting goes live.

The full worked example with before/after is in `reference-workflows.md` Example 2. This file is the workflow and the audit list.

---

## The 7-step calibration workflow

### Step 1: Take the HM's draft

Don't rewrite from scratch — calibrate. The HM's voice has value; the recruiter's job is to make it pass the audit and align it to the intake.

### Step 2: Cross-reference against intake must-haves

Every line in the JD should map to either a must-have or a nice-to-have from intake. If it doesn't, cut it or move it.

Common drift to catch:
- HM's draft says "5+ years experience" but intake calibrated on observable behaviors. Replace with the behaviors
- HM's draft says "Bachelor's required" but the role doesn't require a degree. Move to "preferred" or cut
- HM's draft says "experience at a top-tier company" — brand gatekeeping. Cut
- HM's draft lists 14 nice-to-haves. Pick 3-5. Move the rest internal

### Step 3: Run the inclusive-language audit

See the audit list below. Flag and rewrite every flagged term. Show both versions in the calibration output.

### Step 4: Add the comp band

In pay-transparency states (CA, CO, NY, WA, IL, MD, others), this is mandatory. Recommended everywhere — candidates self-select better with comp transparency.

Format:
- "Base: $X to $Y"
- "Equity: $X to $Y in RSUs (or 0.X% to 0.Y%)"
- "Bonus: X% target / X% maximum"
- "Sign-on: available, varies by level"
- "Benefits: link to one-pager"

### Step 5: Confirm the loop and timing

The candidate wants to know what they're signing up for. Include:
- Number of steps and what each is
- Format (remote / hybrid / in-person)
- Duration per step
- Total end-to-end timing
- What you don't do (brainteasers, whiteboards, take-home tests over 4 hours, etc.) if relevant

### Step 6: Add the "what we don't do well yet" line (if HM allows)

This is the differentiator. Most JDs paper over weaknesses. The ones that name them honestly build trust.

Examples:
- "Our documentation is uneven across services. We know. We're working on it."
- "We're 9 months out of a hard fundraising cycle. Things are stable but lean."
- "On-call cadence is currently 1 week every 4 weeks, which is heavier than industry; we're hiring to bring this to 1-every-6."

If the HM resists, push gently: "candidates trust JDs that name what's hard. They Google-stalk us anyway. Better to address it than not."

### Step 7: Show the diff to the HM

Format the calibration output as:
- Original draft (HM's version)
- Calibrated version
- Changes + rationale (numbered, with reasons)
- Inclusive-language audit results (what was flagged and rewritten)
- Comp-band disclosure status
- Required review queue (HRBP, legal, HM final)

The HM signs off on the calibrated version before posting.

## The inclusive-language audit list

Run this on every JD before posting. Flag, rewrite, log.

### Age proxies (cut)
- "Young," "energetic," "fresh perspective," "digital native"
- "Recent grad" → only if the role IS specifically a new-grad program. Otherwise cut
- "Up-and-coming" applied to a person → cut
- "High-energy" → describe the work pace, not the worker

### Coded masculine (cut or replace)
- "Aggressive," "dominant," "rockstar," "ninja," "wizard," "guru"
- "Manpower" → "workforce" or "staff"
- "Chairman" → "chair"
- "Guys" → "team," "everyone"

### Coded feminine (review in technical roles)
- "Warm," "nurturing," "supportive" — fine in roles where they describe the work; flag if they're filler in roles like engineering where they may code-switch the candidate pool

### Education gatekeeping (review)
- "Bachelor's required" → cut if the role doesn't actually require it; replace with "OR equivalent experience" if HM insists
- "MS or PhD" → only if the role legitimately needs it (research roles); cut elsewhere
- "Top-tier university" → cut. Brand gatekeeping

### Years-of-experience gatekeeping (review)
- "5+ years" → replace with observable behavior. Years aren't a skill
- "10+ years" → only if the role legitimately requires that depth and HM can articulate why
- "Recent" timing language → tighten or cut

### Citizenship overreach (cut or replace)
- "Must be US citizen" → only if the role legitimately requires it (security clearance, govt contract). Otherwise: "Must be authorized to work in the US"
- Same for Canada — only specify citizenship if a legal requirement exists

### Culture-fit language (cut and replace)
- "Cultural fit" → "values alignment with [specific concrete behaviors]"
- "We're like a family" → cut. Cut. Cut.
- "Work hard / play hard" → cut
- "Comfortable with ambiguity" → specify the level: "experience operating with [structural ambiguity description]"

### Language and accent gatekeeping (cut)
- "Native English speaker" → ILLEGAL in most jurisdictions. Replace with "professional fluency in English"
- "No accent" → never appropriate
- "Excellent written and verbal communication" → fine, but pair with an observable behavior in the must-haves

### Location overreach (review)
- "Must be local" → only if business-justified. Replace with "Based in [city] or open to relocating; relocation support available"

### Filler language (cut)
- "Self-starter" → cut or replace with specific behavior
- "Hit the ground running" → cut
- "Wear many hats" → cut or specify the hats
- "Fast-paced environment" → describe the actual pace ("we ship to production multiple times daily")
- "Dynamic team" → cut
- "Passionate" → cut

### Discriminatory shortcuts (always cut, escalate to HRBP if HM insists)
- School-name screens ("MIT/Stanford/Berkeley only")
- "Industry experience required" used as a brand gatekeeper
- "Cultural fit" without behavioral criteria
- Any reference to demographic targeting (age, race, gender, religion, national origin, disability, parental status)
- "Family-friendly" used as demographic targeting → describe the actual benefit instead

## Audit output format

For every JD calibration, produce an audit log:

```
INCLUSIVE-LANGUAGE AUDIT

Flagged and rewrote:
1. "rockstar" (coded masculine) → cut. Title cleaned.
2. "5+ years" (years-gatekeeping) → replaced with behavior-based must-haves
3. "bachelor's required" (education gatekeeping) → cut. Education is not a must-have for this role
4. "native English speaker" (illegal national-origin proxy) → cut. Replaced with "professional fluency in English"
5. ...

Flagged and kept (with rationale):
1. "Excellent communication skills" → kept as nice-to-have; paired with specific behavioral must-have ("documented decisions in writing read by people outside immediate team")

No flags:
- Most of the scope, ownership, and compensation sections passed cleanly
```

## Pay-transparency law cheat sheet

Always confirm current law for your jurisdiction. As of 2025-2026:

**US states requiring pay range in job postings:**
- California (SB 1162) — pay range required
- Colorado (Equal Pay for Equal Work Act) — pay range and benefits
- New York (statewide as of 2023) — pay range required
- Washington (SB 5761) — pay range and benefits
- Illinois (HB 3129) — pay range and benefits, starting 2025
- Maryland — pay range required upon request, expanded recently
- Hawaii — pay range required
- Cincinnati, OH (city ordinance) — pay range required upon request
- Toledo, OH (city ordinance) — pay range required upon request
- Ithaca, NY + NYC + Westchester — pay range required
- Jersey City, NJ — pay range required

**Canada — currently incoming, not all in force:**
- Ontario — Working for Workers Four Act, pay transparency provisions
- BC — Pay Transparency Act, in force as of late 2023
- Federal — Pay Equity Act applies to federal-regulated employers

When in doubt, post the band. Candidate-respecting and legally safer.

## Common HM pushback and how to handle it

- **"I don't want to post the comp band — it'll cap our negotiation room."**
  - Counter: "In CA/NY/CO/etc. it's legally required. Also: candidates self-select better with the band. We get higher-quality inbound and fewer wasted screens. The negotiation room is between the band and the candidate's stated minimum — same conversation either way."

- **"I want the JD to say 'Bachelor's degree required' — it filters out the noise."**
  - Counter: "It also filters out qualified candidates who didn't follow a traditional path. If you're hiring for the skill, the degree isn't the must-have. We can keep it as 'preferred' if you want a soft signal, but the must-haves should be behavioral."

- **"Can we put 'recent grad' in the JD? I'm looking for someone early in their career."**
  - Counter: "That's an age-proxy and a discrimination risk. If the role is a new-grad program, we can say 'new-grad program' explicitly. If it's not, we describe the seniority level by scope and comp — not by age signals."

- **"I want to keep 'rockstar' — it sounds energetic."**
  - Counter: "It also codes masculine and skews the applicant pool. We can communicate energy through scope ('shipping every two weeks,' 'production from day 1') without using the term."

---

Things to verify before posting any calibrated JD:
- HRBP review of comp-band line and benefits one-liner
- Legal review (if your company requires it; some skip for non-regulated roles)
- HM final sign-off on the calibrated version
- Posting goes live on the careers page AND any external boards (LinkedIn, Indeed, function-specific boards) — consistent comp range across all
- The "what we don't do well yet" line, if included, is HM-approved
- The audit log is saved in the req file

Inclusive-language audit: documented in every JD calibration. The audit log is the artifact, not just the rewritten JD.
