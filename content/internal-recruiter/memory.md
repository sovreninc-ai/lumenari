# Memory — Internal Recruiter Pack

## Domain context

An internal recruiter (embedded corporate recruiter, talent partner, TA partner depending on company taxonomy) owns the search end-to-end inside a company. They take a req that's been approved with a JD and a comp band, run intake with the hiring manager to set the search up, source or partner with a sourcer, screen, manage the loop, debrief, extend offers, and close. They run 8-15 reqs at once. They report to a Head of TA or VP People in a larger company, sometimes directly to a CHRO at a smaller one.

The rhythm is meeting-heavy and email-heavy. Mornings are recruiter screens (30 min each, back-to-back), interspersed with intake meetings with new hiring managers and prep calls for upcoming onsites. Mid-day is the connective tissue: confirming HM screens, scheduling onsite loops in Greenhouse / Lever / Ashby, troubleshooting why a panelist is double-booked, prepping candidate-facing emails. Afternoons are debriefs after loops, offer prep with HRBP and finance, occasional close calls. Evenings are sometimes the long-form writing — JD calibration drafts, intake notes that need to be sent back to the HM by tomorrow, candidate rejection emails for the ones the recruiter wants to write carefully.

The job has three customers: candidates (who deserve respect at every stage), hiring managers (who need their seat filled and don't have time for the search process), and the company (which needs hires that work out in onboarding and stay). The recruiter sits in the middle of all three and gets blamed by all three when something breaks.

Success looks like: the seat is filled in time-to-fill within benchmark for the function, the candidate accepts, they show up on the start date, they pass the 90-day check, the HM is satisfied with the calibration match, and the recruiter's reputation holds for the next intake. Pass-through rates by stage hold steady or improve. Pipeline diversity moves directionally well. Hiring manager NPS is positive.

Failure looks like: time-to-fill blows out, the candidate accepts and then no-shows, the HM says at debrief "this isn't what we were looking for" (calibration was wrong), or the offer falls apart in the close because comp wasn't aligned. Worse: a candidate complains about discriminatory treatment, a rejection email goes out that hurts the brand, or the recruiter loses HM trust and gets cut out of intake going forward.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- **Req / requisition** — an open headcount with approved JD, comp band, hiring manager assigned. Lives in the ATS
- **Intake** — the meeting between recruiter and HM at the start of a search to calibrate the role, must-haves, sourcing plan, loop design, scorecard
- **Calibration** — alignment between recruiter and HM on what "good" looks like. Re-calibrated mid-search when feedback shows drift
- **Must-haves vs nice-to-haves** — what the candidate must have to do the job vs what would be nice. Most JDs blur these; calibration separates them
- **Scorecard** — the rubric used at each loop step to grade against the must-haves and nice-to-haves
- **Loop / interview loop** — the full sequence of interviews. Typically: recruiter screen → HM screen → panel onsite (3-5 sessions) → final
- **Debrief** — the panel meeting after the onsite to decide hire/no-hire
- **HM screen** — the hiring manager's interview, usually 45-60 min, between recruiter screen and full onsite
- **Onsite / loop** — the full panel interview, 3-5 sessions of 45-60 min each, can be remote or in-person
- **Comp band** — the approved range for base + variable + equity by level. HRBP-gated. Recruiter cannot offer outside band without exception approval
- **Exception authority** — what the recruiter or HM can deviate from the standard offer (e.g., +10% on base, +$10k sign-on) without escalating to HRBP/CHRO
- **HRBP** — HR Business Partner. The person who pairs with each function on people decisions. Gates comp, offer exceptions, performance conversations
- **Offer extension** — the call/email where the offer is communicated. Usually phone first, written offer 1 hour after
- **Verbal accept** — candidate says yes on the call but hasn't signed paper. Not real yet
- **Signed offer / paper offer** — accepted in writing. ATS marks the req closed
- **No-show / candidate ghost** — accepted candidate doesn't start on day 1. Painful, real, often signals broken close
- **Backfill** — replacing someone who left. Often harder than net-new because of unspoken "we want better than the last one" pressure
- **ATS** — Applicant Tracking System (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday Recruiting, JobVite, iCIMS)
- **Comp tools** — Lattice, Workday, ADP, sometimes Pave / Compa for market data
- **Background check** — third-party check (Checkr, HireRight, Sterling) at offer stage. FCRA-regulated in US, PIPEDA in Canada
- **Reference check** — call or written reference from former manager/peer of the candidate. Coordinated by recruiter
- **FCRA** — Fair Credit Reporting Act (US). Governs background checks. Requires disclosure + consent + adverse action notice
- **Adverse action** — formal process if background check returns info that affects an offer. FCRA-regulated. Multi-step
- **Pay transparency** — laws requiring posted salary ranges in JDs (CA, CO, NY, WA, IL, MD, others). Always check
- **EEO / EEO-1** — Equal Employment Opportunity reporting (US). Self-id collection from candidates, regulated, voluntary
- **No-poach agreement** — bilateral or industry agreement not to recruit from specific companies. Some are legal, some are not — varies. Recruiter should know which apply
- **Counter-offer** — when a candidate's current employer makes a retention offer in response to your offer. Common. Predictable. The recruiter should have prepared the candidate for this
- **Walk-away point** — the comp / role / package level at which the recruiter and HM agree the offer isn't worth extending. Decided in advance, not in the heat
- **Slate** — the active set of candidates being considered for a req (typically 3-6 at the onsite stage)
- **Pipeline** — broader pool, including warm passive candidates not yet in active consideration

## Common workflows

- **New req intake (60-90 min meeting)**: recruiter meets with HM (and HRBP if available). Covers business context, role scope (90 days / 12 months), must-haves (4-6), nice-to-haves (3-5), deal-breakers, comp band confirmation, sourcing approach, loop design, scorecard, the thing the HM didn't say out loud. Output: intake notes the HM signs off on
- **JD calibration**: recruiter takes HM's draft, cuts wishlist down to must-haves, runs inclusive-language audit, adds comp band, confirms loop, sends to HRBP + legal, posts in ATS
- **Sourcing partnership**: recruiter coordinates with sourcer (or does sourcing themselves) — provides ICP, target companies, Boolean approach, signs off on outreach copy, handles inbound applicants
- **Recruiter screen (30 min)**: covers candidate motivation, scope match, comp expectations vs band, logistics (location, notice period, visa, start date), red flags. Output: pass/no-pass decision + notes for HM
- **HM screen prep**: recruiter sends HM a short summary of the candidate, the gaps identified at recruiter screen, suggested questions, scorecard reminder
- **Loop scheduling**: recruiter coordinates panel availability, sends invites, sends candidate prep email (panelists, format, timing, what to expect)
- **Debrief facilitation**: recruiter runs the meeting, anchors on scorecard, round-robin voting, surfaces gaps, HM final decision
- **Offer prep**: recruiter and HRBP work with finance partner to model comp, predict counter, script the extension call
- **Offer extension**: phone call from recruiter (sometimes HM joins) → verbal → written offer 1 hour later → DocuSign / HelloSign / paper
- **Counter-offer handling**: when candidate gets retention offer from current employer, recruiter walks them back to motivations beyond comp
- **Background check + reference check coordination**: FCRA-compliant in US (disclosure + consent + adverse action if needed), PIPEDA in Canada
- **Closing the req**: signed offer → start date confirmed → handoff to onboarding/People Ops → 30/60/90 day check-ins with HM and new hire

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **Skipping intake** because "we already know what we want" — the search will drift in week 3. Always do intake
- **Calibration drift** — HM changes the bar mid-search without re-calibrating with the recruiter. Catch it at debrief, re-anchor on intake notes
- **Discussing comp before band is approved** — recruiter cannot unring this bell. Always: "Comp band is in process; I'll have a number for you by [date]" if asked too early
- **"Culture fit" without behavioral criteria** — replace with concrete behaviors tied to how the team works. Otherwise it's a discrimination risk
- **School-name screens** — "MIT, Stanford, Berkeley only" is illegal-ish and bad sourcing
- **Reference-check questions designed to dig for dirt** — illegal in some jurisdictions, always unethical. Ask about work, scope, growth areas; never personal life or protected-class topics
- **Form-letter rejection emails** — "We've decided to move forward with another candidate" is the worst version. Even at scale, give a real reason
- **Letting the loudest voice win in debrief** — recruiter's job is to anchor on the scorecard and surface dissent properly
- **Offering before final HRBP / finance / legal sign-off** — this includes verbal indications of "the offer will be around X." Don't say it
- **Promising comp ranges in pay-transparency states without posting them in the JD** — legal issue
- **No prep for counter-offer** — predictable, preventable. Coach the candidate at the offer stage that a counter is likely and walk them through their motivations beyond comp
- **Background check workflow that doesn't comply with FCRA** — disclosure, consent, adverse action notice if applicable. Use Checkr / HireRight / Sterling templates
- **Discriminatory shortcuts on any dimension** — age, race, gender, religion, national origin, disability, parental status, citizenship beyond work authorization. Refuse, redirect, re-train if the HM asks for any of this

## Tone / register

A real practitioner sounds direct, candid, slightly tired, deeply specific. They name candidates, hiring managers, HRBPs. They acknowledge that intake is the most important meeting of the search. They push back on hiring managers who want what they can't have. They write candidate rejection emails that people remember as warm. They write offer extension scripts that account for the counter-offer that's coming. They use Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday — not "the ATS." They don't say "rockstar" or "passionate." They say "Here's what we're calibrated on. Here's the candidate. Here's the gap." That's the voice.
