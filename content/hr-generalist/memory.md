# Memory — HR Generalist Pack

## Domain context

An HR generalist is the person who owns "everything HR" at a small or mid-size company. At 25-100 employees, they're often the only HR person, reporting to the CEO or COO. At 100-500, they might be one of 2-4 on a small team, reporting to a head of HR or a CFO/COO. The work spans: comp and benefits, policies and the handbook, employee relations (the awkward conversations), performance management, light recruiting (or coordinating with recruiters), light L&D, onboarding/offboarding, immigration sometimes, equity admin sometimes, and the dozen unexpected things that come up in a given week. The HR generalist is the air-traffic controller of human-related work at the company. They are usually the first to know about a problem and often the last to be thanked for solving it.

The rhythm of the work is reactive on top of proactive. The proactive layer: performance review cycles, benefits open enrollment, annual policy review, compensation planning. The reactive layer: a manager calling about an employee who's been late three weeks running, a complaint that requires investigation, a leader announcing they're leaving and needing a transition plan and announcement, a layoff that needs to be communicated tomorrow morning. Most of the writing happens between meetings or after hours — policy drafts at 7 PM, announcement edits at 11 PM, PIP language at 6 AM before the 1:1. The AI is most useful in those exact moments — turning a verbal context and a few facts into a written document that's structurally clean and flagged for legal review where needed.

Success looks like: policies are read because they're readable, announcements are received cleanly even when the news is hard, performance conversations happen on time and with documentation, the handbook reflects current law and the company's actual practice, the company gets through a year without a wrongful-termination claim or a harassment complaint mishandled. Failure looks like: a 60-page handbook nobody reads, a layoff announcement that leaks before the all-hands, a PIP delivered without counsel review that becomes Exhibit A in a wrongful-dismissal suit, a benefits change announcement that creates a Slack channel of complaints because nobody explained the why.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- **HRIS**: Human Resources Information System. BambooHR, Rippling, Gusto, ADP Run, Paylocity, Workday, Namely, TriNet. The system of record for employees.
- **HRBP**: HR Business Partner. The HR person assigned to a specific business unit or function. Often more senior than a generalist.
- **PEO**: Professional Employer Organization. Outsourced HR, payroll, and benefits provider. TriNet, Justworks, Gusto, Paychex Enterprise. Common at sub-100 companies.
- **ATS**: Applicant Tracking System. Lever, Greenhouse, Ashby, Workday Recruiting, BambooHR ATS.
- **PIP**: Performance Improvement Plan. A formal, time-bound plan with specific expectations for an underperforming employee. Typically 30, 60, or 90 days. Legally sensitive document — always counsel-reviewed.
- **RIF**: Reduction In Force. Layoff.
- **WARN Act**: US Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act. Federal law requiring 60 days advance notice for mass layoffs. State mini-WARNs (California's Cal-WARN, New York's NY WARN) often have stricter thresholds.
- **FMLA**: Family and Medical Leave Act. US federal job-protected unpaid leave. Eligibility: employer with 50+ employees within 75 miles, employee with 12+ months and 1,250 hours.
- **ADA**: Americans with Disabilities Act. US accommodation framework.
- **Title VII**: US Civil Rights Act of 1964, Title VII. Federal anti-discrimination law covering race, color, religion, sex, national origin.
- **FLSA**: Fair Labor Standards Act. US wage and hour law — minimum wage, overtime, exempt/non-exempt classifications.
- **EEO-1**: Annual demographic report filed with the US EEOC by companies with 100+ employees.
- **I-9 / E-Verify**: US employment eligibility verification. I-9 is required for all hires. E-Verify is supplemental, required in some states.
- **CRA**: Canada Revenue Agency. Source deductions, T4s.
- **ROE**: Record of Employment. Canadian federal form issued on separation. Required for EI claims.
- **ESA**: Employment Standards Act. Provincial law (Ontario ESA, BC ESA, Alberta ELC) governing the floor of employment standards — minimum wage, hours, termination notice, vacation.
- **CLC**: Canada Labour Code. Federal jurisdiction (banks, telecom, transport, federal Crown corporations).
- **AODA**: Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act. Accommodation and accessibility framework.
- **Bill 88 (ON)**: Working for Workers Act 2022 — disconnecting from work policy, electronic monitoring policy required for 25+ employee employers.
- **Bill 27 (ON)**: Right to Disconnect — also under Working for Workers Act.
- **Law 25 (QC)**: Quebec's privacy law (formerly Bill 64). Significant for any employer with Quebec employees.
- **PWFA**: Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (US federal, 2023). Accommodation for pregnancy.
- **OFCCP**: Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs. Affirmative action for US federal contractors.
- **Open enrollment**: the annual window for employees to elect or change benefits. Typically a 2-4 week window in October-November for January 1 plan year.
- **Total rewards**: comp + benefits + equity + non-cash perks. The full package an employee receives.
- **Equity / ESPP / RSU / ISO / NSO**: equity types. RSUs are restricted stock units. ISOs / NSOs are incentive / non-qualified stock options. ESPP is employee stock purchase plan. Each has different tax and vesting treatment.
- **9-box / forced ranking / calibration**: performance review frameworks. 9-box is a 3×3 grid of performance × potential. Calibration is the manager round-table to align ratings.
- **Stack ranking**: more aggressive forced ranking, sometimes tied to bottom-percentile cuts. Out of fashion at most modern companies.
- **OKRs / KPIs / SMART goals**: goal-setting frameworks. OKRs are objectives + key results (Google-popularized). KPIs are key performance indicators. SMART goals are specific/measurable/attainable/relevant/time-bound.
- **Disconnect / RTO / hybrid / fully remote**: work model categorizations.
- **Onboarding tier**: day 1, week 1, 30-60-90 day milestones.

## Common workflows

- **Policy update cycle:** annual handbook review → identify gaps from new laws and lessons learned → draft updates → counsel review → leadership review → roll out via email and acknowledged via HRIS.
- **Benefits open enrollment:** preview announcement 4 weeks before window → window opens (2-4 weeks) → reminders at -2 weeks, -1 week, -2 days → window closes → confirmation summaries → carrier files.
- **Performance review cycle (annual):** manager calibration prep → self-assessments → manager reviews → calibration sessions → finalize ratings → comp letters → 1:1 conversations → goal-setting for next period.
- **New hire onboarding:** offer signed → I-9 / E-Verify (US) or ROE setup (Canada) → first-day kit → 30-60-90 day check-ins → 90-day formal review.
- **Performance issue surface:** manager flags concern → coaching conversation → documented warning → PIP if needed → PIP outcomes → separation or recovery. Each step counsel-reviewed at material thresholds.
- **Termination workflow:** business decision → counsel review of grounds and process → severance / release agreement draft → comms plan (manager, peers, team, broader org) → execution day → follow-through (benefits continuation, equipment recovery, knowledge transfer).
- **Internal investigation:** complaint received → preserve evidence → counsel engaged → investigator assigned (sometimes external) → interviews → findings → outcomes → close-out communication. HR does not lead investigations alone.

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **"Pursuant to applicable law" and "the Company shall."** Read aloud to an employee. Cut. Plain English wins.
- **Policies that say "we comply with all applicable laws" without specifying.** Either name the framework you're aiming at or don't make compliance claims.
- **Performance reviews that say "needs to be more communicative" without specifying behaviors.** Vague reviews don't change behavior and don't survive a lawyer's read.
- **PIPs without counsel review.** This is the single highest-risk document HR routinely produces. Always counsel-reviewed before delivery.
- **Layoff announcements that frame it as "exciting restructuring."** People know what's happening. Lying about it loses trust faster than the layoff itself.
- **RTO mandates announced without a why and without a path to exceptions.** This is the comms failure that creates resignation waves.
- **Termination letters that include legal conclusions.** "Your conduct constitutes grounds for termination" should not be drafted by HR alone. Counsel-only.
- **Confusing FMLA / state leave / company policy.** Each is its own framework with its own eligibility, duration, and protections. Don't blend.
- **Anti-harassment policy without a reporting path, an anti-retaliation clause, and an investigation process.** All three are required for the policy to function.
- **Handbook updates that aren't tracked.** A handbook that changes silently is a handbook nobody trusts. Maintain a change log.

## Tone / register

A working HR generalist writes direct, plain English. They lead with the news. They use the actual tool names. They acknowledge what's hard ("I know this isn't the news anyone hoped for"). They don't dress up bad news ("we are excited to announce a strategic restructuring" — no, you're announcing a layoff). They use the second person ("if you need time off, request it through BambooHR"). They never write "the Company" when "we" works. They never use "shall" when "will" works. They send the email and then move on to the next problem. That's the voice.

The disclaimer line — "this is not legal advice, consult employment counsel" — is the most important sentence in any document with legal exposure. It appears in two places in any meaningful document. The HR generalist who skips it is the one who ends up in a deposition.
