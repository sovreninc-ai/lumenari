# Audition Prep, Demo Scripts, Character Work, Agent Pitches

The reference doc. Five sections. Use the templates as-is, or use the structure to write your own.

---

## 1. The audition breakdown framework

Paste your spec + copy and ask for this output shape:

```
ONE-LINE INTENT
[What is this piece operationally trying to do? Not the message — the result.]

SUBTEXT
[What is the brand or character NOT saying that the read has to carry?]

THREE READ DIRECTIONS
A. [Specific acting choice]
B. [Different choice — not "more A"]
C. [Wildcard that might be wrong but might book]

THE TRAP
[The obvious read 80% of submissions will give. Name it.]

MARKUP
[Phrase 1] — Actor is thinking: [intention, not emphasis]
[Phrase 2] — Actor is thinking: [intention]
[Phrase 3] — Actor is thinking: [intention]

SLATE
[Full name + agent / character name / just first name / no slate]
```

### Worked example

**Spec:** "Looking for a warm, conversational midrange — late 20s to late 30s, female-presenting. Think a friend who actually understands money. NOT a finance bro, NOT a robot. Reference: the warmth of NPR's Marketplace correspondents."

**Copy:** "Most apps tell you what you spent. Truebank tells you what to do about it. Three buckets, one rule, zero shame. Open Truebank — money, with less of the math."

**Output:**

ONE-LINE INTENT — make a 28-year-old who avoids their checking account feel like this app won't judge them.

SUBTEXT — the brand is saying "we are not your dad's bank." The read needs to carry "I get why you've been avoiding this."

THREE READ DIRECTIONS
A. Dry-warm, like you're explaining a hack to your best friend over wine. Smile in the eyes, not the mouth.
B. Quietly confident, almost under-energy. Like you're letting the listener in on something. Slow the second sentence.
C. Wildcard: slightly sardonic on the first line, then sincere on "zero shame." Most actors won't try this.

THE TRAP — the bright-smile retail read that lifts at the end of every sentence. Every fintech audition gets that read. Don't.

MARKUP
"Most apps tell you what you spent" — thinking: yeah, I know, I have eight of those too
"Tells you what to do about it" — thinking: here's the actual unlock
"Zero shame" — thinking: I mean this, not as a tagline
"Money, with less of the math" — thinking: warm landing, not announcer

SLATE — first name + agent. Standard commercial slate.

## 2. Demo scripts by category

### Commercial demo (60-90 seconds)

Structure: 5-7 segments, opening with the strongest 8 seconds, varying lanes (retail / tech / pharma / financial / lifestyle / spirit / automotive). Each segment 8-15 seconds.

**Prompt to use:**

```
Write me a 75-second commercial demo script. My lanes:
1. Warm conversational retail (lead with this)
2. Wry tech / SaaS
3. Tender pharma
4. Confident financial / fintech
5. Aspirational lifestyle

Open with the retail segment — strongest 8 seconds. Vary pacing and energy between segments. Use plausible fictional brands. Mark breath points with / and major beats with //. Each segment should be 10-15 seconds when read at performance pace.
```

**Worked example opener (8 seconds, retail):**

"You know that feeling / when you actually have what you need // for once? // That's Beckton & Hall. // Pantry staples, no surprises."

### Animation demo (60-75 seconds)

Structure: 4-6 character segments, each 8-12 seconds, contrasting types. Show range without head-fakes: don't put two villainous characters back-to-back; alternate energy, age, register.

**Prompt to use:**

```
Write me a 70-second animation demo with 5 character segments:
1. Earnest 8-year-old protagonist (lead)
2. Loud, scheming sidekick (opposite energy)
3. Tired adult authority figure (deadpan)
4. Magical creature with a high stakes monologue
5. Slightly wicked teen rival

Each segment: 8-12 seconds. Write character lines, not narrator copy. Include one moment of vulnerability or contradiction per character. Mark vocal cues in brackets [whispered], [breathy], [building], etc.
```

### Video game demo (45-60 seconds)

Structure: barks + lines, 6-10 segments. Game demos are shorter and more action-oriented than animation. Include combat barks, NPC exposition, an emotional cinematic line, and a creature/non-human vocal.

**Prompt to use:**

```
Write me a 50-second video game demo:
1. Combat barks (3-4 lines, varying effort levels)
2. Grizzled NPC giving directions (8 seconds)
3. Companion character emotional cinematic line (10 seconds)
4. Villain monologue (12 seconds)
5. Non-human creature vocal (creature work, 5 seconds — describe the creature briefly)
6. Tutorial / quest hand-off character (8 seconds)
```

### Audiobook demo (90 seconds)

Structure: narrator voice + 2-3 character voices within a single passage. Show that you can hold narrator voice through dialogue and return to it cleanly. Genre-tagged samples are useful (literary / thriller / romance / nonfiction).

### Narration / documentary demo (60-90 seconds)

Structure: 3-4 segments, each 15-25 seconds. Vary subject matter — science, history, sports, social documentary. Documentary copy is longer-form and rewards sustained authority. Limit lift; let gravity do the work.

### E-learning / corporate demo (60-75 seconds)

Structure: 4-5 segments showing clarity at different paces — IT training, healthcare module, finance compliance, soft-skills onboarding. E-learning casts on warmth + clarity, not personality.

## 3. Character analysis template

For animation, video game, audiobook narration, or any character-driven piece.

```
CHARACTER: [Name or description from breakdown]

WANT (active verb, one sentence)
[What this character is trying to do in the scene]

WOUND
[The unhealed thing underneath the want]

VOICE SIGNATURE (2-3 specific vocal choices)
1. [Specific choice — e.g., "drops volume on emotional words"]
2. [Specific choice]
3. [Specific choice, optional]

BREATH PATTERN
[Where does this character breathe? What does that tell the listener?]

ONE DELIVERY NOT TO GIVE
[A wrong choice to react against]

REFERENCE AS TUNING FORK (do not impersonate)
[Existing performance with similar energy — use as direction, not imitation]
```

### Worked example

CHARACTER: Mara, 34, war journalist, late-night radio call-in show host. Tonight she's interviewing the mother of a soldier who didn't come home.

WANT — to make the mother feel heard without making the moment about Mara.

WOUND — Mara lost her own brother in a different war. The interview keeps reminding her, and she's pretending it doesn't.

VOICE SIGNATURE
1. Drops volume on the questions, doesn't raise it. Lets the silence do work.
2. Holds the breath at the top of each question, like she's deciding whether to ask it.
3. Says "yeah" instead of "right" or "I see." It's the most honest word she has.

BREATH PATTERN — long exhales before speaking, almost like she's bracing. No quick inhales. The audience should hear her thinking.

ONE DELIVERY NOT TO GIVE — the warm-radio-host empathy voice. That voice is a costume. Mara isn't wearing one tonight.

REFERENCE AS TUNING FORK — the controlled grief in Frances McDormand's quieter scenes in Three Billboards. Direction, not imitation. Do not Frances. Be Mara.

## 4. Agent pitch templates

Three tiers, by experience level. Replace bracketed sections with real details.

### Tier 1: Zero booked credits, recent training

```
Subject: VO rep inquiry — [your lane], [your market]

Hi [Agent first name],

I'm a [age range], [gender / voice description] voice actor based in [city], primarily in the [primary lane — e.g., "warm conversational commercial and corporate narration"] lane. I'm reaching out because your roster currently leans [observation — e.g., "heavily into promo and trailer"], and I'd like to be considered for the commercial conversational slot I don't see represented.

I trained with [coach name, school, or program] over the last [time], finishing with a commercial demo cut by [demo producer name] in [month/year]. My booth is treated [Whisper Room / Studiobricks / treated room] running [interface + mic]. Source-Connect on standby.

Demo attached. If anything in there feels like a fit, I'd love a 15-minute call to talk roster.

Thanks for your time,
[Your name]
[Demo link]
[Phone]
```

### Tier 2: Some credits, building

```
Subject: VO rep inquiry — [lane] with recent [type] credits

Hi [Agent first name],

I'm a [age range] [gender / voice description] voice actor in [city]. Recent work includes [credit 1], [credit 2], and [credit 3 — specific, named, real]. I'm in the [lane] pocket and looking to expand into [secondary lane the agency books].

I'm writing because [specific reason — e.g., "your agency just signed [actor] and I noticed your roster has been growing in the corporate explainer space, which is where I've been booking direct-to-client"]. I'd like to bring my conversational read under representation.

Booth: [setup]. Source-Connect / ipDTL on standby. Available for representation meetings on [days].

Demo + recent credits attached.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Demo link]
[Phone]
```

### Tier 3: Established, switching agencies or adding a market

```
Subject: VO rep inquiry — [market] expansion

Hi [Agent first name],

I'm represented by [current agency] in [primary market] and looking to add representation in [target market]. Recent bookings include [3-5 specific credits, ideally including a recognizable brand or title].

[Target agency] keeps coming up in conversations with [name of mutual contact, casting director, or another talent on the roster — must be real]. I'd like to formalize that conversation.

Booth: [setup]. Source-Connect, ipDTL, and ISDN. Union: [SAG-AFTRA / ACTRA / Equity / non-union]. Demo + bookings reel attached.

Available for an intro call any time this week or next.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Demo link]
[Phone]
```

## 5. Self-tape sanity checklist

Before you submit, the AI can run this on your audition prep. Paste your copy + your read notes:

```
Check my prep against this list:
1. Did I lock an intent for the piece, not just an emotion?
2. Do I have at least 2 reads that are genuinely different (not "warm" and "warmer")?
3. Did I identify the trap read that most submissions will give?
4. Did I mark 3-5 phrases with what I'm thinking, not what I'm emphasizing?
5. Did I confirm slate convention for this format?
6. Is my booth tone clean — no HVAC, no neighbor, no chair creak?
7. Is the copy timed inside the spec window (with breath room)?
```

The AI returns a pass/fail on each + one suggestion per failure. That's the last gate before record.
