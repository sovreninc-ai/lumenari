## 1. Customer estimate with explanation — CV axle + brake job

```
ESTIMATE
[Shop name]
RO #: [number]
Date: [date]

VEHICLE
2017 Toyota RAV4 XLE AWD
Mileage: 138,000 km
Customer: [name]
Phone: [number]

THE COMPLAINT
You brought it in saying you hear a clicking sound when you turn left, especially in tight parking-lot turns, and you've noticed the brake pedal pulsing on highway-speed braking.

WHAT WE FOUND

1. Left front CV axle is worn.

The CV (constant velocity) joint is the part that lets your front wheel turn while also putting power down to it — it's how front-wheel-drive cars steer and accelerate at the same time. When the inner joint wears, you get a clicking sound on hard turns. Yours has measurable play and a clicking that confirms wear on the left front.

If it's not replaced, the joint will eventually fail completely. Usually that happens when you're pulling out of a parking spot or accelerating hard — you lose drive to that one wheel until it's fixed. Less commonly, it can fail at highway speed, which is more disruptive than dangerous but it's not fun to deal with.

Repair: replace the left front CV axle (we'll use a [OEM / quality aftermarket] axle).
- Parts: [QUOTE: parts]
- Labour: 1.5 hours x [QUOTE: rate] = [QUOTE: labour]
- Subtotal: [QUOTE: subtotal]

2. Front brake rotors are warped, pads at 4mm.

The brake rotors are the discs that the brake pads clamp onto when you press the pedal. When rotors get hot and cool repeatedly, especially under heavy braking, they can warp slightly — which is what you're feeling as the pulsing in the pedal. Your front pads measure 4mm; minimum legal/safe is 3mm. They have life left, but if we machine the rotors and reuse them with these pads, you'll be back in here in 3-4 months anyway for pads.

Recommendation: replace front pads and rotors as a pair. We don't machine rotors below a certain thickness because it doesn't last.

- Parts: [QUOTE: parts]
- Labour: 2.0 hours x [QUOTE: rate] = [QUOTE: labour]
- Subtotal: [QUOTE: subtotal]

3. Inspection note: rear brakes are at 7mm — plenty of life, will revisit next service.

RECOMMENDATIONS BY URGENCY

Tier 2 (real problem, weeks to months — plan it):
- Left front CV axle replacement: [QUOTE: subtotal]
- Front brake pads + rotors: [QUOTE: subtotal]

Tier 3 (maintenance, no rush):
- None this visit beyond standard inspection.

TOTAL ESTIMATE
[QUOTE: combined total] + applicable tax
Diagnostic fee: [QUOTE: diag fee] (waived if you proceed with the work)

WHAT THIS ESTIMATE DOES NOT INCLUDE
If we get into the brake job and find something else — a leaking caliper, a seized slide pin, a worn dust shield — we'll call you with the finding and the cost before adding anything to the job.

SECOND-OPINION NOTE
For a job in this size range, we'd encourage you to get a second opinion if you haven't used us before. Take this estimate to another shop. If they come in lower on the same scope of work, bring it back and we'll see if we can match. If not, you've confirmed our number is fair. Either way, no pressure from us.

VALIDITY
This estimate is valid 14 days — parts prices move, especially on imports.

NEXT STEP
Reply, call, or text to approve and we'll get the work scheduled. Most likely turn-around once approved is 1-2 days depending on parts availability.

Thanks,
[Service writer first name]

- Confirm second-opinion line — shop strategy choice (default include)
```

## 2. "Why does my steering wheel shake on the highway" explainer

```
Hi [customer first name],

Got your question about the steering wheel shake on the highway. Quick rundown so you know what you're dealing with before you bring the car in.

WHAT YOU'RE DESCRIBING
Steering wheel vibrates between 100-120 km/h on the highway, smooth at lower speeds, no shake when braking. Started a couple weeks ago.

COMMON CAUSES (most likely to least likely)

1. Wheel balance is off. Most common cause by far. Tires get small wheel weights stuck to the rim to keep them balanced. If one falls off (cold weather, hitting a curb), you get a vibration at higher speeds because the imbalance becomes noticeable above a certain RPM. Fix: re-balance all four wheels. Takes about 30 min, costs around [QUOTE: balance rate].

2. A tire is wearing unevenly. If your alignment was off for a while, the tread wears in a cupped pattern that creates vibration. Fix: depending on how bad, alignment + tire rotation, sometimes new tire(s). Alignment runs around [QUOTE: rate]. Tires depend on what you're driving.

3. Bent rim or shifted wheel weight. If you hit a hard curb or big pothole recently, you can bend the rim slightly — usually invisible to the eye, but a wheel-balance machine will tell us. Fix: rim straightening at a specialist (some rims can be saved, some can't) or replacement.

4. Less commonly: worn tie-rod end, brake rotor warp showing up at speed (uncommon for shake without brake pedal pulse), or a separated tire (look for a visible bulge in the sidewall — if you see one, don't drive).

WHAT WE'D DO TO DIAGNOSE
Pull all four wheels, visually inspect tires and rims, check tie-rod ends and ball joints by hand, then put each wheel on the balance machine. That's about 45 minutes of diagnostic time. Once we know what we're dealing with, we'll call with the specific cost.

BALLPARK SCENARIOS
- Best case (weight fell off, rebalance fixes it): [QUOTE: low end]
- Middle case (alignment + balance + rotation): [QUOTE: middle]
- Worst case (new tires or bent rim repair): [QUOTE: high end]

When you're ready, give us a call or reply here and we'll get you in.

[Service writer first name]

---
Numbers and assumptions you should plug in:
- Balance rate
- Alignment rate
- Tire price ranges for the customer's vehicle
- Bent rim repair rate (varies by specialist)
- Diagnostic time may be different in your shop
```

---

```
DAY 7 (text from shop):

"Hi [first name] — it's [service writer first name] from [shop]. Been about a week since your brake work. How are they feeling? Any squealing, pulling, or pulsing? All good is the answer we want, but tell us either way."

---

Thanks,
[Service writer first name]"
```
