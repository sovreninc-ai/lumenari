## 1. Pitch deck — brand spot (small business, mid-budget)

```
PITCH — Ridge Coffee Roasters x [Production Co.]
60-second hero film + social cuts
Date: 2026-05-15

---

PROBLEM

Ridge Coffee opened their second roastery in Inglewood eighteen months ago. The Calgary specialty crowd knows the name; the broader market still thinks "fancy coffee" means the chain. You need a 60-second piece that puts the people, the bean, and the room on screen — for the website, social, and the in-store loop — without leaning on the same drone-over-mountains, hands-pouring-latte-art shots every Canadian coffee brand uses.

---

APPROACH

A morning at the roastery, from the green beans hitting the hopper to the first cup poured for a customer at 7:02 AM. No voiceover. The roaster is on camera — Aiden, four years there, the one who actually trains the new staff. We follow his hands more than his face. The room does most of the work: the rumble of the drum, the airflow, the moment the beans crack. Last beat is the first customer, a regular, who walks in for her usual without a word and gets handed her cup.

Built around three visual ideas:
1. The first ten seconds are wide and quiet — a room waking up.
2. The middle thirty are tight on the work — bean color, paddle in the cooling tray, the timer, the cupping station.
3. The last twenty open back up — natural light, the door opening, the handoff.

Hero film: 60 seconds, horizontal, no dialogue, ambient sound + a single piano-driven music bed (licensed via Musicbed).
Social cuts: three 15-second pieces in 9:16 — "the roast," "the handoff," "the room."

---

REEL / REFERENCES

1. [Patagonia "Worn Wear" — Yvon Chouinard segment] — for the hands-on craftsperson framing
2. [Daniel Wolfe x Stella Artois short, 2022] — for the ambient sound design philosophy
3. [Anomaly "Hard Hours" Heineken cut] — for the wordless build to a single human moment
4. Our reel: [link] — clips from the Glenbow doc and the Highwood Distillery spot show the look

---

PRICE

Pre-production (creative dev, shot list, location walk): $1,400
Shoot day (1 day, FX6 + AC + sound on location): $5,200
Editorial (first cut, 2 revisions, final): $3,200
Color grade (DaVinci Resolve, Rec. 709 master): $1,400
Sound design and mix (Pro Tools, ambient + music bed): $1,100
Deliverables (1 hero film, 3 social cuts, captions): $600
Music license (Musicbed, web + social use): $400
SUBTOTAL: $13,300
GST (5%): $665
TOTAL: $13,965 CAD

Payment schedule:
- 50% on contract signing ($6,983)
- 50% on final delivery ($6,982)
Net 14 from invoice.

---

NEXT STEP

If this is the direction, sign the attached quote by Friday May 22. Shoot week of June 8-12 (Aiden's roast schedule). First cut delivered June 26. Final delivery July 17.

Reply with a yes, a no, or a "let's talk" — I'm in the studio Wednesday morning if you want to walk the look in person.

— [Name], [Production Co.]

---
Things to verify before sending:
- Aiden's availability week of June 8 (mentioned but not confirmed)
- Whether the regular customer is willing to be on camera and sign a release
- Music license tier in Musicbed — confirm web + social covers the in-store loop
- GST or PST status if the client's a small business not GST-registered
```

## 2. Treatment — wedding film

```
TREATMENT — Anna + David Wedding Film
[Director name] | 2026-05-18

CONCEPT

A wedding film that feels like the day, not a montage of the day. The piece lives or dies on three moments: the look on Anna's mom's face when she sees the dress, David's vows (he writes them himself; he gave us a draft), and the dance floor at 10:30 PM when the band stops being polite. The rest is texture around those three.

VISUAL REFERENCES

- [Reference: Stillmotion's "Wedding Film" reel, the Vermont barn piece] — for the dance floor energy
- [Reference: Khaki Bedford Films, the Maine coast wedding] — for outdoor light and how it handles natural skin
- [Still frame attached: dock at golden hour] — for the long-lens portrait sequence we want to grab after the ceremony

NARRATIVE ARC

1. Morning prep — Anna with her sisters, David with his brother and dad. 90 seconds total.
2. Pre-ceremony portrait window — 5 minutes max, dock at the lake.
3. Ceremony — full coverage, including vows and the first walk back up the aisle.
4. Cocktail hour — atmosphere shots, candid laughter, the dog (Cooper) running through the field.
5. Reception — speeches (Anna's dad and the maid of honor — these are the funny ones, per their note), first dance, dance floor montage from 9:30 PM onward.
6. Closing — the sparkler send-off, ending on the boat dock at night.

LOOK

Camera: Sony FX6 (A-cam), Sony A7S III (B-cam, run-and-gun)
Lenses: Sigma 24-70 f/2.8 on A, Sony 35mm f/1.4 GM on B, 70-200 for the dock portraits
Lighting: natural only during day, ceremony, and dinner. Aputure 300X for the dance floor with a 200d on a stand outside the tent window for fall-off light.
Color: warm grade favoring orange-amber midtones in golden hour scenes. Neutral indoor for daytime. Punchy contrast for dance floor.
Sound: lavs on both Anna and David for vows. Boom op for the speeches. Music for the highlight film licensed through Musicbed — we'll show you 3 tracks after the first cut.

DELIVERABLES

- Highlight film: 4-6 minutes, 1080p H.264, horizontal, master copy on USB + Vimeo link
- Full ceremony film: 20-35 minutes, single edit with multi-cam coverage
- Speech recordings: stems for each speech, isolated
- Social cut: one 60-second vertical 9:16 cut for Instagram
- Raw camera files: not included (available for additional $400 on a separate drive)
- Music: licensed via Musicbed (covered in package)

TIMELINE

Pre-production (timeline call, shot list): May 27
Wedding day: June 13
First cut highlight film: July 18 (5 weeks)
Your review window: 7 days
Revisions (2 rounds included): through August 8
Final delivery: August 15

Note: we always add 1-2 weeks of buffer to wedding film timelines because color and sound take longer than people expect. The dates above already include that buffer.

CREW

Lead shooter / editor: [Name]
Second shooter: [Name]
Audio: covered by lead shooter with wireless lavs and a Tascam recorder on the boom during speeches.

---
Things to verify before sending:
- Couple's permission to feature the maid of honor's speech (could be R-rated)
- Whether Cooper the dog needs a separate release from his owner
- Reception venue restrictions on additional lighting (some Banff venues prohibit lighting kits)
- Confirm Musicbed sync covers the social cut as well as the highlight film
```

## 3. Client recap email — after a shoot day

```
Subject: Ridge Coffee shoot — wrap recap and what's coming

Hey Karen,

Wrapped at Ridge yesterday around 6 PM. Quick recap and what's next.

What we got:
- Aiden's full roast run, drum to cooling tray, on the long lens — beautiful textures, the bean color shift is going to grade up nicely
- The 7:02 first-pour moment with Margaret (the regular) landed in one take. She walked in, ordered the usual without saying a word, walked out — exactly what we hoped for
- B-roll of the room waking up: the door light at 6:15, steam coming off the espresso machine, the chalkboard
- Three setups we hadn't planned: a tight on the timer, Aiden's hands on the paddle, and the sound of the airflow valve cycling. All going in.

What surprised us in a good way:
- The cupping station mid-morning had two staff dialing in a new lot from Ethiopia. We got 90 seconds of them tasting and arguing — not in the brief, but it's going to make the social cut on "the room" land harder.

What we'll flag in the cut:
- The roaster fan is loud enough that we couldn't get clean ambient sound during the actual roast. We'll layer in the cleaner room tone we grabbed before opening and treat it in the mix. You'll see this in the first cut — it's a quality call, not a problem.
- One light flicker at the espresso machine in the 7:00 AM scene. We have plates we can fix it with in post. No reshoot needed.

Next milestones:
- First cut delivered: June 26 (Frame.io link)
- Your review window: 7 days through July 3

Reminder on the review: timecodes are huge. The structure I'll send with the cut is COPY / PACE / COLOR / AUDIO / B-ROLL / STRUCTURE — drop your notes in that format and we'll knock revisions out fast.

Two rounds of revisions are in the contract; anything beyond gets handled as an add at $400/round.

Talk soon,
```

## 4. Edit-review notes — structured for a brand spot

This is the format you send to clients with the first cut. Coach them to use this back at you.

```
EDIT REVIEW — Ridge Coffee 60s Hero — Round 1 — June 28, 2026

Karen — first cut is up on Frame.io. Link below. Review window through July 3.

How to leave notes:
1. Use timecodes from the video player (e.g., 00:14)
2. Tag each note with one of: COPY / PACE / COLOR / AUDIO / B-ROLL / VFX / STRUCTURE / TYPO
3. One thought per note. If a note has two parts, leave two notes.

Format:
[Timecode] — [Type] — [Note]

Examples of what works:
00:14 — COPY — "Ridge Coffee — Inglewood" should be "Ridge Coffee Roasters — Inglewood"
00:32 — PACE — Hold the wide shot of the empty room 1 second longer
01:08 — COLOR — Aiden's skin reads slightly cool, warmer if possible
01:24 — AUDIO — Music comes up too hot under the timer ding
01:50 — B-ROLL — Can we swap the generic espresso machine shot for the one of Aiden's hands on the paddle?
02:14 — STRUCTURE — Consider moving the cupping scene before the first-pour moment

What doesn't work for either of us:
- "Make it pop"
- "I don't love this section"
- "Can we try something different here?"

If you have a feel-based note, give me a reference: a frame from another film, a song mood, an emotional word. That's enough to act on.

Two rounds of revisions are in the package. After R2, additional rounds are billed at $400 each.

— [Name]

---
Things to verify before sending:
- Frame.io link permissions set to comment-only, not edit
- Karen's preferred email vs. the brand team's shared inbox
```

## 5. Schedule slip — honest email

```
Subject: Ridge Coffee — heads up on the timeline

Hey Karen,

Quick honest update. We're going to push final delivery by 6 days — new target is July 23, was July 17.

Reason: our colorist had a death in the family and is out for the week. The grade is the bottleneck — I don't want to push the cut to someone less experienced just to hit the date, because the bean color shift on Aiden's roast scene is the moment of the film and it needs the right hand on it.

Options:
1. Wait the 6 days. Final delivery July 23. This is what I recommend.
2. I send you a "near-final" version on July 17 with a temp grade so you have something to share if you have a hard deadline (e.g., website launch). I refinish on July 23 and replace the file.

Either way the cut and the sound mix stay on the original schedule — only the grade is affected.

Let me know by Wednesday so I can route the work either way. Sorry for the curveball.

[Name]

---
Things to verify before sending:
- Whether Karen has a launch date tied to the original July 17 (the website redesign was mentioned but not confirmed)
- Cost implication of running a temp grade and a final grade — likely no charge since the slip is on our side
```
