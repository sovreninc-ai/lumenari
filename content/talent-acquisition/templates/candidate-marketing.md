# Candidate Marketing — Talent Acquisition Pack

Outreach scripts that don't sound like spam, candidate-facing landing pages, and event/social copy. The principle running through all of this: candidates have options, three sentences earn the read, specificity earns the reply.

---

## The outreach rule of three (first message only)

A first cold outreach message has three jobs and three sentences:

1. **One specific reason** you're reaching out — something the candidate couldn't have guessed you'd notice. A talk, a repo, a blog post, a project shipped, a public commit message, a tenure milestone, a panel they were on. NOT "I see you work at [company]"
2. **One reason** your role might be interesting to them, tied to that specific reason
3. **One soft ask** — 15 min if curious, no pressure, no calendar wall

That's the whole message. If it's longer than four sentences, cut it.

## Outreach templates by seniority and channel

### Senior IC (Staff / Principal level) — LinkedIn InMail

```
Subject: [Specific reference — title of their talk, name of a recent project, etc.]

Hi [Name],

[ONE sentence — the specific reason. "Watched your KubeCon talk on partial-failure handling — the part where you stopped trying to make retries idempotent and just made consumers crash-resilient is a frame I'd been looking for."]

[ONE sentence — why your role connects. "We're building the platform team at [Company] — same problem space, smaller surface, more autonomy."]

[ONE sentence — soft ask. "15 min if you're at all curious — open calendar, no pressure."]

— [Your name], [your title] at [Company]
```

### Senior IC — email (warmer, slightly longer OK)

```
Subject line: Your [specific thing] / [your name] at [your company]

Hi [Name],

[Open with the specific reason — 1-2 sentences. Email allows slightly more context than LinkedIn.]

[The role and why it might fit, tied to the specific reason — 2-3 sentences. Mention the team's most recent ship or a specific scope you'd own.]

[The honest part — if your company has had a recent layoff, brand event, or other thing that would come up in a Google search, address it here. Not in M3. In M1.]

[Soft ask — 15 min, calendar link or "happy to share more in writing."]

— [Your name]
[Your title] at [Company]
linkedin.com/in/[handle]
```

### Mid-level IC — LinkedIn InMail

```
Subject: [Specific reference — but for mid-level, can be a project type they've worked on, not necessarily a public talk]

Hi [Name],

[ONE sentence — specific. "Noticed you've been working on the React migration at [Company] for the last 18 months — that's a project I'd want to talk to someone who's lived through."]

[ONE sentence — what makes the role different / interesting. "We're staffing a similar migration at [Company], but earlier — we're at the design-decisions stage and looking for someone who's already made the hard calls once."]

[ONE soft ask.]

— [Name]
```

### Engineering Manager — LinkedIn InMail

```
Subject: [Specific — a team they built, a hiring blog post they wrote, a talk on management]

Hi [Name],

[ONE sentence — specific to their leadership style or work. Not "I see you've been a manager for 4 years."]

[ONE sentence — the management role at your company, specific scope. "Director of Engineering, owning two teams (Platform + Data Infra), team-of-teams of ~14 — direct reports stay at 5-7."]

[ONE soft ask.]

— [Name]
```

### Director+ / Senior Leader — email only (LinkedIn for senior leaders is high noise)

```
Subject: A quick note from [your VP / CEO who knows them] [or] [Your name, [your company]]

Hi [Name],

[Open with how this introduction is happening — via a mutual connection if you have one, or a specific public thing of theirs. Senior leaders ignore generic outreach faster than anyone.]

[The role, specific scope, why now. 2-3 sentences. Include comp directionally if you can ("$X+ all-in" not a precise band).]

[The soft ask — typically a referral to your CEO or VP for a peer-to-peer conversation, not "15 min on my calendar."]

— [Name], [title]
```

### Campus / new-grad — email (mass campaigns OK, but personalized batch)

```
Subject: [Your company]'s engineering new-grad program — applications open through [date]

Hi [Name],

[ONE sentence — the program in plain language. "We run a 12-month rotation across Platform, Product, and Infra, with mentorship and 1:1s every other week."]

[ONE-TWO sentences — what makes this different from peer companies. Be specific. "Our hire-to-promote rate to L4 is 78% in 18 months — we mean it on growth."]

[Application link + deadline + 15-min info session calendar.]

— [Name]
[Title] at [Company]
```

(Inclusive-language audit applies: do not use "young," "energetic," "fresh perspective" anywhere in campus copy — describe the program, not the candidate.)

## Candidate-facing landing page structure

Detailed structure with a copy bank for each section. See `reference-workflows.md` Example 4 for the worked example.

### Section 1 — Above the fold (10-second test)

- **One sentence** that says what this team does and why it exists. Not the mission statement.
- **One real photo** of the actual team or workspace. NOT stock.
- **One CTA**, two if you must. "See open roles" + "Talk to a recruiter — 15 min, no pressure."

### Section 2 — What this team does (2-4 short paragraphs)

- The problem space, plain language
- 2-3 recent ships, named, with rough timeframes
- Scale or scope, qualitative — "we run X% of customer-facing traffic" or "we handle X transactions a day." Don't put numbers you can't substantiate

### Section 3 — Who's already here (3-6 named team members)

- Real names
- Real photos (not stock — and not the LinkedIn headshot if you can avoid it; a working-context photo is better)
- One sentence about their work — specific, not "great teammate"
- LinkedIn link
- If anyone declines to be on the page, leave them off — don't force it

### Section 4 — What you'd own (3-5 bullets, specific scope)

- Not "drive impact." Actual stuff.
- Tie to a real project or workstream when possible
- Include on-call expectations if applicable

### Section 5 — How we hire (the loop, named, with timing)

- Number of steps
- What each step is and how long
- What you're looking for at each step
- Realistic end-to-end timing
- Any candidate-friendly accommodation (recorded loops, multi-day splits, etc.)
- What you DON'T do (brainteasers, whiteboard, etc.) if relevant

### Section 6 — What we pay

- Comp band (required if you're in a pay-transparency state)
- Equity directionally
- Benefits one-liner with link to full benefits page
- Geo-banded if applicable ("US, Canada, UK — local bands; ask the recruiter")

### Section 7 — Answers to questions people actually ask

A FAQ that addresses the things candidates would have to ask the recruiter:

- Remote posture (be specific — fully remote in X timezones / hybrid Tu-Th in [city] / onsite)
- Visa sponsorship: yes / no / case-by-case
- Promotion path — what L→L looks like
- What "good" looks like in the first 6 months
- What you don't do well yet (this is the differentiator — most landing pages skip it; the ones that include it build trust)
- Any brand event the candidate would Google (recent layoff, recent press, etc.) — address it directly

### Section 8 — CTA at the bottom

Short. Calendar link. Recruiter name and email.

## Event copy — campus, meetup, conference

### Conference sponsor booth (event-page copy)

```
[Company] @ [Conference] — booth [#]

[Function or product team] is hiring. Stop by booth [#] for a 15-min conversation with a hiring manager — not a recruiter pitch. We'll talk about:

- The [specific work area] we're building
- What "good" looks like in our hiring loop
- Whether your work fits — honest answer in 15 min

Booth hours: [day, times]. [Hiring manager name] will be at the booth [specific times].

If you want to set up a meeting in advance: [calendar link].
```

### Meetup talk (post-event landing page)

```
Thanks for catching [Speaker] at [Meetup name] tonight.

If you want to dig into the work, two things:

(1) The slides + a longer writeup: [link]
(2) We're hiring [function] — the talk gives you a sense of what the team does day-to-day. Open roles: [link]. Or hit [recruiter name] direct: [email].

No newsletter signup. No follow-up email sequence unless you ask for it.

— [Recruiter name]
```

### Campus career fair handout

```
[Company] — [Function] new-grad program

What we're hiring: [N] new-grad engineers / PMs / designers for [year]
Program: 12-month rotation, mentorship, 1:1s every other week, dedicated career-development manager
Hire-to-promote rate to mid-level in 18 months: [X%]
Comp: $[base] + [equity] + benefits. Numbers above are real.

Apply by [date]: [link]
Info session: [date, time, link]
Direct: [recruiter name, email]

We'll be at [career fair name] [date, booth#]. Stop by.
```

## Social copy patterns

### LinkedIn post — open role announcement (recruiter or hiring manager)

```
[ONE specific hook — a recent ship from the team, a problem the role would own, a tradeoff that's true]

Hiring a [role] at [Company]. The work: [2-3 sentences, specific scope].

What I'm not going to do in this post: tell you it's an "exciting opportunity at a fast-paced unicorn." Two things you'd want to know:

- [Honest thing #1 — comp range, remote posture, brand event, etc.]
- [Honest thing #2]

If you've [specific signal — debugged X at scale, shipped Y under Z constraints], let's talk. DM or [calendar link].

(The full JD is on the careers page if you want it, but the post tells you more than the JD does.)
```

### "We're hiring" LinkedIn post — TA voice

```
[Function] at [Company] is hiring. Three roles open right now:

1. [Role 1 — 1 sentence on the work]
2. [Role 2 — 1 sentence on the work]
3. [Role 3 — 1 sentence on the work]

Comp ranges, remote posture, and the full breakdown of who's on the team: [landing page link]

A few things I won't sugarcoat:
- [Honest thing — recent brand event, growth-stage tradeoff, etc.]

If you're [specific signal], hit me direct: [name@company.com]. Otherwise the careers page has all of it.

(Not looking for sourcing help — looking for the right people. Tag if you know one.)
```

## What never goes in candidate marketing

- "Rockstar," "ninja," "guru," "wizard," "passionate," "world-class," "best-in-class"
- "We're like a family"
- "Fast-paced environment"
- "Hit the ground running"
- "Synergistic"
- Anything that promises the candidate the role
- Comp ranges that aren't approved
- Demographic targeting in any form
- "Welcome home!" — wrong industry, doesn't matter, still no
- "Once-in-a-lifetime opportunity"
- "Disruptive innovator"
- "Unicorn" (the company adjective — fine in some technical contexts)

---

Things to verify before sending any candidate-facing piece:

- Comp band is approved
- Hiring manager has signed off on the scope description (especially landing pages)
- Photo permissions for any named team members
- Any brand event mentioned is described accurately
- Inclusive-language audit run on the full piece, not just the headline
- The "what we don't do well yet" line, if included, is approved (some companies don't allow this on careers pages — fight for it anyway)
