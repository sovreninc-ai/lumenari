# Reference Workflows — Restaurant Owner / GM Pack

Real worked examples. Steal whichever ones fit your room. Names and details are placeholders.

---

## 1. Menu copy — three examples done right

**Entree (printed menu):**

> Wood-grilled pork chop, charred Frog Hollow peach, mustard jus, pickled shallot. 38.

**Special (chalkboard, short form):**

> Whole branzino, salt-baked, herb oil, grilled lemon — for two. 64.

**Wine note (by-the-glass list, 25 words max):**

> Mas de Daumas Gassac white, Languedoc — chardonnay-chenin-petit manseng blend. Quince and salted almond. Drinks like a Burgundy that took a vacation. Pairs with the branzino. 18.

What makes them work: every word does work. No adjectives that aren't sensory or technical. Producers named where it matters, omitted where it doesn't.

## 2. Menu copy — same dish, three rooms

The same pork chop, written for three different concepts. Voice changes with the room.

**Neighborhood American, $$:**

> Wood-grilled pork chop, charred peach, jus, pickled shallot. 32.

**Italian-leaning, $$$:**

> Maple Hill Berkshire pork chop, mostarda di pesche, jus di carne, scapece. 44.

**Wine bar / small plates, $$:**

> Grilled pork chop board — peaches, mustard, shallot. Built for sharing. 28.

Notice: same protein, same technique, three different vocabularies. The AI defaults to one voice. You tell it which.

## 3. Review response — 5-star

Original review (Yelp, 5-star, Saturday night):

> "Best meal we've had in months. The squid ink pasta was unreal. Our server (Maya?) was an absolute gem — knew the menu cold, paced us perfectly, didn't push the upsell. We'll be back."

Response:

> Sarah — thank you. The squid ink with the crab and lemon is one of those dishes we keep on the menu specifically because of nights like the one you described. I'll pass your note to the team — they earn it on the floor every weekend. Looking forward to having you back.
>
> — Mike, owner

Why it works: specific dish reference, staff praise (general, not naming Maya in writing on a public site without permission), no exclamation points, signed by first name. Under 70 words.

## 4. Review response — 1-star (hold overnight)

Original review (Google, 1-star, Friday night):

> "Total disaster. Made a reservation for 7:30 and waited 35 minutes past it. The host barely acknowledged us. When we finally sat down, the food took another 50 minutes. By the time it came, my husband's steak was overcooked and my pasta was lukewarm. Server was apologetic but offered nothing. $180 for a stressful, mediocre meal. Don't waste your time."

Response (draft, post 12-24 hours after received):

> Karen — I'm sorry the Friday before last didn't go the way it should have. A 35-minute wait past a reservation is not the standard we hold ourselves to, and reading back through the night I can see what happened on the floor that pushed us behind. I'd like to talk through it directly rather than try to resolve it in a Google reply. Please email me at mike@northhouse.com and I'll have it in my inbox tomorrow morning.
>
> — Mike, owner

Why it works: uses her name, references her specific wait-time complaint, owns without admitting liability on the food (no "our food was bad"), no defensive arguing, no comp offered publicly, direct email channel, signed by first name. Under 100 words.

What was cut in the second pass: a defensive line about the steak temperature ("we cook to order, and our line was running steady that night"). That's an argument, not a response. Kill it.

## 5. Supplier email — firm credit ask

```
Subject: Credit on PO 4827 — Tuesday strawberry order

Hi Dave,

PO 4827 came in Tuesday morning. Two flats of strawberries, about a third of each was bruised and mold-spotted — couldn't put any of it on a plate, had to comp the dessert special for Tuesday and Wednesday services.

Quantity affected: 2 flats, $48 each = $96.
Photos attached, taken Tuesday at receiving.

Please credit the $96 on the next invoice, and let's talk Friday about what changed on your end — this is the second short-quality issue in three weeks and I want to understand what's driving it before I'm placing the next order.

— Mike
North House
```

Why it works: PO #, date, specific quantity, photo evidence referenced, named the resolution ($96 credit), named the bigger conversation (Friday call), no apology from operator's side. Under 100 words.

## 6. Supplier email — nuclear (last resort, switching)

```
Subject: PO 4912 — short again, need to talk

Hi Dave,

Third short-shipment in six weeks: PO 4751 (chicken thighs short 4 cases, June 12), PO 4830 (heritage pork short 2 cases, June 26), PO 4912 (lamb shoulder short 3 cases, July 3).

Each time I've absorbed the cost by 86'ing items mid-service and chasing replacements from Sysco at a higher rate. I can't keep doing that.

I need to know by Friday what's going on at your end and what's changing. If I don't have a clear answer, I'm going to start sourcing my proteins elsewhere starting with the July 17 order. That's not a threat — it's where I am.

Call me direct: 555-XXX-XXXX.

— Mike
```

Why it works: cites the pattern with specifics (PO #s and dates), names the cost the operator absorbed, names a deadline, names the consequence without theatrics, offers a direct conversation. Under 130 words.

## 7. Staff text — covering a no-show

```
Hey team — Jenna called out for tonight. Need someone on the floor 5-close. $22 + tips, can bounce by 10 if it stays quiet. First yes gets it. Reply just to me, not the group.

— Mike
```

Why it works: short, named ask (coverage 5-close), named comp ($22 + tips), named out (bounce by 10), first-yes rule, reply-just-to-me to avoid the group rush. Under 50 words.

What the AI would write if you didn't push back: "Hi team! We have an exciting opportunity tonight — Jenna unfortunately needed to call out, and I'm hoping someone might be able to help us cover her shift..." Kill that on sight.

## 8. Private dining proposal — 24-person rehearsal dinner

```
PRIVATE DINING PROPOSAL — North House
Smith / Chen Rehearsal Dinner — Friday October 17, 2026

Event summary:
- 24 guests
- Friday October 17, 2026, 6:00 PM arrival, 6:30 PM seated dinner
- Semi-private back room (full buyout of the back room — main dining room remains open)
- Family-style service

Menu — three-course family style ($85 per person):

First, served as the table fills:
- Marinated olives, focaccia, whipped ricotta
- Heirloom tomato salad, basil, sea salt, olive oil
- Burrata, peach, basil, balsamic

Second, served family style at 6:45:
- Cavatelli, sausage, broccoli rabe, chili
- Squid ink linguine, crab, lemon, breadcrumb
- Roasted heritage chicken, pan jus, lemon, oregano

Third, plated dessert:
- Olive oil cake, mascarpone, candied citrus
- Espresso

Beverage options:
- Consumption bar: house pour wines, beer, and a curated cocktail list. Billed at consumption.
- Beverage package: $45 per person, three-hour open bar, house wines, beer, two signature cocktails.
- BYOB not permitted (we are licensed and cannot waive).

Pricing summary (food only):
- $85 × 24 = $2,040 food
- 18% service charge: $367 (paid to FOH and BOH staff)
- 5% GST: $120 (note: confirm provincial sales tax at booking)
- Food subtotal with service and tax: $2,527
- Beverage: billed separately at consumption OR + $1,080 for package

Deposit and cancellation:
- 25% deposit ($632) due at signing to hold the date
- Final headcount confirmed 5 business days before event (no reduction in price for headcount drops within 5 days)
- Full cancellation 14+ days before event: full deposit refund
- Cancellation 7-14 days before event: 50% deposit refund
- Cancellation under 7 days: no deposit refund

Setup details included:
- Long banquet seating, white linens, fresh flowers (seasonal)
- Speaker hookup for toasts (3.5mm or Bluetooth)
- Dedicated FOH lead for the night
- Dietary accommodations: please send the final list with headcount; we accommodate vegetarian, dairy-free, gluten-considerate options. Severe nut and shellfish allergies require advance notice.

Next steps:
- Sign and return this proposal + deposit invoice to hold the date
- Final headcount and dietary list due October 10
- Toast / AV walkthrough one week prior if you'd like

Questions: Mike at mike@northhouse.com or 555-XXX-XXXX direct.

Looking forward to it.

— Mike, owner
```

Why it works: dates and times explicit, taxes and service called out separately (never bundled), cancellation terms tiered, dietary language honest ("gluten-considerate" not "gluten-free"), beverage options laid out without burying the BYOB answer, deposit amount specific. The AI will try to give you "starting at $X per person" — make it produce real numbers.

## 9. Pre-shift huddle note (Slack to FOH)

```
Pre-shift, Friday Aug 8:

Specials tonight:
- Whole branzino for two, salt-baked — $64. We have 6.
- Squid ink linguine with crab — $32. Crab is fresh in today, push it.

86 list:
- Lamb chop (sub still hasn't shipped, expect Monday)
- Half of the chenin pour list (running short until Tuesday delivery)

VIPs tonight:
- 7:30 / 4-top under Brennan — friends of ownership, table 12
- 8:15 / 2-top under Lee — food writer from the Tribune, table 6, no announcement, normal service
- 9:00 / 6-top under Cooper — birthday, dessert with candle, no singing

Service notes:
- Resy is showing 95 covers, we'll likely walk to 110+ on walk-ins. Door pace tight after 7:30.
- New server (Tomas) is on floor for sections 3 and 4. Buddy him if you see him in the weeds.

Family meal at 4:30. Lights up at 5.

— Mike
```

Why it works: bulleted, scannable in 60 seconds at a pre-shift huddle, names specific tables and times, calls out the VIP with no announcement (which is the right call for a food writer), names the new server and how to support them. The AI will try to write paragraphs — make it bullet.
