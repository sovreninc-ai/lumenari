## 1. Service-call estimate (no power to half the house)

```
ESTIMATE — Jamie Park — 23 Hawthorn Drive — May 14, 2026

What I saw today:
Tripping double-pole breaker feeding the kitchen, living room outlets, and the basement bathroom. Panel is Square D QO, 100A service, installed 2008 — in good shape. Found a loose neutral connection at a junction box in the kitchen soffit where the original builder spliced two runs of NMD90 14/2. The splice was made with twist-on connectors that had loosened over time, causing intermittent loss of neutral and overheating on the load side.

What I recommend:
Open the junction box, replace both connectors with proper-size copper-rated WAGOs or new wire nuts torqued to spec, verify all conductors are clean and not nicked, close the box back up. Test the circuit under load.

Scope of work:
- Access junction box in kitchen soffit (no drywall cut required — box has existing cover)
- Replace 4 connectors with appropriate-size, copper-listed connectors
- Inspect both sides of the splice for damage; if conductors are heat-damaged, install short pigtail extensions
- Test under load with a 1500W resistive load for 15 minutes; verify no temperature rise
- Verify breaker is in good condition; if breaker shows heat damage, recommend replacement (separate)

Not included:
- Breaker replacement if damaged (would add $45 materials + 15 min labor)
- Any drywall repair (not anticipated, but flagged)
- Investigation of other circuits — if you've noticed flickering elsewhere, let me know

Permits and inspection:
- Not required for this scope (repair of existing wiring)

Price: $285 CAD (flat rate)
Payment: Due on completion. Cash, e-transfer, or credit card.
Warranty: 2 years on workmanship.

Why this number:
You're paying for diagnostic time (about 45 minutes to confirm where the issue actually was — most of the value), materials (under $20), about an hour of repair time, the truck being insured and stocked, and a labor warranty so if this connection backs out again I'm back at no charge.

— Tom, Lic. #E-2293

## 2. Panel upgrade quote (Federal Pacific replacement)

```

1962 bungalow with original Federal Pacific Stab-Lok 60A panel. Branch circuits are a mix of original NMD7 copper and one run of post-1965 aluminum to the addition. What I recommend:
Full service upgrade to 200A. Replace mast, weatherhead, meter base, main panel, and ground electrode system. Re-terminate existing branch circuits onto new breakers in the upgraded panel. ## 3. Customer explanation — "$480 to install a ceiling fan?"

```
Hi Karen — totally fair question. Let me walk you through what that price actually covers.

A ceiling fan install isn't 30 minutes of labor. Here's the breakdown:

1. Confirming the box can support a fan. Most existing ceiling boxes in your house are rated for a light fixture (about 5 lbs). A fan is 15-50 lbs with rotation force. If the existing box isn't fan-rated, code requires replacing it with a fan-rated brace box. That means accessing the joist from above (attic) or below (drywall cut + patch). I'll know which once I'm there.

2. Wiring. A fan with a light needs two switch legs (one for the fan, one for the light), or a single switch leg with a pull chain. If your current switch is a single-pole feeding one light, we either add a wire to the switch box (fishing through the wall — adds 30-60 minutes) or use a remote-control kit. Either way, we make sure the neutral is present at the switch (current code requires it).

3. Mounting and balance. A wobbling ceiling fan is a fan you'll be calling me about in 6 months. Proper mount, balanced blades, secured downrod, leveled canopy.

4. Time and overhead. My truck rolls at about $135/hr fully loaded — that's not just my hourly wage, it's licensing, insurance, vehicle, parts inventory, and the warranty I stand behind. The job is about 90 minutes if the box is fan-rated and 2-3 hours if it isn't.

5. The fan itself, if I'm supplying it. (You said you're supplying — so this isn't in your quote.)

The $480 you're seeing breaks down roughly: $410 labor (assuming box swap needed), $40 materials (brace box, mounting hardware, wire nuts), and the rest is overhead. If the existing box turns out to be fan-rated, I'll knock $90 off when I invoice — I'd rather show you the savings than pad the quote.

Want me to come Tuesday morning?

— Tom
```

Tripping breaker, intermittent power loss to kitchen and basement.

What I found:
Loose neutral connection in a junction box in the kitchen soffit, where the original builder spliced two runs of 14/2 with twist-on connectors. The connectors had loosened over time. Conductors were lightly heat-discolored but not damaged.

What I did:
- Opened the junction box (existing accessible cover)
- Removed the old twist-on connectors

- Installed new copper-rated WAGOs, properly seated
