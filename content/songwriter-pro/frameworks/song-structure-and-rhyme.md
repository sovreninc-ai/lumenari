# Song Structure and Rhyme — Framework Reference

The frameworks the kit operates on. Use this as the underlying structure for concept brainstorms, hook-building, song structure, near-rhyme generation, and full lyric drafts.

---

## Part 1: Song structure templates

### Pop (3:00-3:30)

```
Verse 1     (8 bars)    — establish situation, voice, world
Pre-chorus  (4-8 bars)  — lifts melodically, tightens the emotional question
Chorus      (8 bars)    — the hook, the title, the emotional answer
Verse 2     (8 bars)    — deepens or complicates, often turns the stakes
Pre-chorus  (4-8 bars)  — same melodic frame, possibly new lyric
Chorus      (8 bars)    — usually identical to first chorus
Bridge      (4-8 bars)  — new musical and lyrical angle, builds to final chorus
Final Chorus (8-16 bars) — chorus + chorus, or chorus + tag
```

The pop chorus is the destination. Verses are setup; the chorus is payoff. Hook usually arrives by 0:45-1:00.

### Country (3:00-3:30)

```
Verse 1     (8 bars)    — story setup, place, specifics (the truck, the road, the screen door)
Chorus      (8 bars)    — the emotional center, title-driven
Verse 2     (8 bars)    — second story beat or memory
Chorus      (8 bars)    — same chorus
Bridge      (4 bars)    — turn, often a confession or a reveal
Final Chorus or Tag    — chorus + extended tag, or modulated chorus
```

Country chorus often hits faster than pop (by 0:30-0:50). The verse is where the song earns its specificity.

### Americana / Indie (3:30-4:30)

```
Verse 1     (12-16 bars) — longer, more narrative, more space
Chorus      (8 bars)     — emotional center, often less repetitive than pop/country
Verse 2     (12-16 bars) — second narrative beat
Chorus      (8 bars)
Bridge      (8-12 bars)  — extended, often the song's emotional climax
Final Chorus / Outro     — open-ended, slow fade, or a refrain that builds
```

Americana lives in the verses. The chorus is often less hooky and more thematic.

### Sync (2:30-3:00)

```
Verse 1     (4-8 bars)   — sets emotional tone quickly
Chorus      (8 bars)     — hook by 0:30
Verse 2     (4-8 bars)   — minor variation, drives toward bridge
Chorus      (8 bars)
Bridge      (4-8 bars)   — emotional reset, builds to final
Final Chorus + Tag       — emotional peak in last 45 seconds
```

Sync needs space for dialogue and picture. Instrumental beds matter. Hook lyric in first 30 seconds is non-negotiable.

## Part 2: The concept brainstorm framework

```
Theme: [the universal — heartbreak, leaving, coming home, doubt, joy, regret]

Angle 1: [specific way THIS song treats the theme]
POV: [1st / 2nd / narrative — who's speaking to whom from when]
Hook seed: [phrase that could anchor the song]
Specificity score: [1-10]

[3-5 angles total, ranked by specificity]
```

### Worked example

```
Theme: heartbreak six months later

Angle 1: That ten-second moment when you forget — you're reaching for your phone to text them something you saw — and then you remember.
POV: 1st person, addressed to the ex (but they never hear it)
Hook seed: "I almost called you"
Specificity: 8

Angle 2: Going back to the apartment where you used to live and seeing someone else's coat on the door.
POV: 1st person narrator
Hook seed: "Someone else's coat"
Specificity: 7

Angle 3: The friend who keeps trying to set you up and the polite refusal that doesn't say "I'm still waiting."
POV: 1st person to friend
Hook seed: "Not yet" or "Tell her thank you"
Specificity: 7

Angle 4: Realizing the song you wrote about them isn't true anymore and you're sad about that.
POV: 1st person, self-reflective
Hook seed: "The song's not true"
Specificity: 9

Angle 5: Coming home to a sound you used to love and not loving it anymore.
POV: 1st person
Hook seed: [needs work]
Specificity: 5 (too vague)
```

Writer picks the angle. Angle 4 is the most specific and unusual. Angles 1, 2, 3 are all writable. Angle 5 needs sharpening.

## Part 3: The hook-building exercise

```
Hook candidate 1: "[phrase]"
- Syllable count: [number]
- Singable: [yes / yes with caveat / hard to belt]
- Could be the title: [yes / no]
- Alignment with angle: [tight / loose]
- Cliche check: [clean / borderline / replace]
```

### Worked example (from Angle 1: "I almost called you")

```
Hook candidate 1: "I almost called you tonight"
- Syllables: 7
- Singable: yes
- Title: yes
- Alignment: tight
- Cliche: clean (despite ubiquity, the specific phrasing is fine)

Hook candidate 2: "Almost"
- Syllables: 2 (works as a hook word, builds with melody)
- Singable: yes
- Title: yes
- Alignment: tight
- Cliche: clean

Hook candidate 3: "I went to text you and remembered"
- Syllables: 9
- Singable: harder — too many words for a hook line
- Title: no (too long)
- Alignment: tight
- Cliche: clean
- Note: works as the second line of the chorus, not the hook itself

Hook candidate 4: "Six months and I still"
- Syllables: 6
- Singable: yes
- Title: "Six Months" works as title
- Alignment: tight
- Cliche: borderline ("still" + breakup is well-trodden)

Hook candidate 5: "Almost called you tonight"
- Syllables: 6
- Singable: yes
- Title: yes (variant of #1)
- Alignment: tight
- Cliche: clean
```

Writer would likely pick candidate 1 or 5 — "Almost" works as an even shorter, repeatable anchor.

## Part 4: The near-rhyme generator

Perfect rhymes are limiting and often cliched. Near-rhymes — assonance, slant rhymes, vowel rhymes — open the lyric without losing singability.

### By vowel sound

**Long-I ("eye" sound)**

Perfect (use with caution — high cliche risk):
- lie, cry, fly, try, why, eye, by, sky, die, high

Near (recommended):
- alive, behind, design, surprise, light, find, signed, mine, time, line, fine, twice, white, kind, climbed, decide, mind

Cliche flags: cry/sky/lie/fly together = "I'm crying / on the floor / asking why" territory. Replace.

**Long-A ("ay" sound)**

Perfect (use sparingly):
- stay, day, way, say, pray, play, away, today

Near (recommended):
- faded, raised, paid, made, name, change, brain, parade, escaped, decade, taking, naming, painted, late, race, place, escape

**Long-O ("oh" sound)**

Perfect:
- go, no, slow, low, alone, hold, told, road

Near (recommended):
- rode, broke, code, smoke, woke, focused, suppose, exposed, threshold, almost, growing, holding, owns

**Short-U ("uh" sound)**

Perfect:
- love, above, of, enough, glove, dove

Near (recommended):
- coming, summer, lover, color, brother, become, undone, run, gun, none, money, country, hungry, wonder

Note: short-U is harder to belt in high register. Best for verses, sparingly in chorus tags.

**Long-E ("ee" sound)**

Perfect (some are cliche — see flags):
- me, see, free, be, three, sea
- *Avoid: free + me + be in chorus positions — cliche stack*

Near (recommended):
- breathing, leaving, peace, ease, season, even, evening, recede, secret, complete, breathe, sleeve, achieve

### How to use the generator

User asks: "I need rhymes for 'tonight' — long-I sound, country chorus, would prefer near-rhymes."

AI returns:

```
Target: tonight — long-I sound, "-ight" ending

Perfect rhymes (high cliche risk for country chorus):
- light, right, fight, night, bright, sight

Near rhymes (recommended):
- decide, line, find, alive, design, signed, white, mine, climbed, time, behind, surprise, define, undermine, refine

Singability notes:
- Best for belting: alive, find, mine, time, line (open vowels)
- Avoid in high range: refine, undermine (closed final-N)

Cliche flags:
- right/night/bright in country = "tonight feels right under the bright moonlight" territory; replace
- light + tonight = high overlap risk; use sparingly
```

## Part 5: Worked full-lyric example

**Concept:** Angle 1 from above — "I almost called you" — country, mid-tempo, half-time chorus
**Hook:** "Almost"
**Reference tracks:** Lori McKenna, Chris Stapleton, Ashley McBryde

```
[VERSE 1]
Found your jacket in the hall closet                     (7 syllables)
Same place you left it back in May                       (8)
Saw a red truck on Route 14                              (7)
Looked just like the one you used to drive               (9)

[PRE-CHORUS]
And I picked up the phone                                (5)
Hand was shaking                                         (4)
Thumb was moving on its own                              (7)

[CHORUS]
Almost                                                   (2)
Almost called you tonight                                (6)
Almost asked you how the weather is up there            (10)
Almost                                                   (2)
Almost said the things I never got to say                (10)
Then I almost held my breath, and put it down            (10)
Almost                                                   (2)

[VERSE 2]
Saw your sister at the grocery store                     (9)
She pretended not to see me through the wine             (10)
I remembered when we used to drink the cheap stuff       (10)
On the porch with all the windows open wide              (10)

[PRE-CHORUS]
And I picked up the phone                                (5)
Hand was shaking                                         (4)
But this time I let it go                                (6)

[CHORUS]
Almost
Almost called you tonight
Almost asked you how the weather is up there
Almost
Almost said the things I never got to say
Then I almost held my breath, and put it down
Almost

[BRIDGE]
There's a version of this song where I called           (10)
And you answered and we figured something out           (10)
But that one's not the one I'm singing                  (9)
This one's about almost                                  (6)
And how almost is its own kind of place                 (10)

[FINAL CHORUS]
Almost
Almost called you tonight
Almost said it all the way I should have said it then   (12)
Almost
Almost was the closest thing to brave I've been         (10)
Then I almost held my breath, and put it down            (10)
Almost
```

### What each section is doing

- **Verse 1:** Physical specifics anchor the song in time and place. The jacket, the truck, the date.
- **Pre-chorus:** Body becomes the storyteller. The shaking hand carries the emotion.
- **Chorus:** "Almost" repeats but the lines around it vary — earns the repetition.
- **Verse 2:** Widens the world. Sister at the store. The cheap wine memory adds a beat we hadn't heard.
- **Pre-chorus 2:** Same melodic frame, new resolution — "let it go." Movement.
- **Bridge:** The new angle. Names the meta-fact: this is a song about NOT making the call. That's the song's whole point.
- **Final chorus:** Variant of the chorus that incorporates the bridge's insight — "almost was the closest thing to brave."

### Self-review for this draft

```
Things to gut-check before tracking:
- Verse 1 line 2 ("back in May"): is May the right month for this artist's narrative? Could be "back in March," "back last fall" — adjust to fit story.
- "How the weather is up there" — sweet but might land as too sing-songy in chorus. Test with the artist's vocal style.
- Bridge line "this one's about almost" is the most precious / clever line in the song. If it doesn't land in scratch vocal, replace with a more grounded line.
- Final chorus line 3 ("Almost said it all the way I should have said it then") — high syllable count, might fight the melodic frame. Could be tightened to "Almost said the things I should have said back then."
- Reference tracks are female-leaning Americana. If the cut target is male vocalist, adjust pronouns and references to fit.
```

## How to use this reference

1. Pick the genre and structure that fits the project.
2. Use the concept brainstorm to find the angle.
3. Use the hook-building exercise to find the anchor.
4. Use the near-rhyme generator when drafting lines.
5. Use the full structure template as the scaffold.
6. Draft the lyric. Test it aloud. Flag what doesn't sing.

If your concept can't generate three specific angles, the concept isn't ready. Go back to the theme.
