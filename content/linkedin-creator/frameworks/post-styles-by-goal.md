# Post Styles by Goal

Eight LinkedIn post styles, mapped to three goals. Each includes the structure, the forbidden version (what most creators produce), and a worked example.

The three goals:
- **Authority** — readers trust the user's judgment in this domain
- **Visibility** — the post reaches people outside the user's first-degree network
- **Pipeline** — a specific kind of buyer DMs after reading

---

## 1. The Observation Post (Authority)

**Structure:**
- Hook: a specific pattern only someone in the trenches would have noticed
- Setup: one line of context — what you've been seeing
- Body: 3-5 lines unpacking the pattern with examples
- Close: one sharp line, no question

**Forbidden version:** "Hot take: most founders don't understand X. Agree?"

**Worked example:**

> Every fractional CMO I've watched get fired in Q1 made the same mistake.
>
> They tried to build a brand before the company had a category.
>
> One walked into a SOC 2 audit platform and started planning TikTok content. Another rewrote the homepage three times in two weeks. A third spent her budget on a podcast tour.
>
> The CEOs didn't fire them for output. They fired them for direction.
>
> If the buyer doesn't know what kind of thing your company is, brand work is just expensive noise.

## 2. The Counterintuitive Take (Authority)

**Structure:**
- Hook: a claim that contradicts the common advice
- Setup: name the common advice, briefly
- Body: 2-4 lines of why the common advice fails in the real situation
- Close: the user's actual rule, plainly stated

**Forbidden version:** "Unpopular opinion: meetings are good actually. Thoughts?"

**Worked example:**

> The "founder-led sales until $1M ARR" rule is wrong for technical founders.
>
> The advice assumes the founder is the best persuader in the room. For technical founders selling to other engineers, the founder usually IS the best persuader — but only on the technical fit. They lose on the commercial conversation, which is what closes deals over $50k.
>
> The actual rule: founder-led until you can hire a head of sales who closes deals YOU can't close. Not deals you don't want to close. Deals you genuinely can't.

## 3. The Lost-Deal Post (Visibility)

**Structure:**
- Hook: a specific dollar amount or buyer name (anonymized)
- Setup: one line — what you thought was about to happen
- Body: 3-5 lines of what actually happened, with the turning point named
- Close: the lesson, one line, no preamble

**Forbidden version:** "I lost a deal recently. Here's what it taught me about resilience."

**Worked example:**

> Lost a $340k two-year contract this morning. Won the technical evaluation, won the pricing comparison, won the founder-to-founder meeting.
>
> Lost it in procurement.
>
> Their procurement lead asked for SOC 2 documentation in week three. We had SOC 2 in progress, not certified. The CFO killed the deal in 48 hours.
>
> Pricing didn't matter. Demo didn't matter. We solved for the buyer we wanted instead of the buyers who actually had to sign.

## 4. The Walk-Through (Authority)

**Structure:**
- Hook: a specific problem in the buyer's language
- Setup: one line of why most people get it wrong
- Body: numbered steps, 3-5, each with one specific
- Close: the metric or signal you watch

**Forbidden version:** "5 Things Every Founder Should Know About Sales" (carousel)

**Worked example:**

> How I run a discovery call with a Series A founder who's never bought our category before.
>
> 1. First 5 minutes: ask what they've already tried. Not what's wrong — what they've tried. The list tells me how much they understand the problem.
> 2. Next 10: ask what success looks like in 90 days. If the answer is a feature, we're not aligned. If it's a metric they own, we keep going.
> 3. Middle 20: I don't pitch. I sketch their problem back to them in their language and ask if I got it right.
> 4. Last 15: I name two scenarios where we're a bad fit. If they push back on both, we book the next call.
>
> The signal I watch: do they take notes? If not, they're shopping, not buying.

## 5. The Industry Pattern Post (Authority + Visibility)

**Structure:**
- Hook: a trend with a specific timeframe and a counter-read
- Setup: name what everyone thinks
- Body: 3-5 lines of what's actually happening on the ground
- Close: what you'd watch for next

**Forbidden version:** "AI is changing everything. Here's what it means for your business."

**Worked example:**

> Everyone says outbound is dead. The numbers say outbound is bifurcating.
>
> The bottom 70% of SDR teams I've seen this year are converting at half what they did in 2022. The top 10% are converting better than ever — because their buyer list is curated by hand, their messaging references the prospect's last earnings call, and their reply rates look like warm intros.
>
> Outbound isn't dead. The lazy version is dead.
>
> If your team is sending 200 emails a day and hoping, the next 12 months will be worse, not better.

## 6. The Buyer-Language Post (Pipeline)

**Structure:**
- Hook: the buyer's specific problem, in the buyer's own words
- Setup: one line of why this problem is hard to solve
- Body: 4-6 lines walking through how the user thinks about it
- Close: a soft offer, only when earned

**Forbidden version:** "Are you struggling with X? DM me!"

**Worked example:**

> "We hired a head of sales six months ago and pipeline is down."
>
> I hear this from Series A founders three times a month. The diagnosis is almost never the head of sales.
>
> The pattern: the founder was the best closer the company had. Founder-led sales worked because the founder could talk about the product, the roadmap, and the customer's actual business — all in one conversation. The head of sales walks in and tries to install a process. The process is fine. The process is also generic. The buyers who closed with the founder don't close with the process.
>
> The fix isn't usually firing the head of sales. It's the founder staying on the top 5 deals while the team builds a playbook from those wins.
>
> If you're in this spot and want a second opinion, my DMs are open.

## 7. The Tactical Teardown (Pipeline)

**Structure:**
- Hook: a specific artifact (a pitch deck, a cold email, a comp plan) and the buyer who failed with it
- Setup: one line — what they thought was wrong
- Body: 3-5 lines naming what was actually wrong, with specifics
- Close: the one thing you'd change first

**Forbidden version:** "Here's how I'd fix your sales process — in 7 steps!"

**Worked example:**

> Reviewed a comp plan last week for a Series B head of sales. They thought their AE quotas were too high.
>
> The quotas were fine. The acceleration was broken.
>
> Their plan paid 100% commission at quota and 110% above quota. The top rep had hit 180% three quarters running and was making less per deal than a rep at 90%. Two of his peers had quit in the prior 90 days, both citing the comp plan.
>
> The first thing I'd change: 1.5x accelerator above quota, uncapped. The cost on paper is real. The cost of losing your top rep to a competitor with a real plan is bigger.
>
> Reviewing a plan is one of the things I do most often for fractional engagements.

## 8. The Anti-Cliche Post (Visibility)

**Structure:**
- Hook: a piece of advice everyone gives, named directly
- Setup: one line of why it sounds right
- Body: 3-5 lines of why it's wrong in the user's actual experience
- Close: what to do instead, one line

**Forbidden version:** "Stop saying [common advice]. Here's why it's killing your business."

**Worked example:**

> "Hire slow, fire fast" is the most popular advice that nobody actually follows.
>
> It sounds right because it pattern-matches with the worst hires you've ever made — the ones you knew were wrong in week two and kept for a year.
>
> But the founders I work with who actually execute it aren't slower at hiring. They've just done the reference calls before the offer instead of after. The actual rule is hire only after a reference call with someone who managed the candidate, not who reported to them or sat next to them.
>
> The "slow" part is the references. The hiring move is the same speed.

---

## How to use this reference

1. Pick the goal: authority, visibility, or pipeline.
2. Pick the style that fits the substance you have.
3. Read the worked example to calibrate length and specificity.
4. Paste your rough bullets to the AI with the style name: "Draft this as a Lost-Deal Post."
5. The AI returns a draft. You tighten it to your voice.

If your substance doesn't fit any of the eight styles, the substance probably isn't ready yet. Sharpen the bullets, name the specifics, then come back.
