# Memory — Resume + Job Search Pack

## Domain context

Job searching is two parallel processes running on different timelines. The fast process is application volume: tailoring résumés, submitting through ATS portals, sending follow-ups, prepping for screens. The slow process is the network: warm intros, coffee chats, the friend who knows a hiring manager, the ex-coworker who slacks you a JD. Most jobs in 2026 come from the slow process. The fast process exists to give the slow process something to point at.

A real job search lasts 3-9 months for mid-to-senior roles in a normal market, longer in a soft one. The week-by-week rhythm: Mondays you triage what came in over the weekend and reach out to 5-10 warm contacts; Tuesday through Thursday you submit 5-15 tailored applications a day and run interviews; Fridays you send follow-ups and update the tracker. Burnout is the biggest risk after layoff — pushing 100 applications a week is how people end up demoralized in week six with nothing to show for it. Better: 20 well-tailored applications, 5 warm intros, and one good informational interview a week.

Success looks like an offer with terms you'd take, ideally with a second offer in the wings to anchor against. The interim signal is interview velocity: are you turning applications into screens? Screens into onsites? Onsites into offers? When one of those conversion rates is off, that's where to focus, not on submitting more.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- ATS: Applicant Tracking System (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, Ashby, Taleo). Resumes get parsed by software before a human sees them.
- JD: Job Description.
- KSA: Knowledge, Skills, Abilities — government and large-corp phrasing for what they're hiring for.
- TC: Total Compensation (base + bonus + equity + benefits). The number that matters when comparing offers.
- OTE: On-Target Earnings — for sales roles, base + expected commission at quota.
- IC: Individual Contributor (vs. people manager).
- STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result — the dominant behavioral-interview format.
- BAR: Behavior, Action, Result — STAR's older cousin, less common.
- Pipeline: the set of active conversations you have with companies, by stage.
- Onsite / loop: the final-round interview, usually 3-5 back-to-back sessions, increasingly virtual.
- Take-home: a project the company asks you to do unpaid. Sometimes worth doing, often not. Senior candidates can usually push back.
- Recruiter screen / phone screen: the first 30-min call, almost always with a recruiter, not the hiring manager.
- HM: Hiring Manager. The person whose team you'd join. The most important "yes" in the loop.
- Bar raiser: Amazon's name for the interviewer who is neutral to the team and judges whether you'd raise the bar at the company. Other companies have versions of this.
- Reference check: usually two professional references the company calls after a verbal offer. Sometimes they call backchannel before — assume they do.
- Counter-offer: when your current employer offers more money to keep you. Industry conventional wisdom is don't accept. The reasons you wanted to leave usually return in 6 months.

## Common workflows

- **Tailor a résumé for one JD:** paste master résumé + JD → tailoring prompt → review for keyword preservation → human edit pass for tone and truth → save as `Lastname-Firstname-CompanyName.pdf` → log in tracker.
- **Write a cover letter:** identify one specific thing about the company (a product, a recent launch, a person you respect) → cover-letter prompt → 3 paragraphs, ~200 words → tone check (sounds like you, not a press release).
- **Prep for a behavioral interview:** list 6-8 stories from past 5 years → run each through STAR structure → identify 2-3 that map to JD vocabulary → practice out loud until each fits in 2-3 minutes.
- **Send a post-interview thank-you:** within 24 hours of every interview → one specific reference to something the interviewer said → ~120 words → individualized per person if you have addresses for multiple.
- **Recover from a ghost:** day 7 light ping → day 14 real follow-up → day 30 move on. Update tracker so you don't accidentally engage a stale lead in week 8.
- **Negotiate an offer:** never accept on the call → 24-48 hours to think → respond with one counter-ask (usually base or equity, rarely both) → use competing offers or market data as the anchor, not "I deserve more."

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **Objective statements at the top of a résumé.** "Seeking a challenging role where I can leverage my skills." Recruiters skip the entire top section because of this. Replace with a 2-line summary that names the role and 1-2 specific qualifications.
- **"Responsible for" bullets.** Tells the reader nothing about outcomes. Replace with a verb that implies result: shipped, cut, owned, designed, scaled, mentored.
- **Bullet stuffing with adjectives.** "Highly motivated, results-driven, detail-oriented self-starter." Nobody believes this. Drop all of it.
- **Lying about dates or titles.** Reference checks and LinkedIn cross-references catch this. The cost of being caught is the offer.
- **One résumé for every application.** The ATS will keyword-filter you out before a human sees you. Tailoring is the highest-ROI activity in a job search.
- **Cover-letter opener "I am writing to apply for the position of X."** Of course you are. Cut it. Open with something specific.
- **Following up too aggressively.** Daily pings annoy. A weekly cadence at most, and back off after two attempts.
- **Negotiating against yourself.** "I was thinking $130k but I'm flexible." Don't say that. State your number, stop talking.
- **Forgetting to update LinkedIn before applying.** Recruiters cross-check. If your LinkedIn still says you're at a job you left six months ago, that's a yellow flag.

## Tone / register

A real job-seeker sounds tired and pragmatic. They've already had four conversations this week and don't have energy for a cover letter that opens with "I am thrilled by the opportunity." They want to sound like themselves — competent, specific, and human. Slightly informal is fine for startup applications; slightly formal is fine for finance or enterprise. The voice should never feel cranked out by a chatbot. If a bullet could appear unchanged on 5,000 other résumés, rewrite it. If a paragraph reads like a LinkedIn humblebrag, cut it. Aim for "I'd grab coffee with this person" energy in the cover letter and "this person clearly did the work" energy in the résumé.
