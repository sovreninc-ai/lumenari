# Templates — HEPs, SOAP Notes, Discharge Summaries, Instruction Sheets

Four templates a PT actually uses. Each has a prompt and a worked example. Screen for red flags and individualize before patient delivery.

---

## 1. Home Exercise Program template by region

### Prompt

```
Build a home-exercise program template for [region: cervical / shoulder / lumbar / knee / ankle].

Patient context (no PHI):
- Age range, occupation/activity level
- Acuity (acute / subacute / chronic)
- Stage of healing if post-op (weeks since surgery, weight-bearing status)
- Relevant comorbidities
- Patient goal in their words
- Equipment available at home: [bodyweight only / bands / dumbbells / etc.]

Frequency: [daily / 2x daily / 3-4x per week]
Time the patient will actually do: [10 min / 20 min / 30 min]

Structure: header placeholders, goal in patient language, warm-up if appropriate, 4-8 main exercises with name, image placeholder, sets/reps/load placeholders, cue, common mistake, what to feel, cool-down if appropriate, progression criteria, safety stop signs, when to come back.

Screen the description for red flags first. End with "Screen and individualize" block.
```

### Worked example (excerpt)

**Input:** Subacute (4 weeks) lumbar pain, 45-year-old desk worker, no red flags described, bodyweight only at home, goal "play soccer with my kids without my back hurting on Mondays." 15 minutes 4x/week.

**Output (excerpt):**

> **Lumbar Stabilization Home Program**
> Patient: [placeholder] | Date: [placeholder] | PT: [placeholder] | Frequency: 4 days/week, ~15 minutes
>
> **Goal:** Build the deep core and hip strength that keeps your back stable when you twist, lift, and play with your kids. We're not chasing pain — we're building the support system around it.
>
> **Warm-up (3 minutes)**
> - Walking in place or around the house: 2 minutes
> - Cat-cow: 10 slow reps, breathing with each motion
>
> **Main exercises**
>
> 1. **Diaphragmatic breathing on back**
>    - Image: [placeholder]
>    - Sets/reps: [3 sets x 8 breaths] — adjust per PT
>    - Cue: feel your belly rise more than your chest
>    - Common mistake: chest breathing
>    - What to feel: relaxed, not strained
>    - Safety stop: stop if you feel dizzy
>
> 2. **Dead bug**
>    - Image: [placeholder]
>    - Sets/reps: [2 x 8 each side]
>    - Cue: press your low back into the floor before you move
>    - Common mistake: low back arching off the floor
>    - What to feel: deep core engagement, no pain
>    - Safety stop: stop if pain increases or shoots down your leg
>
> 3. **Glute bridge**
>    - Sets/reps: [2 x 10]
>    - Cue: squeeze your glutes first, then lift
>    - Common mistake: pushing through low back instead of glutes
>    - What to feel: glutes working
>    - Safety stop: stop if low back pain worsens
>
> 4. **Bird dog**
>    - Sets/reps: [2 x 8 each side]
>    - Cue: keep your hips level, like a glass of water on your low back
>    - Common mistake: rotating the hips
>    - What to feel: core working to hold steady
>    - Safety stop: stop if back pain increases
>
> 5. **Hip flexor stretch (half-kneeling)**
>    - Sets/reps: [2 x 30 seconds each side]
>    - Cue: tuck your tailbone, then lean forward gently
>    - Common mistake: arching low back
>    - What to feel: stretch in front of the hip, not low back
>    - Safety stop: stop if back pain increases
>
> 6. **Standing hip hinge (bodyweight)**
>    - Sets/reps: [2 x 10]
>    - Cue: push hips back like closing a car door with your butt
>    - Common mistake: bending at the waist instead of hinging at hips
>    - What to feel: stretch in hamstrings, glutes engaging
>    - Safety stop: stop if any pain in low back
>
> **Cool-down (2 minutes)**
> - Knees to chest hold: 30 seconds
> - Child's pose: 60 seconds
>
> **Progression criteria (PT to confirm)**
> - When all exercises are pain-free at current dose, the PT can progress dead bug to held positions, bird dog to longer holds, glute bridge to single-leg.
>
> **Safety stop signs — call your PT or your doctor**
> - New numbness or tingling in legs, groin, or saddle area
> - Trouble starting or controlling urination, or new bowel changes
> - Sudden weakness in either leg
> - Pain that's worse than before you started the program after 3-4 sessions
> - Any pain that shoots down past the knee
>
> **When to come back:** [next session date placeholder]
>
> ---
> **Screen and individualize before patient delivery:**
> - Red flags screened: cauda equina, fracture, vascular, neurologic progression, constitutional, cardiac — described as none in input
> - Exercise selection vs. patient's centralization/peripheralization pattern (PT confirms)
> - Sets/reps/load filled in based on patient's irritability
> - Equipment list matches what the patient confirmed available
> - Goal language reflects the patient's actual words
> - Frequency realistic for this patient's life

## 2. Daily SOAP note scaffold

### Prompt

```
Scaffold today's SOAP note.

Patient context (no PHI): age range, sex, diagnosis (ICD-10 if known), visit number in episode
Subjective: patient's report (pain 0-10, function, response to last visit)
Objective: ROM, MMT, special tests, outcome measure score if collected, treatments delivered with detail
Assessment: progress toward goals (met / partial / not met / regressed), clinical reasoning
Plan: next visit content, frequency, anticipated discharge

Billing structure: [US — CPT codes with 8-minute rule] OR [Canadian — provincial billing]

End with billing-unit summary and "Screen and individualize" block.
```

### Worked example (excerpt)

**Output (for visit 6 of 10, post-op rotator cuff repair):**

> **S:** Pt reports R shoulder pain decreased from 5/10 to 3/10 since last visit. Sleeping through the night more consistently. Notes new ability to reach behind back to tuck in shirt without significant pain. Has been compliant with HEP daily. No reports of new numbness, weakness, or coolness in extremity.
>
> **O:** Vitals stable.
> AROM R shoulder: flexion 145° (was 130°), abduction 130° (was 115°), ER at 90° abd 70° (was 55°), IR at 90° abd 45° (was 30°). PROM full in all planes, end-feel firm, no pain.
> MMT R shoulder: flexion 4/5, abduction 4/5, ER 3+/5, IR 4/5. Pain-free isometrics at 4 weeks per protocol.
> Outcome measure: DASH 28 (was 42 at eval).
> Treatments delivered:
> - Manual therapy R shoulder — soft tissue mobilization to upper trap, posterior capsule mobilization grade III, scapular mobilization x 15 minutes (97140)
> - Therapeutic exercise — supine flexion AAROM with cane, ER/IR with band level 1 yellow, scapular retraction, prone Y/T/W — 3 sets x 10 each, with cuing for scapular control x 20 minutes (97110)
> - Neuromuscular re-education — closed-chain UE weight-shifts on table, scapular clock progression — 12 minutes (97112)
>
> **A:** Pt progressing per protocol at 4 weeks post-op. AROM gains of 15-20° across planes since last visit. Outcome measure delta -14 points (clinically meaningful improvement). Strength returning as expected for 4-week timeframe — limited by protocol, not by ability. Anticipate progression to resisted exercise per surgeon protocol next week.
>
> **P:** Continue current frequency 2x/week. Progress HEP to include side-lying ER with light dumbbell next visit per protocol. Communicate with surgeon if any change in pattern. Anticipated discharge at 10-12 weeks per protocol.
>
> Billing summary (US):
> - 97140 x 1 unit (15 min)
> - 97110 x 1 unit (20 min)
> - 97112 x 1 unit (12 min)
> - Total time-based: 47 min → 3 units per 8-minute rule
>
> ---
> **Screen and individualize before signing:**
> - Red-flag screen this visit: no new neurologic, vascular, infection signs reported
> - AROM/strength values verified against your measurements
> - Protocol stage (4 weeks post-op rotator cuff repair) — verify against surgeon's specific protocol
> - Billing units justified per 8-minute rule
> - ICD-10 code consistent across episode

## 3. Discharge summary

### Prompt

```
Draft a one-page discharge summary.

Patient context (no PHI): age range, sex, initial diagnosis + ICD-10
Episode info: dates of service, total visits, frequency
Initial findings: impairments, functional limitations, outcome measure baseline
Treatment delivered: categories and key progressions
Final status: outcome measure final, ROM/strength deltas, functional gains
Goals: met / partially met / not met (with why)
Reason for discharge: [goals met / patient choice / plateau / transition / etc.]
HEP at discharge
Recommendations to referring physician
Re-referral criteria

End with "Screen and individualize" block.
```

### Worked example (excerpt)

**Output (for a knee OA episode, 8 visits over 10 weeks):**

> **Discharge summary — R knee OA**
> ICD-10: M17.11 | Episode: [start date]–[end date] | Total visits: 8 over 10 weeks | Frequency: 1x/week then 1x every 2 weeks
>
> **Initial findings:** R knee flexion 105°, extension -5°, quad strength 3+/5, single-leg stance R 8 sec, pain 6/10 with stairs, LEFS 38/80, unable to descend stairs reciprocally.
>
> **Treatment delivered:** Manual therapy to patella and TF joint, progressive resistance program (knee extension, leg press, step-ups, mini-squats), neuromuscular re-ed (balance progression), patient education on activity pacing and joint protection.
>
> **Final status:** R knee flexion 130°, extension 0°, quad 4+/5, single-leg stance R 24 sec, pain 1/10 with stairs (0/10 level ground), LEFS 64/80, descending stairs reciprocally and confidently.
>
> **Goals:**
> 1. ✓ Met — pain-free walking 30 min
> 2. ✓ Met — reciprocal stair descent
> 3. Partially met — return to recreational pickleball: cleared for doubles, not yet singles
>
> **Reason for discharge:** Functional goals met, plateau at this level of intensity, patient ready for self-management with progressive return-to-sport plan.
>
> **HEP at discharge:** Continue progressive resistance program 2x/week (provided printed program). Walk 30 min 4x/week. Mobility routine 5 min daily.
>
> **Recommendations to referring physician:** Patient progressed well; consider revisiting if pain returns or if knee progression to surgical consideration. LEFS delta +26 points, exceeds MCID.
>
> **Re-referral criteria:** Pain return >3/10 lasting more than 2 weeks; new mechanical symptoms (catching, locking, giving way); functional regression; surgical consideration.
>
> ---
> **Screen and individualize before signing:**
> - Outcome measure delta accurate from chart
> - Goals language matches initial documentation
> - ICD-10 code consistent
> - Surgeon/referring physician name and contact filled
> - Re-referral criteria appropriate for this patient

## 4. Patient-facing exercise instruction sheet

### Prompt

```
Build a patient-facing exercise instruction sheet for [exercise name(s)].

Patient context (no PHI): age range, literacy estimate, language preference
For each exercise: name, image placeholder, plain-language setup, numbered movement steps, sets/reps/rest/frequency, cue (what to feel), common mistake (what not to do), safety stop (stop if X)

Reading level grade 6-8. 4-8 exercises per page. End with "Screen and individualize" block.
```

(Output follows the same per-exercise pattern shown in the HEP example above, on a printable per-exercise box layout. Reading level is the load-bearing constraint — clinicians routinely write 12th-grade exercise instructions for grade-6 readers.)
