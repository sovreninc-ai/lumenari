## 1. Discovery call intake questionnaire (full plan)

Use after a 30-45 minute discovery call. Couple should be able to read it in 90 seconds and recognize themselves.

```
Sarah & Jess Lee — Discovery Summary — 2026-02-18

The basics:
- Wedding date: Saturday, October 4, 2026 (firm)
- Venue: Cornerstone Theatre, Canmore — ceremony outdoors on the lower deck, reception in the banquet hall
- Approximate guest count: 120 (range 110-125)
- Service tier proposed: Month-of coordination (couple has done venue, photo, catering themselves; want me from 60 days out)

What they want (in their words):
- "Mountain wedding without it feeling like every other mountain wedding"
- "Family-style dinner, real food, no plated salmon"
- "Dance floor packed by 9, that's the only KPI"
- "Sarah's mom passed three years ago — there will be a moment for her, low-key, not a speech"

What they've already booked / decided:
- Venue: Cornerstone Theatre (signed, deposit paid)
- Photo: Mountain & Maple Studio (signed, 9-hr coverage 12-9 PM)
- Catering: Coup Catering (signed, family-style, headcount lock 14 days out)
- DJ: Sound North Productions (verbal hold, contract pending)
- Officiant: friend ordained for the day (legal license via online ordination — needs verification with AB Vital Stats)

Budget direction:
- Overall envelope: low six figures (~$110K all-in including my fee)
- Where they want to spend: food, photographer, florals, music
- Where they're willing to economize: stationery (digital invites, OK), favors (skip), transportation (couple has their car)

Family / logistical context to know:
- Jess's parents are divorced, both attending, both speaking. Jess's stepfather is also attending and will NOT be in the parent dance order. Walk that one through with Jess in detail at the next meeting.
- Sarah's father is the only living parent of the bride and will walk her in. Brother will do the parent dance with Sarah instead of dad.
- One guest is in a wheelchair — confirm Cornerstone's accessible path to the lower deck and to dance floor
- 8 children expected, ages 2-11 — kids' meal needed from caterer, parking lot drop-off for an after-dinner sitter has been discussed informally

Open questions before contract:
- Confirm DJ contract terms before locking month-of fee (DJ overtime structure matters)
- Florals: do they want me to manage florist selection or have they started?
- Hair / makeup: not yet booked — within my scope or theirs?
- Transportation for out-of-town guests: shuttle from Canmore vs guest cars?
- Tip handling on the day: cash envelopes (typical Alberta) — Sarah & Jess prepare or me?

Next step:
- Send proposal Friday 2/21 with month-of scope + add-on options
- Second meeting 3/1 to walk through florals and hair/makeup decisions
- Contract + retainer by 3/15 if proceeding

---
Things I assumed that you should verify before sending:
- Date "firm" (confirm contract on venue is signed)
- DJ overtime structure (mentioned but not in this summary)
- Online ordination acceptance in Alberta (couple should call AB Vital Stats directly)
```

## 5. Vendor contract review notes

```
Sarah & Jess — Sound North Productions (DJ) contract review notes

What's solid:
- Scope is clear: 7 hours coverage (4:00 PM – 11:00 PM), ceremony amplification + reception
- Setup window is reasonable (1:30 PM load-in, 1.5 hours)
- Deposit + payment schedule is fair (30% sign, 30% 60 days out, 40% day-of)
- Cancellation tiers are standard (full refund 180 days out, 50% 90-180, deposit only inside 90)
- Backup gear clause is included — they bring redundant amp and laptop

What I'd ask Karim to clarify or change before signing:
- Overtime rate: contract says "negotiated on the day." That's a fight waiting to happen. Ask for a firm number ($250/hr is the going rate in the Bow Valley) written in.
- Music database: contract doesn't address custom requests or songs not in their catalog. Ask Karim to add: "Any custom song requests with 30 days notice will be sourced. Day-of additions accommodated if available in standard databases."
- Volume / venue compliance: outdoor ceremony at Cornerstone has a 10:30 PM bylaw noise limit — confirm Karim's contract acknowledges he'll comply and that bylaw fines (if any) are not the couple's burden.
- Image / footage rights: contract is silent on whether Karim can use audio or video from the wedding in his marketing. Either consent to limited use or strike — your call.

What I'd flag for your lawyer if you use one:
- Force majeure: contract is silent on weather, illness, or government order cancellations. Wedding industry post-2020 contracts should address this — what happens if the wedding is rescheduled because of weather (fine, sparklers, ice storm in October is a real risk)? Refund? Credit? New date subject to availability?
- Limitation of liability: standard clause limits damages to amount paid. If you're comfortable with that, fine. If not, a lawyer can negotiate.

My recommendation:
- Three items above (overtime, music database, force majeure) are worth Karim re-papering before you sign. The first one alone could save you $500 in day-of friction.
- Send him a friendly email — "Excited to lock this in, three small items, can we tweak the language?" He'll be responsive. He wants your booking.

A reminder: I help review contracts but I'm not your lawyer. For the legal weight, especially on cancellation and force majeure language, a wedding-aware attorney is worth the $200-400 review fee.

---
Things I assumed that you should verify before sending:
- Bow Valley overtime rate going rate ($250/hr — confirm with 1-2 other planners or recent invoices)
- Cornerstone's specific bylaw noise limit (10:30 PM — verify with venue)
- Karim's image/footage usage practice (some DJs are aggressive about marketing rights, some are silent)
```
