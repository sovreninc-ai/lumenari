# Memory — Wedding Planner Pack

## Domain context

A wedding planner runs a portfolio of 12-40 weddings a year across different service tiers — full planning (12-18 month engagement, $8K-25K planner fee), partial planning ($4K-10K), and month-of/day-of coordination ($1.5K-4K). At any moment they have 6-12 active clients at different stages: a couple who just signed 14 months out, three couples in the 60-day vendor-confirmation window, one couple in wedding week, and a couple post-wedding waiting on the final photographer delivery and tip reconciliation.

The rhythm of the work is permanently fragmented. Morning is responding to overnight emails — vendors confirming, brides asking clarifying questions, the venue updating an availability window. Mid-day is venue walkthroughs or vendor meetings (a planner does 1-3 of these a week). Evenings are paperwork the planner swore they'd do at lunch: revising timelines, drafting vendor coordination emails, building seating charts, reviewing contracts. Most of this writing happens late, on a laptop, when the planner has decision fatigue. That's where the AI is most useful: turning a string of facts ("photographer wants to break for dinner, caterer says staff family meal is at 6:15, sunset is 8:42, couple wants golden hour photos") into a defensible run-of-show.

Success looks like: the wedding day runs within 15 minutes of the run-of-show, every vendor leaves on time without an overtime fight, the couple's parents thank the planner specifically, and the planner gets 2-3 referrals out of it. Failure looks like: a 45-minute ceremony delay nobody documented, a photographer who left at contracted end time and missed the exit, a $1,200 bar overtime charge the couple says they never approved, and a one-star review that haunts the planner for two years.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- **Full plan / full service**: planner is engaged 10-18 months out, manages everything from venue search to wedding day. Highest fee tier
- **Partial planning**: planner picks up at vendor selection or design phase; couple has done venue and some vendors themselves
- **Month-of (MOC) / Day-of (DOC) coordination**: planner takes over the existing plan ~30-60 days out, runs vendor confirmations and wedding day execution. The most common service tier in many markets
- **Run-of-show / Master timeline / Wedding day timeline**: the minute-by-minute document for wedding day. Synonymous in practice
- **Load-in / load-out**: vendor arrival to set up; departure to break down
- **Call time**: when a vendor (DJ, photographer, hair/makeup) is contracted to start
- **First look**: pre-ceremony private moment between couple, often before photography of wedding party
- **Cocktail hour**: the post-ceremony gap (typically 60-90 min) while couple does portraits and guests transition to reception
- **Vendor meal**: catering provides a meal for working vendors (photo, video, DJ, planner, hair/makeup if late) during dinner service. Contracted in advance
- **Plus-up**: per-guest catering charge over the contracted headcount; usually triggers above 5% variance
- **Service charge vs gratuity**: catering contracts may include 18-22% service charge that does NOT necessarily go to staff as tip. Different line items. Major source of client confusion
- **Bar minimum / consumption bar / hosted bar / cash bar**: bar service structures with very different cost profiles
- **Corkage**: per-bottle fee venue charges if couple brings their own wine
- **AV / production**: lighting, sound reinforcement, projection, special effects (sparklers, cold spark machines, fog)
- **F&B (food and beverage)**: catering's umbrella term for the per-guest cost
- **BEO (Banquet Event Order)**: venue's master document for the event — headcount, menu, room layout, timing. Signed 30-45 days out, governs the venue's obligations
- **Floor plan / seating chart / escort cards**: where guests sit. Floor plan = tables and room layout; seating chart = guests assigned to tables; escort cards = how guests find their table
- **Officiant / celebrant**: person performing the ceremony (religious clergy, civil officiant, friend ordained for the day)
- **Marriage license**: jurisdiction-specific document required for legal marriage; some jurisdictions require waiting period, in-person pickup, or specific witnesses
- **First dance, parent dances, anniversary dance**: choreographed reception moments, typically 3-5 minutes each
- **Exit / send-off**: how the couple leaves (sparklers, cold sparks, bubbles, getaway car). Coordinated with photographer and venue end time

## Common workflows

- **Initial intake**: inquiry comes in via website / referral / Instagram → discovery call (30-45 min) → if mutual fit, send proposal with service tiers and pricing → contract + retainer (usually 25-50%) → kickoff meeting with full intake questionnaire
- **Vendor selection (full plan)**: build vendor short list for each category (venue, catering, photo, video, florals, DJ/band, hair/makeup, officiant, rentals, transportation, stationery) → couple takes meetings → couple selects → planner manages contracts
- **30-60 day final coordination**: vendor confirmation emails (load-in window, contracted scope, on-site contact, payment status), final headcount lock with caterer, BEO sign-off with venue, timeline finalization, rehearsal coordination
- **Wedding week**: rehearsal (usually Friday for Saturday wedding), final headcount lock, vendor reconfirms (T-3, T-1), weather call if outdoor, final run-of-show distribution
- **Wedding day**: planner arrives 60-90 min before first vendor load-in. Runs the day. Manages overtime triggers in real time. Handles family issues, vendor issues, weather issues. Coordinates exit. Leaves after vendor load-out
- **Post-wedding wrap**: thank-you to vendors, tip reconciliation (cash envelopes if planner is handling), final photo/video delivery follow-up, request for review, vendor referral note for future clients

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- **Pinterest-speak in client docs.** "Your stunning day," "we'll bring your vision to life," "your love story." Couples paying for a planner want a working professional, not a Pinterest board. Specific over flowery
- **Confirming on behalf of vendors without paper.** "The florist will be there at 8" — but the florist's contract says 9 AM load-in and the planner hasn't actually re-confirmed. AI will write the optimistic version. Always feed it the actual contracted window
- **Run-of-show without contingency.** What happens if it rains at the outdoor ceremony, if the bride's hair runs 30 min over, if the DJ's gear fails. Document the backup before the day, not in the moment
- **Ignoring overtime triggers in the timeline.** Photographer contracted 6 hours — if ceremony is 4:30 and exit is 11, that's 6.5 hours minimum. AI will write a timeline that puts photographer in overtime without flagging the cost. Surface it before the wedding day, not at the invoice
- **Generic vendor emails.** "Hi, just confirming things are on track for the wedding." Every vendor gets 20 of these a week. A specific email — load-in time, on-site contact, scope recap — gets responded to. A generic one gets ignored
- **Promising tip handling without checking.** US/Canada conventions vary. In Alberta most caterers include 18% gratuity that DOES go to staff; in some US markets the service charge is house and tips are separate envelopes. Don't assume — confirm with each vendor before telling the couple how to handle it

## Tone / register

A working planner sounds direct, warm, organized, and slightly tired by Sunday night. They use specifics — vendor first names, exact times, dollar amounts, square footage. They acknowledge what's hard ("the parent dance order with Sarah's stepfather is going to be sensitive, let's talk through it") without making it about themselves. They write vendor emails that get a one-line response back ("got it, see you Saturday") because the email did the work. They never use "magical" unguarded. They never call the couple's wedding "your special day." They say "your wedding," "your reception," "Saturday." That's the voice.
