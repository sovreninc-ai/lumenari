# Reference Workflows — Cafe / Coffee Shop Pack

Real worked examples. Steal whichever ones fit your bar. Names and details are placeholders.

---

## 1. Drink board copy — full bar, written for a single day

```
ESPRESSO TODAY
Heart "Stereo" — plum, chocolate, brown sugar.

POUR-OVER TODAY
Ethiopia Gedeb, washed, Onyx. Lemon, jasmine, white grape.

ON DRIP
Colombia Inza, washed, Heart. Cocoa, apricot, soft brown sugar.

SEASONAL
Cardamom-fig latte — house syrup, cracked cardamom, double shot. Through October.

DECAF
Mexico Chiapas, SW process, Heart. Caramel, walnut, clean.

ALT MILKS — oat, almond, soy. +$0.75.
```

Why it works: every line names the bean, the roaster, and the flavor in fewer words than most cafes use for "indulgent" alone. Decaf and alt milk pricing are out in the open.

## 2. Menu card — long-form (online or printed insert)

```
ESPRESSO
House espresso — Heart Roasters "Stereo" blend, on bar this season. Plum, chocolate, brown sugar. Holds up sweet and round in milk. 4.25 single / 4.75 double.

POUR-OVER
Hand-brewed V60, made to order. Today's bean rotates weekly — ask the bar or check the board. ~6 minutes from order to cup. 6.50.

LATTE
Double shot, steamed milk. 5.50. Add alt milk +0.75.

CARDAMOM-FIG LATTE — seasonal
Double shot Heart "Stereo," cracked cardamom, black mission fig syrup we cook in-house, steamed milk. Tastes like the last of summer. Through October. 6.75. Add alt milk +0.75.

DRIP
Refreshed every 30 minutes. Bean rotates — ask the bar. 3.75 small / 4.50 large.

DECAF
Mexico Chiapas, Swiss water process. Caramel and clean — the way decaf should drink. Available as espresso, drip, or pour-over. Same pricing as caf options.
```

Why it works: prices visible, alt milk upcharge named at the line (not buried), decaf gets equal treatment instead of being an afterthought, seasonal drink has a real ingredient list and a real expiration date.

## 3. Pastry case copy

```
CARDAMOM MORNING BUN (V) — in-house. Brioche, demerara crust, salted butter. Made fresh this morning. 5.50.

ALMOND CROISSANT (N) — from Bakery One, North End. Twice-baked, almond cream, sliced almond. 5.25.

CHOCOLATE CHIP COOKIE (V, GF-i) — in-house, made without gluten ingredients. Brown butter, sea salt, two kinds of chocolate. 4.50.

MORNING SCONE (rotating flavor — ask bar) — from Bakery One. 4.75.

PB&J SANDWICH (V, GF-i avail.) — peanut butter we grind in-house, raspberry jam from Sunny Acres, sourdough. 9.50.

Allergens: pastry case has cross-contact with wheat, dairy, eggs, nuts. Severe allergies — please tell us before you order.
```

Why it works: in-house vs. wholesale credited honestly, allergen flags clear without being clinical, the cross-contact disclosure is in the case copy not just on a wall sign.

## 4. IG post — new bean launch

```
New on pour-over today: Ethiopia Gedeb, washed process, from @onyxcoffee.

This one drinks like a peach iced tea — lemon up front, jasmine in the middle, white grape on the finish. The brightest cup we've poured this month, maybe this season.

Onyx had the Gedeb lot at the top of their cupping table last week and we grabbed half a bag. Probably here through next Tuesday. After that, we'll see what they have us drinking next.

Hand-brewed V60, 6 minutes from order to cup.

Pour-over bar opens at 7.

#yyc #yycliving #braiddistrict #onyxcoffee #specialtycoffee #ethiopiagedeb #pourover
```

Why it works: opens with a specific detail (not "Good morning!"), names the bean and the roaster, gives a real flavor description ("drinks like a peach iced tea"), explains why this bean is on bar this week, has a clear limit (through Tuesday), neighborhood-specific hashtags, no #coffeegram noise. 95 words.

## 5. IG post — seasonal launch (with a reason)

```
Cardamom-fig latte is back on bar today.

Last September, I bought too many figs from a farm stand on the way back from a wedding upstate. Spent the next week cooking them down with cardamom, brown sugar, and a little orange zest, trying to figure out what to do with them. The latte that came out of that week has been our favorite drink to come back to ever since.

This year's version: house-cooked black mission fig syrup, cracked green cardamom, double shot of Heart "Stereo," steamed whole milk (oat works too).

Through the end of October. Then we put it away for another year.

#yyc #specialtycoffee #seasonaldrink #fig #cardamomlatte #braiddistrict
```

Why it works: this drink has a story that's specific to the cafe — "we made this because of X, not because it's that time of year." Names the ingredients honestly. Has a real expiration. The AI defaults to "Pumpkin spice is back!" — kill that.

## 6. Roaster email — wholesale order with a question

```
Subject: Order for the week of Aug 12 + Gedeb question

Hi Alex,

Standard order for the week of Aug 12, delivery Tuesday:
- 5 lb Heart "Stereo" — house espresso bar
- 2 lb Mexico Chiapas SW — decaf
- 1 lb Colombia Inza — drip rotation

For pour-over rotation, anything new on the cupping table this week? The Gedeb was a hit — we sold through it faster than we expected, and a regular asked if we have anything similar coming. If you have another washed Ethiopia or a juicy Kenya, I'd love a half-pound sample.

Also: invoice for last week's order should be paid by Friday — confirming you have the e-transfer payment received from Aug 1.

— Mike
North House
```

Why it works: specific order, real question with context, acknowledges the relationship (regular asked = trust), settles a payment thread cleanly, signed with first name and cafe. Under 130 words.

## 7. Roaster email — asking for a microlot

```
Subject: Asking about the Gesha you posted last week

Hi Alex,

Saw your post about the Pananchic Gesha you brought in. We don't normally chase microlots — but we have a pour-over regular who's been working through every Gesha in the city this year, and I'd love to have him taste yours.

Can we get a quarter-pound to put on pour-over for a week? Happy to pay the microlot retail rate, and I'll write you up properly on IG when we put it on bar — name the farm, the process, your relationship with the producer.

If it's already spoken for, no stress.

— Mike
```

Why it works: acknowledges the cafe isn't a high-volume microlot buyer, gives the roaster a reason to say yes (a real story, social mention, named-producer credit), takes "no" gracefully. Roasters remember this stuff. The AI will write "We'd love to be considered for any premium microlots you have available," which means nothing.

## 8. Review response — 5-star

Original review (Google, 5-star):

> "Came in for a pour-over on a recommendation and ended up staying for two hours. The barista (Sam!) walked us through the menu without any of the coffee-shop snobbery I was bracing for. Got the cardamom latte too — incredible. Will be back."

Response:

> Jess — thanks for the visit. The Gedeb we had on pour-over that day was one of those rare lots — glad it landed for you. And the fig syrup we cook ourselves, so when you say "incredible" that means a lot. The team will be glad to hear about your visit. Come back any time.
>
> — Mike, owner

Why it works: doesn't name Sam by name on a public site (would be different if Sam said it's OK), references specific drinks she mentioned, signed by first name, under 60 words.

## 9. Review response — 2-star (the slow morning)

Original review (Google, 2-star, Saturday morning):

> "Came in at 9 AM Saturday and waited 18 minutes for a pour-over. Two people ahead of me. The drink was good when it came but for $7 I expect faster service. The barista didn't even acknowledge us when we walked in. Not coming back."

Response (drafted, posted ~12 hours later):

> Anna — thanks for the honest note. Saturday morning between 8:30 and 10 is our hardest stretch, and a pour-over takes 6 minutes hands-on once we get to it — when there's already a queue, the wait stacks up faster than we'd like. That doesn't excuse the lack of a hello at the door, though, and I'll talk with the team about that. If you'd like to give us another shot, email me at mike@northhouse.cafe and I'll make sure your next visit goes smoother.
>
> — Mike, owner

Why it works: uses her name, owns the specific complaint (no hello at the door), explains the wait without arguing (the math is real — 6-minute pour-overs queue up), no defensive arguing of $7 pricing, no comp offered publicly, direct email channel, signed by first name. Under 100 words.

## 10. Loyalty program copy

**Counter sign:**

> 10 drinks gets you one free. Sign up at the counter or in the app — first drink free when you join.

**Re-engagement email (lapsed member, 30+ days since last visit):**

```
Subject: It's been a minute

Hey Jess,

We pulled the data and noticed you haven't been in for about five weeks — wanted to make sure nothing was off on our end.

If it's just life: no pressure, swing by when you can.

If we missed something: hit reply, I read everything that lands in this inbox.

What's on bar this week worth a stop: Ethiopia Gedeb on pour-over (peach, jasmine, drinks like an iced tea), and the cardamom-fig latte just came back for the season.

— Mike
North House
```

Why it works: under 90 words, doesn't beg, gives a real reason to come back (specific drinks worth tasting), low-key offer to hear feedback if something went wrong, signed by first name.

## 11. Hiring post — IG and craft coffee job board

```
Hiring: barista, North House, full-time.

What it is:
- 32-40 hours/week, mornings + weekends
- $20-22/hr + tips (averaging ~$5-7/hr in tips)
- Health benefit stipend after 90 days
- Free pound of beans every two weeks

What you bring:
- Specialty coffee experience (pour-over and milk technique we'll dial in together)
- Comfortable on a busy bar
- Show up on time. Care about the cup.

We work with Heart Roasters out of Portland, we cook our own syrups, we treat our regulars like neighbors. Email a paragraph about yourself + any work history to mike@northhouse.cafe. Trail shifts paid.

— Mike
```

Why it works: pays named, hours named, no "competitive compensation" hedging, no "passionate self-starter" cliche, no demand for a resume PDF when a paragraph and history will do, mentions the trail shift is paid (real coffee operators know this matters).
