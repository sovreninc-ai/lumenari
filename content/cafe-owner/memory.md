# Memory — Cafe / Coffee Shop Owner Pack

## Domain context

An independent specialty cafe lives on volume and habit. A neighborhood shop pulling 150-400 transactions a day with an average ticket of $7-12 is doing fine; under that and the operator is probably also working the bar to keep labor down. Coffee program margin is healthy (60-70% on espresso drinks, slimmer on pour-over because of labor), pastry runs 50-65% if the cafe bakes in-house, lower if buying from a wholesale bakery. The owner is often the head barista, the bookkeeper, and the person who unlocks at 5:45 AM for the 6 AM open.

The rhythm is opener → morning rush (6:30-9:30, the bulk of daily revenue) → lull → lunch wave for cafes with food → afternoon slow drinks and laptops → close. Most operational writing — menus, seasonal launches, IG posts, vendor emails, training notes — happens on Monday or whichever day the cafe is closed, or in the 2-4 PM lull. Seasonal menus get planned 4-8 weeks ahead because syrup ingredients, roaster green orders, and pastry collaborations all have lead time.

Success looks like: regulars who order without looking at the board, a Google rating above 4.6, an IG account that gets engagement from neighborhood handles (not bots), a roaster relationship that gets you first dibs on a special microlot, and pastry that sells out by 11 AM. Failure looks like: a seasonal launch that nobody ordered because nobody knew it existed, a one-star review about the rude barista that nobody on the team can place, or oat milk going out of stock for three days because the supplier order got missed.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- Espresso: 18-20g dose in, 36-40g out, 25-32 second pull (typical specialty parameters; varies by bean)
- Single origin (SO): coffee from one farm, one cooperative, or one region — distinct from a blend
- Blend: multiple coffees mixed for a target flavor profile, often what's on bar as the espresso default
- Washed / natural / honey (process): how the coffee cherry is processed after picking — washed is clean and bright, natural is fruity and heavy, honey sits between
- Cupping: the standard tasting method roasters use to score beans
- Tasting notes: descriptors on a bag (e.g., "plum, chocolate, brown sugar") — written by the roaster, the cafe usually re-translates them for the menu
- Pour-over: hand-brewed coffee, V60 / Kalita / Chemex / Origami being common drippers
- Batch brew: large-format drip coffee, refreshed every 30-45 minutes
- Microlot: a small, often single-farm coffee with distinctive character — premium pricing
- Roaster: the wholesale coffee supplier; the relationship matters enormously
- Green / green coffee: unroasted beans
- Bar (the bar): the espresso machine and surrounding workspace; "what's on bar today" = current espresso and pour-over offering
- POS: point of sale (Square, Toast, Clover are common)
- Loyalty program: digital punch card (Square, Toast loyalty, Stamp Me) or paper
- DTM: direct trade — relationship between roaster and farm, less formal than fair trade certification
- Fair trade / organic / Rainforest Alliance / Bird Friendly: certification programs, each with specific criteria
- BOH / FOH: in cafes, less rigid than restaurants — usually just "the bar" and "the kitchen"
- Wholesale account: cafe buying beans from a roaster, often with a margin agreement and equipment service
- Drip / SO drip: a single origin offered as drip coffee, often rotates
- Latte art: the milk pour pattern — rosetta, tulip, heart, swan; sign of barista skill
- Milk steaming: texturing whole milk or alternative milk for milk-based drinks
- Alt milk / NDM: non-dairy milk — oat, almond, soy, coconut, macadamia
- Decaf process: Swiss water, CO2, or solvent-based decaffeination; SW is the specialty standard

## Common workflows

- Weekly bean rotation: roaster delivers fresh beans Mon or Tues. Cafe owner pulls a shot on the new lot, tastes against the previous one, decides which is on espresso bar and which is on pour-over rotation. Updates board and IG. 30 minutes if it goes smoothly.
- Seasonal menu launch: 4-6 weeks before launch, owner brainstorms 3-5 drink ideas based on ingredient availability and what they've been thinking about. Tests 1-2 weeks before launch. Writes copy 3-5 days before. Updates board, menu insert, IG, signage, and trains baristas the day before. Lead times — syrup ingredients, ceramic mugs, IG photography — all stack up if the planning is late.
- Roaster order: weekly or twice-weekly bean order. Standing PAR-based for the house espresso; rotating for pour-overs and SO drip. Owner emails or uses roaster's portal, confirms delivery, receives, counts, stores in airtight containers, dated.
- IG content: target is 3-5 posts per week. Drink shot, pastry shot, behind-the-bar moment, community/regular highlight (with permission), seasonal launch. Stories more frequent. Owner usually shoots on iPhone, edits in Lightroom mobile or just uses the IG editor.
- Vendor coordination: dairy, alt milk, pastry, cups and lids, retail bag pickup. Weekly or twice-weekly orders. The owner is usually managing 6-10 vendor relationships at any time.
- Loyalty program management: monthly review of who's hit their reward, who hasn't been in for 30+ days (re-engagement), seasonal promo (double points, refer-a-friend). Email or push notification to the loyalty list.
- Review response: Google reviews, Yelp, and lately Apple Maps. 5-stars get a same-day thank-you. 1-stars get a thoughtful response within 24 hours. Owner does this on the phone in the lull.
- Hiring / training: a barista turns over every 9-15 months on average. Hiring posts go up on IG and craft coffee job boards. Training is 2-3 weeks for a competent barista to be solo on bar.

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- "Curated," "crafted," "passionate about coffee," "elevated," "third-wave journey," "specialty experience." This is the cliche pile. Real specialty copy uses bean origin, roaster, flavor descriptors, brew method.
- Marketing-calendar seasonals. PSL in September, peppermint in December, lavender in May. The drinks should feel like the operator's personality, not a quarterly checkbox.
- Generic IG captions. "Mood." "Coffee + good company." "Monday motivation." A regular sees 30 of those a week from other cafes.
- Sourcing claims you can't substantiate. "Ethically sourced" is meaningless if you can't name the importer, the roaster's direct trade program, or the certification. Better: name the roaster, let their relationships back the claim.
- "Gluten-free" when the pastry case has cross-contact. Use "made without gluten ingredients" and flag cross-contact verbally.
- Alt milk upcharges buried in fine print. Real cafes name the upcharge at the menu line; hiding it makes baristas look like the villain at the register.
- Treating the roaster relationship as transactional. Roasters give the best lots to the cafes they like working with. Communication matters more than order volume.
- IG that's all drink shots and no humans. Cafes are social spaces. Show the team, the regulars (with permission), the actual cafe — not just the latte art close-ups.
- One-line "no comments" responses to negative reviews. Future guests read both the review AND the response.

## Tone / register

A real cafe owner sounds warm but practical. They say things like "the Kenya is drinking nice this week" or "we cooked the fig syrup yesterday, it's brighter than last batch." They use roaster and producer names without making it a performance. They acknowledge that coffee is a craft without putting on the hood of "third-wave" performance art. They know what their regulars order. They speak about their team in the first person plural ("we") and about their bar in the present tense ("today on pour-over is..."). They never use "curated," "elevated," "crafted," "passionate about" anything, or "perfect cup." They say "good cup," "drinks well," "pulls clean." That's the voice.
