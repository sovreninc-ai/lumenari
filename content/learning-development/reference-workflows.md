## 1. Needs-analysis writeup — pushing back on a "build training" request

Context: HRBP Maria came in asking for "senior-manager retention training" because senior managers at 2 years are leaving. L&D specialist's first move is needs analysis, not design.

```
NEEDS ANALYSIS — Senior-Manager Retention — 2026-05-09

THE REQUEST
Maria (HRBP, Customer Experience function) asked for a "senior-manager retention training" to address turnover at the senior-manager level (~24% annualized at the 18-30 month tenure mark, vs 11% for managers under 18 months and 8% for directors). Target audience: ~80 senior managers across functions. Deadline: end of Q3 (~9 weeks). Budget: $0 external.

WHY I'M PUSHING BACK BEFORE DESIGNING

Senior managers are leaving — but the question is why, and whether a training would change that.

Four diagnostic questions I'd want to answer before scoping anything:

1. What are senior managers doing today that's not working?
   - Hypothesis: they're not actually doing anything wrong. They're leaving for reasons that have nothing to do with skill
   - What we'd need: exit interview themes from the last 18 months at the senior-manager level. I'd want to read these before designing anything

2. What would we see them doing differently if "retention" worked?
   - Retention isn't a behavior. It's an outcome. Behaviors that lead to staying: feeling clarity about the path to director, feeling supported by their manager-of-managers, feeling comp is fair, feeling work is interesting enough to outweigh the alternatives
   - Each of those drives a different intervention. None of them are obviously a training problem

3. What have we ruled out as the cause?
   - Skill: senior managers are generally not leaving because they don't know how to do the job. They've been doing it 18-30 months
   - Will: maybe, but training doesn't fix will
   - Knowledge: unlikely. They know how
   - Process: possible — if they don't have a clear path to director, or if their managers (the directors) aren't running real career conversations, that's a process gap, not a training gap
   - System: comp curve at senior manager, equity vesting at year 2, the market is competitive. None of these are training problems
   - Manager: most likely the highest-leverage place. Director-level managers may not be doing career development for their senior managers. THIS could be a training problem — but the training would be for directors, not for senior managers

4. What business outcome would move if this worked?
   - If we cut senior-manager turnover from 24% to 15% over 12 months, that's roughly ~7 people retained per year, at ~$50-80k all-in cost per replacement (recruiting + ramp). $350-560k annual savings. Worth it
   - But: that math doesn't work if the underlying cause is comp (in which case the comp fix is cheaper than the training won't-fix)

WHAT I'M RECOMMENDING

Not a senior-manager retention training. Three possible interventions, in order of likely impact:

INTERVENTION A — Director-level career-conversation enablement (this is L&D's job)
- Audience: ~25 directors who manage senior managers
- Problem: directors likely aren't running explicit career-pathway conversations with their senior managers. If they were, the senior managers wouldn't be guessing about whether their next role is here or elsewhere
- Solution: a 90-min director enablement workshop + a manager-of-managers toolkit on career conversations + a quarterly cadence
- Time investment: 4 weeks to build, 2 weeks to roll out, ongoing
- Why this is L&D's job: it's a clear skill + tool gap at the director level. Trainable

INTERVENTION B — Career-pathway clarity (this is HRBP + L&D collaboration)
- Audience: all senior managers
- Problem: senior managers may not have clear visibility into what "good enough for director" looks like, what the timeline is, and what they need to do to get there
- Solution: a visible, documented competency model + promotion criteria + sample career conversations. NOT a training; an artifact
- Time investment: 6 weeks to build with HRBP. Distributed via existing channels, not via LMS
- Why this isn't really L&D's job alone: it's HR / compensation / leadership philosophy work that L&D helps articulate

INTERVENTION C — Senior-manager comp benchmark review (this is comp / HRBP)
- Not L&D's job at all. Worth raising with Maria as a parallel question: have we benchmarked senior-manager comp against the market in the last 12 months?

WHAT I'D LIKE FROM MARIA BEFORE I SCOPE FURTHER
1. Exit interview themes for senior managers in the last 18 months (anonymized, just the patterns)
2. Confirmation that the comp piece is being looked at separately (or, if not, that we should raise it)
3. Agreement that the actual primary intervention here is at the director level, not the senior-manager level
4. Time on her calendar to talk through Intervention A scoping in detail

WHAT I'M NOT GOING TO DO
- Design a "senior-manager retention training." The senior managers aren't the audience that needs the intervention. The directors are.
- Promise retention impact from any intervention without the exit-interview data. We're not measuring what we don't yet know

---

What I assumed; what to verify before sending:
- The 24% annualized turnover number at the 18-30 month mark — confirm with HR data team
- The $50-80k cost-per-replacement estimate — Maria's HRBP has the real number
- That directors aren't running career conversations — high confidence inference but Maria's read should override

Bloom's-alignment check: N/A (no objectives yet — this is needs-analysis stage)
Adoption check: by recommending director-level enablement first (smaller audience, higher leverage), the actual scope is more adoptable than an 80-person blended program
Measurement check: Level 4 attribution is feasible if we tie director-cohort enablement to senior-manager turnover at 12 months. Plan for it
Push-back recommendation: training is NOT the right primary tool for the stated audience (senior managers). The trainable intervention is one level up (directors). Pushing back to refine.
```

## 2. Training-program outline — Director-level career-conversation enablement

Context: Maria agreed with the push-back. Now scoping Intervention A.

```
PROGRAM OUTLINE — Director Career-Conversation Enablement — 2026-05-15

THE PERFORMANCE PROBLEM
Directors managing senior managers are not consistently running explicit career-pathway conversations. Symptoms: senior managers report (in 6-month pulse) low scores on "I know what I need to do to advance" (3.2/5 vs 4.1/5 for managers under 18 months), and senior-manager exit interviews surface "didn't see a clear path here" as the #1 theme. We've ruled out: comp (separately reviewed by HRBP), competency model clarity (exists, documented), skill (directors generally can have these conversations when prompted). The gap appears to be: directors don't have a consistent cadence, structure, or accountability for these conversations.

AUDIENCE
~25 directors who manage senior managers, across 6 functions. Mostly tenured (avg 4.2 years in current role). All have run career conversations before but inconsistently.

BUSINESS OUTCOME (attributable with care)
Target: senior-manager turnover at the 18-30 month mark drops from 24% annualized to ≤18% over 12 months. Methodology: track turnover by manager cohort. Acknowledge attribution limits — comp and market also affect outcome.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES (4, each Bloom-tagged)

1. **Run a quarterly career-pathway 1:1 with each senior-manager direct report**, using the 4-question framework, within 4 weeks of the workshop. (Bloom: Apply — uses framework in a new situation)

2. **Diagnose where a senior-manager direct report sits on the path to director** (clear & on-track / clear & off-track / unclear / not interested), using observable behaviors and stated career intent, in the quarterly 1:1. (Bloom: Analyze — distinguishes between categories)

3. **Construct a development plan with each senior-manager direct report**, anchored on the competency model, with 2-3 concrete development activities for the next quarter, by end of the post-workshop quarter. (Bloom: Create — produces new plan)

4. **Surface promotion-readiness assessments in the quarterly talent review**, citing observable evidence from the quarterly 1:1s, for each senior-manager who's diagnosed as "clear & on-track." (Bloom: Evaluate — judges against criteria)

DESIGN APPROACH

Modality: blended — single 90-min live workshop (VILT) + a one-page director toolkit + a quarterly accountability cadence with HRBP partner. Justification: live workshop for practice and norm-setting (the conversation skill is the lever); toolkit for ongoing reference; HRBP accountability because directors will deprioritize without it.

Total learner time investment: 90 min live + ~15 min/quarter on toolkit reference = 90 min upfront + 1 hour/year ongoing

Manager involvement: directors ARE the audience. Their managers (VPs/SVPs) need to publicly signal this is expected — see Manager-Enablement Comms below

Sequence: launch workshop in week 1, first quarterly 1:1 cadence kicks off in week 2, first HRBP accountability check at week 6 (4 weeks post-1:1)

CONTENT BLOCKS

Block 1: The Performance Problem (10 min)
- Open with the 6-month pulse data + exit interview themes
- Frame: we're not introducing a new initiative; we're making something explicit that's been informal
- Bloom: Remember (orient to the data)

Block 2: The 4-Question Framework (20 min)
- Walk through the four questions:
  (1) "Where do you see yourself in 18-24 months — at this company or elsewhere?"
  (2) "What would tell you that the path here is the right one for you?"
  (3) "What's the most important thing you're trying to get better at this quarter?"
  (4) "What do you need from me that you're not getting?"
- Why these four and not others
- Bloom: Understand (the rationale)

Block 3: Practice — Pair Role-Play (40 min)
- Pair up directors. Each takes 15 min as the director, 15 min as the senior-manager direct report
- Use real scenarios from a provided card deck (8 scenarios drawn from anonymized exit interviews)
- Debrief in groups of 4 (10 min)
- Bloom: Apply (uses the framework in role-play)

Block 4: The Diagnosis Move (15 min)
- After the conversation, the director needs to internally diagnose: clear & on-track / clear & off-track / unclear / not interested
- Walk through what observable evidence supports each diagnosis
- Bloom: Analyze (distinguish between categories)

Block 5: Commitment + Close (5 min)
- Each director names: one direct report they'll run this with in the next 2 weeks
- HRBP partner will check in at week 4
- Bloom: Apply (commitment is the first application step)

ASSESSMENT & MEASUREMENT
- Level 1: 2-question post-workshop survey
- Level 2: scenario-response question during workshop (Block 4) + 48h follow-up self-assessment
- Level 3: HRBP partner check-in at week 4 (did the director run the 1:1? what did they learn?), week 12 (have they run with all direct reports?)
- Level 4: senior-manager turnover by director cohort at 12 months, with attribution caveat acknowledged

ROLLOUT PLAN
- Pilot: 1 director from each function (6 total) at week 1-2. Iterate based on pilot feedback
- Broad rollout: all 25 directors at week 3
- Manager-enablement comms: VP/SVP partners send the cascade 1 week before workshop
- Workshop schedule: 3 cohorts of 8-9 directors each, weeks 4-6

RISKS & ASSUMPTIONS
- Risk: directors deprioritize the quarterly 1:1 because no one is holding them accountable. Mitigation: HRBP partnership cadence
- Risk: the 4-question framework feels artificial in real conversations. Mitigation: pilot iteration; revise based on director feedback
- Assumption: VPs/SVPs will actively signal this is expected. Without their public endorsement, this stays an L&D initiative and dies
- Assumption: the competency model and director-level promotion criteria are clear enough to anchor the conversations. Validate before launch

---

What I assumed; what to verify before scoping further:
- The 24% turnover number and the 12-month attribution window (with HR data team)
- That all 25 directors are at similar tenure / experience (some may need a different approach)
- VP/SVP buy-in (need to secure before scheduling pilot)

Bloom's-alignment check: passed — all 4 objectives observable + conditional + standard-anchored, levels justified
Adoption check: minimum viable — single 90-min workshop + 1-page toolkit, not a 6-week blended path. If directors won't open the toolkit, redesign before launch
Measurement check: L1 + L2 (scenario response + self-assessment) + L3 (manager check-in cadence) + L4 (turnover with attribution caveat) — full Kirkpatrick coverage
Push-back recommendation: training IS the right tool for this audience and this problem. The push-back at needs-analysis stage was the right move; now we design.
```

## 3. Facilitator guide — 90-minute workshop excerpt

Context: facilitator guide for the program above. Showing one block in detail — Block 3, the role-play practice.

```
FACILITATOR GUIDE — Director Career-Conversation Enablement — 90 min total

(Earlier blocks omitted for length. Showing Block 3.)

BLOCK 3 — PRACTICE: PAIR ROLE-PLAY (40 min)

SETUP (5 min)
- Move directors into pairs (random, not function-matched — diverse exposure matters)
- Each pair gets one scenario card from the deck (8 scenarios available; we'll cycle through)
- Read aloud: "You're going to take turns running this conversation. 15 min as the director, 15 min as the senior-manager direct report. Then 10 min debrief in groups of 4 (two pairs together)."

ROLE-PLAY ROUND 1 (15 min)
- Pair member A is the director. Pair member B is the senior-manager direct report (using the scenario card)
- Facilitator: walk the room (or check breakout rooms in VILT). Listen for:
  - Are they using the 4-question framework, or defaulting to their normal pattern?
  - Are they listening, or planning their next question while the other person talks?
  - Are they asking follow-ups, or moving on after the first answer?

WHAT TO WATCH FOR (verbatim notes for the facilitator)
- Director who is jumping to advice: redirect them at the swap. "What if you asked one more question before you offered the path forward?"
- Director who is on-script and stiff: tell them at the debrief that the framework is scaffolding, not a script. The questions are entry points
- Pair that finishes early: don't let them stop. Ask the "director" to follow up on a specific thing the "report" said that they glossed over

ROLE-PLAY ROUND 2 (15 min)
- Swap. B is now the director, A is the report.
- Same observations.

PAIR-DEBRIEF (2 min, in their pair)
- "What did you notice about how it felt as the report — what landed, what didn't?"
- "What was hard about being the director?"

GROUP DEBRIEF (8 min, in groups of 4)
- Two pairs come together. 30 sec each on:
  - "What was a moment that surprised you?"
  - "What's one thing you'd do differently in a real version of this?"

WHAT "GOOD" SOUNDS LIKE IN THE DEBRIEF
- "I noticed I was about to give advice and stopped myself. The silence was uncomfortable but it produced something."
- "When I asked what they needed from me that they weren't getting, they hesitated for 8 seconds and then said something I didn't know."
- "The 'where do you see yourself' question is harder to answer than I thought. I should expect my reports to need a beat."

WHAT "OFF" SOUNDS LIKE
- "I just don't have time to do this every quarter." → "We're going to address the cadence at the close. Hold that thought."
- "My report wouldn't actually answer these questions." → "Worth bringing up. What would you want different about how you've been holding the conversation that would make these answerable?"
- "This is just [X framework] we already use." → "Probably overlap. What's the specific question or move here that's different from your usual approach?"

DERAILMENT HANDBOOK FOR THIS BLOCK
- Pair where one person dominates the debrief: at the next swap, give the quieter person a specific prompt to respond to first
- Pair role-playing badly (joking, not engaged): you may need to walk over. Don't shame; reframe: "Let's run this one straight for 5 min. I think there's something here you'll find."
- The skeptic in the group: don't try to convince them in the workshop. Note their objection, commit to following up 1:1
- VILT-specific: if a pair's video is off in breakout, you can't tell what's happening. Ping them in chat: "How's the conversation going? Want me to drop into the breakout?"

CLOSE OF BLOCK 3 (no time budget; flows into Block 4)
- Bring everyone back to the main room
- One-sentence each: what's one observation from your pair you want to take into Block 4?
- Don't process responses — collect and move

(Block 4 and Close follow.)

---

What I assumed; what to verify before publishing:
- 40 min is enough for two rounds + pair debrief + group debrief. Pilot it
- The 8 scenario cards have been written and validated against exit interview themes — confirm with HRBP before workshop
- VILT breakout rooms are configured in [Zoom / Teams / chosen platform]

Bloom's-alignment check: Block 3 maps to objective 1 (Apply). Confirmed.
Adoption check: 40 min for one block is on the high end for VILT. If pilot shows energy drop, consider trimming to 30 min by tightening role-play rounds
Measurement check: not assessed in this block — Block 4 handles the scenario-response item
Push-back recommendation: N/A (design stage)
```

## 4. Post-session survey — measures more than satisfaction

```
POST-WORKSHOP SURVEY — Director Career-Conversation Enablement

(Distributed via Qualtrics, link sent in workshop chat at min 85)

LEVEL 1 — REACTION (2 questions, kept short)

1. On a scale of 1-5, how much of what you learned today do you think you'll use in your work with your direct reports in the next 30 days?
   1 — None of it
   2 — A little of it
   3 — Some of it
   4 — Most of it
   5 — All of it

2. One specific thing that landed for you today, and one thing that didn't:
   [Open text, 2 fields]

LEVEL 2 — LEARNING (3 questions)

3. Scenario: One of your direct reports tells you in the quarterly 1:1, "I'm not sure I see a clear path here. I've been wondering about [Competitor]." What's your next move in the conversation?
   [Open text — looking for: not jumping to retention pitch, not closing down, using one of the framework questions to go deeper]

4. Describe a specific direct report you'd diagnose as "clear & on-track for director" using observable behaviors and stated intent. (No names — describe the diagnosis pattern.)
   [Open text]

5. Rate your confidence in running the quarterly career-pathway 1:1 with each of your senior-manager direct reports in the next 4 weeks:
   1 — Not confident, won't run it
   2 — Will run it, expect it to be awkward
   3 — Will run it, expect it to be okay
   4 — Confident; have a clear approach
   5 — Confident; can also coach a peer through it

LEVEL 3 PRIMING (2 questions)

6. Name ONE specific direct report you commit to running the quarterly 1:1 with in the next 14 days:
   [First name only — for L&D tracking]

7. Who else will know whether you did it? Your VP, your peer director, your HRBP, or no one?
   [Multiple choice + open "specify other"]

WHAT THE SURVEY DOES NOT ASK
- "Rate the facilitator." (We hire facilitators we trust. If you don't, that's a separate problem.)
- "Would you recommend this workshop to a peer?" (Net Promoter for training is theater.)
- "Was the technology working?" (Logistics. Useful to know but doesn't tell us about learning.)

---

What I assumed; what to verify before deploying:
- Director comfort with the open-text scenario question — pilot first. May need rubric for L&D scorer
- The "ONE specific direct report" question — confirm with HRBP that L&D tracking the commitment doesn't trigger a privacy concern. First name only is the threshold

Bloom's-alignment check: scenario question (Q3) tests Apply; diagnosis question (Q4) tests Analyze
Adoption check: 7 questions, ~5 min completion — completion rates should hold
Measurement check: L2 covered (scenarios + self-assessment); L3 primed (commitment + accountability witness)
Push-back recommendation: N/A
```

## 5. Kirkpatrick measurement plan

```
MEASUREMENT PLAN — Director Career-Conversation Enablement

LEVEL 1 — REACTION
Method: 2-question post-workshop survey (Q1 + Q2 from Survey)
Timing: distributed at workshop min 85, response window 7 days
Owner: L&D specialist
Sample expectation: target 80%+ response rate (high for a workshop survey)
What it tells us: whether the workshop was disastrous (low aggregate scores trigger investigation); some directional signal on which content blocks landed
What it does NOT tell us: whether anyone learned anything or will do anything different

LEVEL 2 — LEARNING
Method: scenario response (Q3) + analytical question (Q4) + self-assessment with anchor descriptors (Q5)
Timing: distributed at min 85, response window 48 hours
Owner: L&D specialist (scenario responses scored by L&D + HRBP using rubric)
Scoring: scenario response rated on whether the answer uses one of the 4 framework questions vs defaults to retention pitch / problem-solving. Diagnosis response rated on whether it cites observable evidence. Self-assessment is self-reported only
What it tells us: whether the cognitive objectives were hit at the moment of learning
What it does NOT tell us: whether the director will run the actual conversation

LEVEL 3 — BEHAVIOR
Method: HRBP partner check-in at week 4 — 3-question pulse to the director: "Have you run the quarterly 1:1 with [first name from Q6]? What did you learn? What did the direct report say that surprised you?" + at week 12, expanded check-in: "How many of your senior-manager direct reports have you run this with? What's working in the cadence? What's not?"
Timing: week 4 and week 12 post-workshop
Owner: HRBP partner runs the conversation; L&D collects and themes
What it tells us: whether the behavior is showing up on the job — at all, with whom, with what consistency
What it does NOT tell us: whether the behavior is moving any business outcome yet

LEVEL 4 — RESULTS
Method: senior-manager turnover by director cohort, tracked over 12 months post-workshop. Comparison: director cohorts who went through workshop vs prior-year baseline (same cohort definition)
Timing: 12 months post-workshop completion
Owner: L&D + HR data team
ATTRIBUTION HONESTY: This metric is influenced by comp, market conditions, organizational changes, individual circumstances of every senior manager involved, and dozens of other factors. We can correlate the cohort difference with the workshop; we cannot claim causation. The honest framing is: "Among directors who went through the workshop, senior-manager turnover at 18-30 months was X% over the 12 months post-workshop; among prior-year directors, the same metric was Y%. The difference is suggestive but not attributable to the workshop in isolation."
What it tells us: whether the behavior change correlates with the business outcome we hoped to move
What it does NOT tell us: that the workshop caused the outcome

REPORTING CADENCE
- L1 + L2 summary: 2 weeks post-workshop (to Maria + L&D Director)
- L3 week-4 themes: 5 weeks post-workshop
- L3 week-12 themes: 13 weeks post-workshop
- L4 12-month review: 13 months post-workshop (with attribution caveat front and center)

---

What I assumed; what to verify before locking the plan:
- HRBP partner bandwidth for week-4 and week-12 conversations with each of 25 directors
- HR data team alignment on the turnover-by-cohort methodology
- Whether comparing to prior-year baseline is the right control (vs. directors not yet through workshop in same year)

Bloom's-alignment check: N/A (measurement, not design)
Adoption check: HRBP-led L3 check-ins are the highest risk for non-adoption. If HRBPs can't sustain the cadence, the L3 data falls apart. Confirm bandwidth before promising the measurement plan
Measurement check: full Kirkpatrick L1-L4 coverage. L4 honest about attribution
Push-back recommendation: if HRBP bandwidth is at risk, scale back to L3 spot-check (sample of 8-10 directors at week 4 instead of all 25). Better to have honest partial data than promised data that doesn't arrive
```

## 6. Manager-enablement comms — one-page director comms from VP

Context: VPs of each function send this to their directors one week before workshop launch. Drafted by L&D, signed by each VP.

```
From: [VP first name]
To: [director distribution list]
Subject: Career conversations with your senior-manager team — workshop next week

[VP first name] in the From line.

What you need to do: attend the 90-min Career-Conversation Workshop next week (calendar invite landing today), and run the quarterly career-pathway 1:1 with each of your senior-manager direct reports in the 4 weeks following.

DO THIS
- Block 90 min for the workshop, week of [date]. Cohorts at 3 times — pick one
- Within 2 weeks of the workshop, schedule the first quarterly career-pathway 1:1 with each senior-manager direct report
- Use the 4-question framework you'll learn — don't free-style
- After each 1:1, take 5 min to diagnose where they sit on the path-to-director (using the diagnostic from the workshop)
- At the next quarterly talent review, surface promotion-readiness for any "clear & on-track" direct reports, with evidence from the 1:1

SAY THIS (when you book the 1:1 with your senior-manager direct report)
"I want to start a quarterly cadence where we talk specifically about your career path — what you're aiming at, what's getting in the way, what you need from me. About 30 min. Let's do it in the next two weeks."

HANDLE THIS

- "Why are we doing this now?" → "We're seeing 18-30 month senior managers leaving and the pattern is they didn't have clear visibility into the path forward. I'd rather hear from you what's working and what's not, regularly, than guess."

- "Is this a performance issue?" → "No. This is the career-path conversation, which we should have been running quarterly for a long time."

- "Can I just send you a written response instead of meeting?" → "I want the conversation. Written doesn't surface the things that need follow-up questions."

IF IT GOES SIDEWAYS
- If a direct report says they're already looking elsewhere: don't try to retain them on the spot. Acknowledge it. Set up a longer conversation. Loop in HRBP within 48h
- If a direct report wants something you can't promise (a promotion, a comp adjustment): say so. "I can't promise that. Here's what I can do to advocate."
- If you're not sure how to diagnose someone's path: come find me or [HRBP] in the week after your 1:1. Don't wait

LINK TO LEARNER COMMS
Your senior-manager direct reports will get a note from [HRBP] next week saying we're rolling out a quarterly career-conversation cadence at the director level. They'll know to expect the conversation. They won't get specifics on the framework — that's yours to run with them

WHO TO CONTACT
- Maria [HRBP], Slack @maria-hr — process and pulse data
- Sarah [L&D], Slack @sarah-ld — workshop logistics, materials, questions about the framework

— [VP first name]

---

What I assumed; what to verify before sending:
- The VP is comfortable signing this version (preview before send)
- The 3 cohort time options are confirmed and on the VP calendar
- The HRBP / L&D Slack handles are correct

Bloom's-alignment check: N/A (this is comms, not learning content)
Adoption check: one-page, opens with the action, signed by the VP — passes the adoption test. If a director doesn't open it after this, the program has a bigger problem than the comms
Measurement check: N/A
Push-back recommendation: if VP doesn't sign this personally (i.e., delegates to "L&D Team" or "People Operations"), the program will not get the same adoption. Push back on that delegation
```
