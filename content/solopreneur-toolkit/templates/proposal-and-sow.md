# Proposals, SOWs, and Intake Forms

Three things this file gives you: a proposal generator with three tiering patterns, a fill-in SOW template, and a 12-question intake form to use BEFORE you quote.

The whole point: write fewer proposals from scratch, win more of the ones you write.

---

## Part 1 — Before you quote: the intake form

Send this BEFORE the discovery call. It saves the call for the things that need a conversation, and gives you enough context to write a real proposal in 30 minutes instead of 3 hours.

### Paste this prompt to the AI

```
You are the Solopreneur Co-Pilot.

Generate an intake form for a client who wants [SERVICE TYPE]. The client is [NAME / COMPANY], a [BUSINESS DESCRIPTION + STAGE].

12 questions max. Group them as:
1. Business context (2 questions)
2. The problem they want solved (3 questions)
3. What success looks like (2 questions)
4. Constraints — budget, timeline, decision-makers (3 questions)
5. Logistics (2 questions)

Open-ended where the answer matters. Multiple choice where it doesn't.

Format for a Google Form or a Notion page — plain text questions, no preamble.
```

### Worked example output

For a freelance brand designer running discovery on Priya's SaaS brand refresh:

**Business context**
1. In one sentence, what does your company do and who is it for?
2. How long have you been operating, and what's your team size today?

**The problem**
3. What specifically about your current brand feels off — visual, verbal, positioning, or some mix? Tell me in your own words.
4. Have you tried to fix this before? If yes, what happened?
5. Why now? Why is this a priority this quarter and not next?

**Success**
6. If we do this well, what does someone notice 90 days after launch?
7. What's one competitor or company whose brand you wish yours felt closer to? Name + why.

**Constraints**
8. What's the budget range you're working with? (Pick one: under $5K / $5-10K / $10-20K / $20K+ / not sure yet)
9. When do you need this live by, and what's driving that date?
10. Who needs to approve this — just you, or a team? List names.

**Logistics**
11. Do you have existing brand assets (logo files, fonts, brand guidelines)? If yes, where do they live?
12. How do you prefer to work — async with weekly updates, regular calls, Slack channel, something else?

That's your form. Send before the call. Use the answers as the spine of the proposal.

## Part 2 — The proposal generator

### Pattern A: Good / Better / Best (the default)

Use this for: project work where scope can flex. Web design, brand work, copywriting, dev projects under 4 weeks.

### Pattern B: Fixed / Phased / Retainer

Use this for: ongoing work or projects where the client doesn't know yet whether they need a one-off or an ongoing engagement.

### Pattern C: Outcome-based

Use this for: senior work where you can name a measurable result. Marketing audits with a "+X% target," sales-page rewrites with a "conversion lift over 60 days" guarantee, growth-marketing retainers.

### Paste this prompt to the AI

```
You are the Solopreneur Co-Pilot.

Write a three-tier proposal for [CLIENT NAME], a [BUSINESS DESCRIPTION].

The work: [WHAT THEY WANT]
Their stated budget: [RANGE OR "FLEXIBLE"]
Their timeline: [DATE]
Anything else: [NOTES FROM DISCOVERY CALL]

Use Pattern [A / B / C] from the kit.

Format:
- One opening paragraph that names the problem in their words (not mine)
- Three options, each with: name, one-paragraph description, bulleted deliverables (max 5), price line
- Mark the middle option "(most clients pick this)"
- Payment schedule (50/50 or 50/25/25 unless I say otherwise)
- Timeline summary
- One closing line with a next step

Total under one laptop screen. No "About Me" section. No "Why work with me" section.
```

### Worked example output — Pattern A, Brand Refresh for Priya

> **Brand refresh for [Fintech Company]**
>
> You said your visuals look like every other Series A fintech, but the product is doing something different — and you're losing that signal in screenshots and pitch materials. Here's how I'd approach it, with three scope options.

> **Essentials** — CAD $5,500
> A focused refresh of the elements your customers actually see.
> - Logo refinement (no full rebrand)
> - New color system + type pairing
> - Updated screenshot template + 5 product OG images
> - One-page brand mini-guide
>
> Timeline: 2 weeks from kickoff.

> **Standard — *(most clients pick this)*** — CAD $9,000
> Everything in Essentials, plus the things you'll wish you'd done in 6 months.
> - 30-minute positioning workshop (you + me)
> - Brand voice doc (tone, do's/don'ts, 10 example sentences)
> - Updated pitch deck template (10 slides)
> - 30 days of post-launch tweaks
>
> Timeline: 3-4 weeks from kickoff.

> **Premium** — CAD $14,500
> Standard, plus full design-system depth and a 60-day check-in.
> - Component library (Figma) with light/dark variants
> - Marketing-site landing-page redesign (one page)
> - 60-day conversion review with recommendations
>
> Timeline: 5-6 weeks from kickoff.

> **Payment:** 50% to start, 50% on delivery.
> **Next step:** Reply with the option that fits, and I'll send a SOW + invoice for the deposit. We can kick off Monday May 19.
>
> — Chris

That whole thing fits on one screen. The client picks an option, signs, pays the deposit. No back-and-forth on scope.

## Part 3 — The SOW template

Fill-in version. Use this AFTER the client picks an option. The proposal sells the work; the SOW defines the work.

### Paste this prompt to the AI

```
You are the Solopreneur Co-Pilot.

Draft a SOW for [CLIENT] based on this proposal: [PASTE PROPOSAL OR THE OPTION THEY PICKED].

Use this structure:
1. Scope (what's in)
2. Out of Scope (what's not)
3. Deliverables (named, dated)
4. Timeline + Milestones
5. Fees + Payment Schedule
6. Change Requests (how they're handled, with a default $/hour rate of [RATE])
7. IP + Ownership (transfers on final payment)
8. Termination (kill fee, notice period)
9. Confidentiality
10. Signatures

Plain language. Each clause 1-3 sentences. Append the lawyer line at the bottom.
```

### Worked example output — SOW excerpt

> **Statement of Work**
> Between: [Your Name / Business] ("Designer") and [Client Company] ("Client")
> Date: 2026-05-15
> Project: Brand Refresh — Standard Tier
>
> **1. Scope**
> Designer will deliver the Standard tier as outlined in the proposal dated May 14, 2026: logo refinement, color + type system, screenshot template, 5 OG images, one-page brand mini-guide, 30-minute positioning workshop, brand voice doc, pitch deck template (10 slides), and 30 days of post-launch tweaks.
>
> **2. Out of Scope**
> Full marketing-site redesign, illustration commissions, motion/animation, print collateral, photography direction, and Figma component library are not included unless added via a Change Request.
>
> **3. Deliverables**
> - Discovery + positioning workshop — by May 22
> - First-round visual direction — by May 29
> - Final brand assets + voice doc + pitch deck — by June 12
> - 30-day post-launch tweak window — through July 12
>
> **4. Timeline + Milestones**
> Kickoff: May 19, 2026. Final delivery: June 12, 2026. Post-launch window ends: July 12, 2026.
>
> **5. Fees + Payment Schedule**
> Total fee: CAD $9,000 + applicable taxes.
> - 50% deposit (CAD $4,500) due before kickoff
> - 50% balance (CAD $4,500) due on final delivery
>
> **6. Change Requests**
> Work outside the Scope section is handled as a Change Request, billed at CAD $150/hour or quoted as a fixed add-on, at Designer's discretion. Change Requests require written approval from Client before work begins.
>
> **7. IP + Ownership**
> All deliverables transfer to Client upon receipt of final payment. Designer retains the right to display the work in portfolio and case studies unless Client requests otherwise in writing.
>
> **8. Termination**
> Either party may terminate with 7 days' written notice. If Client terminates after kickoff, the deposit is non-refundable. Designer will deliver all work-in-progress up to the termination date.
>
> **9. Confidentiality**
> Both parties will keep proprietary information confidential, including financial details, customer data, and unreleased product information.
>
> **10. Signatures**
> Designer: ____________________ Date: __________
> Client: _______________________ Date: __________
>
> *Consult a lawyer in your jurisdiction before relying on these clauses.*

## Cheat sheet — the three patterns at a glance

| Pattern | Use it for | Tier shape |
|---|---|---|
| A. Good/Better/Best | Project work, flexible scope | Small / Standard / Premium |
| B. Fixed/Phased/Retainer | Ongoing or ambiguous engagements | One-off / Phased delivery / Monthly retainer |
| C. Outcome-based | Senior work with measurable results | Audit / Implementation / Results-tied bonus |

Pick one. Don't blend.

---

## Common mistakes the kit will flag

- **Single-price proposals.** Default is three. Ask the user to confirm if they want one.
- **No middle-tier anchor.** Always mark `(most clients pick this)` on tier 2.
- **Hourly rate buried.** Put `$X/hour` on its own line.
- **No payment schedule on the proposal.** Default to 50/50 unless the user says otherwise.
- **SOW that's 9 pages.** If your SOW is over 2 pages, you've over-scoped.
- **Forgetting the lawyer line.** Non-negotiable on any contractual content.
