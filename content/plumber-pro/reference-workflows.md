## 4. Sewer scope report

```
SEWER SCOPE REPORT — 412 Maple Drive — May 14, 2026

Equipment used: Ridgid SeeSnake CS6x, accessed via the basement floor cleanout
Distance run: 78 ft total to the city tie-in
Material observed:
- 0-12 ft: cast iron (interior to foundation wall)
- 12-78 ft: clay tile (foundation to street, original to home — circa 1962)

Findings:
- 0-12 ft: Cast iron in fair condition. Minor scale on the interior wall, no pitting or cracking observed.
- 18 ft: Joint offset of approximately 1/2" — modest, water flowing past but a catch point for debris over time.
- 36 ft: First significant root intrusion. Hair roots covering approximately 40% of the cross-section. Coming in at a joint.
- 51 ft: Second root intrusion, more substantial. Approximately 60% of the cross-section restricted. Water passing but slowly. This is the likely cause of the slow drain symptom you've been seeing.
- 51-65 ft: Bellying in the clay tile (sag in the line). Standing water visible in this section. Not catastrophic but it's where solids will settle over time.
- 65-78 ft: Improvement in line condition, runs into the city main.

Recommendation:

Tier 1 — Maintenance (Monitor):
Annual mechanical root cutting and hydro-jet. Keeps the line flowing but doesn't fix the underlying degradation. Approximate cost: $450-650/year.

Tier 2 — Targeted Repair (Recommended):
Cured-in-place liner (CIPP) for the 30-65 ft section (clay tile with root intrusion and belly). Done from the cleanout, no excavation. Eliminates the root intrusion path and stiffens the bellied section. Approximate cost: $4,800-6,500 depending on access.

Tier 3 — Full Replacement:
Excavate and replace the clay from foundation to street with new PVC. Major project, requires city permit, lawn/driveway disruption. Approximate cost: $12,000-18,000 depending on length and access.

What this means for you:
The line isn't an emergency. It's flowing, just slowly. You have months, not weeks, before the slow-drain symptom turns into a backup. If you see water in the basement floor drain or hear gurgling from upstairs fixtures, that's the signal it's gone from slow to blocking.

My recommendation: Tier 2 (lining). It's not the cheapest option but it gets you another 30+ years out of the line without tearing up the yard. Tier 1 is reasonable if you're planning to sell in the next 1-2 years and want to disclose the condition rather than fix it.

Photos and video on file. Happy to walk through with you when you're ready.

— Tom, Lic. #P-1847
```
