# Buyer + Seller Follow-up Email Cadences

Day 0, 3, 7, 14, 30. Full copy, not outlines. Two parallel tracks because a fresh buyer lead needs a different cadence than a seller deciding between three agents next spring.

The emails below are written to be edited. Replace bracketed inputs and personalize the second sentence of each email — that's the one that decides whether it reads as human or as a sequence.

---

## How to use these cadences

1. After you load `optimization-pack.md` in your AI tool, paste the relevant cadence below and say: "Use this as the cadence shape. Personalize for [lead context]."
2. Give the AI the lead's name, the property or context they engaged with, anything they told you ("they're moving from out of state," "they have a closing date constraint," "they're between two areas"), and your sign-off style.
3. The AI returns five emails personalized to that lead.
4. You scan each one in ~30 seconds, edit the one or two lines that don't sound like you, and queue them up.

This is faster than templating in your CRM and produces better-converting emails because every cadence is one-shot personalized instead of mail-merged.

## Buyer track

The buyer track assumes the lead has shown active interest — registered for a search, attended an open house, asked a question on a listing, or been referred. It does not assume readiness to buy this month.

### Day 0 — same day as the inquiry

**Subject:** [neighborhood] homes — saved you a search

```
Hi [first name],

Thanks for reaching out about [specific property or "homes in [neighborhood]"]. A few quick things to get the search useful from day one:

1. I set you up with a saved search on [MLS portal / Zillow / Realtor.ca]. You should get an email with the link in the next hour. New listings hit it the moment they go live, which beats refreshing Zillow.

2. I pulled three listings I think are worth a look this week based on what you described. They're in the email below.

3. If you have 15 minutes this week for a quick call, I'd like to ask the four questions that make the difference between an okay buyer experience and a great one — budget range, timeline, must-haves vs nice-to-haves, and what already turned you off.

When works? I have [two specific time windows].

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes for the AI:**
- If they were referred by a past client: open with "[Past client name] mentioned you were looking — they don't refer people lightly, so I appreciate the intro."
- If they came from an open house: open with "It was good meeting you at [address] on [day]. The light in the back of that house really did sell me on the layout."
- If they're out-of-state: add a paragraph about how you handle remote buyers (FaceTime tours, your inspector network, etc.)

---

### Day 3 — momentum check

**Subject:** Saw two new listings this morning

```
Hi [first name],

Two new ones hit the MLS this morning that fit what you described. I've put them below with my honest take on each — the second one is the stronger fit but I want you to see both so we calibrate.

[Listing 1]
[Listing 2]

Want me to set up a showing on either? I have [day, time window] open.

One more thing — when you saved [property they engaged with on day 0], was it the layout, the price point, or the neighborhood that caught you? It helps me sort signal from noise on what to send.

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- If they haven't responded to day 0 yet, day 3 should be shorter — three sentences max — and end with a single question.
- Don't include actual listing details the AI doesn't have. Leave placeholders for you to fill in real listings.

---

### Day 7 — the calibration email

**Subject:** A few honest questions

```
Hi [first name],

A week in, want to share what I've noticed. The listings I've been sending lean toward [specific characteristic — older charm, newer build, certain price point] — let me know if that's the right read or if I should shift.

Three questions that usually save buyers 2-3 months of looking:

1. What's the highest price you'd write an offer at if the house was perfect — and what's the highest you'd write if it had three things you'd want to change?
2. Are you comfortable with renovation, or is move-in condition non-negotiable?
3. Is there a specific timeline trigger — lease ending, school year, job start — or is this open-ended?

No rush on these. But if you can give me a sentence on each, the search gets dramatically better.

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- If they've been responsive, ask the questions. If they've gone quiet, just ask one.
- Always frame the questions as "this saves you time" — never as "I need to know this to help you."

---

### Day 14 — the market context email

**Subject:** What's happening in [neighborhood] this month

```
Hi [first name],

Quick market update for [neighborhood / their target area]:

- [#] homes listed in the last 14 days
- [#] went under contract
- Median list-to-sale ratio: [%]
- Average days on market: [#]

The short version: [one sentence on what's actually happening — "inventory is tight, expect competition" / "more inventory than last month, you have some leverage" / "specific to your price band, [observation]"].

If you want a full walk-through of what's moved in your price band this month — I'm happy to put 30 minutes on the calendar.

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- The AI will not know real market numbers. Leave placeholders. This is one email you fill in by hand from your MLS market stats.
- If they're in a slow phase ("we're looking but not until fall"), shift this email to: "Here's what to watch over the next 60 days that would change your timeline."

---

### Day 30 — the long-game pivot

**Subject:** Still here when you're ready

```
Hi [first name],

Quick check-in. The market update goes out monthly going forward — same format as the one last week, no action needed unless something in it catches your eye.

Two things to flag:

1. If your timeline has shifted (earlier or later), tell me. The search and the pace change with it.
2. If you've decided this isn't the right time, I'd rather know. I'd much rather pause the search and pick it back up in [3 / 6 / 12] months than send emails you're deleting.

Either way — I'm not going anywhere. Reach out when it's the right time.

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- The "I'd rather know if you're not buying" line is the most important sentence in the cadence. It releases the pressure and almost always gets a reply.
- After day 30, switch to a monthly market update email and a quarterly check-in. Drop the daily-cadence feeling completely.

## Seller track

The seller track assumes the lead has expressed interest in listing — requested a home value estimate, attended a listing presentation, been referred by a past seller, or asked questions on a "sell your home" page. It does not assume they're ready to list this month.

### Day 0 — same day as the inquiry

**Subject:** [their address] — pulled some numbers

```
Hi [first name],

Thanks for reaching out about a possible sale. Before we even get to a meeting, I wanted to send you what most agents skip on the first email — actual numbers.

I pulled comps for [address or neighborhood]. Based on what's sold in the last 90 days and what's currently active, the range I'd start the conversation at is [low] to [high]. That's a starting point, not a final number — the final number depends on three things I can't see from public data: condition, your timeline, and what you've put into the home.

If you'd like a real CMA — the kind I'd put in front of you at a listing appointment — I can have one ready by [day]. It takes me about two hours to do it properly, so I want to make sure it's useful to you before I run it.

Two questions to start:
1. Are you in the "thinking about it" stage or the "want to list in the next 90 days" stage? Both are fine — the conversation is just different.
2. Is there a timeline trigger (job, downsizing, relocation, estate) or is this an "if the number is right" sale?

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- The "I can have a real CMA ready" line distinguishes you from agents who send a Zestimate. Don't skip it.
- Don't quote a range you can't defend. If you don't have comps yet, write "I'm pulling comps now and will have a range by [tomorrow]."

---

### Day 3 — the home value email

**Subject:** Your home value range

```
Hi [first name],

I ran the comps for [address]. Here's what I found:

Recent sales (last 90 days) in the immediate area: [3-4 lines summarizing]

Currently active and pending: [1-2 lines]

My read: a defensible list price range for your home is [low] to [high], with the most likely sale price landing around [middle].

The spread depends on three things:
1. Condition relative to comps — Comp [X] was renovated, Comp [Y] was original. Where does your house sit?
2. Timing — listing in the next 30 days hits [spring / fall / market context]. Different in 90 days.
3. Pre-list prep — there are typically 3-5 low-cost moves that return 2-4x what they cost. Worth discussing if you're 30+ days out from going live.

Want to put 45 minutes on the calendar this week to walk through the full CMA in person? I'll come to you.

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- This email is the actual product. Fill it in with real comps. The AI can format it once you paste in the data — see `cma-prompt.md`.
- If you can't get real comps in 3 days, send a shorter "I'm still pulling the comps, here's why it's taking another day" note instead.

---

### Day 7 — the pre-list prep email

**Subject:** 5 things that move the needle before listing

```
Hi [first name],

If you're thinking about listing in the next 30-90 days, here are the five pre-list moves that consistently return more than they cost. In order of return on investment:

1. **Paint** — light neutral throughout, focused on rooms that show first. Average cost in [your market]: $[range]. Typical return: $[range].

2. **Floors** — if hardwood, refinish. If carpet, replace the worst 1-2 rooms. Not all of it.

3. **Light fixtures and hardware** — kitchen, primary bath, entry. Small cost, disproportionate effect on perceived condition.

4. **Decluttering and storage** — sometimes the biggest move on the list. A storage unit for 60 days often pays back 5-10x.

5. **Landscaping at the curb** — the first photo is the front. Trim, mulch, mow, edge. Skip the rest of the yard if budget is tight.

I have trades I trust for each of these in [neighborhood / city]. Happy to make intros. None of them pay me referral fees — I just don't send my sellers to people who'll do a bad job.

If you want to walk through any of this in person, I have [2 specific time windows].

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- Have specific dollar ranges for your market. If you don't know them, ask three contractors before sending.
- The "none of them pay me referral fees" line is the trust line. Don't include it if it's not true. If it is true, say it every time.

### Day 14 — the market timing email

**Subject:** [Their market] — is this the right window?

```
Hi [first name],

Wanted to send a quick market read specific to your price band and neighborhood.

Last 30 days in [their area]:
- [#] new listings
- [#] under contract
- Average DOM: [#]
- List-to-sale ratio: [%]

What it means for you: [one or two sentences].

Three timing scenarios to think about:

**List in next 30 days:** [pros + cons specific to your market right now]
**List in 60-90 days:** [pros + cons]
**Wait until [season]:** [pros + cons]

There's no universally "right" answer — it depends on your timeline, your condition, and your price band. If you want to walk through the three scenarios with the actual numbers attached, let's set up 30 minutes.

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- The three-scenarios framing is the most valuable part. Sellers default to "wait until spring." Sometimes that's right; often it isn't.
- Fill in real numbers from your MLS market stats. The AI won't have them.

### Day 30 — the long-game pivot

**Subject:** Still here when you're ready to list

```
Hi [first name],

A month in, two things:

1. The market updates will continue monthly. Same format, no action needed unless something jumps out.

2. If your timeline has firmed up (sooner or later), let me know. The strategy is different at each timeline.

I'd rather you tell me you're not selling this year than send emails you're deleting. Either way works for me — I just want to know which one we're in.

Whenever you're ready, the CMA and the pre-list plan are an hour away.

[Sign-off]
```

**Tuning notes:**
- Same release valve as the buyer day-30 email. The honesty of "tell me if you're not selling" is what differentiates this cadence from a drip.
- After day 30, switch to a monthly market update with a quarterly check-in.

---

## The single edit that doubles reply rates

After the AI generates the cadence, find the second sentence of every email and rewrite it to reference something specific about the lead. Not "great to hear from you" — something like "I noticed you saved three listings in the [neighborhood], which is the same area my last two sellers came out of" or "Your note mentioned you're moving from Seattle — the transit comparison alone is going to be a shock in a good way."

That second sentence is the difference between "this person is using software on me" and "this person actually read what I wrote." Worth the 30 seconds per email.
