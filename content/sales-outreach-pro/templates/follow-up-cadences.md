# Follow-up Cadences: Day 0, 3, 7, 14, 21

Full email copy for each touch in a 5-step cadence, plus the bump emails that get the highest reply rate when used right, and the breakup email that ends the sequence cleanly.

The cadence below is calibrated for B2B outbound. Adjust intervals shorter (every 2-3 days) for warm inbound, longer (every 5-7 days) for senior executives.

---

## The cadence at a glance

| Day | Type | Length | Channel |
|-----|------|--------|---------|
| 0 | Cold opener (PAS / BAB / AIDA) | <75 words | Email |
| 3 | Bump — new angle | <40 words | Email |
| 7 | Value add (resource, no ask) | <60 words | Email |
| 14 | Hard ask with social proof | <60 words | Email |
| 21 | Breakup | <40 words | Email |

Variations: at day 5, layer in LinkedIn — connect with a short note referencing the value, not the ask. At day 12, a 60-second video message (Loom) often outperforms email.

## Day 0 — Cold opener

This is the framework email from `cold-email-frameworks.md`. PAS, BAB, or AIDA depending on the signal. Under 75 words. One ask.

**Example (BAB, B2B SaaS, security tooling to a CISO):**

**Subject:** what most CISOs miss

```
What I see at most Series C SaaS companies: SOC 2 audit prep takes 8-12 weeks of one person's time per renewal.

The teams using us: audit prep takes 4-6 days because evidence is collected continuously, not gathered the month before.

The difference is integration depth — we pull from 80+ tools instead of 30, so most evidence is already there when you need it.

Datadog and Notion run their audit prep on us. Happy to introduce.

15 min next Tuesday?

[Sign-off]
```

## Day 3 — The bump, with a new angle

The biggest mistake at day 3 is "just following up on my last email." Don't. The day-3 email should sound like a separate email with new information — not a reminder.

Structure:
1. New angle or new information (one line)
2. Tie to value (one line)
3. Same single ask (one line)

**Example (continuing the security tooling thread above):**

**Subject:** new SOC 2 framework

```
Following AICPA's update last month, the SOC 2 framework now expects continuous monitoring evidence rather than point-in-time snapshots.

This is the move our customers prepared for two years ago. Most teams are now scrambling.

15 min this week to walk through what the change actually requires?

[Sign-off]
```

**Why it works:** The new angle is real (regulatory framework update). The implication is real (their existing process won't pass cleanly). The ask is the same. The prospect doesn't feel pursued — they feel informed.

**Bump emails that don't work:**
- "Just bumping this up in case you missed it" (no new value)
- "Did you see my last email?" (passive-aggressive)
- "Wanted to circle back" (corporate filler)

## Day 7 — Value add, no ask

The day-7 email earns trust by sending value with no ask attached. Most outbound sequences skip this step. It's the highest-ROI email in the cadence because it inverts the dynamic.

Structure:
1. Brief context (one line — "saw you might be working on X")
2. The resource — a link, a doc, a quick observation
3. Acknowledgment that there's no ask attached

**Example:**

**Subject:** SOC 2 readiness scorecard

```
Building on the framework note — we put together a 1-page SOC 2 readiness scorecard. Twelve questions, takes about 10 minutes. Tells you where the new framework is going to expose gaps.

[link]

Not asking for a call this time. Just thought it would be useful given the timing.

[Sign-off]
```

**Why it works:** The recipient doesn't have to do anything. The resource is genuinely useful. The next time you ask for a call, you're a person who sent something useful — not a person who keeps emailing.

**Note:** the resource has to be real. If you don't have a scorecard, write one. If you don't have time, send a 3-paragraph observation. Don't fake this.

## Day 14 — The hard ask, with social proof

By day 14, the prospect has heard from you three times. Now you ask directly. Add the proof point that maps to their stage.

Structure:
1. Reference the value/observation from day 0 or day 7 (one line)
2. The proof point — specific customer, specific outcome (one line)
3. The ask — slightly bigger frame (15 minutes becomes 20)

**Example:**

**Subject:** 20 min next week?

```
Two weeks in on the SOC 2 thread — wanted to make a direct ask.

Notion came to us at exactly your stage and shipped a continuous-monitoring stack in 7 weeks. Their CISO is happy to take a reference call.

20 min next Tuesday or Wednesday to see if there's a fit?

[Sign-off]
```

**Why it works:** Reference-call offers convert because they shift the perceived risk from "is this vendor real" to "this vendor is already endorsed by a peer."

**Note:** never offer a reference call you can't deliver. Always ask the reference customer first.

## Day 21 — The breakup

The breakup email closes the sequence cleanly and frequently produces the highest reply rate of the entire cadence — because prospects feel the social pressure of being asked to confirm a "no" instead of a passive ghost.

Structure:
1. Honest framing (one line)
2. Single direct question (one line)
3. Soft close

**Example:**

**Subject:** close the loop?

```
Three weeks of emails — I'm going to assume the timing isn't right and stop reaching out.

Quick question: was this a "wrong time" or "wrong fit"? Either answer is fine. Knowing helps me not bother you again.

If neither, I'll go quiet. Thanks for the consideration.

[Sign-off]
```

**Why it works:** The "either answer is fine" line removes the awkwardness of declining. Prospects who would never reply to a follow-up will often reply to a breakup with "actually, the timing was bad — circle back in Q3" or "wrong fit, we use [competitor], thanks."

**Variations:**
- For very senior prospects: drop the "stop reaching out" line — it can read as pressure. Use: "Going to assume the timing isn't right. If that changes, I'm here."
- For warm leads who attended a webinar: replace the breakup with a re-engagement: "Anything change since we last connected?"

## After the cadence

If the prospect didn't reply: pause for 90 days. Then re-engage with a one-shot email tied to a new signal — they got promoted, raised, hired, shipped, posted. Not a sequence. One email.

If the prospect replied "wrong time, circle back in Q3": mark the date in your CRM. Reach back out ON that date, not before. Don't drip in the meantime.

If the prospect replied "wrong fit, we use [competitor]": send a single closing email thanking them and asking what they like about the competitor. The answer is competitive intel.

If the prospect replied with an objection: switch to the objection-handling shapes in `playbooks/objection-handling.md`.

## The bump-email pattern (between scheduled touches)

When a prospect was engaged, then went quiet, and you need a bump that's not in the scheduled cadence — use this 8-word template:

**Subject:** [lowercase question]

```
Worth a 15-min look next week?

[Sign-off]
```

Or:

**Subject:** still relevant?

```
Quick check — is [topic from earlier exchange] still on your list?

[Sign-off]
```

Eight to fifteen words. No "wanted to follow up." The brevity is what makes it reply-worthy.

## Channel mixing

The 5-step email cadence is the floor. Strong sequences layer in:

- **Day 5: LinkedIn connect with a 200-character note.** Reference the value, not the ask. "Saw you posted on [topic] — would be useful to compare notes sometime. No agenda."
- **Day 12: Loom video, 60 seconds.** Open with the prospect's name and the specific signal. Show your face. Don't pitch — describe the value in plain language. End with: "If this is useful, here's my calendar. If not, no offense."
- **Day 18: Direct mail (for enterprise deals only).** A handwritten note, a relevant book, a small physical object that ties to the value. Skip if your deal size is under $25K ACV.

Don't pile all channels on without coordination. The prospect gets three emails, a connection request, a Loom, and a piece of mail in three weeks — that's about right. Five emails and three LinkedIn DMs and an unsolicited call is harassment.

## A note on subject lines

Across the entire cadence, subject lines should:

- Be under 30 characters by day 3 onward (mobile preview is the goal)
- Use lowercase if it fits the voice — "soc 2 audit prep" reads more human than "SOC 2 Audit Prep"
- Avoid "Re:" or "Fwd:" fakery
- Avoid generic openers: "Quick question," "Following up," "Touching base"
- Reference one specific noun — the prospect's company, a tool, a number, a date

The strongest subject lines are tied to the content of THAT specific email, not the campaign or product overall.

---

## What this cadence doesn't fix

- Bad ICP targeting (sending great emails to the wrong people)
- Bad deliverability infrastructure (no DMARC/SPF/DKIM, no warmup, shared sender domain)
- Bad pipeline math (sending 50 emails a week and expecting enterprise pipeline)

The cadence above is the writing layer. The other three layers — list, infrastructure, volume — sit underneath and aren't this kit's job.

If your reply rate is below 3%, the problem is usually the list or the infrastructure, not the email copy. Diagnose before you tune.
