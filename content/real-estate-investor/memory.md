# Memory — Real Estate Investor Pack

## Domain context

The user is a working real estate investor — typically operating a portfolio of 1-50 single-family or small-multi doors, or running a fix-and-flip business doing 1-10 deals a year, or working on their first or second small multifamily syndication. They are often still W-2 employed and squeezing the business into evenings and weekends. They live in one or two metros they know cold and occasionally chase a deal out-of-market. They are not the bus-tour guru, they are not the hedge fund acquisitions analyst, and they are not the home shopper. They are an operator.

The week is deal flow (driving for dollars, list pulling, direct mail, agent calls), underwriting (the spreadsheet, the comps, the rehab quote), outreach (mailers, cold calls, agent follow-ups), and document drafting (LOIs, PSAs in coordination with counsel, JV memos, partner updates). Most deals don't happen. The few that do hinge on whether the LOI was clean enough to actually close, the underwriting honest enough to hold up post-closing, and the partner pitch credible enough to fund. Bad documents waste months and burn reputation in small markets.

Real estate is local. Submarkets within metros behave differently. State law on LOIs, securities exemptions for JV structures, wholesaling licensure, eviction procedures, and tenant rights varies enormously. The kit defaults to US conventions but flags Canadian context (Alberta, Ontario, BC are the most common Canadian operator markets) where the rules diverge.

## Vocabulary the AI should know

- SFR: single-family rental
- MFR / small multi / mid multi: multi-family rental (small = 2-4 units, mid = 5-50 units typically)
- BRRRR: Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat
- ARV: after-repair value
- LOI: letter of intent (typically non-binding except for specified clauses)
- PSA: purchase and sale agreement (binding)
- EM / EMD: earnest money deposit
- DD: due diligence (period)
- T12 / T3 / T1: trailing 12 months / trailing 3 months / trailing 1 month operating statements
- NOI: net operating income (revenue minus operating expenses, before debt service and capex)
- Cap rate: NOI / purchase price; going-in cap is at acquisition, exit cap is at sale
- Cash-on-cash return: annual pre-tax cash flow / cash invested
- IRR: internal rate of return
- DSCR: debt service coverage ratio (NOI / annual debt service); DSCR loans qualify on the property's cash flow not the borrower's personal income
- LTV / LTC: loan-to-value / loan-to-cost
- HELOC: home equity line of credit
- Hard money: short-term high-rate financing typically used for flips
- Bridge loan: short- to medium-term financing typically used to acquire and reposition
- Agency financing: Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac multifamily loans
- Subject to / sub-to: buying a property while leaving the seller's existing mortgage in place (controversial, state-dependent, frequent litigation source)
- Seller financing: seller carries a note for some or all of the purchase price
- Land contract / contract for deed: alternative to traditional financing where seller retains title until paid
- 1031 exchange: tax-deferred exchange of like-kind property (US)
- Cost segregation: accelerated depreciation strategy
- Bonus depreciation: accelerated depreciation provision (US, phasing down post-TCJA)
- QOZ: Qualified Opportunity Zone
- Reg D / 506(b) / 506(c): securities exemptions for raising private capital
- Wholesale: contracting to buy and assigning the contract to an end buyer; licensure-sensitive in many states
- Driving for dollars: physically driving neighborhoods looking for distressed properties
- Skip trace: looking up an owner's contact info from address
- Probate / pre-foreclosure / NOD list: distressed-seller list categories
- LLC / SMLLC / partnership / Sub-S: common holding entity structures
- Series LLC: state-specific structure allowing multiple "series" under one LLC
- Land trust: title-holding device, popular in some states for privacy
- Asset protection: structure designed to insulate assets from liability
- Quitclaim / warranty / special warranty deed: deed types varying in title warranties from seller
- Title insurance: protects against title defects; owner's policy covers buyer
- Survey: defines exact boundaries; required by some lenders

## Common workflows

- **Direct mail campaign.** Trigger: investor pulls a list (absentee owners, pre-foreclosure, probate, tax-delinquent). Steps: (1) skip trace, (2) draft letter using the structure in SKILL.md, (3) print and mail, (4) handle inbound calls with the script, (5) track response rate (typical 0.5-2%). Output: mailer copy + call script.

- **Cold call campaign.** Trigger: investor has a list and a dialer. Steps: (1) script the opening, qualifier, offer frame, and close, (2) draft voicemail drop, (3) qualify inbound, (4) book walkthrough, (5) submit LOI. Output: call script + voicemail script.

- **LOI submission.** Trigger: walkthrough done, numbers underwritten, offer to make. Steps: (1) underwrite at conservative ARV and rehab, (2) back into max offer (MAO), (3) draft LOI using attorney-blessed template, (4) email or hand-deliver, (5) follow up. Output: LOI draft + cover note.

- **JV memo for capital partner.** Trigger: deal that requires outside capital. Steps: (1) underwrite the deal at multiple scenarios (base, upside, downside), (2) draft the one-pager and detailed memo, (3) name the structure (split, decision rights, sale trigger), (4) flag securities exposure for attorney, (5) deliver. Output: one-pager + JV memo.

- **Underwriting refresh on a multi.** Trigger: broker package on a small/mid multi. Steps: (1) verify T12 expenses against typical ratios for the asset class, (2) compare in-place rents to market via local sources, (3) build pro forma at multiple exit-cap assumptions, (4) sensitivity-test interest rate and rent growth, (5) decide go / no-go / counter. Output: underwriting note + offer recommendation.

## What to avoid / common mistakes

- LOIs with binding language that look non-binding. Exclusivity, confidentiality, and the obligation to negotiate in good faith can all create binding obligations even within a "non-binding" LOI. Flag every time.
- Wholesaling marketing in jurisdictions where unlicensed wholesaling is problematic (some states are aggressive about this — flag and recommend counsel).
- Sub-to / subject-to transactions that don't disclose the due-on-sale risk to the buyer or the seller. State law varies on enforceability.
- Direct mail or call scripts that misrepresent who's calling ("we're looking for our forever home" when you're an investor).
- Partner pitches that imply guaranteed returns, principal protection, or specific tax outcomes — securities-law violations.
- Pro formas that omit vacancy, capex reserve, property management, or contingency. These are not bonus line items; they are the cost of operating.
- ARV claims based on Zestimates or unsupported comps. Three actual closed comps within 0.5 mi and 90 days, with adjustments, or nothing.
- Fair-housing-fragile language in tenant outreach or marketing ("looking for the right family," "perfect for young professionals"). Describe the unit, not the tenant.

## Tone / register

Hustle-respecting but not bro-y. Numbers-grounded. Comfortable with risk talk because everyone in this business has had a deal go sideways. The voice is an operator who's closed 20 deals and lost money on at least one. No exclamation points. No "transformational," no "won't last," no "lifetime opportunity." Honest about what you've done and haven't. Partner pitches that read like a confident operator beat partner pitches that read like a brochure.
