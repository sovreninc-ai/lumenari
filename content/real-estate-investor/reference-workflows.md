# Reference Workflows — Real Estate Investor Pack

Seven worked templates. Copy and adapt. Every LOI and partner-facing document passes attorney review before sending.

---

## 1. LOI for SFR purchase — worked example

Deal: 1432 N Talbott St, Indianapolis. Off-market, seller responded to direct mail. SFR, 3/1, 1,180 sqft. Buyer is ABC Holdings LLC.

```
LETTER OF INTENT
[Date]

[Seller Name]
[Seller Address]

Re: 1432 N Talbott St, Indianapolis, IN [zip]

Dear [Seller],

ABC Holdings LLC ("Buyer") is pleased to submit this non-binding Letter of Intent to purchase the property located at 1432 N Talbott St, Indianapolis, IN from [Seller Name] ("Seller") on the following principal terms:

1. PURCHASE PRICE
$98,000, payable as follows: $2,000 earnest money deposit to be delivered to the title company within 3 business days of LOI execution; balance of $96,000 in cash at closing.

2. DUE DILIGENCE PERIOD
Twenty-one (21) days from LOI execution. During this period, Buyer may inspect the property (interior and exterior), review title commitment and any survey, and review any rental history, utility records, or property records provided by Seller. Buyer may terminate this LOI for any reason during the due diligence period, with full refund of the earnest money deposit.

3. FINANCING
All cash, no financing contingency.

4. CLOSING
Forty-five (45) days from execution of a definitive Purchase and Sale Agreement, or sooner by mutual agreement.

5. TITLE AND SURVEY
Buyer to pay for title insurance (owner's policy) and any survey. Title to be conveyed by general warranty deed, free and clear of liens other than current-year taxes prorated to closing.

6. AS-IS, WHERE-IS
Property to be conveyed in its current condition. Seller makes no representations as to physical condition beyond ordinary representations regarding title, authority to convey, and known material defects required by Indiana law.

7. EXCLUSIVITY / NO-SHOP
During the due diligence period, Seller agrees not to actively market the property or accept other offers. (NOTE: this clause is intended to be binding even though this LOI is otherwise non-binding — see paragraph 9.)

8. CONFIDENTIALITY
The terms of this LOI are confidential between the parties and their advisors.

9. NON-BINDING
This Letter of Intent is non-binding except for paragraphs 7 (Exclusivity / No-Shop) and 8 (Confidentiality), which are intended to be binding. No agreement to purchase or sell exists between the parties until a fully executed Purchase and Sale Agreement is signed.

10. EXPIRATION
This LOI expires and is automatically withdrawn if not signed and returned by Seller by 5:00 PM Eastern on [date 5-7 days from delivery].

Sincerely,

[Buyer signature]
[Name], Managing Member
ABC Holdings LLC

ACCEPTED AND AGREED:
___________________________  Date: __________
[Seller Name]

---
Not legal, tax, or investment advice. LOIs and partner-facing documents should be reviewed by a real estate attorney before sending. Underwriting outputs are illustrative — investor confirms numbers.

Things to verify before sending:
- Indiana-specific LOI language and material defect disclosure rules — confirm with real estate attorney before using this template
- Paragraph 7 exclusivity language — confirm enforceability and scope under Indiana law
- Deed type (general warranty vs special warranty) — confirm market expectation for off-market SFR in Indianapolis
- Earnest money amount and escrow location — confirm seller is comfortable with title company holding, not seller's attorney
```

## 2. Direct mail letter — worked example

Absentee owner list pull, Indianapolis east side. Owner inherited the property three years ago, lives in Florida.

```
[Investor Letterhead OR Plain — single-page typed]

[Date]

[Owner Name]
[Owner Mailing Address — Florida]

Re: 4218 E Washington St, Indianapolis, IN

Hi [Owner First Name],

My name is [Investor First Name]. I'm a real estate investor based in [Indianapolis / nearby], and I've been buying houses on the east side of Indianapolis for [X years / since 2022].

I noticed your property at 4218 E Washington St has been a rental (or vacant — adjust based on what you know), and I wanted to reach out directly in case you've ever thought about selling. I'd be looking at this as a private cash purchase — no real estate agents, no commissions, no fixing anything up before closing. You'd pick the closing date, within reason.

I'm not promising a top-of-market number. If you're hoping to sell at full retail with a renovated price, an agent and the MLS is the right path. But if you'd like a clean cash offer with a short timeline and no hassle, I'd like to make one.

You can reach me directly at (317) 555-0142. My name's [First Last], and I'll answer if you call between 7 AM and 8 PM Eastern. If I miss you, leave a message and I'll call back the same day.

No pressure — if it's not the right time, I'd appreciate a quick "not interested" so I can take you off my list.

Best,
[First Last]
[Investor / Phone]

Not legal, tax, or investment advice. Underwriting outputs are illustrative — investor confirms numbers.

Things to verify before sending:
- Phone number is a live answer or voicemail-equipped line you actually monitor
```

## 3. Cold call script + voicemail — worked example

Same list, calling instead of mailing. Or following up on mailers that didn't get a response.

```
COLD CALL SCRIPT

OPEN
"Hi, is this [Owner First Name]? My name is [Your First Name], I'm calling about the property at 4218 East Washington Street in Indianapolis. Is now an okay time for a quick question?"

[If "no, who is this / I'm at work / not interested" — respond: "Totally understand. Quick question — would it be okay if I called back later in the week, or would you rather I just take you off my list?" Respect the answer.]

THE QUALIFIER
"I'm a local investor — I buy houses in the area, and I noticed the property on East Washington. I'm not calling to push anything. I was just wondering if you've ever considered selling, or are you pretty set on holding it?"

[LISTEN. The longer they talk, the better. Don't fill silence.]

THE OFFER FRAME (only if there's any opening)
"What I'd be looking at is a private cash purchase — no agents, no commissions, you pick the closing date. I'd want to see it before making an offer, of course, just so we're working with real numbers. Would you be open to a 15-minute walkthrough this week or next?"

[If interest — schedule. If hesitant — offer to email a one-pager on how the process works.]

THE CLOSE (one of two)
- "If now's not the right time, no problem. Want me to check back in a few months, or just leave it?"
- "If it'd be easier, I can email you what the typical process looks like — no commitment. What's a good email?"

---

VOICEMAIL DROP (if they don't answer)

"Hi [Owner First Name], this is [Your First Name] calling about the property at 4218 East Washington Street in Indianapolis. I'm a local investor — not calling about anything urgent, just wanted to see if you'd ever considered selling. If you have a minute to call back, my number is 317-555-0142. That's 317-555-0142. No pressure either way. Thanks, [Your First Name]."

---
Not legal, tax, or investment advice.

Things to verify before sending:
- Phone number is your actual line and is answered live during stated hours
- TCPA compliance — Indiana follows federal TCPA; if cold-calling cell phones at scale, verify your list scrubbing and consent posture
- Do-Not-Call registry scrubbing on the list
```

## 4. JV memo for a flip — worked example

Deal: 8th Ave bungalow in Atlanta, fix-and-flip. Operator has 4 prior flips. Raising $85K from a single capital partner.

```
JV MEMO — [PROPERTY ADDRESS], ATLANTA — [DATE]

THE PROPERTY
1407 8th Ave SE, Atlanta, GA 30315. Bungalow, 2/1, 1,040 sqft on a 6,000 sqft lot. Built 1947. Off-market, under contract via direct mail outreach to absentee owner.

THE NUMBERS

Acquisition: $148,000 (under contract)
Closing costs (purchase): $4,500
Rehab budget: $62,000 (scope attached as Appendix A — full gut, new kitchen, full bath rebuild + add half-bath, HVAC + electrical, refinish floors, exterior + landscape)
Rehab contingency (15%): $9,300
Holding costs (5 months @ ~$1,800/mo: taxes, insurance, utilities, debt service): $9,000
Selling costs: ~$14,500 at projected ARV (6% agent commissions, 1% closing, transfer tax)

ALL-IN COST: $247,300

ARV: $310,000
Comps:
- 1422 9th Ave SE, 2/2 (renovated), sold 47 days ago: $308,000
- 1239 8th Ave SE, 3/2 (renovated, slightly larger lot), sold 62 days ago: $325,000
- 1518 10th Ave SE, 2/1.5 (renovated), sold 81 days ago: $295,000
(Comps map and dets attached as Appendix B)

PROJECTED PROFIT: $62,700
PROJECTED CASH-ON-CASH ON OPERATOR EQUITY: TBD based on structure
PROJECTED HOLD: 5 months acquisition to sale

THE STRUCTURE

This is a single-deal JV between [Investor Name] / ABC Holdings LLC ("Operator") and [Partner Name] ("Capital Partner").

Capital contribution:
- Capital Partner: $85,000 (acquisition + rehab + reserves)
- Operator: $62,300 (acquisition + rehab + reserves) plus 100% of sweat equity (general contracting, project management, sales coordination)

Debt: None on the file. All cash through closing and rehab.

Profit split: 50/50 after return of both parties' capital.

Decision rights: Operator has day-to-day operational control. Capital Partner consent required for: scope changes that materially increase budget (>10% delta), extending hold beyond 7 months, accepting a sale offer below $295K, refinance vs sale decisions.

Sale trigger: Sale when (a) the property is listed and a qualifying offer at or above $295K is received, or (b) both parties agree, or (c) at the 7-month mark, regardless.

Distributions: At closing of sale, after return of both parties' contributed capital, 50/50 split of net proceeds.

THE OPERATOR

[Investor Name] has closed 4 flips since 2022 in Atlanta and the inner-ring suburbs. Three were profitable (ranging $34K-$71K net), one broke even (a 2023 deal where scope discovery in foundation work added ~$22K to the rehab budget and held the project an extra 3 months). Average gross margin on the four: 18% on ARV. Operator currently holds two SFR rentals and has no other active flips at the time of this memo.

THE RISKS

Specific to this deal:
- ARV could come in lower if comp dispersion widens in the next 60 days. Atlanta southeast neighborhoods saw 4-7% movement over the last 6 months — directionally positive but not guaranteed.
- Rehab scope discovery is the biggest single risk. The 15% contingency is on the higher end of what I usually budget, reflecting the 1947 vintage and one wall I haven't gotten behind yet.
- Days-on-market at sale could extend if interest rates move and buyer affordability tightens. Holding cost assumes 5 months; 7 months would compress profit by ~$3,600.

General:
- Real estate is illiquid. Capital is committed for the duration of the project.
- No guaranteed return. Possible loss of contributed principal.
- This is a single-deal JV between two parties. It is not a registered security offering, and Capital Partner is investing as an active partner with consent rights as defined above.
- Operator's prior performance is described above; past results don't predict this deal.

NEXT STEPS

1. Capital Partner reviews this memo and appendices (rehab scope + comp pack).
2. Both parties sign a JV operating agreement (drafted by Operator's real estate attorney, [Counsel Name]).
3. Capital Partner wires $85,000 to title company within 3 business days of agreement signing.
4. Closing scheduled for [date].
5. Weekly written update to Capital Partner from kickoff through sale.

---
Not legal, tax, or investment advice. LOIs and partner-facing documents should be reviewed by a real estate attorney before sending. Underwriting outputs are illustrative — investor confirms numbers.

Things to verify before sending:
- Securities exposure on this JV — confirm with real estate / securities attorney that Capital Partner's consent rights are sufficient to keep this out of "passive investor" territory under federal and Georgia state securities law
- Comps verified in MLS or county records (not just from a wholesaler's claim)
- Rehab scope reviewed by GC, not just operator estimate
- Operator's prior deal results are accurate and not embellished
- ARV and exit assumptions confirmed against current MLS data, not stale by more than 30-60 days
```

## 5. Underwriting note — small multi

Deal: 8-unit small multi offered at $1.4M in Charlotte. Broker T12 attached. Operator pulling apart the package.

```
UNDERWRITING NOTE — [Address], Charlotte, NC

Type: 8-unit apartment building, all 1BR
Asking: $1,400,000 ($175,000/door)
Going-in cap (broker stated): 6.5%
Operator's read: needs work before this is a 6.5%.

INCOME — BROKER T12 VS OPERATOR REWORK

Broker stated gross income: $128,400 ($1,338/door/mo avg)
- In-place: $112,800 (3 units below market, 5 at market)
- "Pro forma" stabilized: $134,400 — broker assumed all 8 units at $1,400/mo

Operator rework:
- Market rent verified at $1,325-$1,400/mo per Zillow + RentRange + a call to two local PMs
- Take $1,375/mo at 95% occupancy (5% vacancy on this asset class is reasonable in Charlotte)
- Stabilized gross: $125,400 — $9,000 less than broker pro forma

EXPENSES — BROKER VS OPERATOR REWORK

Broker stated expenses: $36,200 (28% expense ratio)
This is fiction. 28% expense ratio on a small multi this size is not real. Working from the T12 line by line:

- Taxes: $11,400 (current); likely reassessed at sale to ~$14,800 based on Charlotte's reassessment pattern. Use $14,800.
- Insurance: $6,200 (T12); 2024 quotes coming back at $7,400. Use $7,400.
- Maintenance: $4,800 (T12); deferred — this is too low. Use $7,500 ($940/door).
- Capex reserve: $0 (broker omits — standard for broker pro formas, not real). Add $6,400 ($800/door, 8% of gross).
- Property management: $0 (broker assumes self-management). Use $10,000 (8% of gross, which is real labor cost even if the operator self-manages).
- Utilities (water, sewer, common-area electric): $4,800 (T12).
- Trash: $1,200.
- Other (legal, accounting, advertising): $1,800.

Operator's expense total: $53,900 (43% expense ratio — within the 40-55% sanity band)

NOI ANALYSIS

Broker NOI: $128,400 - $36,200 = $92,200 → asks 6.6% cap at $1.4M
Operator NOI: $125,400 - $53,900 = $71,500 → at $1.4M is a 5.1% cap. Not the 6.5% on the marketing.

DEBT SERVICE

Assume DSCR loan at 70% LTV ($980K), 30-year amort, 7.25% rate (current DSCR market): annual debt service ~$80,400. DSCR at operator NOI: 0.89 — doesn't qualify.

To qualify at 1.2 DSCR, NOI would need to be ~$96,500 — which the deal doesn't deliver at asking.

EXIT CAP ASSUMPTION

Charlotte small multi has traded between 5.5% and 7.0% over the past 24 months depending on quality. Going-in should be at-or-above exit, not below. Operator's assumed exit cap: 6.5%.

OFFER POSITIONING

At a 6.5% cap on operator's stabilized NOI of $71,500, the offer is $1,100,000. That's $300K below ask.

Counter logic if seller pushes back:
- Walk through the expense reconciliation line by line
- Acknowledge market rent growth could close some gap, but the going-in is what it is

DECISION

Pass at asking. Submit at $1,050,000 with the underwriting attached as backup. Walk away if seller won't move below $1,200,000.

---
Not legal, tax, or investment advice. Underwriting outputs are illustrative — investor confirms numbers.

Things to verify before sending or acting:
- All T12 expense lines verified against actual carrier bills / tax records / utility bills (request from broker)
- Reassessment estimate confirmed with Mecklenburg County (NC reassesses on sale)
- Insurance quotes from 2 carriers, not assumed
- Property management cost benchmarked against 2 local PMs
- DSCR lender quote pulled with current credit profile
```

## 6. BRRRR deal writeup — worked example

Deal: 1432 N Talbott St, Indianapolis (the same property from the LOI in workflow 1, post-close walkthrough of the full BRRRR cycle). The point of this writeup is to show the cycle end-to-end: purchase, rehab, rent, refi, what stays in the deal.

```
BRRRR ANALYSIS — 1432 N Talbott St, Indianapolis IN

STRATEGY
Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. Single-family, 3/1 → 3/2 (adding bath during rehab), buy-and-hold long-term, refi to pull capital back out for the next deal.

ACQUISITION
Purchase price: $98,000 (off-market, LOI accepted)
Closing costs (buyer side): $2,400 (title, recording, attorney fee, prorations)
Acquisition financing: hard money, 12% IO, 2 pts origination ($1,960 in points), 6-month term
- Loan amount: $98,000 (100% of purchase, hard money lender comfortable on the ARV)
- Cash to close from buyer: $4,360 (closing + points, no down payment)

REHAB
Scope: cosmetic + add a 3/4 bath in the existing back-hall closet area (plumbing already runs through the wall behind it), new HVAC, refinish hardwoods, new kitchen cabinets + counters + appliances, new roof.
Budget: $45,200 base + 15% contingency ($6,800) = $52,000 total
- Bath addition: $11,400 (plumbing rough + finish, tile, vanity, toilet, fan)
- Kitchen: $14,800 (cabinets $7,200, counters $2,400, appliances $3,200, paint + labor $2,000)
- HVAC: $8,800 (full system, ductwork acceptable)
- Roof: $7,200 (architectural shingle, full tear-off)
- Floors: $2,400 (refinish, not replace)
- Interior paint + minor drywall: $1,800
- Exterior touch-up + landscape: $1,400
- Contingency: $6,800 reserved

Rehab funded out of pocket from operator's reserve account. Hard money lender did not draw rehab (some HML do, this one didn't on this deal).

Hold-through-rehab costs (3.5 months): $4,900 (taxes, insurance, utilities, hard-money interest at 12% IO on $98K = $980/mo)

ALL-IN COST AT END OF REHAB: $98,000 + $2,400 + $1,960 + $52,000 + $4,900 = $159,260

RENT
Stabilized monthly rent: $1,550 (verified against three recent leases within 0.5 mi for similar 3/2 SFRs, all $1,500-$1,625)
Occupancy assumption: 95% (5% vacancy is honest for B-class east-side Indy SFR)
Effective annual gross rent: $1,550 × 12 × 0.95 = $17,670

Operating expenses (annual):
- Taxes (post-reassessment estimate): $1,800
- Insurance: $1,200
- Maintenance: $1,200 (~7% of gross, mid of 5-10% band, mid because rehab is fresh)
- Capex reserve: $1,200 (7% of gross)
- Property management: $1,400 (8% of gross — operator self-manages but books the labor)
- Total opex: $6,800

NOI: $17,670 - $6,800 = $10,870

REFINANCE
ARV (current, post-rehab): $189,000 — pulled three closed comps within 0.4 mi in the last 60 days at the time of the refi appraisal ($186K, $191K, $195K — mine is mid-pack on finish quality, slightly under on lot size)

DSCR loan terms (DSCR lender quote pulled, locked):
- 70% LTV: $132,300 cash-out
- 30-year amort, 7.40% rate, 1 point ($1,323 in points)
- DSCR calc: NOI $10,870 / annual debt service $11,008 = 0.988 DSCR — does NOT clear most DSCR lenders' 1.0-1.2 floor

Adjust the file to clear the lender:
- Lender accepts gross rent / PITI rather than NOI / debt service for some DSCR products
- Gross rent / PITI: $1,550 × 12 = $18,600; PITI annual = $11,008 + $1,800 taxes + $1,200 insurance = $14,008; ratio = 1.33 — clears
- (This is product-specific. Operator confirmed the calc directly with this lender's underwriter.)

Refi proceeds (after closing costs): ~$130,000

CAPITAL POSITION AFTER REFI
Cash in deal pre-refi: $4,360 (acquisition) + $1,960 (HML points) + $52,000 (rehab) + $4,900 (carry) = $63,220 of operator capital tied up
Hard money payoff at refi: $98,000 (principal — no prepay penalty after month 3)
Refi proceeds available after HML payoff: $130,000 - $98,000 = $32,000
Net capital remaining in deal: $63,220 - $32,000 = $31,220

POST-REFI CASH FLOW
Monthly rent: $1,550
PITI (P&I $920, T $150, I $100): $1,170
Monthly net before opex reserves: $380
Monthly opex (PM, maintenance, capex reserve allocated monthly): $317
Monthly cash flow: $63

Cash flow is thin — $63/mo on $31,220 of remaining capital = 2.4% cash-on-cash on remaining equity. This deal works as a long-term hold because (a) the remaining $31,220 capital recycles into the next deal, (b) loan paydown + appreciation build equity, (c) rent growth at 2-3%/yr expands the cash-flow margin over time.

WHAT THE "INFINITE RETURN" MARKETING GETS WRONG
$31,220 is still in this deal. This is not an infinite-return deal. It's a 19.6% capital efficiency event (pulled $32K of $63K back out at refi) on a long-hold rental that needs years for the cash flow to make sense in isolation. The right way to underwrite this is total return over hold + equity build, not "cash-on-cash on $0 remaining."

DSCR + LIQUIDITY POSITION
DSCR at the calc the lender used: 1.33. Real DSCR (NOI / debt service): 0.99. The lender funded; the deal is real-world barely covering. Operator's liquidity reserve on this property: 4 months PITI = $4,680 set aside in a property-specific account.

EXIT OPTIONS
- Long hold (10+ years) — the base case
- Sell at 5-7 year mark if Indianapolis east-side appreciation accelerates: assume ARV growth 3%/yr, value at year 5 is ~$219K, equity ~$108K
- 1031 into a small multi at year 5-7 (CPA-driven decision)

---
Not legal, tax, or investment advice. LOIs and partner-facing documents should be reviewed by a real estate attorney before sending. Underwriting outputs are illustrative — investor confirms numbers.

Things to verify before acting:
- DSCR lender calc method confirmed in writing before rehab spend commits — different products use NOI/DS vs gross-rent/PITI
- ARV appraisal risk — appraiser may come in below $189K; build downside scenario at $180K (refi proceeds drop to ~$126K and capital remaining rises to ~$35K)
- Rent comps reverified at month-of-lease-up, not at month-of-purchase
- Reassessment estimate confirmed with Marion County
- Insurance quotes from 2 carriers pre-rehab and post-rehab — premiums can change materially after major work
- Tax strategy on the cash-out refi proceeds — refi cash is generally not taxable but cost-seg eligibility, depreciation schedule, and any partial dispositions are CPA work
```

## 7. Deal analysis framework — the AI-instructable prompt for running a deal

When the investor needs to run a fresh deal through the kit, this is the framework. Paste the deal context and ask the AI to walk it through the 70% rule / MAO math + the underwriting line items.

```
DEAL ANALYSIS PROMPT — paste over the deal context

Run this deal through the standard framework. Use these defaults unless I override:
- Vacancy: 5-10% (default 7% for B-class SFR, 5% for A-class, 10% for C-class)
- Maintenance: 5-10% of gross rent (default 7%, raise if rehab is older than 3 years)
- Capex reserve: 5-10% (default 7%, raise if roof/HVAC/major systems aging)
- Property management: 8-10% (default 8%, book the labor even if self-managing)
- Rehab contingency on flips/BRRRR: 10-20% (default 15%, raise to 20% on pre-1960 vintage or deals with unknown subgrade conditions)
- Selling costs on flips: 8-10% of ARV (commissions + closing + transfer tax + staging + marketing)

OUTPUT IN THIS ORDER

1. STRATEGY CHECK
State the strategy (flip / BRRRR / hold / wholesale / value-add multi) and confirm the asset class fits (don't BRRRR a property the rental market won't lease).

2. ARV / VALUE BASIS
State the ARV or stabilized value with the comp basis. If three valid comps aren't provided, flag and refuse to underwrite further. "Valid" = closed within 90 days, within 0.5 mi, same bed/bath count or adjusted, similar finish quality.

3. ACQUISITION COST STACK
Purchase + closing + acquisition financing points if applicable + any wholesale fee if applicable.

4. REHAB STACK (if applicable)
Itemized scope + contingency line. Refuse to produce a rehab estimate without a scope breakdown; instead, ask the investor to paste their scope from their GC or estimator.

5. HOLDING COSTS
Through stabilization (BRRRR/hold) or through sale (flip). Itemize taxes, insurance, utilities, debt service.

6. SELLING COSTS (flips) or REFI COSTS (BRRRR)
For flips: commissions, closing, transfer tax, staging.
For BRRRR: refi points, closing, appraisal.

7. ALL-IN COST or ALL-IN BASIS

8. THE MAO BACK-CALC
For flips: MAO = (ARV × 0.70) − rehab − closing − selling − desired profit. State each input and the desired profit assumption.
For BRRRR: MAO = backed in from "what does this need to refi at to leave my target capital." Show the math: refi proceeds at 70-75% LTV minus HML payoff minus refi closing = what's pulled out. Solve for purchase price that leaves the target capital position.
For holds: MAO = backed in from "what does this need to cash flow at my target COC on remaining equity, after all opex and PITI."

9. RETURN METRICS
- Flip: projected profit ($), ROI (profit / cash invested), annualized return if hold > 6 months, IRR if hold > 12 months
- BRRRR: capital recovered at refi, capital remaining, cash-on-cash on remaining capital, total return projection over 5/10-year hold
- Hold: cash-on-cash year 1, going-in cap, DSCR, projected stabilized cap

10. SENSITIVITY
At minimum: ARV ±5%, ±10%; rehab overrun +15%, +25%; for multi, interest rate ±100bps, rent growth 0%/2%/4%, exit cap ±50bps.

11. GO / NO-GO / COUNTER
Honest one-paragraph read. If the deal doesn't work, say it doesn't. If it works only under aggressive assumptions, name them and tell me what the conservative read says.

12. DISCLAIMER + SELF-REVIEW
Required footer + "Things to verify before acting" block.

REFUSAL CONDITIONS
- No comps provided → refuse
- Rehab budget not itemized → request itemization
- Exit cap below going-in cap on multi → flag as cap-rate-compression-dependent, model with going-in = exit instead
- Anything that produces a "guaranteed" or "won't lose" frame → refuse and rewrite
```

This prompt itself is the artifact. Save it in your project knowledge and call it when you have a new deal. The AI runs the framework against the deal context you paste.
