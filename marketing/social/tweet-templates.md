# Lumenari — 10 Evergreen Tweet Templates

Each template includes: when to use it, the format, and 2 example fills using real Lumenari kits/products.

Voice: Chris, first person. Direct, decision-oriented. Numbers over adjectives. Slightly contrarian.

---

## 1. Announcement

**When to use it:** New kit shipped, new bundle live, milestone hit, feature launched. Anything where the news IS the value. Keep it factual and let the news do the work.

**Template:**
```
[NEW THING] is live.

[1-2 short factual lines on what it does or what changed.]

[Price/access detail].

[Optional: link]
```

**Example fill 1:**
> The Veterinarian kit is live on Lumenari.
>
> Client communication, intake forms, post-op messages, follow-up scheduling. Calibrated for small-animal clinics.
>
> $19. Works in Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Codex, Gemini.
>
> lumenari.io/kits/veterinarian

**Example fill 2:**
> The Healthcare bundle is live.
>
> 5 kits — Pharmacist, Nurse Practitioner, Physical Therapist, Veterinarian, plus the new Clinic Manager kit.
>
> $69. ~30% off vs. buying separately.
>
> lumenari.io/bundles/healthcare

## 2. Behind-the-scenes

**When to use it:** Weekly. Founder-update style. What you actually shipped, in plain language, no hype.

**Template:**
```
This week on [project]:
- [Concrete shipped item 1]
- [Concrete shipped item 2]
- [Concrete shipped item 3]
- [Optional: 1 thing that broke / 1 lesson]

[Optional: 1-line reflection.]
```

**Example fill 1:**
> This week on Lumenari:
> - 3 new kits (Veterinarian, Estate Planning, Travel Planning)
> - Wizard now handles 5 more roles
> - Stripe checkout latency cut by 40%
> - Caught 1 bad bug in the API
>
> Boring. Necessary.

**Example fill 2:**
> This week on Lumenari:
> - Custom GPT export pipeline rebuilt
> - 14th API integrator went live
> - Killed the Crypto Trader kit (wrong fit)
> - Wrote the Photographer kit voice-profile flow
>
> Killing a bad kit beats shipping it loud.

## 3. Case study

**When to use it:** When you have (or have approximation of) a real user outcome with numbers. Always flag hypotheticals as `[hypothetical]` to keep your credibility.

**Template:**
```
[Hypothetical/Real flag] [Persona]. [Context]. Used [Lumenari kit] for [duration].

[Specific outcome with numbers].
[Specific consequence — what they did with the time/money].

[Kit cost]. [Punchline framing].
```

**Example fill 1:**
> [hypothetical] Solo agent in Edmonton. 22 listings/year. Used Lumenari Real Estate kit for 60 days.
>
> Time saved on listing copy + comp analysis: ~6 hrs/week.
> Used that time to door-knock instead. Closed 3 extra deals.
>
> The kit cost $19. The deals were five figures.

**Example fill 2:**
> [hypothetical] One-truck plumber in southern Alberta. Bought the Lumenari Plumber kit + Trades bundle for $69.
>
> Hours per week on quotes/admin: 9 → 4.
> Got those 5 hours back on the truck. ~$2K/month more revenue.
>
> 19x ROI in month 1.

## 4. Listicle

**When to use it:** When you have 3 sharp points that fit in a single tweet. Forces compression. Avoid 5+ items — that's a thread, not a tweet.

**Template:**
```
3 things nobody tells you about [topic]:

1. [Sharp point — counterintuitive ideal]
2. [Sharp point — practical]
3. [Sharp point — earned-from-experience]

[Optional 1-line takeaway.]
```

**Example fill 1:**
> 3 things nobody tells you about building an AI kit catalog:
>
> 1. The template matters more than any individual kit
> 2. The buyers who refund teach you more than the buyers who don't
> 3. Curation is harder than generation, and that's the moat
>
> Build the template first.

**Example fill 2:**
> 3 things nobody tells you about cold outreach in 2026:
>
> 1. Volume is dead. Better is alive.
> 2. The research takes longer than the writing — that's the work
> 3. If your subject line was generated by a "subject line tool," it shows
>
> Send fewer. Send better.

## 5. Quote

**When to use it:** When you have a quotable insight that stands alone. One-line setup, one-line context. The quote is the post.

**Template:**
```
"[Quotable insight, ideally <20 words]"

— [Source: you, a customer, a tester, a contrarian framing]

[Optional: 1-line of context or follow-up.]
```

**Example fill 1:**
> "Generic prompts produce generic output. A structured kit changes the shape of what comes back."
>
> — The thesis behind every kit on Lumenari.

**Example fill 2:**
> "I went to school to learn light. Nobody told me 60% of the job is writing emails."
>
> — A wedding photographer testing the Lumenari Photographer kit.
>
> The kit handles the 60%. Voice intact.

## 6. Controversial take

**When to use it:** When you have a defensible opinion that invites real disagreement. NOT for cheap rage-bait. The test: would you defend this on a podcast for 20 minutes?

**Template:**
```
Hot take: [Specific contrarian claim].

[1-2 lines of why — earned reasoning, not rant.]

[Optional: invitation to disagree.]
```

**Example fill 1:**
> Hot take: prompt engineering is the wrong frame.
>
> The win isn't a cleverer sentence. It's giving the model a structured role, context, examples, and constraints.
>
> That's a kit, not a prompt.

**Example fill 2:**
> Hot take: the "10,000 prompts for $19" packs are not a deal. They're a search problem you now own.
>
> Curation is the product. Always was.

## 7. Before/after

**When to use it:** When the same task with vs. without a kit produces a clear, demonstrable difference. Best when paired with a screenshot or a short example in the replies.

**Template:**
```
Same task. Two outputs.

WITHOUT [kit/structure]: [1-line summary of generic output]
WITH [kit/structure]: [1-line summary of structured output]

[Lesson — short, sharp.]
```

**Example fill 1:**
> Same listing notes. Two outputs.
>
> Without the kit: "Beautiful 3-bed home with hardwood floors and abundant natural light."
> With the Real Estate kit: comp-aware narrative with 4 specific neighborhood signals, buyer-targeted framing, and a 25-word MLS variant.
>
> Structure beats cleverness.

**Example fill 2:**
> Same prospect. Two cold emails.
>
> Without the kit: "Hi {{firstName}}, we help SaaS companies scale outbound."
> With the Sales Cold Outreach kit: opens with a trigger event from the last 14 days and ties it to a role-specific pain in 3 lines.
>
> Reply rate roughly doubled.

## 8. FAQ

**When to use it:** When the same question keeps coming in your DMs or replies. Answer it publicly so 100 people get the answer instead of one.

**Template:**
```
People keep asking [question]. Short answer: [1-line answer].

Longer answer:
- [Reason or detail 1]
- [Reason or detail 2]
- [Reason or detail 3]
```

**Example fill 1:**
> People keep asking why Lumenari kits work in Claude AND ChatGPT AND Cursor AND Codex AND Gemini.
>
> Short answer: every kit ships in 4 formats.
>
> - SKILL.md (Claude, Cursor)
> - Custom GPT (ChatGPT)
> - Optimization pack (Codex)
> - memory.md + quick-start (Gemini)
>
> Buy once. Use everywhere.

**Example fill 2:**
> People keep asking if Lumenari has an enterprise tier. Short answer: no.
>
> - Teams just buy the bundles they need
> - No "talk to sales" page
> - No volume discount maze
> - The Operations bundle for a 50-person team is a Stripe checkout
>
> Simple pricing IS the product.

**Template:**

- [Real number 3]
- [Real number 4]

[1-line honest reflection.]

> Onward.

>

> - 38 bundle purchases

## 10. Gratitude

**When to use it:** When you actually mean it. Specific names, specific actions, specific impact. Vague "thank you, community!" tweets are noise — they hurt more than they help.

**Template:**
```
Thank you to [specific person/group] for [specific action]. [Specific impact it had.]

[Optional: 1-line of what's next or how others can do similar.]
```

**Example fill 1:**
> Thank you to the 14 integrators who hit the Lumenari public API in the first 30 days. Three of you flagged a pagination bug I never would have caught solo.
>
> If you're building anything in the agent space and want early API access, my DMs are open.

**Example fill 2:**
> Thank you to the plumber who tested every version of the Trades pack. He rebuilt my drafts twice with "no plumber would write that," and he was right both times.
>
> The kits in your category get better when someone in the category will tell you the truth.
