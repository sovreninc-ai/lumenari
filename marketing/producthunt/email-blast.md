# Email Blast — Launch Morning

Sent at 9:00am PT on launch day to the personal network. Voice is friend writing to friend. The product needs to be discoverable from the email even by people who've never heard of ProductHunt.

---

## Subject line — 3 variants

1. **Launching Lumenari on ProductHunt today** — direct, no mystery, respects the inbox. My pick.
2. **The thing I've been building between shifts is live** — works for the close circle who already know what I've been building. Personal, slightly intriguing.
3. **Something I'd love your honest take on** — softest of the three, leans on relationship. Use only for people who would open it because it's from me, not because of the subject.

## Preview text — 2 variants

1. **The catalog of AI skill kits I've been building for the last 6 months. Free to try, feedback welcome.**
2. **100 curated AI skill kits, live today. No pressure to buy — but I'd love your honest read on it.**

---

## Body (220 words)

Hey friends,

Quick one — Lumenari is live on ProductHunt today. It's the project I've been building between pipefitter shifts for the last 6 months and I wanted you to know about it before anyone else.

What it is: a curated catalog of 100 AI skill kits. Each kit is a structured pack that gives Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Codex, or Gemini the rules, context, and format it needs to actually be useful for a specific job — writing client briefs, planning a soccer practice, building a sales discovery script, debugging a Postgres query, the kind of thing.

The catch most people hit with AI is that generic prompts produce generic output. The skill kit fixes that. There's a free recommendation wizard on the homepage that finds the right kit for your use case in under a minute — try it before anything else.

What ProductHunt is, if this is the first you're hearing of it: a daily leaderboard of new products that the builder/tech crowd cares about. Doing well there gets the product seen by the right audience for the next year.

If you've got 30 seconds today, take a look. Honest feedback (good or bad) is way more valuable to me than an upvote.

Site: lumenari.io
ProductHunt: [link]

Thanks for reading.

Chris

P.S. If you do end up on the PH page and want to leave a comment, that'd genuinely help — the algorithm weighs comments harder than votes, and a real reaction from someone who knows me carries more weight than 10 strangers clicking up.

---

## Who to send this to

**Tier 1 — closest circle (~20 names):** family, lifelong friends, the people who've watched me build for 3 years. They don't need persuading; they need the link. Personalize the first line of each one — name + one specific reference ("hope soccer season's settling down"). Send manually, not via mail merge.

**Tier 2 — supportive professional contacts (~30 names):** former coworkers, people from the trades community, parents from the soccer club, anyone who's expressed real interest in the side projects. Use the email as written, lightly personalized. Don't expect every one to engage — sending is the point.

**Tier 3 — extended network (~30 names):** people I've had real conversations with in the last year but who aren't day-to-day. Soften the opener ("you came to mind because [one specific reason]"). Skip the P.S. — the soft ask reads pushy to a semi-warm contact.

Hard pass on: people I haven't spoken to in 18+ months, anyone I only know via LinkedIn connections, anyone I've never had a real exchange with. Mass-blasting old contacts is what kills future trust.
